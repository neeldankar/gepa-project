You will be given three fields as input:
- `claim`: a factual statement making one or more assertions or relationships about people, events, or entities.
- `notes`: a list of extracted fact statements, possibly partial or overlapping, relevant to the claim.
- `context`: a list of detailed background or encyclopedic excerpts from various topics, people, events, or entities related to the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: A distilled, concise, and accurate set of fact statements synthesized from both `notes` and `context` that directly address the truthfulness or components of the `claim`. These should:
   - Correctly affirm or refute each key assertion in the `claim`.
   - Include relevant temporal, identity, and role-based facts.
   - Clarify any misattributions, such as mistaken identities, wrong dates, or incorrect participations.
   - Avoid redundancy and contradiction.
   - Be factual and well-supported by evidence in the inputs.

2. `titles`: A list of relevant document or topic titles from the `context` that best capture or summarize the key evidence or entities involved in verifying or debunking the claim. These usually correspond to people’s names, specific events (e.g., championship seasons, films), organizations (e.g., production companies), or notable works (e.g., films, songs). These titles serve as documents that support your conclusions and can be considered key "gold documents" relating to the claim.

General approach and domain knowledge to apply:
- Understand the domain context for each claim: e.g., motorsports seasons with driver participation and winners; films with casts, production studios, and release years; animation projects with voice actors; etc.
- Recognize seasonal and yearly mappings in championships (e.g., "28th season" = year 2000).
- Differentiate individuals by nationality and role (e.g., distinguishing Finnish from Spanish rally drivers, or American from French actors).
- Verify claims about co-starring actors, production companies, and roles using evidence in notes and context.
- When multiple versions or sequels exist (e.g., "The Lion King" 1994 vs. 2019 remake), ensure facts correspond to the correct iteration.
- Acknowledge contradictory evidence to clearly indicate why a claim is false or partially false.
- Use the input information to produce factually accurate and well-organized output, omitting irrelevant details.

Keep in mind from prior feedback that:
- It is critical to retrieve and list all relevant document titles that cover all key aspects needed to verify/refute the claim.
- The new notes must summarize and clarify precisely, avoiding repeated or conflicting information.
- The titles should pinpoint major entities or works central to the claim for traceability.

In summary, your outputs should enable a reader to understand exactly which facts from the input support or refute the claim and where to find these facts in the referenced documents or topics.
