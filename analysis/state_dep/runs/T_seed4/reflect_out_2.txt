- `claim`: A factual or evaluative statement relating to a specific topic (e.g., historical events, people, operas, TV shows, sports tournaments).  
- `notes`: A list of sentences or brief assertions extracted from some sources, which may confirm, deny, or partially inform the claim. These notes might contain repeated or overlapping information and may include irrelevant or incomplete facts.  
- `context`: A list of paragraph-like segments or snippets providing background information, detailed descriptions, or metadata about entities, events, or subjects relevant to the claim.

**Task description:**  
1. **Interpret the claim** carefully, understanding what factual information or relationship it asserts (e.g., about persons, historical events, dates, compositions, TV series timelines, competitions, or biographical details).  
2. **Analyze the notes and context** to verify or clarify the claim. Identify which pieces of information directly support or contradict the claim, or provide crucial clarification or resolution of ambiguities in the claim.  
3. **Filter the notes into a concise and relevant set (`new_notes`)** that:  
   - Contains factually correct, corroborated assertions from the `notes` and `context` that directly address key points in the claim.  
   - Removes duplicates, redundancies, or irrelevant material.  
   - Summarizes factual verification or refutation for the claim.  
4. **Produce a list of document titles (`titles`)** that correspond to the most relevant or authoritative sources, entities, or topics which substantiate, clarify, or dispute the claim based on the given context. Titles should reflect:  
   - Key people, events, shows, operas, sports tournaments, or other named entities involved in the claim.  
   - Specific works or productions, including alternate versions (e.g., multiple operas with the same name by different composers).  
   - Any related series, roles, or timelines necessary to appropriately contextualize the claim.

**Domain-specific factual considerations:**  
- Claims often involve historical details (dates, event outcomes), biographical data (e.g., when a player started competing), or artistic works (e.g., operas and their composers, TV series and their airing dates).  
- Operas may share titles but have different composers and premiere dates; distinguish versions accordingly.  
- TV series details may involve actor roles and timelines; a distinction must be made if an actor joined a different series later.  
- Sports facts may reference match results, players, and tournaments; correctly identify players and their match outcomes.  
- Clarify possible confusion caused by similarly named entities (e.g., multiple operas named "Manon Lescaut", different tennis players named Albert, or multiple TV series with overlapping info).  
- Use precise, historically and contextually accurate facts to confirm or deny claims.

**Generalizable strategy:**  
- Read the claim carefully to identify required facts or relationships.  
- Cross-check notes against context to filter for relevance and correctness.  
- Remove duplicate or irrelevant notes.  
- Present a minimal, factually supported subset of notes that address the claim explicitly.  
- Select document titles that correspond specifically to key entities or topics involved in the claim, ensuring no major relevant source is omitted.  
- Avoid including unrelated or only tangentially related titles, but include all necessary ones to support or refute the claim fully.

This approach ensures that the output is a factually coherent, concise, and properly sourced summary suitable for validating or understanding the claim's truthfulness or accuracy.
