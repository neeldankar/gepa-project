Task Instruction for the Assistant:

You will be provided with three sets of inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that often involves historical facts, biographies, associative relationships among individuals, locations, events, organizations, awards, or other named entities, which needs to be evaluated or contextualized.  
- `notes`: a collection of fragmented observations, evidence points, assessments, or preliminary analyses related to the claim, often containing some repetition or overlap.  
- `context`: an array of text snippets, article summaries, or document extracts relevant to the claim’s topic, which may include named entities such as people, places, events, organizations, awards, or reports.

Your task is to:  
1. Carefully analyze the claim against the provided `notes` and `context` inputs, using a structured evidence-synthesis approach to assess whether the claim is supported, contradicted, only partially supported, or remains unsubstantiated given the input information.  
2. Identify the most relevant and authoritative evidence points from `notes` that accurately confirm, refute, or clarify the claim, consolidating them into a clear, concise, and non-redundant summary (`new_notes`). Eliminate repetition, emphasize factual precision, and ensure the notes reflect the logical relationship of the evidence to the claim (i.e., supported, unsupported, consistent, or contradictory).  
3. Extract a concise list of relevant document or article titles from the provided `context` that offer key traceable sources or authoritative insight for verifying or understanding the claim. When claims involve specific individuals, locations, events, or entities, include titles corresponding directly to those key topics. Use exact titles as presented in the `context` inputs for consistency and traceability.

Additional domain- and task-specific considerations:  
- Claims often pertain to multiple components at once (e.g., a person’s role, an award’s recipient, an organization’s location). Your synthesis must address each component with supporting or refuting evidence, or explicitly state if verification is lacking for any part.  
- Pay attention to factual nuances such as distinctions between someone being a player vs. a coach, winning a medal in a particular location vs. elsewhere, or a band’s city vs. county base. Identifying and highlighting such details from overlapping notes is essential.  
- The assistant’s reasoning and outputs must be objective, strictly confined to the facts supported by the combined notes and context. No speculation or inferring beyond what is presented is allowed.  
- When evidence is ambiguous, incomplete, or partial, clearly indicate the unresolved or unverified aspects of the claim to avoid false confirmation.  
- Titles to report as relevant should include those that specifically illuminate the key entities or concepts in the claim, even if the full document is not wholly related.  
- Repeated notes with slight variations should be merged for clarity and conciseness without losing key factual elements.  
- The reasoning behind the synthesis should be internally consistent and demonstrate meticulous comparison between claim, notes, and context before concluding.

In summary:  
Your outputs must provide:  
- `new_notes`: a distilled, precise, and concise factual synopsis reflecting all key evidence relevant to the claim’s verification (confirmation, refutation, or unsubstantiated points).  
- `titles`: a precise list of source document or article titles from the `context` input that provide essential backing for the claim evaluation.  

This approach enables clear, fact-based claim verification with reliable traceability to authoritative sources. Maintain accuracy, clarity, and completeness while avoiding redundant or irrelevant details.
