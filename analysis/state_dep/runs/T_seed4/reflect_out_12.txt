Task Instruction for the Assistant:

You will be given three input fields: 
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that needs to be evaluated or contextualized,
- `notes`: a list of observations, assessments, or evidence fragments related to the claim, extracted from various sources or preliminary analyses,
- `context`: a list of reference texts, article summaries, or document snippets relevant to the topic of the claim.

Your task is to process these inputs and produce two output fields:
1. `new_notes`: a concise, refined set of notes that synthesize the most relevant and precise evidential points from the input `notes`, focusing on confirming, refuting, or clarifying the claim based on the given `context`. The new notes should eliminate redundancy, emphasize factual accuracy, and clearly indicate whether the claim is supported, unsupported, or logically consistent given the backing information.
2. `titles`: a list of document or article titles from the provided `context` that are most pertinent and useful for verifying or understanding the claim. These should reflect key sources or named entities involved in the claim’s subject matter.

Key domain- and task-specific considerations:

- Claims often revolve around historical facts, biographies, or associative relationships among individuals, locations, or events.
- Context sources can include named people, places, events, organizations, reports, or awards. Recognizing and including these as titles helps provide the traceable background for claim validation.
- Notes can contain repetition or overlapping evidence; distill them to remove redundancy and increase clarity about the claim’s verification.
- The reasoning behind new_notes should reflect whether the claim is corroborated, contradicted, unsupported, or logically consistent with the context.
- When claims reference specific individuals, events, or entities (e.g., “Rainer Höss,” “Backstreet Boys residency,” “Poet-diplomat Nobel laureates”), identify the corresponding key documents or contexts that best capture authoritative information about these.
- If the claim merges multiple components (e.g., a location + ownership, or multiple persons grouped in one statement), ensure new_notes and titles address each component comprehensively.
- In absence of explicit confirmation in the context, clearly note that verification is lacking or that some parts of the claim remain unsubstantiated.
- When generating titles, prefer the exact document or article names as in the context list, even if they appear partial or abbreviated, to ensure consistency and traceability.
- Maintain objectivity: do not speculate beyond what the context and notes support.
- Applying a structured reasoning approach—carefully compare the claim to the evidence/context, identify supporting or refuting details, and summarize results in new_notes—is essential for consistent and reliable outputs.

In summary: Your outputs must provide a clear, factual synopsis of evidence bearing on the claim, and supply precise references (titles) to key source documents from the context that validate or illuminate the claim. Your response should be complete yet concise, factual yet synthesized, to facilitate traceable claim verification.
