You will be given input fields: `claim` (a factual assertion or statement) and `notes` (a list of factual sentences, observations, or evidence snippets related to the claim). Your task is to produce the output field `query`, which should be an information-seeking question (or a set of concise search queries) that can be used to verify or investigate the truthfulness of the claim by retrieving relevant evidence or documents.

Instructions and background details:

1. **Input format:**
   - `claim`: a statement that asserts a fact or relationship between entities, events, or characteristics.
   - `notes`: a list of extracted factual notes or knowledge snippets which may provide partial, ambiguous, or confirming/contradicting evidence about the claim.

2. **Goal:**
   - Generate one or more precise, information-seeking queries that could guide a retrieval system or a fact-checker to find relevant documents, evidence, or data that confirm or refute the claim.
   - The queries should be focused, specific, and formulated based on the key entities, relationships, attributes, and temporal or categorical constraints implied by the claim and supported or challenged by the notes.

3. **Guidelines for formulating queries:**
   - Identify all pivotal entities (people, places, works, organizations) and their roles or actions in the claim.
   - Recognize relationships or attributes whose truthfulness is to be determined (e.g., "X acted in movie Y and also in movie Z," "The main flavor of drink A and drink B," "The type/status of universities associated with individual C").
   - Extract key qualifiers such as dates, roles, nationalities, product flavors, types, or statuses that are relevant to validating the claim.
   - If notes indicate ambiguity, absence, or partial information, include queries that could resolve these unknowns (e.g., "Who is the Welsh actor in [movie]?", "What is the flavor of the drink advertised with X?", "Is the university associated with Y public or private?").
   - Frame queries that retrieve entities or attributes in a way that facilitates comparison or cross-validation related to the claim’s two or more components.

4. **Domain-specific considerations observed from examples:**
   - Movie-related claims often require querying film titles, actor nationalities, or co-acting in sequels and original movies.
   - Beverage claims may involve flavor profiles tied to geographic origin or advertisements linked to songs.
   - University claims involve historical roles of individuals (chairmanship) and classification of universities (public/private).
   - When a claim spans multiple entities or attributes (e.g., actor nationality plus roles in multiple films), the queries should request that information to be able to corroborate or refute the claim holistically.
   - Queries may be natural language questions or structured query language snippets (SPARQL-like) where appropriate, reflecting the structured nature of the underlying data or knowledge base.

5. **Generalizable strategy:**
   - Use the claim to identify discrete factual assertions.
   - Consult the notes to detect gaps or confirmatory evidence.
   - Formulate queries that target retrieval of evidence to fill gaps or verify key facts.
   - Aim for queries that can yield grounded evidence for verification steps such as: identifying actors and their nationality, matching role appearances in films, verifying flavor profiles and ad campaigns, or confirming institutional affiliations and classifications.

In summary:  
Given a `claim` and supporting or conflicting `notes`, produce a precise, clear, and information-rich query or series of queries that a fact-checking system or search retrieval process can execute to gather the evidence needed to verify the claim.
