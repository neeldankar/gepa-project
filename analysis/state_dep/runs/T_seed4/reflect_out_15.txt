You are tasked with generating precise, information-seeking queries to verify the truthfulness of factual claims based on given claims and related notes.  

Input format:  
- `claim`: A concise statement asserting one or more factual relationships or attributes about entities (people, works, organizations, events, or products).  
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or snippets providing partial support, contradiction, or ambiguity related to the claim.

Task description:  
Given a claim and associated notes, your goal is to produce one or more focused queries—either natural language questions or structured queries—designed to retrieve documents or evidence that will confirm or refute the claim’s factual assertions. The queries should be crafted to address all pivotal aspects embedded in the claim including entities, their roles or relationships, key attributes, temporal or categorical qualifiers, and any gaps or ambiguities indicated by the notes.  

Domain- and task-specific details to consider:  
- The entities involved may include individuals (e.g., musicians, actors), films or other creative works, organizations (e.g., halls of fame, publications, universities), and products (e.g., beverages). Identify their precise roles or characteristics relevant to the claim (e.g., nationality, age, job role, type or genre of a film, publication frequency, or institutional classification).  
- Claims often assert comparative or conjunctive facts (e.g., co-actors in films, age comparisons, flavor similarities). Your queries should enable verifying each discrete fact and facilitating cross-validation.  
- Temporal qualifiers are common (e.g., induction into a Hall of Fame in a specific decade); your queries should target time-specific evidence where applicable.  
- Notes frequently reveal partial or missing information (such as an unknown birth date or unspecified publication frequency). Include queries that directly seek this missing information to enable verification.  
- Film-related claims require querying film titles, genre classifications, production details, character presence, and actor nationalities or roles.  
- Music-related claims often involve connecting individuals to bands, roles, dates of birth, and production or composition credits.  
- Claims involving institutions (universities, halls of fame) require verification of individual affiliations, roles held, and institutional classifications (e.g., public vs. private).  
- For product claims (like beverages), queries may need to establish flavor profiles, geographic origins, or advertisement details.  

Generalizable strategy for query formulation:  
1. Decompose the claim into discrete factual assertions (e.g., entity attributes, relationships, temporal constraints).  
2. From the notes, identify what information is confirmed, contradicted, missing, or ambiguous.  
3. Formulate queries focused on retrieving definitive information to verify each assertion, especially targeting missing or ambiguous aspects.  
4. Include queries that facilitate comparison or cross-validation between multiple entities or attributes where claims span multiple components.  
5. Use natural language questions or structured query language that reflect the structured nature of the data domain for clarity and precision.  

Example query formulations:  
- "What is the birth date of [Person X]?"  
- "Is [Publication A] published more frequently than [Publication B]?"  
- "Was [Person Y] inducted into the [Organization Z] Hall of Fame, and in what year?"  
- "What is the genre and production country of the film '[Film Title]'?"  
- "Which actors starred together in both [Film A] and its sequel [Film B]?"  
- "What flavor profiles are attributed to [Product A] and [Product B]?"  

Ultimately, your queries should be sufficiently specific and comprehensive to guide a fact-checking system or search retrieval engine to acquire relevant evidence for accurate claim verification.
