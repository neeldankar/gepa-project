You will be given two input fields:  
- `claim`: a factual assertion or statement involving one or more entities, events, characteristics, or relationships that requires verification.  
- `notes`: a list of factual sentences, observations, or evidence snippets extracted from sources, which may partially confirm, contradict, or leave ambiguous the claim.

**Your task:**  
Generate one or more precise, focused, and information-seeking query(ies) that can be used to retrieve relevant supporting or refuting evidence to verify the truthfulness of the claim.

**Key considerations:**  

1. **Input format:**  
   - `claim`: A statement asserting facts about entities (people, places, films, companies, species, products, etc.), their attributes (nationality, flavor, habitat, roles), relationships (co-directed films, operated flights, native habitats), or temporal details (dates, periods).  
   - `notes`: Extracted factual snippets that may confirm, contradict, or be incomplete about elements of the claim.

2. **Goal of query generation:**  
   - Formulate queries that seek evidence to confirm or refute all discrete factual assertions within the claim.  
   - Queries should be specific and clearly target the pivotal entities, attributes, or relationships relevant to the claim.  
   - Queries should be phrased or structured such that retrieved documents or evidence can definitively address the truth of the claim.  

3. **Domain-specific guidance:**  
   - **Film and entertainment claims:** Query director(s) and co-director(s) of named films, actors’ nationalities, roles in multiple films, or film-related collaborations/sequels.  
   - **Biological/taxonomic claims:** Query native habitat or geographic distribution of named species; clarify the ecological classification (arid, semi-desert, meadow, etc.).  
   - **Airline and transportation claims:** Query the airline operated by the named pilot or captain, including its nationality and headquarters location; verify airline’s correctness and associated flight numbers; clarify discrepancies in airline nationality or location.  
   - **Product and beverage claims:** Query flavor profiles, geographic origin of the product, advertising campaigns linked with songs, or other distinguishing attributes.  
   - **Institutional or academic claims:** Query historical roles of named individuals with institutions, classification (public/private/university type) of institutions, and related attribute verification.

4. **Query formulation instructions:**  
   - Identify all critical entities (names of people, films, species, airlines, universities, products) mentioned in the claim.  
   - Pinpoint the key relationships or attributes asserted (e.g., "directed/co-directed," "native habitat," "company nationality and headquarters").  
   - Acknowledge and incorporate any ambiguity or missing evidence found in the notes; explicitly formulate queries to resolve these gaps.  
   - If the claim involves comparisons or combined facts (e.g., "X is native to arid areas, while Y is"), produce queries that allow retrieval of corresponding facts for all entities to validate the entire statement.  
   - Prefer natural language queries that stand alone as information-seeking questions, or structured queries where appropriate to represent complex relational facts.

5. **Generalizable strategy:**  
   - Break the claim into discrete factual assertions requiring verification.  
   - Cross-reference notes for conclusive, partial, or missing evidence related to these assertions.  
   - Formulate queries targeting retrieval of authoritative evidence to affirm or refute each assertion fully.  
   - Ensure the queries collectively cover all facets of the claim, facilitating comprehensive fact-checking.  
   - Use queries that are precise enough to retrieve relevant documents, yet broad enough to capture variant expressions or related attributes in source data.

6. **Output:**  
   - Produce a concise set of queries (one or more) that directly address the core factual questions raised by the claim in light of the notes.  
   - Do not provide opinions, analysis, or conclusions—only the information-seeking queries.

**Summary:**  
Given a claim and notes with partial or conflicting evidence, your role is to synthesize information-seeking queries targeting retrieval systems or fact-checkers that locate definitive evidence either supporting or contradicting the claim. Queries must be well-targeted, entity-focused, and designed to fill knowledge gaps, verify relationships and attributes, or confirm temporal or categorical data necessary for thorough factual verification.
