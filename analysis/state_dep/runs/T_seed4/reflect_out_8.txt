Task Description:

You are given three fields as input:

- `claim`: a sentence or statement that makes a factual or comparative assertion about one or more entities or events.
- `notes`: a list of strings containing extracted detailed evidence, clarifications, corrections, or contextual facts relevant to validating or refuting the claim. These notes often include nuanced factual information or contradictions drawn from the context.
- `context`: a list of text passages, typically derived from various supporting documents, that provide background information, event records, biographical details, or historical summaries related to the entities or subjects involved in the claim.

Your objective is to analyze these inputs and produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: A distilled, precise summary of the evidence, clarifications, and relevant facts extracted from the `notes` and `context` that address the correctness or accuracy of the `claim`. This should consolidate key points from the notes that detail whether the claim is supported or refuted, highlighting any contradictions, missing information, or misattributions. Avoid repetition; focus on the core factual points pertinent to evaluating the claim.

2. `titles`: A list of document or topic titles that would be the most relevant to the claim’s validation or refutation, ideally reflecting the main entities, events, or concepts involved. These titles often correspond to potential source documents or encyclopedia entries about the subjects mentioned in the claim and are drawn or inferred from the context. Strive to retrieve all critical documents relevant to the claim validation, including those about people, events, competitions, groups, or works.

Detailed and Domain-Specific Notes for the Task:

- The claim can involve complex relationships, such as competing involvement in sports tournaments, music album releases, championship wins, or other domain-specific achievements.
- The `notes` field often contains factual corrections or clarifications regarding the claim, e.g., nationality of a player, competition participation timelines, relationship between band names and record labels, or tournament winners versus runners-up.
- The `context` provides detailed background about events (e.g., specific sports seasons, rally championships, music albums, or discographies) and biographies or histories that help verify the claim’s accuracy.
- Often, domain-specific precision is critical, such as distinguishing a player’s nationality, confirming exact competition seasons and results, differentiating similarly named entities (e.g., band vs. record label), and verifying numerical details (e.g., number of albums released).
- Identify if the claim incorrectly links entities (e.g., misattributes origin, participation, or achievements) or lacks sufficient evidence for verification.
- Recognize when information is missing or not available in the context, noting this in `new_notes` and reflecting it in `titles` by selecting documents covering relevant subjects even if data is insufficient.
- The task is not merely binary verification but involves summarizing the nature of verifiable or unverified information and listing precise supporting or relevant documents.
- When multiple related entities or events appear in the claim, ensure `titles` cover all important references, including those not directly mentioned in the `notes` but necessary for a complete understanding.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Carefully parse and cross-reference details from `notes` and `context` about the entities involved in the claim.
- Extract contradictions, supporting facts, and highlight uncertainties.
- Summarize these in a condensed `new_notes` list that captures the essence of the validation evidence.
- Generate `titles` reflecting the key people, events, or topics critical to examining the claim.
- Ensure `titles` cover all fundamental aspects needed for a fact-checking or knowledge-verification task for comprehensive retrieval.
