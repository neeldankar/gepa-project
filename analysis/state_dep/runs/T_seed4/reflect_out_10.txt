Your task is to generate a single, precise, and comprehensive information retrieval query from a given factual claim and related notes, designed to retrieve all authoritative and relevant documents needed to fully verify or refute the claim.

Task Description:

1. **Input**  
   - `claim`: A natural language statement containing one or more factual assertions about specific entities (people, places, organizations), their attributes (birth years, roles, nationalities, professions), relationships (e.g., one person directed a film, band memberships), contextual or comparative facts (e.g., film release dates order, size comparisons), and negations or exclusivity (e.g., “not both members of same band”).  
   - `notes`: A set of evidence snippets or factual data partially related to the claim, providing detail, clarification, or contradictions.

2. **Goal**  
   Produce a single, unified, information retrieval query that optimally balances breadth and specificity such that it enables retrieval of *all* gold-standard documents necessary to fully verify or refute the claim, covering every vital factual component.

3. **Key Responsibilities and Approach**

   A. **Comprehensive Claim Decomposition**  
      - Identify all core factual components in the claim, including:  
        - All relevant entities with exact proper names (e.g., full personal names, precise film titles, complete magazine names).  
        - Attributes tied to entities, such as birth year, nationality, documented roles (e.g., an actor’s role in a specific film), or time-based attributes (e.g., release years).  
        - Relationships between entities (e.g., person X directed film Y; person A was contesting person B).  
        - Comparative or hierarchical facts (e.g., film A released before film B; magazine X and National Journal are not both periodicals).  
        - Negations or exclusivity (e.g., two magazines are not both periodicals).

   B. **Synthesis of Evidence from Notes**  
      - Use notes to:  
        - Confirm or refine exact factual details (spellings, proper titles, verified dates).  
        - Identify contradictions or highlight common confusions (e.g., mixing two distinct actors with similar names).  
        - Extract domain-specific terminology and relationships vital for constructing accurate queries.  
        - Note missing or ambiguous information that must be accounted for in query breadth.

   C. **Query Construction Principles**  
      - The query must explicitly include all relevant entities, relationships, attributes, and comparative elements extracted from both the claim and notes—no key factual component should be omitted.  
      - Employ precise keywords and phrases drawn directly (or logically inferred) from the claim and notes; avoid aliases, unrelated or overly generic terms to maintain precision and avoid noise.  
      - Incorporate multi-entity and multi-relation conjunctions or logical structures enabling retrieval of documents covering all claim facets at once, rather than isolated parts.  
      - Avoid overly narrow phrasing that excludes essential documents nor overly broad wording that introduces irrelevant data.  
      - Use domain-appropriate phrasing for nuanced contexts, for example:  
        - For nationality + birth year + show/commercial role, include these identifiers in the query together.  
        - For geographic or political claims, specify entities hierarchically (e.g., county-city-country) and comparisons (size, population).  
        - For entertainment/artistic claims, include exact film titles, release years, director names, voice actors associated with characters, and temporal order if relevant.  
        - For band memberships, include all relevant musician names, full band genre descriptions if known, song titles, and roles such as “lead singer”.  
        - For temporal claims, specify exact release dates or years.

   D. **Explicit Negation and Exclusivity in Queries**  
      - If the claim asserts something about “not both” or exclusivity between entities or attributes, incorporate terms or structures that are likely to retrieve documents discussing these contrasts or exclusivities.

4. **Stylistic and Practical Notes**  
   - Use concise but explicit phrasing that leaves no aspect of the claim uncovered.  
   - The query should conceptually act as a unified retrieval request, not a patchwork of disconnected keyword sets.  
   - The query need not be a grammatically perfect sentence but must be semantically rich and internally coherent for retrieval systems.  
   - Where ambiguous or uncertain details remain after analyzing notes, include broader terms or alternative names within the query scope to capture all relevant authoritative documents.

In summary, this task requires transforming multi-faceted claims, supported by partially overlapping note evidence, into well-structured, comprehensive, and domain-tailored queries that ensure retrieval of all necessary documents to validate or invalidate the claim confidently and fully.
