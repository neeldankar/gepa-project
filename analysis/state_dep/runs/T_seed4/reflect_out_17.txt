You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a single output field: `query`.

Detailed Task Description:
- The `claim` is a statement or assertion that may contain factual information or multiple interconnected factual points.
- The `notes` field is a list of evidential statements, facts, clarifications, or context snippets that relate directly or indirectly to the claim. These notes can confirm, refute, partially support, or provide background information relevant to the claim.
- Your objective is to synthesize the information from the `claim` and the related `notes` to produce a single clear and concise `query` that could be used to retrieve additional relevant information or documents to verify, clarify, or understand the claim better.

Key Points and Domain-Specific Details:
- The claims often involve precise dates, names (e.g., people, bands, TV shows, military units, films), sequence or timeline events, genre classifications, relational connections (e.g., collaborations, shared titles), or verifiable facts drawn from structured or semi-structured knowledge.
- Notes frequently point out gaps, contradictions, or confirmations about the claim’s specifics, such as formation dates, episode titles, airing schedules, band genres, direct collaborations, or who directed a film.
- The query should focus on the missing, uncertain, or disputed information identified through comparing the claim and notes. This includes verifying dates, verifying connections between entities (people, bands, shows), confirming classifications (e.g., genre), or clarifying relationships or factual correctness.
- The `query` should be phrased as a focused question (or set of linked questions) aimed at resolving the residual uncertainty, guiding retrieval of the most relevant documents or evidence.
- When the notes suggest an inconsistency or error in the claim, phrase your query to pinpoint the factual detail to confirm or deny that element.
- When multiple aspects of the claim are present but not all are fully supported by the notes, your query should aim to cover the key unclear points without redundancy.
- Use explicit names, titles, dates, or descriptors referenced in the claim and notes to formulate the query in a way that would facilitate precise information retrieval.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Identify the central factual assertions or connections in the claim.
- Compare these with the evidence and gaps highlighted in the notes.
- Isolate the key unresolved fact(s) or relationship(s).
- Construct a clear, focused retrieval question that targets these unresolved points, enabling precise validation and supporting retrieval of the most pertinent documents.

Ultimately, the produced `query` is a research question designed to direct evidence retrieval for verifying or clarifying the claim based on its supporting and contradictory notes.
