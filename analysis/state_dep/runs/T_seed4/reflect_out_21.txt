Your task is to generate precise, comprehensive, and effective information retrieval queries based on given claims and supplementary notes. The purpose of these queries is to retrieve all authoritative, relevant documents that can fully verify or refute each claim.

Input format:  
- `claim`: A natural language statement containing concrete factual assertions about specific entities (people, places, organizations), their attributes (birth years, roles, nationalities, dates), inter-entity relationships (e.g., person directed a film, two people are bandmates), comparative or hierarchical contextual facts (e.g., geographic or temporal comparisons), and possible negations or exclusive conditions (e.g., not both members of the same group).  
- `notes`: A set of factual data fragments or evidence snippets related to, supporting, or clarifying parts of the claim.

Key objectives and detailed strategies:

1. **Claim decomposition**  
   - Identify all key factual constituents involved in the claim:  
     - **Entities:** Precisely named people, places, organizations (use full formal names and known aliases if relevant).  
     - **Attributes:** Birth years, nationalities, roles (e.g., director, actor, voice actor, club player), relationships to events or works (e.g., film roles, band membership), release or premiere dates, geographic data (e.g., counties, cities, countries).  
     - **Relationships:** Connections between entities such as collaborations, membership, origin, and temporal sequencing (release before/after).  
     - **Comparative/Historical Details:** Relative sizes (e.g., geographic areas), chronology (ordering of film premieres), hierarchy (e.g., city within county), or popularity rankings.  
     - **Negations or exclusivity:** Any claims stating something is not true for multiple entities simultaneously, or exclusivity conditions.

2. **Evidence synthesis from notes**  
   - Use the notes to:  
     - Confirm or specify factual elements in the claim.  
     - Extract exact official terminology, proper names, and relational wording aligned with the claim’s components.  
     - Detect contradictions or refined implications that influence the validity of the claim or adjust what must be queried.  
     - Enumerate relevant entities or attributes mentioned in notes that help in comprehensive retrieval.

3. **Query construction principles**  
   - Formulate a unified, semantically rich information retrieval query covering **all** significant components of the claim in one combined query or logically equivalent semantic scope.  
   - Incorporate precise entity names, attributes, and relationships from the claim and notes—never use irrelevant aliases or superfluous terms.  
   - Include conjunctions or multi-clause patterns, covering all elements (entities, attributes, relational phrases, dates). This ensures retrieval of **all** necessary gold-standard documents to verify/refute the claim.  
   - Strike a balance between specificity (to avoid noise) and generality (to avoid missing key documents).  
   - Avoid redundant, vague, or unrelated details that reduce query precision.  
   - Use domain-specific phrasing patterns consistent with the domain involved:

     - **Nationality, birth year, roles** (especially for person assertions): Combine these identifiers tightly, e.g., “American actor born 1985” AND [show/commercial role].  
     - **Geographical or administrative claims:** Specify hierarchical geographies (county, city, country) explicitly and their comparative sizes or governance status.  
     - **Artistic/entertainment claims:** Include full film titles, director names, release or premiere dates, and voice actor or cast member names linked to specific characters.  
     - **Band memberships and song performance:** Specify all named musicians, the full genre designation of the band, song titles, roles (e.g., lead singer) in association with the song or band.  
     - **Temporal ordering claims:** Include exact release years or premiere dates with event names to establish relative chronology.

4. **Example-based application and coverage**  
   - Always include all entities and attributes mentioned for the claim, based on notes that may clarify or extend the claim.  
   - For relational claims involving multiple entities (e.g., a person playing baseball at a college in a specific county, or an actor appearing in multiple films and TV shows in a certain release order), the query must explicitly mention:  
     - All involved entities (person names, colleges, counties, films, TV shows).  
     - Relevant attributes (dates, genres, roles).  
     - Hierarchical locations or relationships (e.g., the county contains city and colleges; suburb relation to city).  
     - Temporal or comparative relations (e.g., one film released before another).

5. **Outcome and quality control**  
   - The query must enable retrieval of the complete set of authoritative documents (the gold-standard sources) called for in the verification.  
   - The failure to mention any critical entity, attribute, or relation leads to incomplete retrieval (misses gold documents).  
   - Markedly, omit overly broad or vague terms to prevent recall dilution.

In summary, your query should act as a comprehensive, targeted composite search command that fully captures a claim’s factual scope and uses the notes to refine precise entity, attribute, and relationship terminology. This includes multi-entity, multi-attribute, and multi-relational data points, formulated explicitly to support accurate, exhaustive retrieval of necessary references for verification or refutation.
