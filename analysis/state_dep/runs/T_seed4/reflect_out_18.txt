- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that needs to be evaluated or contextualized,

1. `new_notes`: a concise, well-synthesized set of notes that extract and highlight the most relevant, precise, and factually accurate evidence from the initial `notes` and the `context`. Focus on clarifying, confirming, or refuting the claim by filtering out redundant or repetitive points, emphasizing accuracy, and indicating clearly whether the claim is supported, unsupported, contradicted, or logically consistent given the evidence. Avoid speculation and include explicit statements about verification gaps or unsubstantiated parts if the context does not provide direct confirmation.

2. `titles`: a curated list of document or article titles from the input `context` that are key to understanding, verifying, or clarifying the claim. These should be exact titles as given in the `context` input and directly linked to the entities, events, or subjects involved in the claim. Include titles that cover critical people, places, events, or organizations relevant to the claim, even if the claim has multiple components.

Domain- and task-specific considerations:

- The claims often entail historical facts, biographical details, or associative relationships among individuals, organizations, events, or locations. Pay attention to proper identification of named entities and their roles.

Understanding and faithfully incorporating these domain-specific nuances is essential.
- Some claims merge multiple components (e.g., multiple individuals as judges, event relationships, or nationality + sports participation). Your response should individually address each claim component comprehensively—confirming, refuting, or noting lack of evidence as applicable.
- When `notes` include overlapping or near-duplicate statements, distill them into singular clear points without loss of factual detail.
- The reasoning you apply (although not output explicitly) should involve carefully cross-checking each claim element against both `notes` and `context` and resolving contradictions or gaps logically.
- Your `new_notes` should explicitly indicate whether the claim is corroborated, contradicted, unsubstantiated, or ambiguous based on evidence.
- For the `titles`, prioritize documents that specifically contain authoritative or detailed records about the named entities in the claim, e.g., artist biographies, tournament reports, film descriptions, or award ceremonies.
- Do not infer or fill gaps beyond the provided information; rather, state when data is missing or verification is unavailable.
- Keep your outputs clear, factual, succinct yet complete enough to enable traceable claim verification.

In summary: Deliver a clear, factual, and logically structured synthesis of evidence pertaining to the claim in `new_notes` and a precise collection of relevant document titles in `titles`. This will aid in reliable fact-checking and provide traceable source references for each key aspect of the claim.
