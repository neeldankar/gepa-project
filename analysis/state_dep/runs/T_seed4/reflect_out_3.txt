You will be given two inputs: a `claim` (a factual statement or assertion) and `notes` (a list of factual statements, evidence, or context related to the claim). Your task is to generate a `query` that can be used to verify or refute the claim by retrieving relevant documents or information. The generated `query` should be focused, specific, and target the key entities, facts, or relationships required to check the claim’s validity.

Key details and domain-specific considerations for this task:

1. **Input format:**
   - `claim`: A concise factual claim that may involve people, events, dates, professions, films, sports teams, roles, or other specifics.
   - `notes`: A list of short factual snippets or evidence related to the claim. These often clarify or provide contradictory information about the claim’s components but may not cover all necessary facts.

2. **Goal of the query:**
   - To confirm or disprove the claim by retrieving authoritative documents or information sources that fill in missing facts or clarify ambiguous points.
   - The query should include key entities (e.g., people, teams, films), and specific attributes or relationships to verify (e.g., profession, dates, roles, participation).
   - If the claim involves multiple related facts (e.g., linking individuals, dates, and roles), the query should address all relevant relationships to decisively evaluate the claim.

3. **From the examples, effective strategies include:**
   - Identify the crucial entities or subjects mentioned in the claim (e.g., names of people, films, teams).
   - Determine which connected facts or relationships in the claim are uncertain or contradicted/missing from the notes.
   - Frame the query explicitly to verify these entities’ attributes, associations, or roles (e.g., “Who starred alongside X in film Y?”, “Who managed team Z in year W?”, “Did A and B share the same profession?”).
   - Ask directly about the truth of key claim components rather than general or unrelated questions.
   - When the claim involves multiple parts, include all key verifications in a single coherent query.

4. **Domain-specific knowledge inferred from examples:**
   - Film domain: Verify cast and crew roles (actors, directors, co-stars, film titles).
   - Sports domain: Confirm founding dates, player careers, managerial records, team memberships, and timelines.
   - Biographical/professional domain: Confirm professions and collaborations of named people.
   - The importance of precise matching of years, professions, and relationships is essential.

5. **Style and format:**
   - The query should be concise but complete enough to guide an information retrieval system to the exact pieces of evidence required.
   - Use clear, natural language questions that comprehensively cover all the claim’s essential aspects.

In summary: Given a `claim` and supporting or partial `notes`, produce a single query (or set of tightly related questions) designed to retrieve missing or clarifying evidence that will confirm or refute the claim by focusing on entities, their attributes, and their relationships as highlighted by the claim and notes.
