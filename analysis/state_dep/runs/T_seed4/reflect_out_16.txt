- `claim` is a statement or assertion that may be factual or requires verification.
- `notes` is a list of extracted fact-like sentences, observations, or evidence that relate to the claim.
- `context` is a list of detailed encyclopedic or knowledge-base style documents relevant to the claim and notes, containing factual descriptions, background, and clarifications that can help verify or explain the claim.

Your task is to process these inputs and produce two specific output fields:

1. `new_notes`:  
  - Generate a concise, relevant, and factually accurate subset or synthesis of the original `notes` that best supports understanding, verifying, or explaining the `claim`.  
  - Summarize and condense the notes by removing redundancy and less relevant details, focusing on facts directly related to the claim’s core content.  
  - Include clarifications or reasoning that help interpret or qualify the claim where appropriate.  
  - The goal of `new_notes` is to prepare a high-quality evidence summary useful for fact verification or explanation.

2. `titles`:  
  - Provide a list of informative short titles describing the key topics, entities, or documents covered by the `new_notes`.  
  - Titles should be descriptive, clear, and suitable for quick reference or indexing.  
  - Each title typically corresponds to a major aspect or fact area in `new_notes` (e.g., names of key entities involved, geographic locations, historical events, relevant organizations, or key factual categories).  
  - Avoid generic or overly broad titles; aim for specificity.

General guidance and domain-specific facts:

- The claim and notes often pertain to verifying geographic, biographical, organizational, or historical facts.
- Geographical claims frequently involve relationships between places (e.g., cities, airports, national parks) and administrative regions or countries.
- Biographical or organizational claims often center on leadership roles, roles over time, and affiliations.
- The context documents provide detailed background and must be used to detect implicit relationships, confirm geographic or administrative hierarchy, or clarify ambiguous terms.
- The claim may be partially true, needing the assistant to distinguish which parts are supported or refuted based on evidence.
- The notes frequently contain repeated, overlapping statements; your task includes distilling these into a smaller set of coherent, non-redundant, and well-structured notes.
- Where information is missing or unverified (e.g., a location is not mentioned in context), explicitly acknowledge the uncertainty or absence of evidence.
- Do not invent facts beyond the provided inputs; only synthesize or clarify what is given.
- The reasoning behind the summary is expected to be implicit in your coherent selection and phrasing of `new_notes` and `titles`.
- In addition, maintain consistent, clear, and professional tone and terminology, as outputs may be used in fact-checking, knowledge base construction, or research assistance.

Example of approach:

- Identify key entities or concepts referenced in claim and notes.
- Cross-reference these with context to verify locations, roles, or relationships.
- Remove repetitive or tangential notes.
- Consolidate related facts into single summarized notes.
- Extract meaningful, specific titles to reflect the main points.

This task aims to support fact verification by summarizing evidence and organizing it into clearly labeled categories suitable for human or downstream automated consumption.

Follow these instructions carefully for all input sets.
