Your task is to generate precise and effective information retrieval queries based on claims and corresponding notes, with the goal of retrieving all authoritative and relevant documents necessary to fully verify or refute the claim.

Input format:  
- `claim`: a natural language statement involving specific factual assertions about entities (people, places, organizations), their attributes (e.g., birth years, roles, countries), relationships between entities, and contextual facts (e.g., geographic comparisons, film release dates, band memberships).  
- `notes`: a collection of factual data points and evidence snippets partially related or supporting the claim.

Key objectives and strategies:  

1. **Comprehensively decompose the claim**  
   Identify all core factual components expressed or implied in the claim, including:  
   - Entities and their precise names (full names, countries, organizations).  
   - Attributes relevant to those entities (birth year, nationality, roles such as director, voice actor, band member).  
   - Relationships or linkages between entities (one person directed a film, two people belong to a band, films’ relative release dates).  
   - Comparative or hierarchical contextual facts (e.g., country sizes compared, temporal order of releases).  
   - Any negations or exclusivity claims (e.g., “not both members of the same band”).

2. **Synthesize evidence from the notes**  
   Use the notes to:  
   - Confirm or clarify factual details present or absent in the claim.  
   - Identify exact terminology, proper names, and relationships that align with the claim’s components.  
   - Note any contradictions or implications that affect the final verification.

3. **Construct a comprehensive, focused query that:**
   - Uses precise entity and relationship terms taken directly or logically inferred from the claim and notes, avoiding aliases or unrelated terms.  
   - Includes all factual components relevant to the claim in a single query (or equivalent semantic scope) to maximize retrieval completeness.  
     For example: if the claim compares two directors’ countries by geographic size and references the final film by one director, the query must include: both directors’ names, the countries involved, terms related to geographic size, and the film title.  
   - Combines multiple conditions implicitly or explicitly by using conjunctions or separating clauses—covering all elements to enable retrieval of all corresponding gold documents.  
   - Balances specificity and generality: specific enough to target the exact claim’s entities and relationships, but not so narrow as to exclude useful documents.  
   - Avoids redundant or unrelated information that could dilute precision or add noise to results.

4. **Domain-specific nuances from examples:**  
   - When the claim involves nationality + birth year + show or commercial role for a person, combine these identifiers precisely.  
   - For geographic or administrative claims, specify entities within hierarchical relationships (e.g., counties, cities, countries) and their relative sizes or governance.  
   - For artistic or entertainment claims: include exact film titles, director names, release years, and voice actors linked to characters.  
   - For band membership and song performance claims: name all relevant musicians, the band’s full genre description if known, and the specific song titles and roles (e.g., lead singer).  
   - For temporal ordering, include exact release years or original release dates for movies or albums.

5. **Use concise, yet explicit phrasing to ensure coverage of all claim aspects, enabling correct retrieval of all gold-standard documents needed for verification or refutation.**  

In summary, your query should act as a reliable, unified information retrieval request capturing all factual components of the claim and leveraging the notes to maximize accuracy and completeness. This involves multi-entity, multi-attribute, and multi-relation inclusion with carefully chosen keywords and logical structuring to guarantee comprehensive coverage of the claim's facts.
