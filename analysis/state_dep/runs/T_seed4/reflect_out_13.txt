You are given two input fields: 

- `claim`: a statement asserting facts or relationships involving entities, events, characteristics, or attributes.  
- `notes`: a list of factual sentences, observations, or evidence snippets potentially related to verifying the claim. The notes may be partial, confirming, ambiguous, or contradictory.

Your task is to produce an output field named `query`, which consists of one or more precise, information-seeking questions or concise search queries aimed at verifying or investigating the truthfulness of the claim. These queries are designed to guide a fact-checking system or retrieval engine towards relevant documents, evidence, or data that confirm or refute the claim.

---

### Task Details and Domain-Specific Considerations

1. **Key Entities and Roles:**  
   Identify all essential named entities mentioned or implied in the claim and notes (e.g., persons, bands, films, operas, plants, parks, record labels, universities). Detect their roles, attributes, or relationships involved in the claim.

2. **Relationships and Attributes to Verify:**  
   Extract discrete assertions from the claim, such as:  
   - Collaborations or interactions between entities (e.g., bands working with record labels or other bands)  
   - Nationalities or origins of entities (e.g., a band or record label being British or American)  
   - Structural characteristics (e.g., number of acts in an opera versus a film)  
   - Geographic or taxonomic distribution (e.g., plant species subspecies locations in national parks)  
   - Institutional statuses or historical roles (e.g., whether a university is public/private or if a person was chairman)  
   - Temporal qualifiers (e.g., dates of establishment, years of collaboration)  
   - Product attributes (e.g., flavor profiles, advertising campaigns)  

3. **Fact-Checking Gaps and Ambiguities:**  
   Use notes to identify:  
   - Contradictions (e.g., record labels mistaken for bands)  
   - Missing information or ambiguities (e.g., whether a band collaborated with another)  
   - Overlaps or distinctions (e.g., plant subspecies presence on specific islands or in specific parks)  
   - Confirmations that narrow or clarify claim components (e.g., film director or inspiration lineage)  

4. **Framing Queries:**  
   Formulate queries that:  
   - Precisely target verification of each component assertion within the claim  
   - Resolve open questions or counter-evidence highlighted in notes  
   - Are sufficiently specific to facilitate retrieval of authoritative sources (e.g., entity-specific pages or documents)  
   - Enable cross-validation of related attributes (e.g., comparing national origins of two entities mentioned together)  
   - Can be natural language questions or structured (e.g., SPARQL-like) queries depending on context but always clear and concise  

---

### Generalizable Strategy

- Decompose the claim into distinct verifiable assertions.  
- Contrast these against the information from the notes to spot gaps or conflicting facts.  
- Generate focused queries to retrieve evidence that:  
  - Identifies and characterizes all relevant entities or concepts  
  - Clarifies timelines, collaborations, or inspirations  
  - Checks definitions of categories or roles (e.g., “Is Sleeper a band or a record label?”)  
  - Maps geographic or thematic distributions as needed (especially for biological or environmental claims)  
  - Confirms structural or compositional facts (e.g., number of acts in works, or pub/private status of institutions)  
- Use queries that allow verification of each point independently and in relation to others for holistic claim validation.

---

### Examples of Domain-Specific Queries:

- Music domain:  
  - “Did the Scottish punk band APB work with both Young American Recordings and Sleeper, and what are their origins?”  
  - “Is Sleeper a band or a record label?”  

- Film/Opera domain:  
  - “How many acts are in ‘Il trovatore’?”  
  - “Is ‘Magic Hunter’ (1994) directed by Ildikó Enyedi and loosely inspired by the opera ‘Der Freischütz’?”  
  - “Does ‘Magic Hunter’ have formal acts, and how are they structured?”  

- Botanical/Geographic domain:  
  - “What is the distribution of Echium wildpretii subsp. trichosiphon across Canary Islands?”  
  - “Which national parks in the Canary Islands host Echium wildpretii subsp. trichosiphon?”  
  - “Where is Teide National Park located and when was it established?”  

- Institutional domain:  
  - “Was [Person X] chairman of [Institution Y] during [Year/Period]?”  
  - “Is [University Z] public or private?”  

---

### Output Requirements

- Provide one or more explicit and distinct queries.  
- Queries must incorporate all pivotal entities and qualifiers needed to verify the claim’s components.  
- Ensure queries are unambiguous and targeted, ideally answerable by a single document or set of authoritative sources.  
- Where multiple components or possible ambiguities exist, produce multiple queries addressing each.  

---

By following this structured, domain-aware approach, your queries will efficiently support fact-checking systems to gather grounded evidence needed for comprehensive claim verification.
