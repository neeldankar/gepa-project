Given the input fields:

- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that combines entities, events, dates, roles, or relationships.
- `notes`: a list of textual evidence snippets (often repeated) containing relevant factual information derived from external sources or documents.

Produce the output field:

- `query`: a precise, focused, and clear question (or set of questions) formulated to verify or refute the claim by retrieving specific missing or supporting information from trusted source documents.

Detailed task description and guidance:

1. **Purpose:**  
   Your goal is to transform the given claim and evidence notes into an effective search or verification query that can be used to retrieve all necessary documents that support or refute the claim. The query should target the key entities, relations, dates, or roles involved in the claim and focus on any gaps identified in the notes.

2. **Context and domain specifics:**  
   - Claims often involve named entities (e.g., people, films, military units), roles or titles (e.g., director, actor, infantry regiment), dates (e.g., years movies were released or regiments were founded), and relationships between these elements.  
   - Notes are often duplicated snippets referencing various supporting facts, some directly relevant and some indirectly, about the claim's entities.  
   - Ground-truth documents (referred to as "gold documents" in the feedback) include detailed authoritative information likely needed to verify the claim, e.g.:  
     - For movie-related claims: director, co-director, actor names, release years, film titles, and sequels.  
     - For military or historical claims: the name and establishment date of regiments featured in films.  
   - Pay attention to aliases, sequels, or related works (e.g., "28 Weeks Later" is a sequel to "28 Days Later").

3. **Strategy to form the query:**  
   - Identify the core fact(s) the claim asserts (e.g., a person’s role in multiple films, the founding year of a regiment due to a specific film).  
   - Compare the information in the notes to the claim to find which key detail or document is not yet retrieved or remains unconfirmed.  
   - Frame the query specifically to target that missing piece, often in the form of a direct question about:  
     - The roles and involvement of named individuals in certain movies or events.  
     - The relationship or timeline between films, actors, or units mentioned.  
     - Founding dates, identities, or attributes of named entities.  
   - Use precise and unambiguous wording in the query to improve retrieval accuracy, including titles, years, and proper names.

4. **Additional considerations:**  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; focus on what is essential to confirm or deny the claim.  
   - When multiple entities or data points are involved, ensure the query captures their connection as expressed in the claim.  
   - Reflect the complexity of claims involving multiple related facts by combining them in a single query when appropriate.

By following this approach, the assistant ensures that the generated query will efficiently lead to retrieval of all necessary factual evidence needed to verify or refute the claim.
