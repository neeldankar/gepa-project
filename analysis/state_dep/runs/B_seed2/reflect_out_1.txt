Given input fields:
- `claim`: a factual or semi-factual statement involving people, events, titles, roles, relationships, or attributes.
- `notes`: a list of extracted facts, findings, or contextual information relevant to verifying or clarifying parts of the claim.

Produce the output field:
- `query`: one or more precise, fact-seeking natural language questions that directly target the key factual elements or relationships in the claim which require verification or additional evidence.

Detailed task description:

1. **Goal**: Your task is to decompose the claim into specific factual questions that can be used to retrieve or verify documents or sources. The query should cover all major factual components or entities referenced in the claim that need fact-checking.

2. **Contextualization**: Use the `notes` as a mini-database or knowledge snapshot providing context, prior findings, or known facts relevant to the claim. They often contain partial or incomplete information. Extract from the notes:
   - Verified facts
   - Contradictions or uncertainties
   - Named entities and their attributes (e.g., birthdates, roles, nationalities)
   - Known relationships (e.g., who directed which film, awards received)
   
3. **Core subtasks**:
   - Identify the main entities relevant to the claim (persons, films, teams, events).
   - Identify the relationships or facts claimed about these entities (e.g., birth order, directorship, award nomination).
   - Determine what factual gaps or uncertainties remain after considering the notes.
   - Formulate direct questions targeting these gaps or ambiguities.
   - If the claim contains possible conflations or mixing of facts, include queries that disambiguate or clarify these points.
   
4. **Query construction best practices**:
   - Phrase queries as clear, explicit questions.
   - Include entity names and relevant attributes explicitly.
   - Combine related factual points where it helps completeness and clarity.
   - Where the claim involves checking an attribute or fact for multiple entities (e.g., verifying the nationality and birthdate of a director), bundle these logically.

5. **Domain-specific nuances observed**:
   - In film related claims: Verify director identities, nationalities, and birthdates.
   - In acting/awards claims: Identify if an actor from a specific work received a particular award nomination for a named role.
   - In sports claims: Verify coach/trainer identities, specific subjective titles attributed by one person to another, team associations, and historically significant achievements (e.g., first Native American Olympic medalist).
   - Claims often reference multiple related entities and mixed facts; the queries must tease apart these elements to address potential confusions.
   
6. **Overall strategy**:
   - Use the notes to understand what is already known or ruled out.
   - Identify the missing or ambiguous piece(s) needed to verify the claim fully.
   - Prioritize formulating queries that would retrieve missing documents or relevant information cited in feedback as "remaining to be retrieved."
   - Make sure queries are comprehensive enough to cover all aspects of the claim’s verification.

In summary, given a claim and supporting notes, your output should be one or more succinct, factual questions designed to guide targeted retrieval or verification needed to confirm or refute the claim, explicitly referencing the entities and facts involved.
