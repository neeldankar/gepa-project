Task Description:
You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a `query` field. The query should be a structured search or information retrieval query designed to verify, fact-check, or retrieve documents relevant to the claim, leveraging the contextual information in the notes. The output query should aim to capture all key entities, relationships, dates, and facts that are necessary to fully support or refute the claim based on the knowledge snippets in the notes.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A single text string containing a factual or composite claim that may involve multiple entities, events, dates, and relationships.
- `notes`: A list of unstructured textual facts or sentences that provide domain-specific context or background knowledge relevant to the claim.

Output:
- `query`: A well-formed search or database query string, or a structured SPARQL-like query expression, that effectively retrieves documents or data verifying all significant components of the claim. 

Key Aspects and Best Practices to Include:
1. Identify and include all important entities mentioned in the claim, especially people, places, works (like films, publications), events (e.g., awards, premieres), and dates.
2. Extract the key relationships and attributes to be verified (e.g., who wrote what, who starred in which film, dates of release or induction).
3. Use the `notes` to capture authoritative or clarifying details (correct spellings, exact dates, relationships) to refine or disambiguate query terms.
4. Ensure the query tries to retrieve all critical supporting documents or knowledge components needed to verify the claim fully. For example, if the claim involves multiple entities and events, the query should target all of them, possibly by combining conditions.
5. When possible, formalize the query in a structured format (e.g., SPARQL-like query) that explicitly targets relevant triples or dataset properties (e.g., `dbo:releaseDate`, `dbo:starring`, etc.).
6. In cases of ambiguous or incorrect information in the claim (e.g., misspelled names), correct or clarify these using the notes before including them in the query.
7. The query should be focused, precise, and not overly broad, but comprehensive enough to address all factual parts of the claim in a single retrieval step if possible.
8. Queries may include filters, ordering, or limits to efficiently get the most relevant supporting data.
9. Avoid vague or general questions; the query should be actionable and designed for knowledge base or document retrieval systems.

General Strategy:
- Read the claim closely and break it down into key factual components.
- Leverage the notes to confirm, correct, or augment these components.
- Construct a query that pulls together all essential pieces of evidence, ensuring completeness.
- Strive to include multiple relevant entities and their relationships in a combinatorial manner that reflects the logical structure of the claim.

Example:
For a claim involving a film’s release date relative to another film and an actor’s role in that other film, produce a structured query retrieving the films’ release dates and confirming the actor’s starring roles accordingly.

---

Summary:
Produce precise, structured queries that synthesize key entities, dates, roles, and relationships from both the claim and notes, aiming for maximal coverage of all factual claims for verification purposes.
