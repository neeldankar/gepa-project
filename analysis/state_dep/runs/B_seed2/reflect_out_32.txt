### Task: Formulating Precise Verification Queries from Claims and Evidence Notes

#### Input Fields:
- **claim**: A factual statement or assertion comprising entities such as people, organizations, films, bands, historical units or events, dates, roles, or relationships between these entities.
- **notes**: A collection (list) of textual evidence snippets—sometimes repeated—derived from external authoritative sources. These include partial or indirect information that relates to components of the claim.

#### Required Output:
- **query**: A clear, concise, and focused question or set of questions aimed explicitly at retrieving key factual information from trusted documents that can verify or refute the claim. The query should pinpoint missing or uncertain facts identified after analyzing the notes.

---

### Detailed Task Description and Guidance:

1. **Purpose:**  
   Your objective is to construct an effective, targeted verification query based on the claim and supporting notes. This query will be used in information retrieval systems to find authoritative documents that either prove or disprove the claim’s assertions.

2. **Domain-specific context and information considerations:**  
   - Claims often revolve around entities such as:  
     - **People:** actors, directors, musicians, historical figures (names, birth dates, roles, careers).  
     - **Films & Media:** film titles, release years, sequels, novel adaptations, character portrayals, production roles.  
     - **Music:** bands, record labels, collaborations, national origins.  
     - **Military/Historical:** regiments, founding dates, roles in events, film features referencing these units.  
   - Known domain-specific nuances include:  
     - Distinguishing different entities with identical names or aliases (e.g., bands named "Sleeper" from different countries).  
     - Recognizing sequels or derivative works (e.g., "28 Weeks Later" as a sequel to "28 Days Later").  
     - Recognizing relationships like an actor playing a historical figure in a film adaptation of a novel.  
     - Differentiating record labels' nationality vs bands’ nationality.  
   - Notes may contain contradictory, incomplete, or indirect clues but rarely all needed facts.

3. **Key Strategy for Forming Effective Queries:**

   **a. Identify the core assertions in the claim:**  
   Extract the main factual elements such as:  
   - Names of people, entities, productions, or units involved  
   - Roles or titles asserted (e.g., director of a film, actor in a role)  
   - Dates or durations (e.g., release year, establishment year, working period)  
   - Relationships or connections (e.g., actor played character in film adapted from this novel)  
   
   **b. Compare against the notes:**  
   - Note which elements are confirmed by existing notes.  
   - Pinpoint which aspects remain unconfirmed, ambiguous, or missing.  
   - Identify entities or connections that need disambiguation (e.g., same name entities, multiple adaptations).  
   - Recognize contradictions or gaps (e.g., claims about nationality when notes state contradictory or no info).

   **c. Frame precise questions targeting missing or unclear pieces:**  
   - Use focused questions designed to recover detailed supporting evidence about those uncertain aspects.  
   - Include exact proper names, titles, years, and roles to reduce ambiguity.  
   - When multiple facts must be verified, combine related questions logically, preserving clarity and precision.

4. **Examples of domain-specific query focuses:**

   - **Films/Actors:**  
     “Who starred in [film title]? Did this actor portray [character] in [another film] based on [novel name]?”  
     “What is the release year of [film]? Is [person] credited as director/co-director?”  
     
   - **Music:**  
     “What bands worked with [record label]? Is [band name] British or Australian? How long did [band] collaborate with [label]?”  
     
   - **Military/Historical:**  
     “When was [regiment name] founded? Was it featured in [film title]? What is the relationship between the regiment's founding and [film/event]?”

5. **Wording and Query Construction Tips:**
   - Avoid vague or broad queries; focus only on missing or uncertain facts from the claim-notes comparison.  
   - Use precise proper nouns (film titles in quotation marks), full names, and relevant dates.  
   - When multiple questions help collectively verify a claim, combine them explicitly with clear separation (e.g., “Who...?”, “Is there...?”).  
   - Ensure queries are unambiguous and adhere to the specificity required to distinguish similarly named entities or overlapping facts.

---

### Summary of Ideal Workflow:

- Parse the claim to identify all factual items and connections.  
- Analyze notes thoroughly for explicit affirmations, contradictions, or absent data points.  
- Locate ambiguous entities or missing data critical to the claim's truth.  
- Formulate a set of clear, detailed questions precisely targeting these gaps.  
- Phrase queries suitable for retrieval of authoritative "gold" documents likely to contain the needed evidence.

---

By following these instructions, the assistant can systematically generate effective verification queries that maximize successful retrieval of supporting factual evidence, improving claim validation accuracy.
