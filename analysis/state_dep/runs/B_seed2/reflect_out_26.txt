Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single coherent `query` that can be used to fact-check or verify the `claim` against relevant information in documents or knowledge sources.

Detailed Instructions:
1. Analyze the `claim` carefully to identify the key factual assertions or components within it.
2. Review the `notes` provided, which contain relevant factual excerpts, supporting or contradicting evidence, or contextual information that helps evaluate the claim.
3. Use the notes to understand the scope and nature of the claim, including any named entities (people, places, titles, works, awards, dates, chart positions) and relationships referenced.
4. Formulate a clear, precise, and focused `query` that:
   - Directly targets the core factual elements of the claim needed for verification.
   - Includes relevant specifics such as exact names, dates, chart names, awards, or locations mentioned in the claim.
   - Is phrased as a natural language question or set of questions aimed at retrieving definitive factual answers.
5. Your reasoning should address possible ambiguities, conflicting evidence, or nuances from the notes, aiming to extract the most salient questions that, if answered, would confirm or refute the claim.
6. The goal is to produce a small set of well-phrased questions that would assist in retrieving the correct documents or data points that confirm or deny the claim's truthfulness.
7. Avoid broad or vague queries; be as targeted and specific as possible without copying the claim verbatim.
8. When the claim involves multiple components or conditions, explore breaking it down in the query to cover each component thoughtfully, ensuring full verification coverage.

Additional Domain-Specific Information That May Be Required:
- Music-related claims might involve chart rankings (e.g., Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks), song recording histories, discographies, and associations to specific singers or albums.
- Literary claims may require knowledge of specific poets, diplomats, Nobel Prize winners, nominations versus awards, and professions of individuals.
- Geographic or botanical claims may involve locations within political or natural boundaries (e.g., national parks in the Canary Islands), species distributions by island or region, or establishment dates of parks.
- Recognize the importance of distinguishing between:
   - Peak chart positions versus nominations or awards.
   - Original recordings or first recordings by artists.
   - Winning a prize versus being nominated.
   - Different but related locations within a broader region or archipelago.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Parse the claim to identify precise factual assertions.
- Use the notes to clarify or find contradictions relating to those assertions.
- Formulate queries that target the verification of critical facts, focusing on entities, hierarchical relationships, dates, and qualifying phrases.
- Address multiple claim components separately if needed to ensure complete coverage.
- Express queries in a way that supports effective document retrieval or fact verification by subsequent systems or humans.

Example:
If a claim states: "A song from Kathy Mattea's discography peaked at #1 on Billboard Hot Country Singles chart and was first recorded by a country singer," and notes indicate conflicting chart peaks and artist recordings, the final query might be:  
"Did any song from Kathy Mattea's discography ever peak at #1 on the Billboard Hot Country Singles chart, and if so, was that song first recorded by a country music singer?"

Your output is only the `query` field — a well-phrased, precise question or questions for fact-checking the claim based on the notes.
