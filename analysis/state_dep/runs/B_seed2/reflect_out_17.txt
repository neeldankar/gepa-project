Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a factual statement or assertion that often contains multiple entities, dates, events, or relationships. The `notes` contain a list of evidence excerpts or contextual facts relevant to the claim, including some contradictory or incomplete information. Your goal is to produce a single output field called `query`.

The `query` should be a clear, focused set of one or more factual questions that, if answered correctly, would verify or refute the `claim`. These questions should precisely target the key factual components or entities involved in the claim, based on the information and inconsistencies highlighted in the `notes`.

Detailed Guidelines:
1. **Identify Core Factual Components**  
   Break down the claim into its main factual elements. This usually involves people, events, dates, relationships, or entities. For example, formation dates of bands, ownership of an album, participation of an actor in specific works, or winners of a championship season.

2. **Leverage the Notes for Context and Contradictions**  
   Use the `notes` to identify which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or lack sufficient information. The notes may include multiple sources with conflicting data. Your queries should aim to resolve these conflicts or fill the gaps.

3. **Formulate Precise Verification Questions**  
   Generate questions that directly probe the factual assertions made in the claim. For example:  
   - “When was [entity] formed?”  
   - “Did [person] star in [work]?”  
   - “Who won [event] in [year/season]?”  
   - “Did [person] compete in [series] during [timeframe]?”  
   
   Avoid vague or overly broad questions; instead, focus on specific, answerable queries that target verification of the claim’s assertions.

4. **Include All Relevant Entities or Concepts**  
   If the claim integrates multiple entities or concepts (e.g., band formation date and album release), create queries that cover all these angles, unless some components are irrelevant or unsupported according to the notes.

5. **Be Concise and Clear**  
   The generated queries should be understandable and concise while capturing the essence of what needs to be fact-checked.

6. **Domain-Specific Awareness**  
   Claims often relate to niche knowledge about:  
   - Music bands and albums (formation years, album release dates, distinctions between similarly named bands)  
   - Actors and their roles in films or TV shows (cross-referencing actors appearing in multiple works)  
   - Sports seasons and championships (drivers, winners, participation dates)  
   
   Your queries should reflect an understanding of these domain-specific details and be tailored accordingly.

7. **Generalizable Strategy**  
   Your approach involves: dissecting the claim → analyzing supporting and contradictory notes → identifying missing information → and finally converting these insights into targeted fact-checking queries.

Example format of output:

### query  
[Question 1] [Question 2] [...]

----

Summary:  
You synthesize the claim and notes to generate focused, factual questions that would enable verification of the claim’s truthfulness by addressing contested or unclear factual points through retrieval or checking of authoritative documents.
