Task Description:  
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a single output field called `query`, which consists of one or more precise, factual questions aimed at verifying or refuting the claim. The `claim` typically contains multiple factual elements such as people, entities, dates, relationships, events, or locations. The `notes` provide relevant evidence excerpts or contextual data that partially support, contradict, or leave uncertain aspects of the claim, often with conflicting or incomplete information.

Your main goal is to synthesize the claim and notes to produce targeted, answerable fact-checking questions that, if answered definitively, will clarify the truthfulness of the claim’s key components.

Detailed Guidelines:

1. **Identify Core Factual Elements in the Claim:**  
   Break down the claim into discrete factual assertions. These may include:  
   - Names and roles of people (e.g., actors, directors, founders)  
   - Specific works or creative outputs (e.g., films, albums, TV shows)  
   - Dates or timeframes (e.g., birth/death dates, event years, release years)  
   - Affiliations or relationships (e.g., band membership, school district composition)  
   - Geographic or jurisdictional details (e.g., census-designated places, districts)  

2. **Analyze the Notes for Verification Context:**  
   Examine the notes thoroughly to detect:  
   - Facts that confirm or corroborate claim elements  
   - Contradictory information casting doubt on certain parts  
   - Missing or ambiguous details where verification is inconclusive  
   - Multiple sources or perspectives that may conflict  

3. **Generate Focused, Domain-Specific Verification Questions:**  
   Construct clear and concise questions directly targeting the claim’s factual components, aiming to resolve inconsistencies and fill information gaps. Examples include:  
   - “What are the birth and death dates of [person]? Were these dates overlapping with [another person]?”  
   - “Which schools are part of [school district], and what geographic areas do they serve?”  
   - “Who wrote, directed, or produced [specific film], and in what year(s) was it released?”  
   - “Did [person] play [role] in [film/TV show]? Was [film/TV show] directed by [another person]?”  
   - “Who founded [entity], and what projects did they direct or release in [year]?”  
   Avoid vague or overly broad questions; prioritize those that can be precisely answered to directly verify the claim.

4. **Cover All Relevant Entities or Components of the Claim:**  
   When the claim involves multiple elements (e.g., a band's formation date and their album release), your questions should collectively address each aspect relevant to the claim.

5. **Maintain Clarity, Brevity, and Specificity:**  
   Questions should be straightforward and unambiguous, keeping language simple but precise to facilitate exact fact retrieval.

6. **Domain Awareness and Contextual Sensitivity:**  
   Incorporate understanding of niche domain specifics within your queries, such as:  
   - In music: distinguishing between band members vs. similarly named bands, formation dates, and album release associations.  
   - In film and TV: actor roles vs. creative roles (writer, director, producer), film release years, and known collaborations.  
   - In sports: participants and winners of seasons/events, competition dates, and driver involvement.  
   - In education: school district composition, geographic coverage, and school designations (e.g., junior-senior high).  

7. **Generalizable Approach:**  
   Your strategy is as follows:  
   - **Dissect the claim** into key factual parts.  
   - **Examine notes** to find supporting evidence, contradictions, and missing info.  
   - **Identify ambiguities or conflicts** that prevent straightforward verification.  
   - **Formulate precise, answerable questions** to resolve these factual uncertainties.  

Example Output Format:  
### query  
[Question 1] [Question 2] [...]
