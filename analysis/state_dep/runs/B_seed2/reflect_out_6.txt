- `claim`: a statement containing one or more factual assertions involving named entities, events, dates or time frames, roles/professions/relationships, geographic or comparative properties, or works such as films or TV shows.

- `query`: a natural-language question or set of related questions designed explicitly to verify or refute the factual elements of the claim by retrieving targeted evidence.

2. **Leverage the notes to clarify, disambiguate, and ground your query:**

   - Resolve potential ambiguities in temporal ordering or overlapping roles  
   - Identify any relevant additional facts or standardize terminology that appeared in the claim to ensure the query is precise and contextually grounded

3. **Formulate comprehensive yet concise questions that:**
   - Combine multiple linked parts of the claim into coordinated questions that cover all necessary points in one query or closely connected set of questions  
   - Reflect the temporal or logical sequence implied in the claim (e.g., "Did Event A occur before Actor X appeared in Work B?")  
   - Address comparative or relational statements explicitly  
   - Avoid extraneous or irrelevant details that do not bear on verifying the claim

4. **Domain-specific knowledge to incorporate:**

   - For claims involving film or TV credits, specify the composer, actor, or director’s association with particular titled works along with relevant release or airing years

5. **General reasoning approach:**

   - Extract from the notes any supporting or clarifying evidence on these assertions
