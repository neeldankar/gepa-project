Task Description:
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to generate a single output field called `query`.

- The `claim` is a factual statement or assertion that often includes multiple entities, dates, events, relationships, or specific domain knowledge.
- The `notes` contain relevant evidence excerpts or contextual facts related to the claim. These notes may support, contradict, or provide incomplete information about the claim. There may be multiple sources within the notes, some of which might conflict with one another or leave gaps.

Your task is to distill from this information one or more focused, clear, and concise factual questions in `query`. These questions, when answered correctly, would verify or refute the claim by directly addressing the key factual components or contradictions highlighted in the notes.

---

Detailed Guidelines:

1. **Dissect the Claim into Core Factual Elements**  
   Identify the claim’s main factual components such as:  
   - People (e.g., band members, actors)  
   - Events or actions (e.g., awards won, songs sung)  
   - Dates and timelines (e.g., formation or release years, periods of activity)  
   - Relationships or associations (e.g., membership in a band or company ownership)  
   - Specific domain entities (e.g., song titles, railway stations, sports championships)

2. **Use Notes to Spot Support, Contradictions, or Gaps**  
   Carefully analyze the notes for:  
   - Information that confirms parts of the claim  
   - Contradictory or conflicting details from different sources  
   - Missing or ambiguous data relevant to the claim  
   Focus your queries on resolving these conflicts or filling these informational gaps.

3. **Formulate Specific, Clear, and Fact-Focused Questions**  
   Your queries should:  
   - Target precise verification of claim components (e.g., "Who won the 1967 Grammy Award for Album of the Year?" rather than "Did Frank Sinatra win awards?")  
   - Be domain-aware and use terminology accurate to the context (e.g., "Is Nivek Ogre a member of Skinny Puppy?", "Where is the Quaker Ridge station located?", "Who sings on the Radiohead song ‘All I Need’?", "Which county contains the NYW&B Railway terminus?")  
   - Avoid vague, broad, or overly complex questions—focus on atomic, verifiable facts.

4. **Cover All Relevant Entities and Concepts in the Claim**  
   When multiple aspects or entities are involved, create queries addressing each critical factual element unless clearly irrelevant or unsupported by the notes.  

5. **Conciseness and Clarity**  
   The questions must be concise, straightforward, and easy to understand, capturing exactly what needs verification without unnecessary verbosity.

6. **Incorporate Domain-Specific Knowledge**  
   Your queries should reflect domain expertise relevant to the claim’s content, e.g.,  
   - Music domain: band member lineups, side projects, album releases, award histories  
   - Film/TV domain: actors’ roles, character associations, production details  
   - Transportation domain: railway stations, route termini, geographic jurisdictions  
   - Sports domain: competition participants, winners, season details  

   Use appropriate phrasing and background knowledge in line with these domains to ensure the queries make sense within their specific contexts.

7. **Generalizable Strategy**  
   Apply this stepwise reasoning:  
   - Parse the claim into key facts and entities  
   - Analyze notes to identify what is confirmed, contradicted, or uncertain  
   - Identify information gaps or factual conflicts  
   - Convert these into targeted, fact-checking questions that, when answered authoritatively, will conclusively verify or refute the claim

---

Example question formats to guide you:

- "When was [band name] formed?"  
- "Who are the members of [band/organization]?"  
- "What awards has [person/entity] won?"  
- "Does [person] sing on the song [song title]?"  
- "Where is [station/building] located (city, county)?"  
- "Is [person] a member of [band/group]?"  
- "Which county or city does the [railway line/station] terminus belong to?"  
- "Did [person] participate in [event or season]?"  
- "Who won [championship or award] in [year]?"

---

Summary:  
You synthesize the claim and notes to generate targeted factual questions that confront contradictions, ambiguities, or missing information, enabling an authoritative fact check. Your output `query` should be a precise, clear, and domain-aware set of questions addressing every core factual element identified in the claim and notes.
