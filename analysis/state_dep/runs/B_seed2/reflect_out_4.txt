Given an input containing two fields:

- `claim`: a statement or assertion that makes one or more factual claims, potentially combining multiple entities, events, or relationships.
- `notes`: a list of text passages or facts related to the claim, often containing partial evidence relevant to verifying or contextualizing parts of the claim.

Your task is to produce an output containing:

- `query`: a concise but comprehensive natural language question (or set of related questions) that, if answered, would confirm the truthfulness of the claim or clarify ambiguities present within it.

Detailed instructions and considerations:

1. Understand the claim fully by identifying its key entities, relationships, and assertions, including any comparisons, groupings, temporal or attribute-based statements.

2. Examine the `notes` to identify relevant factual information that supports or contradicts parts of the claim, paying special attention to inconsistencies or corrections within the notes.

3. The query must focus on retrieving missing or uncertain evidence necessary for evaluating the claim’s validity. This includes:
   - Questions that disambiguate contested details (e.g., cast members, production companies, or genres).
   - Clarifications about relationships, such as collaborations or inspiration sources.
   - Specific factual details like counts, classifications, or attributes that are crucial to the claim.
   
4. When multiple components or entities appear in the claim, structure the query to address all aspects that affect its truthfulness, either in a single multifaceted question or in closely related sequential questions.

5. The query should be formulated to retrieve precise and authoritative information, suitable for search or fact verification against a knowledge base.

6. Avoid including speculative or confirmatory statements in the query; focus strictly on what information is needed to verify or falsify the claim based on available notes.

7. When relevant, refer explicitly to named entities (films, bands, persons, works) and include specific details such as dates, genres, or known attributes used in the notes to ground the query.

8. Preserve domain-specific distinctions—such as differentiating between different types of artistic works (film vs. opera), or between collaboration and inspiration—and craft the query accordingly.

Following this approach will improve retrieval of all necessary evidence documents for complete fact verification of the claim.
