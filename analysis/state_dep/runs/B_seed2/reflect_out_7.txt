You will be given three inputs:

- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion whose truthfulness or accuracy needs to be evaluated.
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets extracted from various documents relevant to the claim. These notes may have overlapping content, partial repetition, and varying levels of detail or relevance.
- `context`: a list of concise summaries or metadata about source documents that may provide additional authoritative or background information relevant to the claim.

Your task is to produce **two outputs**:

1. `new_notes`: a refined, concise, clear, and non-redundant subset or synthesis of the input `notes` that directly addresses the factual elements required to verify or falsify the claim. This output should:

   - Extract and combine key factual information related to the claim.
   - Remove duplicate or near-duplicate statements.
   - Exclude irrelevant or peripheral information that does not bear on the claim’s verification.
   - When possible, merge closely related facts into a single coherent and precise note.
   - Focus entirely on facts that support verifying or disproving the core relationships or attributes asserted in the claim (such as specific people, dates, events, places, works, or organizational affiliations).

2. `titles`: a list of the source document titles from `context` whose content is essential to verify or falsify the claim based on the facts in `new_notes`. Titles must:

   - Match core entities, facts, or relationships identified within `new_notes`.
   - Represent key sources containing relevant, background, biographical, event-related, or organizational information critical to assessing the claim.
   - Be exact document titles as presented in the given `context`.
   - Include all titles necessary to cover all key factual points used in the evaluation process, but no extraneous titles.

---

### Detailed guidelines and important considerations:

1. **Understand the claim thoroughly**:  
   Identify the explicit factual assertions and relations the claim states, such as:

   - Dates (e.g., birth, death, event dates).  
   - Personal or professional relationships (e.g., teacher-student, fellow performers).  
   - Participation in events (e.g., performances, games, releases).  
   - Geographic or organizational affiliations.

2. **Analyze and filter `notes` rigorously**:  
   - Remove all exact duplicates and near-duplicates.  
   - Exclude notes that do not address the key verification elements of the claim.  
   - Prefer notes that are the most explicit, precise, and factually complete regarding the claim components.  
   - Combine facts from multiple notes into a single clear and stand-alone statement when beneficial.  
   - Avoid any inference or addition beyond what is directly supported by the notes or context. This includes avoiding assumptions, interpretations, or extrapolations.

3. **Use the `context` to select relevant titles**:  
   - Match entity names and important factual terms found in the refined notes with titles in the context.  
   - Prioritize documents that provide foundational information about people, events, venues, works, or organizations mentioned.  
   - Include multiple titles if the key facts arise from multiple sources.  
   - Do not include titles that do not contribute directly to understanding or verifying the claim.

4. **Focus on clarity, precision, and completeness:**  
   - Each note in `new_notes` should be self-contained and unambiguous.  
   - Make sure all relevant date or temporal relations are clearly stated if they are central to the claim.  
   - Capture key event details like location, participants, dates, and outcomes if applicable.

5. **No fabrication or unsupported facts:**  
   - Do not introduce facts or context-specific knowledge not present in `notes` or `context`.  
   - Avoid adding explanations or justifications; only produce the refined factual notes and relevant titles.

6. **Reflect verification readiness implicitly through output:**  
   - Your outputs should enable a straightforward verification or refutation of the claim by an informed reader without needing further information.

7. **Examples of commonly handled claim components include:**  
   - An actor’s participation in specific films or stage works and their nationality.  
   - Musician’s discography details linking particular songs to albums, and timing of career milestones.  
   - Birth and death dates, and relationships like shared teachers with other notable persons.  
   - Participation in sports or cultural events, sponsored or hosted by specific organizations.

By following these instructions, you will produce concise, fact-focused notes and authoritative source titles that together optimally support the verification or falsification of the given claim.
