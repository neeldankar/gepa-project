Given the input fields:
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that may be complex and potentially contains inaccuracies or conflated information,
- `notes`: a list of relevant factual snippets or document excerpts which may contain supporting, contradicting, or clarifying information related to the claim,

your task is to produce the output field:
- `query`: a clear, precise, and focused natural language question (or set of questions) that would guide retrieval or verification efforts to fact-check the claim using the information in the notes.

Your approach should follow these domain-specific guidelines and reasoning strategies:

1. **Understand the Claim Context and Details:**
   - Analyze the `claim` to identify key entities, relationships, temporal or location contexts, and implicit assumptions.
   - Note any possible conflations or errors in the claim (e.g., mixing locations, dates, or roles).
   
2. **Leverage the Notes to Identify Key Verification Points:**
   - Use the `notes` to detect factual details already known or contradicted.
   - Identify which aspects of the claim are supported by the notes, which are contradicted, and which remain uncertain or unstated.
   - Extract factual nuggets such as dates, persons’ roles, locations, event outcomes, or relationships needed to validate the claim.

3. **Formulate Queries that Facilitate Complete and Specific Verification:**
   - Frame queries that explicitly target verification of the main factual components of the claim.
   - When the claim contains multiple key facts or sub-claims, include multiple related questions for comprehensive coverage.
   - Include queries that resolve ambiguities or test conflicting information found in notes (e.g., who did a particular role, where an event took place, correct dates).
   - Ensure queries are self-contained and unambiguous, clearly referencing the entities and relationships involved.

4. **Domain-Specific Examples to Guide Query Creation:**
   - For geographical or historical claims, queries should determine event locations, dates, and involved parties.
   - For film-related claims, queries must establish release dates, relationships between works (e.g., sequels), and composer roles.
   - For historical and biographical claims, queries should clarify familial relationships, roles held, actions performed by individuals, and links between events and persons.

5. **Generalization and Practical Use:**
   - The goal of the generated `query` is to serve as an effective retrieval and disambiguation tool, facilitating the acquisition of additional documents or evidence necessary to confirm or refute the claim.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; aim for specificity and relevance to the critical factual claims.

Summary: Your task is to transform the claim and the supporting notes into targeted, multi-part factual questions designed to verify or falsify key components of the claim, taking into account contradictions or gaps present in the notes. These queries should be clear, focused, and reflect the nuanced reasoning necessary for complex fact-checking scenarios.
