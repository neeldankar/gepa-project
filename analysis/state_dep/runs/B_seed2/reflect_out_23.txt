Given the input fields:
- `claim`: a factual statement whose truthfulness needs to be verified.
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets, documents, or facts that contain information relevant to the claim.

Your task is to produce the field:
- `query`: a precise and focused natural language question or set of questions designed to retrieve or verify the critical factual information that confirms or refutes the claim.

To perform this task effectively, follow these guidelines:

1. **Understand the Claim**: Break down the given claim into its key factual components or assertions, especially those that require verification. Identify any entities (people, organizations, places), dates, events, categories, or relationships involved.

2. **Analyze the Notes**: Review the provided notes to determine:
   - Which key facts related to the claim are already explicitly supported, contradicted, or missing.
   - Which sub-claims or details lack sufficient evidence or remain ambiguous.

3. **Identify Missing or Critical Information**: Focus your query on retrieving information about those specific sub-claims, entities, relationships, or factual details that are essential to verify the claim but are either missing or uncertain from the notes.

4. **Construct Clear, Specific Queries**: Formulate your `query` as natural language questions that:
   - Target verification of each crucial factual element or assertion in the claim.
   - Avoid overly broad or ambiguous wording.
   - When useful, include multiple related questions if the claim involves compound facts.
   - Use the correct names, dates, and terminology based on the domain-specific entities or concepts involved (e.g., political parties, music bands, burial locations) to ensure clarity and precise retrieval.

5. **Domain-Specific Considerations**:
   - For political or organizational claims, queries should clarify leadership, dates of succession, affiliations, and exact party names.
   - For musical group claims, queries should address the correct genre classification and membership counts.
   - For biographical or historical claims involving persons and burial information, queries should confirm identity, profession, burial location, and geographic or administrative contexts of the burial sites.
   - When claims involve dates and specific events, ensure queries explicitly ask for dates or event confirmation to distinguish between similar occurrences.

6. **Reasoning Structure**: It is good practice (not required in output) to internally reason through the claim and notes, confirming which facts are verified and which require further information, so your query efficiently targets the unresolved points.

By following this methodical approach, you ensure the query you produce enables effective fact-checking by targeting all necessary and sufficient information relevant to the claim verification task.
