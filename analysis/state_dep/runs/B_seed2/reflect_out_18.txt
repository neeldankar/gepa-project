You will be given three input fields:

- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion whose truthfulness needs to be assessed.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets (extracted from various documents) relevant to the claim. These notes may overlap in content or detail and may contain redundancies or partial information.
- `context`: A list of concise summaries or descriptions of source documents that may provide additional evidence related to the claim. Each summary includes the document title and brief key information.

Your task is to create two output fields:

- `new_notes`: A carefully refined, concise, and non-redundant subset or synthesis of the input `notes`. This set should include only those facts that are directly relevant and critical for verifying or falsifying the claim. Remove duplicates, near duplicates, irrelevant details, and merge closely related facts clearly and precisely. The new notes should stand alone, providing clear factual information that directly supports evaluation of the claim.
- `titles`: A list of the exact document titles from the `context` whose content contains the key information crucial to assessing the claim’s validity. Titles should be selected based on direct relevance to entities, facts, dates, locations, or organizational affiliations mentioned in the new notes. Include all titles necessary to cover the critical evidence.

Detailed Guidelines:

1. **Comprehend the Claim Thoroughly:**  
   Identify all factual components and relationships in the claim, such as dates, people, organizations, locations, events, or comparative attributes (e.g., birth dates, group membership, event participation).

2. **Analyze Input Notes Critically:**  
   - Extract core factual assertions from the input notes that address the claim’s components.  
   - Remove exact or near-exact repetitions.  
   - Exclude any notes irrelevant to verifying or refuting the claim.  
   - When appropriate, combine multiple related facts into a single, concise, clear statement for better readability and reduced redundancy.

3. **Leverage the Context to Identify Relevant Titles:**  
   - Match critical entities or facts found in the new notes to document titles in the context list.  
   - Include titles that provide background, biographical, event-specific, or organizational information directly related to the claim’s facts.  
   - Cover all key points; it may be necessary to include multiple titles if each contributes essential evidence.  
   - Titles must be exact as provided in the context.

4. **Focus on Specific Factual Coverage:**  
   - For claims involving dates or age comparisons, emphasize notes containing relevant birthdates or event dates or their absence.  
   - For event-related claims (e.g., participation, sponsorship), include notes citing the event, involved parties, sponsors, locations, and temporal details.  
   - For geographical claims, include notes clarifying location hierarchies, administrative oversight, or proximity specifics.

5. **Avoid Introducing or Fabricating Information:**  
   Use only facts explicitly found in the input notes or the information implied clearly by the context summaries. Do not add unverifiable or speculative details.

6. **Clarity and Self-Containment:**  
   The new notes should be factual, clear, and self-sufficient for a reader to understand the core evidence pertaining to the claim without needing additional resources.

7. **Output Formatting:**  
   - `new_notes`: an array of strings, each a precise factual sentence or synthesis relevant for verification.  
   - `titles`: an array of strings listing relevant document titles exactly as they appear in the input `context`.

Purpose:  
This task aims to distill from potentially redundant, partially overlapping, or tangential information a clean, focused set of factual notes and their source document titles that together enable straightforward fact-checking of the claim.

By following the above process, you produce outputs that facilitate clear, evidence-based verification or falsification of claims while minimizing noise and duplications.
