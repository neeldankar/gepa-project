Task Description:  
You will receive two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a single output field, `query`, containing focused factual questions designed to verify or refute the claim. The `claim` is a factual statement often involving multiple entities, events, dates, or relationships drawn from specialized domains such as botany, music, dog breeds, or sports. The `notes` provide relevant evidence excerpts or contextual facts concerning the claim’s components, including contradictions, incomplete data, or partial confirmations.

Your goal is to extract from the claim its core factual components and then use information, inconsistencies, or gaps within the notes to formulate precise and concise factual questions. These questions should directly test the specific elements of the claim that need verification. This approach aids in clarifying ambiguities, resolving conflicts, or filling knowledge gaps by prompting fact-checking or retrieval from authoritative sources.

Detailed Guidelines:

1. **Identify Core Factual Components**  
   Break down the claim into its fundamental factual elements—typically involving:  
   - Names and statuses of entities (e.g., “Banana Joe V Tani Kazari” could be an individual dog or a breed name)  
   - Relationships or attributes (e.g., did "The Vines" frontman write "Get Free")  
   - Quantitative data (e.g., number of species in a genus)  
   - Nationalities, dates, events, or achievements linked to named individuals or groups

2. **Leverage the Notes for Context, Contradictions, and Gaps**  
   Review the notes attentively to:  
   - Identify supporting information confirming elements of the claim  
   - Detect contradictory or alternative facts that question claim components  
   - Recognize where no evidence exists or the information is incomplete  
   Use these insights to target questions that resolve these ambiguities or contradictions.

3. **Formulate Precise, Fact-Focused Questions**  
   Focus questions on verifying exact details in the claim:  
   - Clearly reference relevant entities or concepts (e.g., “Is ‘Villanuco de Las Encartaciones’ a recognized dog breed?”)  
   - Ask about specific attributes or facts (e.g., “How many species does the genus Darwinia contain?”)  
   - Avoid vague or broadly framed questions; questions must be answerable with factual data.  
   - Use domain-specific terminology appropriately (e.g., terms like “breed,” “frontman,” “species count”)

4. **Cover All Significant Claim Elements**  
   The claim may integrate multiple facts—ensure the query set addresses all key points, unless some parts clearly lack evidence or are irrelevant based on the notes.

5. **Maintain Clarity and Conciseness**  
   Keep the queries straightforward, avoiding unnecessary words or complex phrasing.

6. **Domain-Specific Awareness**  
   Recognize and adapt to the domains involved:  
   - **Botany:** Species counts, genus identification, common names (including multilingual terms like French “Saule”)  
   - **Dog Breeds:** Distinguish between individual dog names, registered names, and recognized breeds; geographic origin of breeds  
   - **Music:** Roles of band members, authorship of songs, nationalities of musicians or actors  
   - **Sports:** Participants, winners, timeframes, and competition names  

7. **Generalizable Strategy**  
   Your problem-solving method involves:  
   - Dissecting the claim into discrete factual assertions  
   - Analyzing notes for confirmation, contradiction, or absence of information  
   - Transforming these insights into specific, targeted verification questions that would confirm or disprove the claim’s components effectively

Example: If the claim involves a plant genus’s species count and the French common name, questions might be:  
- “Which genus does the French plant name ‘Saule’ refer to?”  
- “How many species belong to the genus Darwinia?”  
- “Which genus has more species: Darwinia or the genus corresponding to ‘Saule’?”

Output Format:  
Produce a single output field named `query` containing the formulated questions, separated by spaces or line breaks for readability. The questions should be phrased clearly and precisely to enable direct fact-checking.

Summary:  
Synthesize the claim and notes to produce a concise set of factual questions that resolve claim ambiguities, contradictions, or gaps, enabling accurate verification based on nuanced domain knowledge and evidence.
