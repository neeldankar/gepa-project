You will be provided with three input fields:

- **claim**: A factual statement or assertion that needs to be checked for truthfulness.
- **notes**: A list of textual snippets pulled from various documents relevant to the claim. These notes may overlap in information, have different levels of detail, and may contain redundancies or unrelated content.
- **context**: A list of concise descriptions or summaries of source documents that may offer further background or authoritative information on entities, events, or facts related to the claim.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

- **new_notes**: A refined and synthesized subset of the input `notes` that directly and unambiguously addresses the key facts necessary to verify or falsify the claim. In this step:
    - Identify core factual elements in the notes that relate precisely to what the claim affirms or denies (e.g., dates, locations, event participation, biographical details, relationships among entities).
    - Remove duplicates and near-duplicates to avoid redundancy.
    - Exclude notes that do not contribute meaningful or relevant factual information related to claim verification.
    - Combine closely related facts into clear, concise standalone statements to facilitate straightforward validation or rebuttal.
    - Avoid introducing any new information not present in the inputs.

- **titles**: A carefully selected list of the most relevant document titles from `context` whose content underpins the facts in `new_notes`. Selection criteria include:
    - Titles that refer directly to key entities, events, or facts necessary for supporting or refuting the claim.
    - Titles providing background, authoritative detail, or essential biographical or event-related information connected to the claim.
    - Include all such documents required to cover the factual points included in `new_notes`.
    - Titles should be exact matches from `context` and not paraphrased.

### Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Guidelines

1. **Understand and Decompose the Claim:**
   - Determine what the claim asserts factually (e.g., a person's birthdate, event participation, organizational affiliation, relationship between entities).
   - Identify all critical elements needed to verify or falsify the claim.

2. **Analyze and Refine Notes:**
   - Extract core facts relevant to the claim.
   - Identify redundancies and remove repeated or near-identical notes.
   - Filter out irrelevant or tangential information.
   - If multiple notes cover the same fact with varying degrees of detail, prefer the most comprehensive and clear phrasing.
   - When facts are split across multiple notes, merge them carefully into a comprehensive, standalone fact.
   - Ensure facts are presented clearly to be independently understandable without external context.

3. **Match Facts to Context Titles:**
   - Link each core fact in `new_notes` to the most relevant source document(s) from `context`.
   - Select titles that focus on entities or events critical to the claim’s verification.
   - Include titles even if they provide partial but essential coverage.
   - Avoid including unrelated context documents even if loosely connected by entities.

4. **Maintain Precision and Scope:**
   - Do not infer or fabricate facts beyond what the `notes` and `context` provide.
   - Emphasize dates, personal details, specific event outcomes, or unique identifiers when relevant to the claim.
   - When verifying negations or absences (e.g., absence of evidence for a fact), include explicit statements about lack of information if present in the notes.

5. **Output Clarity and Usefulness:**
   - `new_notes` should be concise but complete with respect to verifying the claim, avoiding ambiguity.
   - `titles` should help a fact-checker directly access authoritative sources corroborating each fact.

### Examples of Common Claim Types and Corresponding Note Focus

- **Biographical claims:** Emphasize birthdates, professions, notable achievements, affiliations, and other personal data.
- **Event participation or outcomes:** Emphasize dates, participants' names, results, locations, and roles.
- **Geographical or organizational relations:** Emphasize hierarchical or locational data, regional relationships, and entities’ bases.
- **Factual negations or unknowns:** Include clear notes indicating the absence of information if explicitly stated.
- **Multiple entities or comparisons:** Make sure to capture distinguishing facts clearly and separately for each entity mentioned.

By synthesizing and precisely matching note facts with authoritative document titles, your outputs will enable clear, efficient verification or falsification of the claim without extraneous information.
