You will be given three distinct inputs:

- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion whose validity must be assessed. Claims often involve entities (people, bands, sports clubs, films, etc.) and their specific attributes or relationships, such as dates (birth, debut, event), locations (birthplace, band base, club location), memberships (band memberships, league participation), sponsorships, roles in films, or other verifiable facts.

- `notes`: A list of textual snippets extracted from various documents relevant to the claim. These notes may be repetitive, overlapping, or vary in granularity and detail. They contain factual information that can support or refute the claim.

- `context`: A list of concise summaries or descriptions of source documents that can provide background or additional verification information concerning the claim or its pertinent factual elements.

Your task is to produce:

1. `new_notes`: A refined, synthesized, and concise subset from the input `notes` that:
   - Directly addresses key factual elements necessary to verify or falsify the claim.
   - Eliminates all redundancy and near-duplicates.
   - Removes irrelevant information not contributing to the claim’s verification.
   - Condenses and combines closely related facts into clear, standalone factual assertions.
   - Prioritizes specificity and clarity, ensuring facts are presented precisely as supported by the input.
   - Avoids introducing any information or inference unsupported by the input data.

2. `titles`: A list of the most relevant document titles drawn from the provided `context`. These should be:
   - Clearly linked to the core facts and entities cited in the refined notes.
   - Sufficient to cover all necessary factual points supporting or contradicting the claim.
   - Representative of background, biographical, event-related, or organizational information related to the claim.
   - The exact titles as given in `context`, ensuring traceability to original sources.

Key considerations and domain-specific facets to keep in mind:

- **Claims involve nuanced factual relationships:** Dates (birth, debut, release years), locations (cities, counties, countries), organizational affiliations (band members, football clubs in leagues), sponsorships (principal sponsors of leagues), filmography details (film debuts, co-starring roles), and geopolitical hierarchies.

- **Overlap and redundancies in notes:** Input notes frequently repeat the same facts or slightly rephrase data. You must carefully deduplicate and synthesize so only unique, essential facts remain.

- **Hierarchical geographical relations:** When claims involve geographical bases or birthplaces, emphasize notes clarifying hierarchical relations (e.g., a city within a county, county within a country).

- **Multiple entities sharing attributes:** When members or participants belong to multiple groups (e.g., a musician in several bands), present clear cross-relations focusing on ownership of facts matching the claim.

- **Sponsorship and event details:** When claims mention sponsors or event participation, notes must include the event, sponsor, locations, teams/clubs, and relationships among these.

- **Domain-specific terminology:**
  - Film roles: debut year, lead or co-star, film genres.
  - Music bands: formation dates, locations, member names and roles.
  - Sports clubs: current league memberships, league sponsorships, home grounds.
  - Legal disputes or organizational histories are relevant if they affect claims about band ownership or continuity.

- **No fabrication or inference:** Only output facts explicitly present in the notes or context; do not assume or infer beyond what is stated.

- **Focus on facilitating straightforward verification:** Notes and titles together should allow an immediate fact check of the claim without ambiguity or missing detail.

- **Formatting:**
  - Use clear, concise sentences within new_notes.
  - Use exact document titles from context without modification in titles.

- **Cross-check for completeness:** Ensure all key facts used in new_notes correspond to selected titles.

By following these guidelines, your output will provide a distilled, factual core of evidence supporting or refuting the claim and the precise sources needed for verification, effectively handling input noise, domain complexity, and multifaceted information.
