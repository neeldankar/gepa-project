Given the input fields:

- `claim`: A statement or assertion that contains factual content to be verified or investigated.
- `notes`: A list of textual evidences, facts, or known data points relevant to the claim, potentially containing supporting or contradicting information.

Your task is to produce a `query` field that will guide retrieval or verification steps to validate or refute the claim.

Detailed task description and considerations:

1. **Objective of the `query`:**  
   Generate a concise, precise, and information-seeking question or query that, when searched or answered, would help verify the truthfulness or accuracy of the `claim` based on the provided `notes`. The query should be designed to retrieve authoritative or relevant documents that confirm, deny, or clarify the factual elements of the claim.

2. **Use of Evidence from `notes`:**  
   - Analyze the `notes` carefully to identify key factual elements, relationships, or contradictions relevant to the claim.  
   - Detect any inconsistencies or ambiguities within the `claim` when compared to `notes` and reflect these in the query formulation.  
   - Focus on entities, dates, places, relationships, or events that are critical to resolving the claim's truthfulness.  
   - When the claim contains geographically incorrect information or temporal mismatch (for example, an event stated to have happened in a wrong place), highlight this for queries that seek to resolve the contradiction.

3. **Incorporate relevant domain-specific knowledge implied by the notes:**  
   The notes may include details such as:  
   - Entity types: people (with birth/death dates), organizations, places, events, species, sports clubs, films, etc.  
   - Temporal data: birth/death years, event dates, or periods of activity.  
   - Geographic data: locations associated with entities or events.  
   - Authority relations: creator/production credits, home stadiums, native habitats.  
   Use these to shape queries that directly address the core factual elements of the claim.

4. **General strategy for query construction:**  
   - Identify the main factual question(s) that must be answered to validate the claim.  
   - Include key entities or concepts from the claim and notes in the query to ensure retrieval of focused documents.  
   - If the claim contains partial truth, the query should attempt to clarify or distinguish conflicting parts (e.g., "Where was X held?" vs. "Is X associated with Y?".)  
   - If relevant, include temporal constraints (e.g., "alive between year A and year B") or geographic clarifications.  
   - Formulate queries as natural, answerable questions to facilitate precise retrieval.

5. **Output format:**  
   - Produce only the `query` field as a single natural language question or query string that encapsulates the verification goal.

By following this approach, your generated queries will capture the essential factual conflicts or confirmations evident between the claim and notes, enabling effective retrieval of authoritative evidence to support claim validation.
