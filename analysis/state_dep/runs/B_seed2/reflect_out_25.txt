You will be given three inputs:

- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that needs to be verified as true or false based on evidence contained in notes and context.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets extracted from various documents that contain facts potentially relevant to verifying the claim. These snippets may overlap, repeat, or vary in detail.
- `context`: A collection of document summaries, each including an exact document title and a brief description or snippet. These represent source materials potentially containing needed background or verifying information.

Your task is to process these inputs and produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: A carefully selected, distilled, non-redundant, and precise subset or synthesized version of the input `notes` that isolates the key factual information directly supporting or contradicting the claim.  
   - Remove duplicates and unrelated or irrelevant details.
   - When facts overlap or relate closely, synthesize them into clear, stand-alone factual statements.
   - Do NOT add any information not explicitly present in the notes or context.
   - Each fact in `new_notes` should be precise, verifiable, and focused exclusively on addressing the claim.

2. `titles`: A list of exact document titles from the `context` set.  
   - Include all document titles necessary to cover the critical facts that appear in your `new_notes`.
   - Titles must correspond directly to the entities, events, dates, or relationships cited in the distilled notes.
   - Exclude any context documents irrelevant to verifying or refuting the claim.
   - Ensure comprehensive coverage by including all documents needed to verify all key points from `new_notes`.

---

Detailed guidelines and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Understand the Core Factual Elements in the Claim:**  
   Identify the types of facts involved, which may include any of the following, depending on the domain:  
   - Dates (birth, premiere, event dates)  
   - Locations and geopolitics (e.g., city, state, census-designated place distinctions)  
   - Organizational ownership or affiliations (e.g., venue owned by a parent company)  
   - Event participants (players, actors, characters, teams)  
   - Taxonomic terminology and hierarchy (e.g., genus, species, subfamily, generic name distinctions)  
   Recognize nuanced terminology differences (e.g., a "generic name" is taxonomically different from a genus or subfamily rank).

2. **Thoroughly Filter and Synthesize Notes:**  
   - Extract only the essential factual statements necessary to confirm or deny the claim.  
   - Collapse near-duplicates into one definitive statement.  
   - Exclude irrelevant or tangential notes.  
   - When multiple facts relate to a single point (e.g., number of species, premiere dates, or actor roles), merge them carefully to clarify the verification.  
   - Keep formulations neutral and factual, avoiding any inference or speculation not present in the provided data.

3. **Match Facts Precisely to Context Titles:**  
   - The selected document titles should provide the foundational or validating information of the facts distilled in `new_notes`.  
   - Avoid partial or incomplete coverage — if a fact depends on a document, include that document fully.  
   - Exclude any document titles that do not contribute to verifying a factual statement in `new_notes`.

4. **Maintain Clarity and Integrity:**  
   - Each note should stand alone as a concise, complete fact.  
   - Avoid mixing facts from unrelated entities or events within a single note.  
   - Refrain from introducing information outside that provided in the inputs.

---

**Generalized Strategy:**

1. Parse the claim carefully to identify all relevant facts and entities.  
2. Review and filter notes for relevance strictly tied to these facts.  
3. Synthesize overlapping or repetitive facts into single crisp statements.  
4. Identify all essential facts necessary for verification and ensure coverage in the `new_notes`.  
5. Select corresponding document titles from the provided context for every fact in `new_notes`, ensuring complete documentation for verification purposes.

---

**Domain-Specific Notes:**

- For **taxonomy claims**, accurately differentiate between generic names, genus, species counts, and taxonomic hierarchies; species counts usually associate with genus-level entities.  
- For **entertainment/events claims**, recognize that venues may have multiple names and ownership layers (e.g., "The AXIS auditorium at Planet Hollywood Resort & Casino" is owned by Caesars Entertainment).  
- For **sports claims**, nationality or origin claims require exact biographical evidence, and defending champions are identified by event and year specifics.  
- Geographical precision is critical, such as distinguishing between places like Las Vegas and Paradise, Nevada (a census-designated place).  
- Repeated notes should be consolidated into one definitive statement for clarity and brevity.

---

By following these instructions, your outputs (`new_notes` and `titles`) will be focused, factual, and sufficient to verify or refute the claim according to the evidence provided, facilitating straightforward fact-checking without introducing unsupported assumptions.
