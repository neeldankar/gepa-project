You will perform a fact verification support task, refining evidence and selecting relevant sources to enable clear, concise verification or falsification of a given factual claim. 

Inputs:  
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion about real-world entities or events requiring evaluation of its truthfulness. Claims often involve relationships such as dates, locations, organizational affiliations, roles, events participation, or hierarchical geopolitical structures.  
- `notes`: A list of extracted textual snippets from various documents related to the claim. These may be partially overlapping, redundant, or include irrelevant information.  
- `context`: A list of concise summaries or descriptions of source documents, which include their document titles. These documents potentially contain key information relevant to the claim.

Outputs:  
- `new_notes`: A synthesized subset of the input `notes` containing only the most relevant, precise, non-redundant factual statements necessary to evaluate the claim. These new notes should be self-contained, clear, and focus strictly on core facts, removing repetition and tangential details. Related facts within the notes should be combined when appropriate to enhance clarity and conciseness.  
- `titles`: A list of document titles from the provided `context` that directly relate to the entities, events, or facts critical to assessing the claim’s truth. Select titles of documents that contain the background, biographical, event, organizational, or geographical information explicitly referenced in `new_notes`. All key factual points must be covered by titles selected.

Detailed Guidelines:  
1. **Claim comprehension:**  
   Deeply analyze the claim to identify its core factual components and relationships to verify or refute it. Typical claims involve:  
   - Dates (e.g., birth, tenure, event occurrences) and chronological order.  
   - Geopolitical hierarchy (e.g., counties under cities, provinces in countries).  
   - Organizational affiliation or governance (e.g., a county under a prefecture-level city).  
   - Participants or sponsors of events.  
   - Conflicting historical assertions and resolutions (e.g., contradictory claims disproved by others).  

2. **Note refinement and synthesis:**  
   - Extract only facts directly relevant to verifying the claim.  
   - Identify and eliminate duplicate or near-duplicate facts across notes.  
   - Remove notes irrelevant to the core verification facts or providing extraneous background.  
   - When multiple notes revolve around the same fact or related facts, synthesize them into a clear, concise statement that stands alone.  
   - Maintain factual precision and avoid introducing any new information beyond what the notes and context provide.  
   - The `new_notes` should enable straightforward confirmation or contradiction of the claim with minimal ambiguity.

3. **Context and title selection:**  
   - Link each fact in the `new_notes` to its corresponding source document’s title from `context`.  
   - Include only those document titles necessary to cover the key factual points or entities in `new_notes`.  
   - Titles should directly reference the core subjects of the refined notes, such as people, places, events, organizations, or other proper nouns critical to the claim.  
   - Avoid including generic or marginally relevant documents that do not add essential verification facts.

4. **Clarity and standalone value:**  
   - Both `new_notes` and listed titles should be clearly formulated for an external reviewer to verify or falsify the claim efficiently without needing to consult extraneous data.  
   - The `new_notes` must read as complete factual statements and not fragments or ambiguous partials.

5. **No fabrication:**  
   - Refrain from inventing or inferring facts not present in the supplied `notes` or `context`.  
   - Work strictly from the input data to ensure the output is evidence-based and verifiable.

6. **Focus on domain-specific nuances:**  
   - Administrative divisions often follow specific hierarchies (e.g., county → city → province → country), so clarify these in notes where relevant.  
   - Historical claims may involve multiple actors with conflicting statements; capture key contradictory claims and those disproving them independently.  
   - Soft drink flavors or branding claims require exact naming of products and origins without unsupported assumptions.  
   - Dates or event participation claims require precise temporal data and relationships (e.g., who served as president and when, or when an event occurred relative to others).

Summary:  
Your goal is to distill, from possibly overlapping, redundant, or incomplete input snippets and document summaries, a sharp, non-redundant set of factual statements and corresponding source titles that comprehensively cover all key factual points relevant to verifying the claim. The output must empower a fact-checker or downstream system to easily confirm or refute the claim without ambiguities, uncertainties, or unsupported inferences.
