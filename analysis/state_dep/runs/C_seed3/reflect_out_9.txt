Task Description:

You will be provided with two fields as input:  
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion containing multiple pieces of information often related to specific entities, events, or relationships;  
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets or evidence passages that contain relevant information regarding the entities, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to produce one output field:  
- `query`: a concise, clear, fact-checking question (or set of questions) designed to verify the factual content of the `claim` against authoritative documents or knowledge sources.

Detailed instructions:

1. **Purpose of the Query:**  
   The `query` should be formulated to retrieve or verify all critical aspects of the claim. This involves creating a focused question (or logically connected questions) that queries about the relationship, attributes, or facts contained within the claim so that it can be fact-checked or supported/refuted by reliable references.

2. **Key Considerations:**

   - **Disambiguate entities if necessary:**  
     If the claim mentions entities by partial or ambiguous names, clarify or specify them in the question to improve retrieval accuracy (e.g., specify "The AXIS auditorium located within Planet Hollywood Resort & Casino in Las Vegas").

   - **Include all key elements of the claim in the query:**  
     The query must comprehensively cover all factual components that require verification, such as locations, ownership, roles, attributes, or event participation.  
     For example, if the claim asserts the location and ownership of a venue, both facts should be combined or split into related questions in the query.

   - **Use explicit and precise phrasing:**  
     Frame the query as a direct question or set of related questions addressing the claim's factual components, avoiding vague or overly broad questions (e.g., "Is X located at Y and owned by Z?").

   - **Account for domain-specific facts:**  
     The claims often involve domain-specific concepts such as film directing roles (e.g., "co-director"), sports events and players (e.g., tennis tournaments, defending champions), venue ownership and residency shows, etc. Formulate the query to include these domain specifics accurately.

3. **Generalizable Strategy:**

   - Break the claim into its atomic factual elements. For example, if the claim is compound ("Entity A has property X and Entity B has property Y"), ensure that the query covers both parts either jointly or as linked questions.  
   
   - Use information provided in the `notes` to capture the proper nouns, attributes, and relationships that may be necessary to phrase a precise and retrievable query. For example, if notes mention the name "The AXIS auditorium," use it rather than a generic "auditorium."

   - When the claim or notes indicate definitions or domain knowledge (e.g., "a co-director is recognized as a director"), incorporate that understanding into the query to verify the claim's assertion rather than relying on implicit knowledge.

4. **Avoid Missing Critical Documents or Evidence:**

   - Since the query will be used for document retrieval or fact verification, ensure it is broad enough to retrieve all relevant evidence but focused enough to exclude unrelated documents.

   - If the claim references multiple distinct entities or events, try to incorporate them either in combined questions or via clearly linked sub-questions.

Summary:  
Given a claim and supporting notes, generate a fact-checking query that accurately and explicitly asks about the key factual claims, incorporating relevant domain-specific terms and clarifying ambiguous references. The query should make it possible to retrieve and verify all critical parts of the claim with the right documents or data sources.
