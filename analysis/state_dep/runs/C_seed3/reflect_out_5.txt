Task Description:
You are given two inputs: a textual claim and a set of notes (structured as an array of relevant factual statements and evidences). Your task is to produce a corresponding `query` that can be used to search authoritative sources or knowledge bases to verify or refute the claim. The `query` should be formed as precise and focused natural language questions that target the key entities and factual relationships involved in the claim.

Inputs:
- `claim` (string): A factual or semi-factual statement that needs to be verified.
- `notes` (list of strings): A collection of relevant evidence snippets, extracted facts, or context related to the claim. These notes provide supporting or contradicting information, often with specific entities (e.g., people, films, dates) and relations.

Outputs:
- `query` (string): One or more natural language questions designed to retrieve documents that confirm or deny the claim. The queries should explicitly mention the key entities (e.g., names of people, films, years) and relationships to verify. The formulation should be as focused as possible to maximize retrieval of gold documents that determine the claim’s truthfulness.

Key Points and Domain Knowledge:
- Claims frequently involve relationships between people, films, and years (e.g., actors, composers, film titles, release or production years).
- Important domain-specific concepts include:
    - Filmography and familial relationships: identifying actors, their family members (e.g., father), and their respective credits.
    - Composer’s career milestones: first film scored by a composer, soundtracks for specific films.
    - Documentary film production years and collaborations between individuals.
    - Multiple entities and temporal constraints may be involved in verifying claims.
- The notes provide factual context and evidence that should be used to identify key entities and relationships to be queried.
- The queries should explicitly address these key entities and their relevant relationships to allow for retrieval of specific supporting documents.
- When multiple entities or claims are involved, queries may be formed as multiple questions or compound questions targeting all essential parts needed for verification.

Generalizable Strategy:
1. Parse the claim and identify the main entities (people, films, years, events).
2. Review the notes to confirm or find which entities and relations are relevant and supported.
3. Formulate focused natural language questions that direct retrieval tools to documents linking those entities and facts, such as:
    - Identification questions (“Who is the star of film X?”)
    - Relationship verification (“Did person A compose film Y?”)
    - Temporal verification (“Was film A released before film B?”)
4. Make queries comprehensive enough to cover all verification points, but concise to avoid redundancy.
5. Include temporal, role-based, or familial constraints when relevant to clarify ambiguous or multi-step claims.

Examples of generated query types include:
- “Did [Person A] compose the soundtrack for [Film X], and was [Film Y] their first scored film?”
- “Who is the star of [Film X]? Who is their father? Did the father star in [Film Y] released in [Year]?”
- “What year was the documentary [Film X] produced or released?” 

Your goal is to produce queries optimized for retrieving the most relevant and sufficient information to verify the claim's validity, guided by detailed contextual notes and the claim’s key components.
