You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`.

- The `claim` is a natural language statement that makes one or more factual assertions, often referencing specific people, places, events, dates, titles, or relationships.
- The `notes` field contains a list of text snippets or summarized pieces of evidence extracted from various documents. These notes either support, partially support, contradict, or provide no direct information about the claim. They often contain domain-specific and contextually rich details such as dates, roles, affiliations, titles, and known conflicts or relationships between historical or fictional entities.

Your task is to produce a `query` that guides the retrieval of external documents or information necessary to verify or refute the claim. The generated `query` should:
1. Explicitly identify and disambiguate all key entities and relationships in the claim, especially proper nouns (people, places, organizations, works such as films or TV shows, events, dates).
2. Focus on fact-checking the specific assertions made in the claim. This includes:  
   - Verifying whether persons attributed with certain actions or roles indeed held those roles or performed those actions in the given chronology or context.  
   - Confirming appearances or participation in named media (films, TV shows) or events with specific years or timelines.  
   - Determining administrative or geographical relationships in claims about locations or jurisdictions.  
   - Clarifying contested or conflicting statements involving multiple parties (e.g., who made which claim, who disputed it, who held a certain position first).
3. Incorporate contextual details from the notes that reveal which parts of the claim are unsupported or require deeper investigation, thus refining the query focus.
4. Formulate the query as a clear, concise, and fact-checking question or search prompt that can be used to retrieve authoritative documents that verify or refute the claim.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Carefully analyze the notes to understand what information is missing, contradicted, or confirmed about entities and events mentioned in the claim.
- Use those gaps or conflicts as the basis for your query to maximize retrieval of documents that fill in the evidentiary void.
- Include relevant time frames, relationships, and specific attributes cited in the claim to reduce ambiguity.
- When the claim involves multiple related entities (e.g., attributions of statements, oppositions between persons, or documentary evidence), ensure the query explicitly connects these entities and their relationships to avoid missing context.

The ultimate goal is to produce a query that efficiently retrieves the most relevant documents needed to fully verify or refute the original claim, focusing on the multi-entity and multi-faceted nature of complex factual claims.

Example pattern of the query output:
- "Who starred in [Film title] released in [year], and did that actor appear in [TV show] in [year] and [other show] in [year]?"
- "Is there a town administered by [local government] that is located near [geographic features]?"
- "Did [Person A] first serve as [position] in [year], claim that [Person B] was [event], and was disproved by [Person C], whom [Person D] opposed?"

Ensure your produced queries are self-contained, clearly specify all entities and temporal details, and target the fact patterns crucial to confirming or disconfirming the claim using authoritative external sources.
