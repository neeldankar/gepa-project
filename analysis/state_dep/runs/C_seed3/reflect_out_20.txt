Given a `claim` and a list of `notes` related to that claim, your task is to produce a `query` (or set of queries) that will effectively retrieve the most relevant documents or information needed to verify or fact-check the claim. The `query` should target the key entities, facts, or relationships mentioned or implied in the claim, especially focusing on information that is ambiguous, missing, or necessary for comparison or verification.

Key points and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Identify Entities and Concepts**: Extract all relevant entities (people, places, events, objects, organizations) from the `claim`, including alternate names or related aliases mentioned in the `notes`. For example, identifying that "Saule" is the French name for Salix.

2. **Clarify Relations and Attributes**: Determine what relationships, attributes, or data points are required to confirm or refute the claim. This often includes numerical comparisons (e.g., counts of species), dates (birthdates, event dates), or participation/involvement (team participation in an event).

3. **Incorporate Context from `notes`**: Utilize information or hints from the `notes` to guide the query toward more precise retrieval. For instance:
   - When a band, album, or song is misspelled or ambiguously referenced, identify the accurate entity (e.g., "Playin' Around" vs. "Playin' to Win").
   - Recognize sponsorship linkages between companies and events, and their geographic relevance.
   - Note distinguishing attributes such as species counts or birthdates.

4. **Formulate Structured, Multi-Part Queries**: The `query` may consist of multiple connected questions that cover all essential verification angles within the claim. For example:
   - "What is the number of species in genus A and genus B? Which has more?"
   - "Who was the lead guitarist in the band that recorded album X, and what are the birthdates involved?"
   - "Which football bowl games were sponsored by companies based in location Y? Did team Z participate?"

5. **Aim to Maximize Document Retrieval**: Construct queries targeting key gold entities or titles that are known or likely to provide definitive factual answers, especially those explicitly called out in the feedback and notes (e.g., specific people, bands, albums, events, and sponsorship companies).

6. **General Approach**:
   - Parse claim and identify verification needs.
   - Cross-reference notes for additional entity names, clarifications, or relevant data points.
   - Generate queries specific enough to retrieve authoritative documents yet broad enough to capture possible variations or related facts.
   - Prioritize missing entities or knowledge gaps indicated in notes or feedback for inclusion in query.

The output should be a `query` string or question(s) designed to find the most relevant information to verify the claim, explicitly incorporating the domain-specific information and nuances revealed in the claim and notes.
