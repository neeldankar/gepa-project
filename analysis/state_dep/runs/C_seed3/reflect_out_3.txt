Task Objective:  
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single output field called `query` that formulates one or more specific, factual questions derived from the claim and informed by the given notes. These questions should guide information retrieval or fact-checking efforts related to the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A single or multi-sentence statement making one or multiple factual assertions. The claim may contain multiple components or entities tied together in complex relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets or excerpts (usually factual data points, biographical details, or domain-specific facts) relevant to the entities and topics mentioned in the claim. The notes may contain both confirming and conflicting information about parts of the claim.

Core Task Description:  
- Analyze the claim to identify all distinct factual components that require verification. This may include dates, relationships between individuals or entities, administrative or geographical facts, authorship or attribution details, and other specific assertions.  
- Use the notes to determine what information is relevant or lacking in order to evaluate the claim’s veracity. Often, the notes provide partial confirmation or refutation of parts of the claim; your goal is to encapsulate all necessary verification points as questions.  
- Formulate clear, concise, and precise questions (queries) that directly address the factual elements of the claim. These questions should be answerable by consulting authoritative documental sources (e.g., Wikipedia articles, biographies, official records).  
- If the claim contains multiple interconnected assertions, produce combined or multiple questions that reflect all sub-points needed for thorough fact-checking.  
- Use domain-specific terminology and proper names mentioned in the notes and claim when constructing queries to ensure they are targeted and specific. Avoid vague or overly broad questions.  
- When appropriate, include compound questions that check for relationships, comparisons, or consistency between entities or attributes, as seen in music composer information or geographical administration details.

Generalizable Strategy:  
- Identify entities, dates, and relationships mentioned in the claim.  
- Cross-reference these with the notes to assess which pieces of evidence are available and which need exploration.  
- Convert the claim’s assertions into explicit questions that retrieve any missing or confirmatory information needed to adjudicate the claim.  
- The resulting query should enable retrieval of all gold-standard or authoritative documents related to the claim’s entities and assertions.  
- The queries should sometimes be compound (e.g., “Who composed this opera? Who composed this other opera? Are they the same?”) rather than simple yes/no questions, to reflect the complexity of the claim.

Domain-Specific Considerations Highlighted by Examples:  
- Verifying dates of death for individuals (including full dates).  
- Verifying educational or teaching relationships between historical or biographical figures in the arts or sciences.  
- Verifying local government or administrative relationships for towns or regions, including proximity to natural landmarks.  
- Comparing composer attributions and performance roles in historical opera productions from different time periods.  
- Using specific entity names (people, places, works of art) as search terms within the queries.  
- Using explicit factual questions rather than implicit or vague phrasing, to facilitate retrieval of all relevant documents.

Summary of Instruction:  
Given a complex factual claim and a set of relevant notes, produce targeted, clear and specific information retrieval questions that cover all factual elements of the claim and guide the fact-checking process comprehensively. Ensure your queries use domain-appropriate terminology and address the distinct factual components to enable retrieval of the full set of relevant authoritative documents.
