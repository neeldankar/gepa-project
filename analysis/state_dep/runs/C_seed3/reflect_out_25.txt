Task Description:
You will be provided with three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A statement or assertion whose validity or factual support needs evaluation.
- `notes`: A list of extracted factual sentences or evidences related to the claim and/or relevant entities mentioned in the claim. These notes might contain redundant, repeated, or partially relevant information.
- `context`: A list of documents or document snippets providing broader background, factual details, or supporting evidence from which the notes may have been derived. This context may include relevant domain-specific entities, dates, places, or historical details.

Outputs:
- `new_notes`: A distilled, concise, and factually coherent list of notes that directly support or refute the claim. These should summarize the core factual information gleaned from the provided `notes` and `context`, removing redundancies and avoiding unsupported speculation. If the claim is not fully supported, `new_notes` should include relevant evidence that explains misalignments or contradictions (e.g., mismatched dates, entities, or events).
- `titles`: A list of relevant document or topic titles that best represent the sources or categories of information crucial to assessing the claim. These titles should reflect key entities, events, or concepts central to the claim as supported or contradicted by the gathered facts.

Key Considerations and Strategy:
1. **Claim Verification and Reasoning**: Analyze the claim critically by cross-referencing the `notes` and `context`. Identify factual consistencies or contradictions related to dates, entities, roles, places, or events described in the claim. Highlight where timelines, affiliations, or facts do not align.

2. **Condensed, Relevant Notes**: From the potentially verbose or repeated `notes`, extract a minimal set of clearly relevant, non-redundant factual statements that directly bear on the claim’s truthfulness. Incorporate re-phrased or synthesized points where beneficial for clarity.

3. **Domain-Specific Expertise**: Focus on domain-relevant details such as:
   - For companies and locations: precise headquarters, state/country affiliations, and relationships between them.
   - For sports teams and player-manager timelines: accurate foundation dates, team affiliations, playing and managing tenure, and historical name changes.
   - For music bands and albums: album release years, band formation dates inferred from debut albums or related historical context, and membership or collaboration clues.

4. **Titles Selection**: The chosen document or topic titles should map clearly to key factual anchors required to verify the claim. For example: company profiles, airport names, sports team histories, manager and player biographies, band histories, or album titles.

5. **Transparency on Uncertainty**: If the context or notes lack definitive information to fully confirm or deny the claim, the `new_notes` should explicitly state the absence of evidence or ambiguity.

6. **Consistency With Feedback**: Prior corrections highlighted the need to:
   - Include all relevant key documents or topics (even if indirectly mentioned) to ensure complete representation.
   - Avoid unsupported assumptions without evidence.
   - Maintain succinctness while ensuring all necessary factual support is present.

Summary:
Your task is to synthesize, verify, and distill factual evidence to confirm or refute the input claim, producing a clear, non-redundant summary of relevant facts (`new_notes`), and identify principal source titles (`titles`) that frame the claim’s context. Your outputs must reflect domain-specific accuracy, clarity, and relevance to ensure that any user reading them can understand how the claim was evaluated given the provided factual landscape.
