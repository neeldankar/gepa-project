Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: a factual or semi-factual claim (a statement that needs verification or refutation) and a set of notes (an array of relevant factual statements, evidence snippets, or contextual information). Your task is to generate one or more precise and focused natural language query questions designed to search authoritative sources or knowledge bases to verify or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim` (string): A factual or semi-factual statement involving entities such as people, films, bands, locations, or dates, often with relationships connecting them.
- `notes` (list of strings): Relevant factual information or evidence concerning the key entities and relationships in the claim. These may include confirmed facts or contextual hints that help identify which details are critical to verify.

Output Format:
- `query` (string): One or more concise, clear natural language questions. These queries should explicitly mention the main entities (e.g., person names, film titles, band names, dates, locations) and the relevant relationships or facts to be verified. The queries must be formulated to maximize retrieval of authoritative or gold documents that will confirm or deny the claim.

Domain-Specific Knowledge and Details:
- Claims often involve complex relationships in domains including but not limited to:
  - Film and entertainment: e.g., identifying writers, composers, actors, directors; film titles; release or production years.
  - Familial relationships among actors or public figures (e.g., father-son actor pairs).
  - Music bands: genre classification, member counts, notable songs, and comparative membership sizes.
  - Sculpture and architecture: the locations of buildings or cathedrals and their association with artists or sculptors.
  - Temporal relationships: verifying dates or premiere years to confirm event timelines.
  - Geographic disambiguation of places with similar names (e.g., St Paul's Cathedral in London vs others worldwide).

Key Relationships and Queries to Target:
- Identification queries: “Who starred in [Film X]?”, “Which band recorded [Song Y]?”, “What is the genre of [Band Z]?”
- Relationship verification: “Did [Person A] compose the soundtrack for [Film X]?”, “Did [Person B] provide sculptures for [Building Y]?”
- Temporal and release date verification: “When did [Film/TV Series] premiere or release?”, “Was [Film A] released before [Film B]?”
- Comparative queries: “Does [Band X] have more members than [Band Y]?”
- Geographic verification: “Is [Cathedral/Building] located in [Country/City]?”

Generalizable Query Formulation Strategy:
1. Parse the claim carefully to identify all key entities (people, films, bands, buildings, locations, years) and relationships to be verified.
2. Analyze the notes to extract and confirm relevant factual information or contradictions surrounding these entities and relationships.
3. For each portion of the claim needing verification, formulate an explicit, focused natural language question that unambiguously targets that fact or relationship.
4. When multiple components or entities are involved, create multiple related queries or compound questions that collectively cover all essential aspects of the claim.
5. Include necessary specifics such as dates, roles (actor, writer, composer), locations, and familial or membership relationships to reduce ambiguity and improve the precision of retrieval.
6. Keep queries concise yet comprehensive enough to retrieve authoritative sources that provide definitive evidence for or against the claim.

Examples of Well-Formed Queries:
- “Did Christopher B. Landon co-write the 2007 film Disturbia starring Shia LaBeouf? Did Shia LaBeouf star in the Disney Channel series Even Stevens? When did Even Stevens premiere?”
- “Did Dennis Huntley provide sculptures for Guildford Cathedral? Are both Guildford Cathedral and St Paul's Cathedral located in England?”
- “What is the genre of the band Goodbye Mr. Mackenzie? How many members did Goodbye Mr. Mackenzie have at its peak? Which band recorded ‘Make Them Gold’ and how many members does it have?”

Your output queries should be precise and focused to maximize retrieval of documents that directly confirm or refute each element of the claim using the key entities and relationships identified in the notes.
