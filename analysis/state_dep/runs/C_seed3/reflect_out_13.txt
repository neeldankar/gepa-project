Task Description:
You will be given a factual or semi-factual claim that involves one or more entities such as people, films, bands, locations, or dates and the relationships among them. Your goal is to generate one or more precise, clear, and focused natural language query questions explicitly designed to search authoritative sources or knowledge bases to verify or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim` (string): A statement requiring verification or refutation. It may contain multiple entities and complex relationships, such as familial ties, professional roles, participation in events, or geographic or temporal assertions.
- `notes` (list of strings): An array of relevant factual statements, evidence snippets, or contextual information about the claim’s key entities and relationships. These include confirmed facts, clarifications, contradictions, or pointers that help identify which aspects need verification.

Output Format:
- `query` (string): One or more concise and unambiguous natural language questions that target the essential facts, entities, and relationships in the claim. The queries should maximize retrieval of authoritative, definitive documents validating or disproving the claim.

Domain-Specific Knowledge and Details:
- Entities may include persons (with roles such as actors, commandants, professors), films and their attributes (writers, composers, lead actors, genre, premiere or release dates), music bands (genres, member counts, notable songs), architecture and sculpture (artists associated with buildings/cathedrals), places (with possible geographic ambiguity among identically named locations), and temporal facts (dates of premieres, durations of service, birth months).
- Relationships of importance include:
  - Identification and membership: e.g., “Who starred in [film]?”, “Is [band] composed of more than five members?”
  - Familial relations and roles: e.g., “Is Rudolf Höss the grandfather of Rainer Höss?”, “Was the grandfather the commandant of Auschwitz?”
  - Geographic verification: confirming the correct location of buildings/cathedrals or schools.
  - Temporal facts: verifying birth months, premiere/release years, length of service in a role.
  - Cross-entity comparisons: e.g., “Does band X have more members than band Y?”
  - Confirmation of professional or functional roles: e.g., “Was [actor] also a professor?”, “Did [person] provide sculptures for [building]?”

Generalizable Query Formulation Strategy:
1. Parse the claim carefully to identify all key entities, their attributes (dates, roles), and the specific relationships that need verification.
2. Study the notes to understand what facts are already known, what may contradict the claim, and which parts of the claim require explicit confirmation.
3. For each critical part of the claim, formulate explicit, focused natural language question(s) that:
   - Mention the main entity/entities by proper names/titles.
   - Specify the role, attribute, or relationship under scrutiny.
   - Include relevant temporal (dates, birth months) or geographic details to minimize ambiguity.
4. When the claim involves multiple components (e.g., a list of entities or both temporal and relational claims), create multiple related queries or a compound question that collectively verify or refute all key elements.
5. Ensure queries are succinct yet comprehensive enough to retrieve authoritative documents with definitive evidence.
6. Avoid over-broad or ambiguous questions that might retrieve irrelevant data; queries should target facts needed to confirm/refute the claim directly.

Examples of Well-Formed Queries:
- “Did Christopher B. Landon co-write the 2007 film Disturbia starring Shia LaBeouf? Did Shia LaBeouf star in the Disney Channel series Even Stevens? When did Even Stevens premiere?”
- “Did Dennis Huntley provide sculptures for Guildford Cathedral? Are both Guildford Cathedral and St Paul's Cathedral located in England?”
- “What is the genre of the band Goodbye Mr. Mackenzie? How many members did Goodbye Mr. Mackenzie have at its peak? Which band recorded ‘Make Them Gold’ and how many members does it have?”
- “Was Rudolf Höss the grandfather of Rainer Höss? Was Rudolf Höss the longest-serving commandant of Auschwitz concentration camp? Did Rudolf Höss conduct any negotiations with owners of Auschwitz concentration camp?”
- “Who starred in the 1955 American neo-western film The Far Horizons? Were any of the lead actors born in August? Was any lead actor known to be a professor?”

Notes on Common Challenges and Best Practices:
- Carefully distinguish between similarly named entities or places with potentially confusing geographic overlap.
- Verify all entities are correctly identified and relationships properly parsed before formulating queries.
- When the claim involves lists (e.g., sets of schools or band members), queries should confirm complete and correct enumeration.
- Use the notes to identify misinformation or erroneous elements in the claim and target queries to expose or correct these.
- Address temporal ambiguities by specifying exact dates or timeframes whenever possible.
- For familial relationships and heritage claims, explicitly query for precise lineage and roles over time.

By following these guidelines, your queries will effectively retrieve authoritative documents that confirm or refute the claim comprehensively and precisely.
