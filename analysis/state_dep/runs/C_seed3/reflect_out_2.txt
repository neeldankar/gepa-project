Task Description:

You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to process these inputs and produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A factual assertion or statement that needs verification or assessment.
- `notes`: A list of textual evidences or observations derived from available information relevant to the `claim`. These notes may include facts that support, refute, or are inconclusive about the claim.
- `context`: A list of passages or short encyclopedic-style entries containing background information, descriptions, or summaries related to entities, locations, persons, or other subjects potentially relevant to the claim.

Outputs:
- `new_notes`: A concise synthesized list of essential evidences derived from `notes` and `context` that directly relate to verifying the truthfulness or details of the claim. These should be factually accurate, remove redundancy, and emphasize whether the claim can be confirmed, contradicted, or remains unverifiable based on the given data.
- `titles`: A list of relevant document or article titles extracted from the `context` that are crucial for understanding or verifying the claim, including gold-standard key documents or entities involved in the claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. Assess the claim carefully and identify its core factual assertions.
2. Examine the `notes` to identify points supporting, contradicting, or lacking evidence about the claim.
3. Use `context` to confirm and enrich the understanding of entities or topics involved, including names, places, relationships, or other relevant factual information.
4. When `notes` and `context` provide corroborating evidence, synthesize this into clear, succinct `new_notes` that focus on key facts, eliminating repetition or verbosity. When evidence is absent or inconclusive, state this uncertainty explicitly.
5. Identify and list in `titles` the minimal but sufficient set of named topics, entities, or documents necessary to verify the claim or to contextualize the claim fully. Include known title names for people, places, works (e.g., book titles or songs), geographical or institutional entities, or any other relevant proper nouns referenced in the claim or evidence.
6. Avoid including irrelevant or unrelated titles; focus on precision and relevance to the claim’s verification.
7. If the claim involves comparative or relational facts (e.g., attributes of multiple entities), ensure the notes and titles address each part separately and clearly.
8. When there is a lack of direct evidence, acknowledge the limits of the current information explicitly in `new_notes`.

Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Entity variants: Persons may be known under variant names (e.g., Norman Alfred William Lindsay vs. Norman Alfred Lindsay). Recognize and unify variants properly.
- Differentiation of similar entities: Some locations or items have similar names but different identities (e.g., Golden Gate National Cemetery vs. San Francisco National Cemetery); be precise in referencing.
- Contextual relevance: Only include titles that are directly relevant to the claim and the verification process, not generic or loosely related items.
- Evidence appraisal: The goal is to confirm, refute, or state the uncertainty of the claim based on factual evidence, not speculation.
- Use clarity when evidence is missing, emphasizing that the claim cannot be verified or disproved with the present information.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Extract key facts from notes and context.
- Cross-reference entities mentioned in the claim with titles in the context.
- Prune redundant or uncertain notes to a minimal clear set.
- List titles that cover all critical elements of the claim (people, places, items).
- Explicitly acknowledge evidential gaps.
- Maintain factual accuracy and relevance throughout.

Example:
Given a claim about a person’s burial place within a national park, notes about various cemeteries, and context detailing the cemeteries and the park, extract notes that confirm or deny the burial association, list the exact cemetery and national park titles, and state clearly if no information is found.

This instruction aims to ensure detailed, structurally clear, and factually grounded outputs to aid claim verification, research, or fact-checking tasks that involve synthesizing multi-source factual evidence and pinpointing relevant supporting documents.
