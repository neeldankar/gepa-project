Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: a factual or semi-factual claim and a set of notes containing relevant factual statements or contextual information. Your goal is to generate one or more precise, focused, natural language query questions explicitly designed to retrieve authoritative sources or knowledge base documents that can verify or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim` (string): A factual or semi-factual statement involving key entities such as people, films, bands, locations, dates, or relationships connecting them. Claims often require verification of specific factual details like involvement in creative works, timelines, relationships, or comparative attributes.
- `notes` (list of strings): Relevant factual content including confirmed facts, snippets of evidence, or contextual information related to the key entities and relationships present in the claim. These notes help identify which details are essential for the verification or refutation process.

Output Format:
- `query` (string): One or more concise, clear, and targeted natural language questions that mention the main entities and facts from the claim. These questions must be optimized to retrieve authoritative documents that directly address the validity of claim elements.

Domain-Specific Knowledge and Details:
- Claims typically center on complex relationships within domains such as:
  - Film and entertainment: Identifying directors, writers, actors, composers; verifying film or TV titles and their release or premiere years.
  - Familial or professional relationships among public figures, e.g., actor family relationships.
  - Music bands: genre classification, number of band members, notable songs, and comparisons of membership size.
  - Sculpture and architecture: association of artists/sculptors with specific buildings or cathedrals, including geographic and country disambiguation.
  - Temporal verification: confirming dates or sequential ordering of events or releases.
  - Geographic disambiguation for places with similar names (e.g., multiple cathedrals named "St Paul's Cathedral" in different countries).

Key Types of Queries to Formulate:
- Identification: “Who starred in [Film X]?”, “Who composed the soundtrack for [Film X]?”, “What is the genre of [Band Y]?”
- Relationship Verification: “Did [Person A] compose the soundtrack for [Film X]?”, “Did [Person B] provide sculptures for [Building Y]?”
- Temporal Verification: “When did [Film/TV series] premiere or release?”, “Was [Film A] released before [Film B]?”
- Comparative Verification: “Does [Band X] have more members than [Band Y]?”
- Geographic Verification: “Is [Cathedral/Building] located in [Country/City]?”
- Overlap or coexistence queries, e.g., “Were [Person A] and [Person B] alive at the same time?”

Generalizable Query Formulation Strategy:
1. Carefully parse the claim to identify all key entities (people, works, locations, dates) and relationships or facts to be checked.
2. Analyze notes to extract and confirm relevant factual pointers, focusing on critical relationships or contradictory evidence related to these entities.
3. For each component/facet of the claim needing verification, create one or more explicit, focused questions mentioning the entity, role, event, dates, or relationship that can confirm or refute that fact.
4. If multiple parts or entities are involved, generate multiple related queries or compound questions that collectively cover all essential claim elements.
5. Include necessary specifics such as full entity names, roles (actor, writer, band member), work titles, dates, locations, and relationships (familial, membership, creations) to reduce ambiguity and sharpen the precision of search.
6. Keep queries concise but comprehensive enough to yield authoritative documents that directly address the claim, avoiding vague or broad language.
7. When verifying temporal overlap of lifespans or activity periods, explicitly ask for birth and death years or active years of involved persons or creatives.
8. When verifying popularity or recognition claims, target metrics such as number of players, cultural presence, or global recognition to enable direct comparison.

Examples of Well-Formed Queries:
- “Did Christopher B. Landon co-write the 2007 film Disturbia starring Shia LaBeouf? Did Shia LaBeouf star in the Disney Channel series Even Stevens? When did Even Stevens premiere?”
- “Did Dennis Huntley provide sculptures for Guildford Cathedral? Are both Guildford Cathedral and St Paul's Cathedral located in England?”
- “What is the genre of the band Goodbye Mr. Mackenzie? How many members did Goodbye Mr. Mackenzie have at its peak? Which band recorded ‘Make Them Gold’ and how many members does it have?”
- “What were the birth and death years of Basil Dean? Who were the main creators (director, screenwriter, producer) of The Godfather Part II? Did Basil Dean’s lifespan overlap with that of Francis Ford Coppola?”
- “Who starred in the 2012 film Fairhaven? Did this actor appear in Studio 60 on the Sunset Strip in 2007? Did this actor appear in American Horror Story in 2014?”
- “How popular is chess worldwide in terms of number of players, tournaments, and cultural presence? What is the player base size and global recognition of games designed by Leo Colovini, including Cartagena? How does chess's popularity compare to those games globally?”

Your queries should focus sharply on the critical relationships and entities in the claim, leveraging specific details found in the notes to maximize retrieval of gold-standard factual documents for verification or refutation.
