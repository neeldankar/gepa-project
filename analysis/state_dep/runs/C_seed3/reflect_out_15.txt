Task Description:
You are given two input fields:
- `claim`: a factual claim or statement involving named entities and relationships between them.
- `notes`: a collection of supporting facts, snippets, or evidence related to entities and concepts mentioned or relevant to the claim.

Your goal is to produce a field named `query` that formulates precise and focused search questions or queries designed to retrieve relevant documents or evidence to verify or refute the claim. 

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Analyze the claim:** Understand all factual components, including entities (people, works, events, organizations, etc.), relationships, attributes, and any temporal or geographical details mentioned.

2. **Examine the notes:** Identify which parts of the claim are explicitly supported, contradicted, or missing in the notes. Focus especially on gaps or inconsistencies in the claim's details or on entities or facts implied but not directly stated.

3. **Identify missing or key information needed:** From the reasoning process, pinpoint which key aspects of the claim require further evidence or clarification. This involves recognizing:
   - Precise factual details such as dates, production countries, roles (e.g., judges, sponsors), or inspiration sources.
   - Expert or domain-specific knowledge such as the structure of operas vs films (e.g., acts), known roles in TV shows, official sponsorship names, and associations between teams, events, or companies.
   
4. **Formulate queries that are comprehensive yet as specific as possible:** 
   - Break down complex claims into multiple sub-queries if needed.
   - Each query should clearly ask for verifiable facts that directly support or refute specific parts of the claim or explore its assumptions or named entities.
   
5. **Queries should be fact-checking in nature:** They should aim to retrieve canonical, authoritative documents or sources that will allow validating the claim or exposing inaccuracies. Avoid vague or overly broad queries.

6. **Incorporate relevant domain-specific terminology:** For example, when dealing with operas, include terms like "acts"; for TV shows, specify "judges" and cast roles explicitly; for sports events, include team names, event names, sponsors, and locations.

7. **Avoid redundant or irrelevant queries:** Use insights from the notes to exclude already confirmed facts when appropriate, instead focusing queries on unresolved or questionable parts.

8. **Use natural, clear language in queries:** Queries should be phrased as explicit questions or statements of fact that a document search system can directly answer.

Summary:
Given the `claim` and the supporting `notes`, produce one or multiple concise queries that target the missing, contradictory, or critical pieces of information necessary to evaluate the validity of the claim using document retrieval methods. Queries should be domain-aware, precise, and directly linked to the core issues in the claim.
