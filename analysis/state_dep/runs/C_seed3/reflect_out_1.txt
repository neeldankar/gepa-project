Task Description:
You will be provided with two inputs: a `claim` (a statement potentially containing multiple factual assertions) and a set of supporting or related textual `notes` (usually a list of sentences drawn from documents relevant to the claim). Your task is to produce a single, concise `query` field — a natural language question — that targets the verification or disambiguation of the key factual components of the claim based on the provided notes.

Goal:
The query should be designed to retrieve or identify the critical pieces of factual evidence that would confirm or refute the claim. It acts like a focused research question that a knowledge retrieval system or fact-checker would use to locate the most relevant authoritative documents. The query should thus explicitly mention the main entities, relationships, and factual assertions contained in the claim that are supported or contradicted by the notes.

Key Points and Domain Knowledge from Examples:

1. Claims can involve multiple entities, events, and time frames. The query should connect these elements clearly and reflect the claim’s implied or explicit factual questions.

2. The notes provide factual context, background, or direct evidence related to the claim’s components. Your query should align with these evidential elements, focusing on known entities or facts (e.g., person names, album titles, team nicknames, roles, dates, place names, and titles of works).

3. The query should isolate the controversial or check-worthy aspects of the claim. For example, instead of restating trivial known facts, emphasize those components that require verification such as:
    - Confirming identity or roles of persons (e.g., who named a player “greatest,” who coached what team).
    - Associations between entities (e.g., whether a specific player played for a certain club/season).
    - Specific time-bound events or achievements (e.g., winning medals in a home country).
    - Accurate linking of titles/nicknames with entities (e.g., Manchester United as “the Red Devils”).

4. The query should combine multiple aspects of the claim if they form a logical single question, but avoid unnecessary complexity or ambiguity.

5. Use proper names and titles exactly as found in the claim or notes to minimize confusion (e.g., full names of people, exact album names, and team names).

6. The query acts as a “fact verification question” that upon answering with retrieved information should lead to confirmation, partial confirmation, or refutation of the claim.

7. Avoid speculative language or adding information not in the claim or notes. The query should only reflect what is necessary and sufficient for verifying the claim.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Parse the claim to identify distinct factual assertions and key named entities.
- Inspect notes to understand which entities and facts are confirmed, contradicted, or need clarification.
- Formulate a clear, focused, and concise natural language question that directly addresses the factual nexus of the claim.  
- Ensure the query is optimally targeted for document retrieval systems to retrieve the most relevant evidence.

Input/Output Format:
Input:
- `claim`: a text string stating the claim.
- `notes`: a list of strings, each a factual sentence possibly extracted from multiple relevant documents.

Output:
- `query`: a single natural language question that can be used to verify the claim by retrieving or checking relevant documents/facts.

Example:
Claim:  
"The album that included the cover of the song Stormy, is the studio album by Carlos Santana, a musician who first became famous in the late 1960s and early 1970s."

Notes:  
["The song 'Stormy' was originally released by Classics IV in 1968 and covered by Santana in 1978 on the album 'Inner Secrets'.", "'Inner Secrets' is a studio album by Carlos Santana.", "Carlos Santana first gained fame in the late 1960s and early 1970s.", ...]

Query (example):  
"Did Carlos Santana cover the song 'Stormy' on his studio album 'Inner Secrets,' and did he first become famous in the late 1960s and early 1970s?"

---

Summary Instruction:
Given a claim and its associated notes containing relevant factual information, generate a precise natural language query question that targets the key factual claims for verification. The query must mention the main entities and specific claims to confirm or refute, reflecting details present in the notes. The query's purpose is to enable retrieval of core supporting evidence or refutations and to remain tightly aligned to the claim's fact pattern without introducing unrelated or speculative content. Use proper names, dates, titles, and roles explicitly, combine related elements logically, and avoid redundancy or ambiguity.
