Task: Given an input claim (a statement or set of statements asserting certain facts) and a set of notes (textual evidence or extracted facts related to the claim), produce a structured query aimed at retrieving information necessary to verify the claim.

Input format:  
- `claim`: A concise statement or compound statement making factual assertions that need verification.  
- `notes`: A list of sentences or fragments providing partial evidence, contextual information, or explanations related to the claim.

Output format:  
- `query`: A natural language information retrieval query (or set of queries) that explicitly asks for the key facts, entities, relationships, classifications, or verifications needed to confirm or refute the claim.

Detailed Instructions:  

1. **Understand the Claim**: Parse the claim carefully to identify the key factual assertions it makes. This frequently involves multiple entities, their attributes, or relationships (e.g., entity classifications, collaborations, native habitats, authorship, directorship).

2. **Analyze the Notes**: Use the notes as evidence that partially address the claim. They often contain facts such as classifications (e.g., musical genres), biographical data (e.g., professions or roles), native habitats, or connections between people or works.

3. **Infer Missing Verification Points**: Identify any implicit missing information or ambiguous components in the claim that need explicit verification to assess truthfulness. For example, if the claim is about two bands both being classified as rock bands, ensure the query asks for:
   - Confirmation of the first band’s genre.
   - Identity and genre of the band the second entity collaborated with.
   
4. **Formulate Concise, Focused Queries**: Compose queries that directly request the verification points identified. Typically this includes asking about:
   - Classification/status of particular entities (e.g., "Is Cast a rock band?")
   - Identification of key entities involved (e.g., "Which bands has Jacob Gentry collaborated with?")
   - Relationships or connections between entities (e.g., "Is there any connection between the co-writer of 'Che strano chiamarsi Federico' and the TV series 'Yes Minister'?")
   - Verifying ambiguous or incomplete claims (e.g., habitats of a species when the claim is incomplete)

5. **Include All Necessary Components**: When a claim contains multiple embedded factual assertions or compound statements, ensure the query covers all relevant questions needed for verification.

6. **Leverage Domain-Specific Knowledge**: The task often involves niche factual domains, such as:
   - Music genre classifications (rock, alternative, electronic, etc.)
   - Collaborations between musical artists or bands
   - Native habitats of biological species
   - Authorship/directorship of films and TV shows, and potential conflations between works or creators
   Use this knowledge to accurately identify what facts matter for verification.

7. **Balance Specificity and Generalizability**: Queries should be specific enough to retrieve the exact facts needed but generic enough to cover possible variations in data sources or document titles.

8. **Output only the query in natural language**: The output should be framed as clear, direct questions designed for information retrieval, avoiding unnecessary verbosity or explanation.

Example pattern for query construction:  
- "Is [Entity A] classified as [Category]?"  
- "Which [entities] has [Person] collaborated with?"  
- "What is the native habitat of [Species]?"  
- "Who directed [Work A]? Is there any connection between [Person/Role] and [Work B]?"

By following these steps, the assistant will produce precise, comprehensive queries that retrieve all necessary documents to verify the claim fully and accurately.
