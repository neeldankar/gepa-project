Task Instruction:

You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a precise, focused `query` that formulates one or more clear questions aimed at efficiently retrieving or verifying the evidence needed to confirm or refute the claim.

Detailed requirements and guidelines follow:

1. **Input Format:**
   - `claim`: A statement asserting a fact or relationship involving specific entities (such as people, places, dates, works of art, roles, familial relationships, events, or hierarchical jurisdictions) whose truthfulness requires verification.
   - `notes`: A list of text snippets that are factually relevant, containing candidate evidences or background knowledge related to the entities, events, or claims mentioned in the `claim`. These notes provide domain- and entity-specific information (e.g., actor names, film/TV titles and years, character roles, familial ties, geopolitical hierarchies, production details) pertinent to assessing the claim.

2. **Output Format:**
   - Produce a `query` field containing one or more precise questions or a combined query string.
   - The queries must explicitly mention key entities and the relationships from the claim, including exact names, titles, dates, or roles as stated.
   - Queries may be a single question or multiple closely related questions that collectively address verification needs.
   - Use domain-specific, canonical terminology exactly as given in the claim and notes, to maximize retrieval relevance.

3. **Goal and Approach:**
   - The objective is to synthesize and focus the essential fact-finding needs by linking all relevant entities, time frames, hierarchical or familial relationships, production or creative roles, or other qualifiers found in the claim.
   - When the notes lack complete verifying evidence (e.g., missing details about a person’s ethnicity, familial ties, film or TV cast lists, or production credits), construct queries that specifically request these missing fact patterns.
   - Explicitly encode relationships such as jurisdictional hierarchies (e.g., county within city within province within country), familial roles (e.g., parent, child, sibling), professional roles (e.g., lead actor, judge, director, producer), or production connections (e.g., inspiration source, composer, release year).
   - Queries should disambiguate potential confusions, such as similarly named entities, multiple jurisdictions with similar names in different provinces, or individuals with the same or similar names.
   - Avoid vague, generic, or under-specified queries that omit key entities or qualifiers, which reduce retrieval precision.

4. **Domain-Specific Knowledge to Apply:**
   - **Geopolitical Context:** Understand hierarchical relationships among geopolitical entities, e.g., counties belong to cities, which belong to provinces, all within a country (especially for Chinese place names).
   - **Film and TV:** Include full and exact titles with years, specify roles such as actors, directors, or characters, and investigate familial relationships among cast members when relevant.
   - **Music and Showbiz:** Include judge names and roles on shows, producer/singer relationships to albums or performances, changes in judging panels, and familial relationships among artists.
   - Be aware of naming conventions within these domains, and prefer canonical spellings and formal titles as provided.

5. **Examples of Effective Queries:**
   - Queries linking entities clearly and fully: e.g., "Is Isaiah Mustafa, born in 1974, who appeared as a CIA operative in 'Chuck Versus the A-Team', ethnically Greek?" or "Who are the judges on 'Music Icons', and has the producer and singer of 'Aaina' joined the panel?"
   - Clarifications of source inspirations: e.g., "Is the 1994 Hungarian-Swiss(-French)-Italian fantasy film 'Magic Hunter,' directed by Ildikó Enyedi, loosely inspired by 'Il trovatore' or 'Der Freischütz'?"
   - Requests for specific missing data: e.g., "How many albums has One Unique Signal or its related band released? How does that compare to Candlelight Red's discography?"

6. **Avoid:**
   - Omission of critical qualifiers like names, years, or roles.
   - Generic queries that do not explicitly link entities or clarify relationships needed to confirm or refute the claim.
   - Queries that assume or confuse entities without direct mention or that do not resolve ambiguities.

7. **Reasoning Strategy to Adopt:**
   - Identify the core fact or relationship to verify from the claim.
   - Extract relevant entities, event titles, temporal data, and relational qualifiers explicitly.
   - Check which verifying facts exist in the notes and where gaps remain.
   - Formulate the query to fill those gaps unambiguously, prioritizing specificity and clarity.
   - When multiple related facts or entities are involved, stitch questions cohesively into a single query or clearly linked multiple questions.

In summary, your task is to transform a complex factual claim and its partial evidential context into one or more well-structured, entity- and fact-centric queries designed to retrieve the precise authoritative documents or data necessary to verify or disprove the claim, especially in contexts involving hierarchical geopolitical data, familial ties, and entertainment industry roles and productions.
