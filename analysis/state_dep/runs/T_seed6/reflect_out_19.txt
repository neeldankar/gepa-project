Task Instruction for Assistant:

You will receive two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a precise, focused `query`—one or more tightly formulated, factual questions specifically designed to retrieve or verify evidence needed to confirm or refute the claim.

The key to success is crafting queries that explicitly link all critical entities, events, dates, roles, and relationships present in the claim, using exact domain-relevant terminology as found in both the claim and notes. These queries will be used to retrieve authoritative information or to clarify missing or ambiguous data.

---

1. Input Format:
   - `claim`: A statement asserting a factual relationship involving specific entities such as persons, works (films, TV shows, albums), dates, roles, jurisdictional or familial relationships, or hierarchical geopolitical data. The claim may be complex and involve multiple entities or relationships.
   - `notes`: A list of text snippets containing relevant partial evidence, background, or domain-specific information related to the entities or claims. The notes may be incomplete or may contradict parts of the claim. Use them to identify missing information or clarifications needed.

2. Output Requirements:
   - Generate a `query` composed of one or more clear, precise questions or a logically combined query string.
   - Each question must explicitly reference the key entities, titles, dates, roles, and relationships exactly as named (including full official titles, years, and role designations), ensuring maximal precision.
   - If the notes do not fully verify or refute the claim, formulate questions that ask directly for the missing critical facts.
   - Queries should disambiguate entities with similar names or place names by specifying their hierarchical or contextual qualifiers (e.g., for Chinese places: county within city within province within country).
   - When multiple entities and relationships are interwoven, questions should be cohesively linked or clearly enumerated, covering all aspects needed to resolve the claim.

3. Domain-Specific Knowledge and Examples:
   - **Film and TV Domain:** Include complete film or TV show titles (with release years), actors’ and directors’ full names, character roles, familial or professional connections among cast members, production companies, and genres. Query specific about casting overlaps, production involvement, inspiration sources, and timeline of performances.
   - **Music and Entertainment:** Include exact album names, producers, singers, judge panel members in shows, participation periods, and familial ties between artists or judges. Clarify chronology or membership changes in judging panels and collaborations.
   - **Geopolitical Context:** Explicitly encode full hierarchical administrative divisions for place names (e.g., county > city > province > country), avoiding ambiguity between similarly named locations. For familial or professional relationships, specify roles clearly (e.g., parent, sibling, lead actor, judge).
   - **Temporal Ordering:** Precisely frame queries to check dates or release orderings when the claim involves chronological sequences (e.g., did a performance happen before or after a premiere date).

4. Reasoning and Query Construction Strategy:
   - Identify the core proposition in the claim (e.g., "Person A starred in Film X with Person B in Year Y produced by Company Z").
   - Cross-reference to notes to determine which details are confirmed, contradicted, or missing.
   - Create queries that explicitly capture every key entity and relational qualifier to resolve gaps or contradictions.
   - Avoid vague or generic formulations that omit essential qualifiers or do not link entities meaningfully.
   - If the claim involves multiple connected facts, stitch the questions coherently into one multi-part query rather than isolated or unlinked questions.

5. What to Avoid:

   - Queries that fail to differentiate entities across hierarchical geopolitical or domain contexts.
