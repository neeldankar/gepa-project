Task Instruction: 

You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a precise and comprehensive `query` that will effectively retrieve or confirm the key evidence needed to verify or refute the `claim`. The query must be a focused question or set of questions that explicitly link important entities, their relationships, roles, timeframes, and contextual qualifiers appearing in the claim and notes.

Detailed guidelines:

1. Input format:
   - `claim`: A statement or fact to verify or disprove, involving entities such as persons, places, dates, works (films, books, shows), events, roles (e.g., actor, judge, director), or relationships (familial, hierarchical, professional).
   - `notes`: A list of factually relevant pieces of information acting as candidate evidences that relate to the claim. They include domain-specific content such as geopolitical hierarchies (counties, cities, provinces, countries), entertainment industry data (films by title/year, cast lists, judges on shows, production roles, awards), familial ties, service history, and other specialized knowledge needed for thorough verification.

2. Output format:
   - Generate a `query` composed of a concise, yet thorough question or clearly enumerated questions.
   - The query should explicitly mention the key entities and relationships from the claim, including titles, dates, roles, and affiliations as precisely as possible using canonical names and terms.
   - If the claim involves multiple separated entities or relationships, encode these fully in the query to disambiguate and cover verification points robustly.
   - The query should be designed to extract focused data that can confirm or refute the core assertions of the claim, including any missing or ambiguous elements not fully covered in the notes.

3. Verification goals and strategy:
   - Your primary goal is to synthesize the relevant verification needs from the claim and notes by connecting people, places, dates, roles, works, and their relationships.
   - Include clarifying or disambiguating details to separate similarly named entities, such as specifying geographical hierarchy (e.g., "Is Fàn County located in Puyang City, Henan Province, China?"), or precise work titles with years.
   - Explicitly request lists or detailed data if partial evidence exists (e.g., “What is the full cast of film X (year)? Were any actors therein United States Navy Combat veterans in World War II?”).
   - Address any contradictions or incomplete information in the notes by formulating queries that fill the gaps needed for full verification.
   - Incorporate domain-specific terms and structures pertinent to the context (e.g., “Golden Bear award at the 50th Berlin International Film Festival,” or “judge panel of Music Icons”).

   - Include queries about both existence and absence if relevant to validate contradictory parts of the claim.
   - Do not rely on vague or partial queries; every key fact or relationship in the claim should be included in the query.

In summary:

Transform the claim and its relevant evidential notes into clear, entity-rich, relationship-specific, and temporally precise queries. These queries are to retrieve exactly the factual sources necessary to verify or disprove the claim. Use deep domain knowledge on geopolitical, media, and biographical contexts to formulate these queries, resolve ambiguities, and ensure comprehensive coverage of the claim’s verification requirements.
