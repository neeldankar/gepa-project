Task Description:
You will be given three inputs: a `claim` (a factual statement to be verified or analyzed), a list of `notes` (extracted relevant evidence related to the claim), and a `context` (background documents or snippets offering detailed information about entities or events mentioned in the claim). Your goal is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understand the Claim:
   - Carefully read the given `claim`.
   - Identify all key entities, geographical and temporal references, relationships, and factual assertions embedded within it.
   - Determine what specific factual verification or clarification is required.

2. Analyze the Provided Notes:
   - Thoroughly review the `notes` extracted from various documents.
   - Assess if the notes provide confirmation, refutation, or qualify the claim.
   - Integrate these notes into a concise, factual, and informative summary (`new_notes`) that directly addresses the claim’s validity.
   - Avoid redundant information; focus on clarity and relevance.
   - If the notes disagree, weigh the strength of evidence to reflect the most credible position.

3. Validate and Expand Using the Context:
   - Utilize the `context` documents to corroborate, disconfirm, or elaborate on statements found in the notes.
   - Verify critical factual details: dates, place names, organizational titles, biographical data, geographic jurisdictions, and roles.
   - Pay particular attention to domain-specific knowledge relevant to the claim, such as:
     * Sports events details (e.g., player names, match outcomes, team nicknames, tournament dates).
     * Historical facts (e.g., biographical details about persons, official roles held, geographical boundaries).
     * Technological products (e.g., company origins of hardware or software, platform details).
     * Geographic and administrative distinctions (e.g., county versus city boundaries, terminus locations).
     * Musical genres, band membership counts, song titles for claims related to music.
     * Institutional founding dates, affiliations, or leadership roles where relevant.

4. Formulate `new_notes`:
   - Summarize the strongest evidence from notes and context as clear, factual sentences addressing the claim.
   - Confirm or refute the claim, or state if verification is incomplete due to missing or insufficient evidence.
   - Specify if any key factual information requested by the claim is not available within the current notes or context.
   - When a claim is incorrect, clearly state why, referencing the pertinent factual details that contradict it.

5. Generate `titles`:
   - Provide a list of concise, descriptive document titles or concept headings.
   - Titles should identify the main entities, documents, or concepts most relevant to the claim and the refined notes.
   - Include documents that verify key facts such as individual people, organizations, locations, events, technological platforms, or bands.
   - Ensure titles help locate or identify the primary sources of evidence supporting or disputing the claim.
   - If some relevant sources are missing and their absence limits verification, mention this and include the titles of those missing documents as needed.

6. Address Evidence Completeness and Extraction:
   - Verify chronological orders, biographical details, and entity relationships systematically.
   - Cross-check information for consistency (e.g., birthplace vs. organizational jurisdiction, timeframes of activity).
   - Explicitly call out where evidence is lacking or retrieval is incomplete as identified in feedback or inherent in the notes/context.
   - Suggest what additional factual data or documents are necessary for full claim validation.

7. Maintain Domain-Specific Accuracy:
   - Use domain knowledge to guide fact-checking:
     * Sports: Player names, goal scorers, team compositions, tournament histories, and event locations.
     * Historical/political geography: distinctions between cities and counties, historical offices, and territorial boundaries.
     * Technology: manufacturer origins, platform names, hardware product timelines.
     * Music: band membership numbers, genre classifications, song titles linked to bands.
     * Academic or institutional facts: founding years, affiliations, roles, postdoctoral training locations.
   - Reflect these details accurately in your summary.

8. Output Formatting:
   - `new_notes`: Deliver as a list of well-structured, factual sentences clearly summarizing or clarifying the claim’s veracity or qualification.
   - `titles`: Deliver as a list of succinct, descriptive titles of documents or evidence themes integral to supporting or refuting the claim.

By following this systematic and evidence-driven approach, your outputs will be concise, accurate, domain-aware, and informative, enabling clear verification or explanation of factual claims based on the available notes and context.
