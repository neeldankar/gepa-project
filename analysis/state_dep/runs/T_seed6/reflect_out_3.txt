Task Instruction:

You will be provided with two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` field consisting of a concise, clear, and precise question or set of questions that will effectively retrieve or verify the evidence required to validate or refute the claim.

Key Aspects of the Task:

1. **Input Format:**
   - `claim`: A factual or verification statement involving entities, events, or relationships that require verification. Entities can include people, places (with hierarchical geopolitical contexts), dates, productions (films, TV shows, music works), familial relationships, roles, or other specialized domain-specific facts.
   - `notes`: A curated list of factually relevant candidate evidences or information pieces. These notes relate to the entities and events in the claim and may include domain-specific knowledge such as cast lists, familial ties, awards, side projects, production roles, jurisdictional hierarchies, or terminology clarifications.

2. **Output Format:**
   - `query`: Formulate a direct and unambiguous question or set of questions designed to extract the exact evidence needed to verify the claim.
   - The query must explicitly mention the key entities and their relationships as they appear in the claim, including necessary qualifiers like dates, titles, roles, or hierarchical details.
   - Multiple questions can be used if needed to cover all aspects, but they should be tightly focused and related to clarifying specific connections, counts, relationships, or attributes that the claim depends upon.
   - Use precise, domain-specific terms and canonical spellings as given to maximize retrieval relevance.

3. **Domain-Specific Knowledge and Coverage:**
   - **Geopolitical Entities:** Include hierarchical jurisdiction details among counties, cities, provinces, and countries to resolve location-based claims (e.g., verifying if a county belongs under a specific city within a province and country).
   - **Film and TV Context:** Identify productions by exact title and year to resolve ambiguities. Confirm cast and crew members, parental or familial relationships between actors, their roles (e.g., lead, judge, director), production companies, and connections between related productions.
   - **Music and Showbiz:** Clarify judges and their roles on shows, replacements on panels, relationships of singers or producers to albums or shows, and side projects or affiliations between artists and bands to ensure accurate attribution.
   - **Biographical and Academic Connections:** Distinguish professions precisely and confirm shared or differing professional fields (e.g., paleontologist vs novelist).

4. **Verification Strategy:**
   - Identify and link all key entities and relationships in the claim, including familial ties, roles, and hierarchies.
   - If existing notes partially cover the evidence but omit critical details for final verification, include queries to fill those gaps (e.g., "Who starred in X film? Who is father of Y actor?").
   - Queries should explicitly seek disambiguation, confirm or exclude fact patterns, and highlight the relationships central to validation.
   - When comparing counts or measurements (e.g., species numbers, awards), ask explicitly for those counts with respect to the named entities.
   - When nicknames or titles may cause ambiguity (e.g., "German Frank Sinatra"), explicitly query for identity and related achievements.
   - Avoid generic or vague requests that do not tie the core entities or relevant roles together.

5. **Generalizable Query Construction Approach:**
   - Start the query by identifying key entities in the claim and their relationships.
   - Formulate questions to retrieve explicit attributes or facts central to the claim’s truth (e.g., profession, number of awards, familial link).
   - Cover all aspects that the claim depends on, especially if the claim is comparative or relational.
   - Where appropriate, include temporal or contextual qualifiers (e.g., date of film, time of show participation).
   - Make sure the produced query targets the known relevant sources or information likely required for verification, as inferred from the notes.

6. **Examples of Effective Queries:**
   - "Which genus is referred to as 'Saule' in French? How many species are in the genus Darwinia? How many species are in the genus Salix? Between Darwinia and Salix, which has more species?"
   - "Who is the Skinny Puppy band member involved with the Rx side project? How many awards has this member won? Who is known as 'German Frank Sinatra'? How many awards has he won? Are there records comparing the awards won by 'German Frank Sinatra' and the Rx member?"
   - "What is Stephen Jay Gould's profession, who coined 'exaptation' with Elisabeth Vrba? What is Ann Beattie’s profession? Do they share the same professional field?"

7. **Avoid:**
   - Queries that lack connection between the key entities and their relationships.
   - Omitting critical qualifiers like event years, exact titles, or roles leading to ambiguous retrieval.
   - Relying on partial or vague information that cannot yield focused verification results.

Summary:
Your task is to translate complex, entity- and relationship-rich claims, supplemented with partial evidence notes, into targeted, domain-aware, and relationship-explicit queries. These queries should allow for the retrieval of gold-standard evidence necessary to confirm or refute the claim in diverse specialized domains such as geopolitics, film and TV production, music and showbiz, and academic or biographical contexts.
