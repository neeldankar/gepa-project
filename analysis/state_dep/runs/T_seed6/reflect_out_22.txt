Task Instruction for Query Generation to Verify Claims

You will be provided with two input fields:  

- `claim`: A factual or verification statement involving one or more entities such as people, places, dates, works (films, TV shows, music), professional or familial relationships, roles, events, teams, or hierarchical geopolitical locations. The claim asserts a fact whose truthfulness needs to be confirmed or refuted.  
- `notes`: A collection of factually relevant information snippets or candidate evidence related to the entities or claims expressed. While these notes may partially overlap or complement each other, they may omit key information necessary for full verification.  

Your goal is to synthesize and output a precise, focused, entity- and relationship-centric `query` (or set of related queries) that explicitly encodes all relevant aspects needed to retrieve or verify authoritative evidence that can confirm or refute the claim fully and unambiguously.

---

Detailed Task Description and Guidelines:

1. **Explicit Entity and Relationship Encoding**  
   - Identify and explicitly name all entities from the claim using their canonical domain-specific spellings (e.g., full personal names, official place names, exact film or show titles with production years).  
   - Explicitly encode key relationships and qualifiers mentioned or implied by the claim, including but not limited to:  
     - Family relationships (e.g., parent-child, siblings, spouses among actors or public figures).  
     - Professional roles (e.g., film director, actor, singer, judge, coach).  
     - Hierarchical geopolitical locations (e.g., a county within a city, which is within a province/state and country).  
     - Event participation (e.g., a person’s involvement in a particular sports event or historical event).  
   - If multiple similar or related entities appear (e.g., multiple correspondents or political figures), clarify their roles and relationships to avoid ambiguity.

2. **Domain-Specific Verifications**  
   - **Geopolitical entities**: When the claim references locations, jurisdictions, or administrative divisions, confirm their hierarchical relationships and verify whether entities are in the same or different political units, specifying country and administrative levels.  
   - **Entertainment industry**:  
     - Film/TV shows: Verify official cast and crew listings with roles and production years (e.g., "Bombay Talkie (1970)").  
     - Familial relationships among actors, producers, or singers must be explicitly queried.  
     - When verifying roles (director, producer, singer, judge on shows), request official credits or authoritative cast/crew lists.  
   - **Sports and biographies**: Verify correct attributions of quotations, medals or awards, sports events, athlete nationalities or ethnicities, and official roles or titles.  
   - When the claim involves multiple assertions (e.g., an athlete’s ethnicity and medal wins), your queries should seek to verify all relevant parts coherently.

3. **Query Construction Principles**  
   - Construct clear, focused, and unambiguous questions that seek factual verification of the exact entities and relationships in the claim. Avoid vague or overly broad questions.  
   - Use full canonical names, official titles, complete names of works (with years), and precise roles when mentioning entities in the query.  
   - Formulate compound claims as multiple related questions targeting each key piece of verification needed. Separate complex verification angles.  
   - When the notes lack direct evidence, design queries to explicitly retrieve missing authoritative information such as official cast/crew lists, verified quotes, detailed athlete profiles, or confirmed familial ties.  
   - Disambiguate entities or locations formally (e.g., "San Antonio, Texas, United States" vs. "San Antonio, California, United States") when necessary.

4. **Examples of Factual Information to Request**  
   - Identity and official role(s) of individuals referenced in the claim (e.g., "Who was the President of Cuba in 1940?").  
   - Official cast or crew lists of a named production, specifying production year and identifying roles for persons named.  
   - Hierarchical political geography relationships to confirm shared jurisdiction or country.  
   - Verified quotes or claims attributed to named individuals, and who contradicted them.  
   - Confirmation of awards, medals, ethnic or national identities for named athletes related to specific events.  
   - Familial relationships (e.g., "Are X and Y siblings or related?").  
   - Relationships or disputes among correspondents, public figures, or journalists if central to the claim.  

5. **Output Format**  
   - Your output `query` should be one or several precise questions composed as natural language queries, explicitly referencing all necessary entities, dates, and relationships to retrieve gold-standard verification documents.  
   - Avoid generic, vague, or incomplete queries — every query must be traceable to a specific verification need expressed or implied by the claim.  

---

Summary:  
Your task is to produce highly precise, fact-driven queries that explicitly mention all entities, relationships, and qualifiers necessary to fully confirm or refute the given claim. This includes detailed political/geographical hierarchies, professional and familial roles, event participation, and exact productions, helping retrieval systems identify and extract the authoritative "gold standard" evidence for verification. The queries should address all subclaims and ambiguities in the original claim, maximizing specificity and minimizing vagueness.
