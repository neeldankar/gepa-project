Task Description:

You will be provided with two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a precise and focused `query` that can be used to retrieve or verify the exact evidence needed to confirm or disprove the factual claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual or verification statement involving named entities (people, places, events, works, roles, dates, relationships, organizations, jurisdictions, etc.) that requires validation.
- `notes`: A list of factually relevant pieces of information or candidate evidences (“sources” or “documents”) related to the entities, relationships, or events mentioned in the claim. These notes may include domain-specific information, hierarchical or relational data (e.g., administrative boundaries, familial or professional relationships, roles in productions, or award records).

Output Format:
- Produce a `query` field containing one or more direct, unambiguous questions or a precise query string aimed at retrieving the critical documents/evidence required to validate or refute the claim.
- The query must explicitly mention core entities and their relationships as described in the claim, incorporating domain-specific terminology and canonical names exactly as given.
- When multiple entities or relationships exist (such as hierarchical jurisdictions, familial ties, or shared roles), encode these clearly in the query.
- If gaps in information exist or the notes lack complete evidence, construct queries to explicitly request missing but necessary details (e.g., complete cast lists, administrative boundaries, familial relations).

Goal and Strategy:
- Your primary goal is to synthesize the essential fact-checking needs by linking relevant entities, roles, events, and relationships in the claim.
- The query should be concise yet comprehensive, designed to retrieve documents that are gold-standard evidence for verification.
- Emphasize clarity and specificity, avoiding vagueness or overly broad phrasing that can reduce relevance.
- Disambiguate entities with similar or partial identifiers by adding qualifiers such as titles, years, locations, roles, or relationships.
- Cover all components of the claim explicitly, especially when it contains multiple assertions or complex relationships.

Domain-Specific Notes and Knowledge (for formulating queries):
- Geopolitical and Administrative Hierarchies: Understand and reflect county-city-province-country relationships. For example, when querying about locations, include full administrative chains (e.g., “Is Puyang City located in Henan Province, China?” or “Which cities and institutions does Clark County, Nevada include?”).
- Film, TV, and Performing Arts: Include exact titles with release years; specify roles such as lead actor, producer, director; and confirm familial ties relevant to claims (e.g., “Was actor X the father of star Y in film Z (year)?”). Include production companies or show names when relevant.
- Music Industry and Talent Shows: Include judges’ names, roles, replacements, producers, and performers with show names and album titles. Queries should clarify relationships between producer, singer, and their participation in shows.
- Nobel Laureates and Literary Figures: Confirm laureate identity, nationality, professions (e.g., poet-diplomat), and literary accomplishments explicitly.
- Always use canonical or exact naming conventions as presented in the claim and notes to maximize retrieval precision.
- Avoid ambiguous phrasing or queries lacking linkage between entities and key claim facts.

Examples of Good Queries:
- Explicitly mention key entities and their relationships: “Did Craig Heyer play baseball at the College of Southern Nevada located in Clark County, Nevada, which also contains the city of Las Vegas?”
- Resolve geographical or administrative hierarchies: “Is the town administered by Eurobodalla Shire council? What towns does this council govern? Is the town located near Higgin’s Creek and Deua National Park?”
- Clarify missing evidence or distinguish entities of similar nature: “Was there an Austrian poet-diplomat who won the Nobel Prize in Literature? Provide the name and verify literary credentials. Also, confirm Alan Sillitoe’s status as a British writer.”

Important Points and Avoidances:
- Do not make queries generic or omit key qualifiers such as dates, roles, or hierarchical info.
- Avoid queries that fail to link entities or omit relationships vital to the claim’s verification.
- When evidence appears incomplete, frame queries to explicitly seek the missing critical details.
- Your queries should enable retrieval of the smallest possible set of gold-standard documents that comprehensively verify or refute the claim.

Summary:
Transform complex, entity-rich claims and partial supporting notes into sharply focused, entity-relational queries that tightly connect all key factual components. Use domain-specific, hierarchical, and relational knowledge embedded in the claim and notes to craft queries that achieve maximal precision and recall for verifying nuanced, multi-faceted factual statements across geopolitical, entertainment, literary, and cultural domains.
