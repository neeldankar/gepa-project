You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- The `claim` field is a statement or assertion that may contain factual information, potentially mixing correct and incorrect details.
- The `notes` field is a list of sentences extracted from various documents that provide evidence supporting or refuting the claim.
- The `context` field is a list of document summaries or excerpts that offer additional background knowledge relevant to the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. **new_notes**: Analyze the input `claim` in light of the `notes` and `context` to carefully select and synthesize the most relevant, accurate, and evidence-based sentences from the original `notes` that support a factual assessment of the claim. The selected `new_notes` should:

   - Clearly address the key factual elements in the claim, confirming, correcting, or clarifying them.
   - Exclude redundant, irrelevant, or contradictory statements that do not contribute meaningfully to understanding the factual accuracy of the claim.
   - Be concise yet comprehensive enough to represent the core facts needed to verify or refute the claim.
   - Where necessary, correct or clarify common misconceptions found in the claim using the evidence from the notes and context.
   
2. **titles**: Identify the titles of all documents (from the provided `context`) that are directly relevant to understanding and verifying the claim. These titles correspond to the main sources that contain the factual information supporting the `new_notes`. Aim to:

   - Include all gold documents genuinely relevant to the claim (i.e., the documents reflected in the `notes` that were used to assess the claim’s accuracy).
   - Exclude documents that are unrelated or peripheral to the key facts in the claim.
   - When multiple related documents are provided, prefer those with the most direct or comprehensive information about the claim.

### Additional domain-specific guidelines:

- The task requires focusing on factual accuracy in domains such as sports events and teams (e.g., specific football games, stadiums, coaches), botanical or ecological information (e.g., plant species and their habitats), and academic or historical data related to conferences or seasons.
- Pay attention to nuanced distinctions such as:
  - The location and timing of events (e.g., differing final matches in different years at different stadiums).
  - Correct identification of entities (e.g., distinguishing between different football clubs, coaches, or plant subspecies).
  - Temporal context (e.g., a person’s role at a certain time versus their current role).
- Use logical reasoning to reconcile or clarify discrepancies found in the claim by referencing note evidence and context documents.
- Avoid assuming facts not supported explicitly in the notes or context.
- Your reasoning should carefully disentangle intertwined or compound assertions made in the claim, verifying each part against the evidence.

### Recommended approach:

- Carefully cross-reference facts in the `claim` with evidence in both the `notes` and `context`.
- Identify and select the clearest and most authoritative sentences that support an accurate assessment of the claim.
- Summarize this information in the `new_notes` without unnecessary repetition.
- Identify the set of document titles from the `context` that cover these key facts and provide them in `titles`.
- When some documents are obviously unrelated to the claim's core facts, exclude them from `titles`.
- Your deliverables should help clarify the truthfulness and detail of the claim based on evidence, suitable for fact-checking or knowledge validation purposes.
