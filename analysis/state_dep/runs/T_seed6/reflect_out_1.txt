Task Instruction:

You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a `query` field that formulates a precise, focused question or set of questions that would efficiently retrieve or verify the evidence needed to validate or refute the `claim`. 

Details and guidelines to follow:

1. **Input format:**
   - `claim`: A factual or verification statement involving entities (people, places, dates, works, relationships, roles, events, etc.) whose truth needs to be verified.
   - `notes`: A list of factually relevant pieces of information or candidate evidences (called sources or documents) that relate to entities, events, or claims mentioned in the `claim`. The notes contain domain and entity-specific information such as names of people, locations, productions, roles, dates, familial relationships, jurisdictional hierarchies, or other specialized knowledge that is pertinent to the claim verification.

2. **Output format:**
   - Produce a `query` field, which is a concise but comprehensive question or query string that is designed to retrieve documents or evidence most relevant to confirming or disconfirming the claim.
   - The query should explicitly mention key entities and relationships from the claim.
   - The query can be a single question or multiple related questions formulated clearly and unambiguously.
   - Use domain-specific terms or entity names as precisely as they appear to maximize the relevance of search or retrieval results.

3. **Goal and strategy:**
   - The primary goal is to synthesize the essential verification needs by linking relevant entities, events, roles, timeframes, or relationships indicated by the claim.
   - The goal is to generate a query that retrieves the exact gold standard documents needed for verifying the claim.
   - If the claim involves multiple entities with implied relationships (e.g., jurisdiction, familial ties, membership, roles in productions), encode these relationships explicitly in the query.
   - If the existing notes lack complete evidence, construct queries that attempt to fill these gaps by requesting specific missing details (such as full cast lists, parental relationships, or location hierarchies).
   - Queries should be designed to disambiguate potential confusions and confirm or exclude specific fact patterns.

4. **Domain-specific knowledge to consider:**
   - Geopolitical entities: County-to-city hierarchical jurisdiction within provinces and countries (e.g., Fàn County is under Puyang City in Henan Province, both cities within China).
   - Film and TV entities: Films (by name and year), cast and crew members, familial relationships between actors (e.g., father of star), production companies, and roles (e.g., lead actor, judge).
   - Music and showbiz domain: Names of judges, roles, replacements on shows, producer/singer relationships to musical albums or shows.
   - Use well-known naming conventions and canonical spellings as given.
   - Awareness of context-specific ambiguities (e.g., different people named similarly, cities in same country but different provinces).

5. **Examples of good query characteristics:**
   - Explicit mention of both entities whose relationship or co-location is addressed (e.g., Are Puyang City and Pu'er City both located in China?).
   - Inclusion of necessary roles or relational qualifiers when verifying family ties (e.g., Who starred in "Bombay Talkie" (1970), and is any actor the father of a "Sultanat" star?).
   - Querying for presence or absence of particular roles or people when claim status is unclear (e.g., Are Meesha Shafi, Strings, Ali Azmat, and Junaid Khan judges on "Music Icons", and has the producer and singer of "Aaina" joined the panel?).

6. **Avoid:**
   - Generic or vague queries that do not link key entities explicitly.
   - Omitting important qualifiers like titles, years, or roles that would reduce retrieval accuracy.
   - Queries relying solely on partial information without connecting to the claim’s main verification point.

In summary, your task is to transform claims and their partial evidential notes into focused, entity- and relationship-centric queries that enable retrieving the precise evidence required to verify those claims, especially in complex, domain-rich contexts involving hierarchical geopolitical information, familial connections, and role assignments in entertainment media.
