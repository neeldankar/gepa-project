Task Instruction:

You will receive two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` (or a set of queries) that explicitly and precisely formulates the factual questions required to validate or refute the given claim based on the relevant entities, relationships, and domain contexts presented or implied by the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving one or more entities (people, bands, films, songs, locations, dates, roles, events, jurisdictions, titles, ethnicities, and hierarchical relationships) whose truthfulness must be checked.
- `notes`: A collection of factually relevant information snippets that may partially or fully contain evidence relating to the entities, relationships, or claims in the `claim`. These notes provide context but may not be comprehensive.

Goal:
Formulate one or more precise, focused, and unambiguous questions designed to retrieve or verify the authoritative "gold-standard" evidence necessary to fully confirm or disconfirm the claim in context.

Key Requirements and Guidelines:

1. Explicit Entity and Relationship Encoding:
   - Clearly identify and name all key entities in the claim using their canonical domain-specific spellings (e.g., full names of people, full and official titles of bands, movies, or songs).
   - Explicitly encode every relationship or role mentioned or implied, including professional roles (e.g., band member, frontman, director, co-director, judge), family ties, hierarchical geopolitical structures (e.g., county within city within province within country), and participation in events.
   - Disambiguate similar or ambiguous entities by providing domain-specific qualifiers such as dates (e.g., production year), geographic context (e.g., Norwegian county Hordaland), or roles (e.g., co-founder versus member) to avoid confusion.
   - When multiple entities with relationships appear, break down or link these relationships explicitly, such as specifying which band a particular musician is a member of, or clarifying which person holds what role.

2. Domain-Specific Contextuality and Factual Information:
   - Geopolitical claims require clarifying the administrative hierarchy and confirming if a specified entity is located within a certain jurisdiction, including country and sub-national divisions.
   - Entertainment domain claims may involve cast and crew roles, work titles with production years or release dates (e.g., "Bombay Talkie (1970)"), familial relationships among artists, and roles such as singers and producers.
   - Music claims might involve identifying band membership, songwriter credits, and nationality or country of origin of artists.
   - Sports-related claims require verifying athlete identities, their ethnic/national status (e.g., Native American), event participation, awards or medals earned, and teams or coaches associated with them.
   - Genres, membership counts, and comparisons between bands or ensembles must be explicitly confirmed, including official genre classifications and membership rosters.
   - For all claims involving works of art, music, or media, ask for official or complete cast, crew, and credits lists where necessary.

3. Query Construction Principles:
   - Frame queries as targeted, specific, and fully explicit questions that collectively seek to establish every essential component of the claim: entity identities, roles, relationships, event participation, dates, hierarchical or geographic qualifiers, and contrastive or comparative elements.
   - When claims include compound components or multiple entities, break down the verification needs into separately focused but clearly linked questions to cover all facets.
   - If the notes lack direct evidence on certain key points, queries must be designed explicitly to request missing foundational information (e.g., official band membership lists, verified songwriter attribution, ethnic background, or precise geographic jurisdiction).
   - Avoid vague, generic, or overly broad queries that do not decisively connect or disambiguate the key claim elements.
   - Always include domain-specific terminology and canonical spellings or official names as revealed in the claim and notes.

4. Incorporation of Task Insights from Past Examples:
   - When multiple bands or works are mentioned (e.g., several bands with overlapping membership), queries must specify which bands are involved and their exact relationships to the named entity.
   - For geographic claims involving locations and administrative units (e.g., counties and cities), specify the place hierarchy and verify if entities share the same jurisdiction.
   - When nationality or origin differences are part of the claim, queries must explicitly request verification of each individual’s nationality or country of origin, and whether they are the same or different.
   - When genre or membership count comparisons are made, queries must validate both classification and numeric data of each group or entity and the comparison itself.
   - Include identity clarification for potentially ambiguous individuals (e.g., if a claim names someone involved in a band or movie without further identifiers, ask for full official names, roles, or co-participant names.

In summary, your output query must synthesize a well-structured, domain-appropriate, and entity-relationship explicit set of questions that precisely target all knowledge dimensions necessary to confirm or refute the claim with authoritative evidence. This ensures comprehensive retrieval of gold-standard documents and enables thorough fact verification.
