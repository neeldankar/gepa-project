Task Description:
You will be given three inputs: a `claim` (a factual statement to be verified or analyzed), a list of `notes` (extracted relevant pieces of information from documents or sources related to the claim), and a `context` (background information and detailed documents or snippets related to entities or events mentioned in the claim). Your goal is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understand the Claim:
   - Carefully read and analyze the `claim`.
   - Identify key entities, relationships, facts, and temporal or contextual information relevant to the claim.

2. Leverage the Notes:
   - Review the `notes` which are pre-extracted supporting or contradictory statements related to the claim.
   - Integrate and synthesize these notes to form a concise, informative summary (`new_notes`) that directly addresses the claim by confirming, refuting, or qualifying it.
   - The `new_notes` should be coherent, factual, and reflect the strongest evidence found within the notes and context.
   - Avoid repetition or unnecessary verbosity—focus on clarity and relevance.

3. Use the Context to Validate and Expand:
   - Use the `context` documents to corroborate or disconfirm information found in the notes.
   - Identify which documents or passages are most pertinent to the claim and the information in the notes.
   - Ensure factual accuracy regarding details such as dates, names, affiliations, and roles.
   - Pay special attention to domain-specific facts related to entities (e.g., historical dates, biographical details, organizational roles, affiliations, genre or membership numbers in music bands, factual confirmations about scientific training, or historical positions).

4. Generate Titles:
   - Derive a list of concise and descriptive titles (`titles`) corresponding to the key documents, entities, or concepts strongly related to the claim and the refined notes.
   - Titles should be specific, reflect the content focus, and help identify the most relevant pieces of evidence or sources supporting or contesting the claim.
   - Include titles that cover all main entities or facts addressed in the notes, including any missing but required documents identified from the feedback analysis.
   
5. Address Incompleteness:
   - In cases where evidence is missing or the claim cannot be fully verified, explicitly note that status in `new_notes`.
   - Include references to documents or aspects where evidence is lacking, indicating the limits of support for the claim.

6. Generalizable Strategy:
   - Systematically verify chronological order, biographical details, and entity relationships from context and notes relative to the claim.
   - Synthesize evidence into a factual, logically consistent summary representing the claim’s support status.
   - Identify missing or unreferenced but necessary documents or facts to fulfill a comprehensive verification, as seen in feedback for retrieval completeness.

7. Factual and Domain-Specific Knowledge:
   - When relevant, include:
     * Precise establishment years for institutions or organizations.
     * Biographical details about persons (e.g., postdoctoral training institutions, faculty positions, or familial relations).
     * Specific roles held by individuals (e.g., commandant of concentration camps).
     * Accurate genre classification and membership counts for musical groups.
     * Specific song titles linked to bands.
     * Historical or factual clarifications about events or roles.
   - Ensure the output reflects these details factually and clearly.

Format of Outputs:

- `new_notes`: A list of one or more well-formed sentences accurately summarizing or clarifying the claim with reference to the evidence.
- `titles`: A list of succinct, relevant document or concept titles that represent key sources or focal points used in supporting or refuting the claim.

Example considerations (drawn from examples):

- For chronological claims, confirm dates of establishment and training.
- For personal role claims, confirm familial relationships and roles with documentation.
- For comparative membership claims in music, verify group genres and number of members from context.
- Explicitly state gaps or lack of evidence when supporting details cannot be found.
- Titles should cover individual people, organizations, events, and key concepts related to the claim.
