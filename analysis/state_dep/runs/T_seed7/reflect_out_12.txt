Task Objective:
Given three structured inputs — `claim`, `notes`, and `context` — your task is to synthesize and refine the provided information into two precise outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. These outputs are designed to assist in clarifying, verifying, or refuting the input claim by concentrating on direct factual relevance and domain-specific accuracy.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A single declarative statement typically asserting a factual relationship, historical fact, geographic or organizational attribute, identity, or event. Claims often contain entities such as people, places, organizations, or events, sometimes with domain-specific nuances (e.g., sports positions, movie genres, botanical habitats).
- `notes`: A list or collection of factual statements or excerpts that relate to the claim. These may contain overlapping, partially redundant, or conflicting information extracted from various sources. Some notes might be irrelevant or tangential.
- `context`: More extended descriptive passages or articles related to the entities, concepts, or topics in the claim or notes. Context often clarifies ambiguous terms, differentiates homonyms, provides background, or offers historical/taxonomic/geographic detail.

Output Requirements:
1. `new_notes`:
   - Select a concise subset of the input notes that directly supports or refutes the claim.
   - Remove irrelevant, contradictory, or repetitive information.
   - Merge overlapping notes to improve clarity and coverage when appropriate.
   - Emphasize domain-specific facts necessary to evaluate the claim (e.g., sports roles, plant native habitats, geographic administrative hierarchies, movie production details).
   - Preserve factual accuracy and avoid ambiguity or unsupported statements.
   - Address mistaken or conflicting claim elements by explicitly noting corrections if apparent.
   - Avoid unnecessary verbosity; prioritize succinctness and precision.

2. `titles`:
   - Provide a list of Wikipedia-style canonical article or entity titles relevant to the claim.
   - Include titles directly referenced or alluded to in notes and context that contain evidence supporting or contesting the claim.
   - Add related titles that provide essential background or clarify domain-specific terms relevant to understanding the claim (e.g., specific team names in sports, botanical genus or species, geographic locations, film titles, notable persons).
   - Avoid including overly broad or unrelated titles.
   - Ensure consistent formatting and naming conventions appropriate for encyclopedic references.

Domain-Specific and Niche Considerations:
- Sports Claims:
  - Understand distinctions between sports with similar names (e.g., "football" as soccer vs. American football).
  - Identify player roles, positions, coaching histories, and timelines specific to seasonal or conference affiliations.
  - Recognize team nicknames versus player names, avoiding conflations without clear evidence.
- Film and Media Claims:
  - Confirm production type (animated, live-action, direct-to-video), genre, country of origin, and notable character appearances.
  - Distinguish between different adaptations or versions (e.g., original vs. remake).
  - Recognize notable actors/voice actors and their roles.
- Botanical and Biological Claims:
  - Accurately identify native habitats, ecological biomes, or geographic distributions of species.
  - Differentiate between species names and common names or habitat types (e.g., meadow vs. semi-desert).
  - Avoid overstating or inferring unsubstantiated habitat overlap.
- Geographic or Organizational Claims:
  - Verify administrative or hierarchical relationships (e.g., parks within national recreation areas, cemeteries within parks).
  - Distinguish similarly named but distinct entities (e.g., San Francisco National Cemetery vs. Golden Gate National Cemetery).
- Biographical Claims:
  - Carefully validate professions, historical roles, and titles (e.g., athlete vs. military officer).
  - Note corrections or contradictions within input about personal attributes.

Generalizable Reasoning Strategy:
- Cross-reference claim components with notes and context to identify direct confirmations or contradictions.
- Use context to disambiguate potentially confusing terms or entities that share names but differ in domain or identity.
- Synthesize multiple overlapping notes into clear, non-redundant factual statements.
- Explicitly call out and correct incorrect or unsupported elements of the claim within `new_notes`.
- Select article/entity titles that best equip a user to verify or deepen understanding of the claim and its domain.
- Ensure output clarity and precision to serve as a reliable summary and evidence guide.

Formatting and Style:
- Use clear, concise language.
- When referencing person nicknames or official names, use quotation marks for clarity.
- Present `titles` as a list of canonical Wikipedia-style article titles.
- Avoid unnecessary verbosity or subjective commentary.
