Task Description:
You will be provided with two fields as inputs:
- `claim`: A factual or assertive statement that makes one or more specific factual claims.
- `notes`: A list of background factual statements or snippets relevant to the claim, potentially including multiple references, repetitions, or partial information.

Your task is to produce a single field:
- `query`: A concise and precise natural language question or set of questions designed to retrieve key information that verifies or disputes the claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Analyze the claim carefully** to understand the factual assertions it contains. Claims often combine multiple elements (e.g., persons, roles, events, compositions, relationships).

2. **Review the notes thoroughly** to extract relevant factual details related to each element in the claim. Notes may include:
   - Names of individuals, their relationships, roles, or attributes.
   - Titles of works (e.g., movies, musical compositions), their creators or participants.
   - Historical facts or documented events.
   - Possible contradictions or clarifications.
   - Redundancies and repeated information.

3. **Identify the key factual points that must be verified** in order to confirm or reject the claim. This often involves confirming:
   - Identities and connections (e.g., actor X appeared in movies A and B and is best known for role C).
   - Specific roles or actions attributed to persons (e.g., who conducted negotiations; who held a position).
   - Authorship, nationality, and other attributes (e.g., composer of a musical piece and nationality).
   - Relationships or lineage (e.g., grandfather or grandson relations).

4. **Compose a precise and focused query that:**
   - Targets the critical entities, relationships, or attributes necessary for verification.
   - Frames the question so the retrieved documents will best help establish the truthfulness or falsity of the claim.
   - When multiple factual points exist in the claim, incorporate them logically in the query to retrieve all relevant evidence at once.
   - Avoid unnecessary verbosity, but include sufficient context to avoid ambiguity.

5. **Use natural, clear, and unambiguous language** that a search system or knowledge base can interpret effectively.

6. **In cases where the claim combines separate factual elements incorrectly, explicitly query for confirmation or contradiction about the combined assertions.** For example, if one person is said to have done two roles but notes show those roles were done by different persons, make that ambiguity explicit in the query.

Objective:
- The query should be aimed at retrieving all relevant documents needed to verify the claim fully.
- Queries should be comprehensive enough to maximize recall of relevant evidence but focused enough to avoid irrelevant results.

Domain-Specific Knowledge:
- Names and relationships often matter (e.g., verifying family relations such as “grandfather of Rainer Höss”).
- Specific attribution of roles or authorship is critical (e.g., who composed a piece, or who played a role in a TV show or film).
- Verification sometimes requires disproving alternate claims or candidates (e.g., ruling out Johann Christoph Pepusch as the composer).
- Negations or exclusions (“not Johann Christoph Pepusch”) in the claim must be explicitly included in the query.
- Precise phrasing of work titles, roles, and personal names (with accurate spelling) is essential.
- When claims refer to complex or nuanced historical events or roles, the query should clearly differentiate between related but distinct actions (e.g., conducting negotiations vs. being a commandant).

Generalizable Strategy Employed:
- Break down the claim into atomic verifiable facts.
- Cross-reference these facts with the notes to understand which are supported, contradicted, or absent.
- Formulate queries that explicitly ask for the validation of these atomic facts and their connections.
- Include alternative possibilities where the notes suggest ambiguity or conflicting roles.
- Incorporate any negations or exclusions present in the claim into the query to avoid retrieving documents that would mislead verification.

By following these guidelines, you ensure that the `query` field is optimized for finding documents that conclusively verify or refute the `claim` based on the provided `notes`.
