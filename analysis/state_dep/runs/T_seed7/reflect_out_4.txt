Task Objective:
Given three fields — `claim`, `notes`, and `context` — generate two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. The goal is to synthesize and refine the information provided to assist in validating or clarifying the `claim` based on available evidence.

Input Details:
- `claim`: A declarative statement that typically asserts a factual relationship, historical event, identity, or attribute involving people, places, organizations, or events. Claims often contain domain-specific knowledge (e.g., sports history, academic appointments, geographical locations).
- `notes`: A collection of factual statements or excerpts extracted from various sources that are relevant to verifying or negating the claim. These notes may include partially overlapping or conflicting information, and should be carefully evaluated for accuracy and relevance.
- `context`: Longer background texts or articles related to entities, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim and notes. Context passages provide detailed domain-specific information that helps verify claims or clarify ambiguities.

Output Requirements:
1. `new_notes`: A concise, accurate, and factually relevant subset of the input notes that directly pertain to verifying the claim. This output should:
    - Filter out irrelevant or misleading notes.
    - Combine multiple notes if necessary to improve clarity and coverage.
    - Emphasize domain-specific facts needed to support or refute the claim.
    - Avoid repetition while preserving key facts.

2. `titles`: A list of relevant Wikipedia-style article titles or named entities derived from the context or notes that would help a user understand or verify the claim. This should include:
    - Titles of articles directly referenced or alluded to in notes or context that contain evidence.
    - Related titles that fill knowledge gaps or contextualize important information (e.g., team names, person biographies, event titles).
    - Prioritize retrieval of gold-standard or strongly relevant documents related to the claim.

Detailed Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Sports-related claims often require understanding of player roles, match outcomes, team nicknames, and historical event details. For example, distinguishing “football” as American football vs. association football (soccer), identifying goal scorers in specific tournaments, or understanding coaching roles within defined seasons or conferences.
- Academic or professional claims relating to people’s roles (e.g., coaching positions, administrative titles) should respect timeline nuances (such as tenure years) and official positions held at precise times.
- Geographic or organizational claims about location-based relationships must consider administrative subdivisions (states, cities, countries) and specific place-based entities like airports or headquarters, verifying hierarchical relationships (e.g., states being part of a country).
- Claims involving colloquial names or nicknames should be treated cautiously; ensure no conflation between person names and team or organization names unless supported by clear evidence.

Generalizable Reasoning Strategy:
- Closely cross-reference claim elements with facts in notes and deeper context to identify contradictions or confirmations.
- Identify and exclude irrelevant or contradictory information that does not directly address the claim.
- Use context snippets to disambiguate similar or potentially confusing terms (e.g., identical names in different domains).
- Select note excerpts that best support or refute the claim and summarize them effectively in `new_notes`.
- Extract article or entity titles that a knowledgeable user or researcher would consult to validate or deepen understanding of the claim.

Formatting and Clarity:
- Provide outputs in clear, succinct text.
- Use quotation marks for person nicknames or specific official names to maintain clarity.
- When creating `titles`, use consistent, canonical Wikipedia-style naming conventions.
- Avoid unnecessary verbosity; focus on factual precision and relevance.
