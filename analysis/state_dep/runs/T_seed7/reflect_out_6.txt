Given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, generate a `query` designed to retrieve evidence supporting or refuting the factual accuracy of the claim.

Task Details:
1. The `claim` is a factual statement or assertion that requires verification.
2. The `notes` contain snippets or pieces of supporting information extracted from documents relevant to the claim.
3. Your goal is to produce a precise, information-seeking query that, when executed against a factual knowledge base or document repository, will retrieve the specific documents or pieces of evidence necessary to verify the claim’s truthfulness.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Details:
- The query should focus on retrieving entities, attributes, or relationships explicitly mentioned or implied in both the claim and the notes.
- It must reflect the core factual questions underlying the claim, targeting key facts such as:
  - identities, nationalities, and roles of persons (e.g., actors in movies, birthplaces)
  - connections between entities (e.g., "Which actor appeared in both Movie A and Movie B?")
  - attributes or classifications (e.g., "Are both X and Y recognized dog breeds?")
  - precise verification targets, such as locations, dates, and rankings where relevant
- The query should be framed in a way that facilitates retrieval of all necessary relevant documents or structured data entities to confirm or deny the claim.
- It should avoid ambiguity and include explicit references to proper names, titles, or key descriptors from the claim and notes, ensuring high precision.
- If the claim involves multiple parts or comparisons (e.g., two people or breeds), the query should seek evidence for all parts to fully verify the compound claim.
- When appropriate, the query can be formulated as a natural language question or as a structured database query if it better suits the underlying task.
- Use domain knowledge explicitly when clarifying terms or equivalences. For example:
  - "28 Weeks Later" is the 2007 sequel to "28 Days Later" (2002).
  - "Affenpinscher" is a recognized dog breed.
  - Birthplaces can be linked to states or ranked by population size.  

Generalizable Strategy:
- Analyze the claim to identify the factual components requiring verification.
- Cross-reference the claim elements with the notes to determine relevant attributes, relationships, or entities.
- Construct a query that directly asks for the confirmation or denial of these elements, with an emphasis on completeness (retrieving all potentially relevant documents).
- Aim for clarity and precision to maximize retrieval of pertinent evidence and minimize irrelevant documents.

Example:
For a claim about an actor’s nationality and filmography, a query would specifically ask:  
"Which actor, identified as Welsh, appeared in both the 2007 film '28 Weeks Later' and 'Avengers: Age of Ultron'?"  

For a claim about birthplaces and population ranks:  
"Retrieve the birthplace of Daniel Gunkel and verify if it is Frankfurt, which is located in Hesse and is the fifth-largest city in Germany; also retrieve the birthplace of Werner Gunter Jaffé Fellner."

For a claim about dog breeds:  
"Are both Banana Joe V Tani Kazari and Villanuco de Las Encartaciones recognized dog breeds?"

These queries help retrieve all gold documents necessary to verify claims effectively.
