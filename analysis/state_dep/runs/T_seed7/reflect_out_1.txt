Task Description:
You are given two fields as input: `claim` (a factual statement or assertion) and `notes` (a list of evidential sentences or facts related to the claim). Your goal is to produce a `query`—a clear, explicit information-seeking question that can help verify or investigate the factual accuracy and components of the claim based on the evidence or evidential gaps revealed in the notes.

Detailed Instructions:
1. Carefully parse the `claim` to identify all key factual elements, entities, events, or relationships that it asserts or implies.
2. Examine the `notes` to assess:
   - Which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or not addressed by the notes.
   - What critical factual details or evidence are missing from the notes that would be necessary to verify the claim fully.
3. Consider that the notes often:
   - Provide biographical data or professional roles (birth dates, professions, filmography, authorship) that help confirm or refute assertions about persons or works.
   - Identify relationships or non-relationships between entities (e.g., linking people to works, or clarifying misattributions).
   - Clarify publications' nature (e.g., whether a magazine is a periodical).
4. Use the notes to identify specific knowledge gaps or ambiguities in the claim's factual assertions. The generated query should explicitly seek those pieces of information needed to definitively validate or invalidate the claim.
5. Formulate the query as a precise, concise question or set of questions that:
   - Target the specific factual elements in the claim that are uncertain or require external confirmation.
   - Reference relevant named entities, works, or categories where appropriate (e.g., specific people, movies, publications).
   - Are phrased in a natural, straightforward manner suitable for search, database query, or knowledge retrieval.
6. If the claim conflates unrelated facts, ask clarifying queries that differentiate those facts.
7. Avoid introducing assumptions beyond what is in the claim and notes; focus on explicit information needs.
8. The reasoning before the query (not requested as output here but often useful internally) should demonstrate understanding of the relationship between claim and notes, showing which evidence is missing or contradictory.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Decompose the claim into atomic factual assertions.
- Cross-reference each assertion against the notes for evidence status (supported, contradicted, unknown).
- Identify gaps or contradictions in the notes for each assertion.
- Formulate one or more pointed questions addressing those gaps or contradictions to direct fact verification.

Examples of domain-specific knowledge to capture for queries:
- Verification of actors’ birth months and professional roles (e.g., "Was Fred MacMurray born in August, and was he a professor?")
- Whether a film/documentary/director relationship is accurate and whether certain works are related to certain series or persons.
- Whether certain publications qualify as periodicals, and if a particular work was written for a magazine or as a standalone book.
- Relationships between co-writers, directors, and their works.
- Distinguishing between real-life facts and fictional portrayals (e.g., playing a professor vs. being a professor in real life).

Summary:
Produce a clear, factual, and well-scoped query that precisely targets missing or uncertain evidence needed to verify the claim. The query should be informed by detailed analysis of the notes, focusing on gap identification and potential misinformation correction.
