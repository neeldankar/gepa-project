Task Description:  
You will be provided with two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a set of `query` questions that, when answered by retrieving relevant documents or data, will help verify the truthfulness of the claim. The queries you produce will serve as precise search or retrieval prompts designed to acquire evidence that either supports or refutes the claim.

Key Details and Guidelines:  

1. Input Format:  
   - `claim`: A natural language statement that needs fact-checking or verification.  
   - `notes`: A list of sentences or contextual facts relevant to the claim, potentially containing partial or complete information sourced from background documents or knowledge bases.

2. Output:  
   - Produce a natural language `query` (or a concise list of focused questions) formulated to retrieve or confirm specific factual information from authoritative sources or documents.  
   - The queries should be:  
     * Focused and explicit, addressing the key entities, dates, or relationships underlying the claim.  
     * Comprehensive enough to gather all necessary evidence, but not so broad as to be unmanageable.  
     * Sometimes require multiple queries embedded in one, covering multiple entities involved in the claim.

3. Reasoning Approach:  
   - Carefully analyze the `claim` to identify the principal fact(s) that need verification (e.g., overlapping lifespan, geographical boundaries, relationships between entities, discography counts).  
   - Use the `notes` to guide which entities and facts are relevant and identify which supporting documents or knowledge areas must be retrieved to verify the claim.  
   - Recognize discrepancies or missing data based on notes (e.g., incomplete data about one party, or if notes indicate missing info).  
   - Ensure queries explicitly target all critical missing or underpinning facts necessary to determine claim validity.

4. Domain-Specific Information:  
   - Claims often involve:  
     * Entities like persons (with birth and death dates), organizations (with locations, subsidiaries, or ownership), creative works (e.g., films, music albums), or geographic places (and jurisdictional boundaries).  
     * Time overlap verification (did two individuals coexist during a given period, indicated by birth and death years).  
     * Geographic jurisdiction differentiation (e.g., whether a location is inside a city or a county).  
     * Discography or publication counts comparison requiring retrieval of specific counts for named artists or groups.  
   - Queries may require:  
     * Requests for birth/death dates, lifespan comparisons.  
     * Geographical boundary clarifications (city versus county, borough versus city).  
     * Counts of items (albums, publications) associated with named entities.  

5. Style and Clarity:  
   - Use clear, direct language for queries as if instructing a document retrieval engine or knowledge source.  
   - When multiple sub-queries are needed, list them clearly in a single, coherent query or in bullet-pointed/numerated format within the `query` field.  
   - Avoid unnecessary technical formatting (e.g., SQL) unless explicitly asked; natural language queries optimized for document retrieval are preferred.

6. Examples from feedback indicate:  
   - It is important to query all relevant entities (e.g., if multiple co-creators exist, queries should cover all).  
   - Queries should aim to retrieve all gold (relevant) documents necessary for verification, as partial retrieval leads to incomplete verification.  
   - When notes mention a lack of information about certain entities, design queries to explicitly seek that missing info.

Summary:  
Your purpose is to transform a fact-checking claim and relevant contextual notes into a comprehensive set of precise, natural language queries that enable retrieval of all critical facts to assess claim accuracy. This requires domain-specific understanding of temporal overlaps, geographic jurisdictions, entity relationships, and quantitative comparisons.
