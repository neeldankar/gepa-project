You are given two inputs:  
- `claim` — a complex factual or comparative statement involving multiple entities, events, or attributes (such as people, films, songs, bands, places, dates).  
- `notes` — a list of factual, contextual, or background information relevant to the claim, which may include authoritative names, titles, dates, relationships, memberships, and other domain-specific facts needed to assess the claim.

Your goal is to produce a single output field, `query`, consisting of one or more concise, well-formed, and factually targeted questions. These questions should be designed to retrieve or verify the essential factual information necessary to fully assess the truthfulness of the `claim`.

Key requirements for constructing the `query`:

1. **Decompose the claim into all its core components and relationships**  
   - Identify every entity (e.g., people, films, songs, bands, places, dates) mentioned.  
   - Identify attributes and relations attributed to those entities (such as membership, release or premiere dates, director, genre, co-starring actors, production companies, chronological sequences among events, or comparative statements such as country size or temporal order).

2. **Formulate direct, clear, and unambiguous fact-oriented questions that collectively cover all critical aspects of the claim.**  
   - Each question should address a discrete factual item or relationship required for verification.  
   - If the claim involves chronological or sequential assertions (e.g., "performed after," "final film directed by"), include questions that establish relevant timelines or order of events.  
   - For membership or involvement claims, explicitly ask about presence or absence in groups, casts, or credits.  
   - For comparative claims (e.g., "is X from a smaller country than Y?"), design questions that explicitly articulate the comparison criteria.

3. **Ground questions in domain-specific context and authoritative facts found or repeated in the notes.**  
   - Use exact names of entities, titles, dates, or recognized terminology from the notes to maintain specificity and ensure alignment with authoritative sources.  
   - Avoid vague language; specify full titles (e.g., "Wonder Woman (2017 film)"), full names, and relevant qualifiers (e.g., "third season of Fargo", "442nd Infantry Regiment").

4. **Ensure the questions, when answered, collectively enable verifying the entire claim without ambiguity or gaps.**  
   - The query should be sufficient for a search or a knowledge base lookup to confirm or refute the claim fully.

5. **Format the output as a single field labeled `query` containing the constructed question(s).**  

---

**General approach for generating `query`:**

- Parse the claim carefully to identify all entities and relationships.  
- Cross-check with the notes to identify key facts for precise phrasing (e.g., actor names linked to films, film release dates, director names, regiment start dates).  
- Identify chronological sequences or comparative elements within the claim to incorporate them explicitly in the questions.  
- Compose one or multiple simple factual questions that, combined, cover all elements necessary to verify the claim.  
- Prioritize clarity, conciseness, and factual targeting over verbosity.

---

**Example:**  
Claim: _"Director Robert Stevenson was from a geographically smaller country than another director. That director directed his final film Romance of Astree and Celadon."_  

Query:  
"Was Robert Stevenson from a geographically smaller country than Éric Rohmer, who directed the final film Romance of Astree and Celadon?"  

This question encapsulates both the geographical size comparison and the identification of the director linked to the named film, thereby targeting all facets of the claim for verification.
