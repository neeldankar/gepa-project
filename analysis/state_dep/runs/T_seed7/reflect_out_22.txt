You are given three inputs:

- `claim`: a statement or assertion to be evaluated,
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets or extracted facts relevant to the claim,
- `context`: a list of longer documents or passages that provide background information, evidence, or detailed facts related to the claim.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

1. **new_notes**: a curated and concise subset of the input `notes` that are most relevant and directly support or refute the factual aspects of the claim. The new_notes should:
    - Focus on key factual points that clarify or verify elements of the claim.
    - Avoid redundancy, overly broad or irrelevant information.
    - Highlight important domain-specific details needed to assess the claim accurately.
    - Reflect nuances such as taxonomic rank distinctions (e.g., "subfamily" vs. "genus" in biological classification) or specific person-film relationships (e.g., filmmaker collaborations, film production years).
    - Include information clarifying identity or status of entities (people, species, or works) mentioned in the claim, especially when the claim involves possible category errors or requires disambiguation.

2. **titles**: a list of document or article titles from the given `context` that are most important for substantiating or understanding the claim. These should be:
    - The most relevant documents containing factual evidence about key entities or events in the claim.
    - Selected to cover all critical facets of the claim, including persons, species, events, or concepts explicitly mentioned or implicitly related.
    - Comprehensive enough to address any parts of the claim that require external validation (e.g., taxonomic classifications, film production details, sports results).

**Detailed reasoning process (to be done internally but reflected briefly in explanations if required):**

- Identify whether the claim contains any category or conceptual errors (e.g., misclassifying a subfamily as a genus).
- Verify factual details such as dates, relationships between entities (e.g., athletes, films, biological taxa), and specific numeric counts when available.
- Consider taxonomic ranks and biological classification rules if dealing with biological claims.
- Resolve ambiguities such as overlapping names, multiple entities with similar names, or multiple films with similar titles by referring to context.
- Recognize domain specific knowledge needed, e.g., common taxonomic ranks, typical career starting ages for athletes, well-documented film production years.
- Use the context to select the most relevant documents even if not all are directly mentioned in notes, to improve coverage and validation of the claim.
- Avoid selecting unrelated or loosely connected documents.
- Prioritize selecting and summarizing the most precise and discriminative facts from the notes over broad or speculative statements.

**Summary:**

Given a claim supported by sets of notes and context documents, your goal is to:

- Extract a refined, relevant subset of notes that directly address factual components of the claim with domain-aware understanding,
- Identify and list the titles of the key context documents necessary to validate or understand the claim comprehensively,
- Incorporate domain-specific insights such as taxonomic hierarchy, detailed historical or sports event knowledge, or filmography data,
- Emphasize clarity, relevance, and precision in the output.

This approach allows subsequent fact verification, evidence collection, or knowledge synthesis steps to efficiently utilize your outputs.
