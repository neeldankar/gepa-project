Task Objective:  
Given three input fields—`claim`, `notes`, and `context`—generate two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. The objective is to accurately evaluate and clarify the `claim` by synthesizing and filtering the given information, helping a user verify or refute the claim with evidence.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A declarative statement asserting a fact, relationship, event, or attribute involving people, organizations, places, or events. Claims often require domain-specific interpretation and disambiguation.  
- `notes`: A collection (list) of factual assertions, source excerpts, or evidence potentially relevant to assessing the claim. Notes may include overlapping, partially contradictory, or redundant information.  
- `context`: Longer passages or articles providing domain-specific background and detailed knowledge about entities or events mentioned in the claim and notes. Context helps disambiguate terms and clarify details necessary for fact evaluation.

Output Requirements:  
1. `new_notes`:  
   - Produce a concise, well-synthesized subset of the input notes focused exclusively on validating or refuting the claim.  
   - Filter out irrelevant, tangential, or conflicting notes not directly aiding the claim’s assessment.  
   - Merge or rephrase notes to prevent repetition while preserving all critical factual details supporting or contradicting the claim.  
   - Retain domain-specific technicalities or key timeline details essential for accuracy.  
   - Emphasize clarity and precision, avoiding unnecessary verbosity.

2. `titles`:  
   - Provide a list of canonical, Wikipedia-style article titles or named entities derived from the notes or context that are strongly relevant for understanding or verifying the claim.  
   - Include titles of persons, organizations, events, or works explicitly referenced in the information provided.  
   - Also include related articles covering domain-specific concepts that contextualize or fill knowledge gaps necessary to interpret or validate the claim (e.g., team names, official roles, event titles, geographic entities).  
   - Avoid including irrelevant or tangential titles; prioritize gold-standard or primary source articles.

Domain-Specific Considerations and Nuances:  
- Sports: Understand distinctions such as types of football, team nicknames, player positions, coaching roles with respect to seasons or conferences, match outcomes, historical timelines, and roles in specific tournaments or seasons.  
- Academia and Professional Roles: Pay close attention to official titles, tenure periods, exact appointments, and timeline nuances to avoid inaccuracies about roles held at particular times.  
- Geography and Organizations: Verify hierarchical geographic or administrative relationships (e.g., city–county–state–country), headquarters locations, and organization origins.  
- Names vs. Nicknames vs. Organizations: Be alert to colloquial names or nicknames and avoid conflating them with person names unless explicitly supported by evidence.

General Reasoning Strategy:  
- Carefully cross-check and align elements of the claim against facts found across notes and context to detect confirmations or contradictions.  
- Exclude extraneous, misleading, or irrelevant information that does not contribute to verifying the claim.  
- Use context to disambiguate similar names or concepts (e.g., different individuals with same/similar names, organizational vs. personal names).  
- Combine complementary notes when this enhances clarity and coverage but avoid duplicative statements.  
- Select the most representative and authoritative notes that directly address the claim’s core statements or components.  
- Extract article or entity titles that a user would logically consult to fully understand or fact-check the claim.

Formatting and Presentation:  
- Output should be clear, concise, and factual.  
- Use quotation marks for nicknames or specific official titles or terms to maintain unambiguous clarity.  
- Present `new_notes` as succinct, paraphrased or direct factual statements strictly relevant for claim validation.  
- Present `titles` as a clean list of Wikipedia-style canonical article titles without verbosity or narrative.  
- Do not include unnecessary explanations or reasoning in the output fields; reasoning is for internal processing only.

Additional Notes:  
- Prioritize retrieving gold-standard or core articles referenced or alluded to in the materials, as identified in examples.  
- Ensure timeline accuracy for claimed roles, dates, and organizational affiliations.  
- For claims involving contradictory assertions (e.g., whether a person ruled at a given time, or whether a company headquarters belong in a country), clarify the correct fact using corroborated evidence.  
- When multiple notes support the same fact, condense and unify them.  
- When the claim connects multiple entities (people, organizations), ensure linkage is explicit and supported.

Summary:  
The task is a precise evidence synthesis and verification exercise combining factual filtering, domain expertise (sports, politics, aviation, film, etc.), and disambiguation. The assistant should transform a noisy, redundant input into a sharply focused factual summary and an informative list of linked articles to fully support a clear user understanding and claim validation.
