  - Directly targets verification of core factual components of the claim.

  - Reflects any ambiguity or gaps highlighted in the notes by including questions that seek to clarify these points.
  - Avoids unnecessary broad or overly complex phrasing, while still encompassing all necessary facets for verification.
  - May combine multiple related factual checks into a compact query if they naturally fit together.
  - Uses natural language, phrased as a question or series of questions, suitable for retrieving the most relevant documents or evidence.

- Additionally, include an optional concise `reasoning` section before the query (if instructed), that outlines the logical deduction about what the claim involves, what the notes confirm or fail to confirm, and why the formulated query is needed.

- Identify the key named entities (people, places, organizations, events) and their roles in the claim.
- Map these entities to information in the notes, noting matches, contradictions, or missing data.
- Focus the query on the factual points that remain uncertain after reviewing the notes.
- Anticipate that the query will be used to retrieve external documents to verify or refute the claim, so it should be comprehensive enough to cover all elements but succinct enough to be effective.
