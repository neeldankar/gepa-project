You are given two input fields: `claim` (a complex factual or comparative statement involving entities, events, or attributes) and `notes` (a list of factual, contextual, or background information relevant to the claim).

Your task is to produce a single output field, `query`, which is a well-formed, concise, and factually targeted set of questions designed to retrieve or verify the core pieces of factual information necessary to assess the truthfulness of the `claim`. The questions should be:

1. Directly derived from the logical structure of the `claim`, addressing each component or relationship expressed in it.
2. Fact-oriented, aiming to identify relevant entities, properties, relationships, or events that need to be verified.
3. Specific enough to pinpoint relevant authoritative documents or data sources that can confirm or refute the `claim`.
4. Inclusive of all key elements from the claim that require evidence, such as names of people, films, songs, bands, dates, geography, or attributes referenced.
5. Clear, unambiguous, and suitable for querying a factual knowledge base or search engine.
6. Where comparative or relational claims are made (e.g., "is X from a smaller country than Y?"), questions should explicitly reflect those comparisons.

Use the `notes` to understand domain context and to identify authoritative names, works, titles, or facts that appear repeatedly and are relevant to formulating the query. The `notes` may contain background or clarifications about entities, dates, memberships, or any relevant fact.

General approach:
- First, identify all key entities mentioned in the claim (e.g., individuals, bands, films, songs, places).
- Identify the relationships or properties attributed to those entities in the claim.
- For claims involving sequences or time (e.g., "final film", "acted in 1970"), ask about relevant chronological facts.
- For contradictory or negative claims (e.g., "is not a member", "no evidence exists"), design questions to verify membership or involvement.
- Formulate the query as one or more simple, factual questions aimed at retrieving data needed to prove or disprove the claim in full.

Finally, your output should only contain the single field `query` with these constructed questions.

Example (based on understanding from given examples):

Claim: "Director Robert Stevenson was from a geographically smaller country than another director. That director directed his final film Romance of Astree and Celadon."

Generated query:  
"Was Robert Stevenson from a geographically smaller country than Éric Rohmer, who directed the final film Romance of Astree and Celadon?"

Remember: The goal is to craft a question or set of questions that, when answered, directly enable verifying the entire claim.
