Task Objective:
Given three inputs — a `claim` (a factual statement or assertion), a set of `notes` (factually relevant excerpts or statements drawn from sources), and additional `context` (longer, detailed background passages) — generate two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. The task aims to distill, verify, and synthesize evidence supporting or refuting the `claim` by careful evaluation of the notes and context with domain-specific rigor.

Input Details:
- `claim`: A declarative statement asserting a fact about entities, events, roles, or relationships—often including domain-specific concepts (sports, academic roles, geographical facts, video games, artworks, etc.). Claims often hinge on precise facts such as locations, timelines, identities, participation in events, or relationships between entities.
- `notes`: A collection of factual statements, excerpts, or partial facts extracted from various sources that relate to the claim. Notes may overlap, partially contradict, or include irrelevant information and should be critically filtered to retain only those facts that directly pertain to validating or clarifying the claim.
- `context`: Extended background passages or articles that provide in-depth, domain-specific knowledge about the entities, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim and notes. Context helps disambiguate ambiguous terms, decipher domain nuances (e.g., tennis tournament formats, platform origins), and augment or clarify evidence found in the notes.

Output Requirements:
1. `new_notes`:  
   - Provide a concise, accurate, and relevant subset or synthesis of the input notes that are directly useful for verifying the claim.  
   - Exclude irrelevant, misleading, or contradictory details that do not contribute to the claim’s verification.  
   - Combine related notes to avoid redundancy and improve clarity without omitting critical information.  
   - Emphasize domain-specific factual points crucial for establishing or disputing the claim, such as event dates, specific roles, locations, or participant identities.  
   - Maintain factual precision, integrity, and clarity, using exact terminology, especially in domain contexts.

2. `titles`:  
   - Produce a list of relevant Wikipedia-style article titles or named entities connected to the claim, notes, or context that a researcher would consult to understand or validate the claim.  
   - Titles should include those explicitly referenced in notes or context as containing core evidence. Additionally, include related or contextual titles that help fill knowledge gaps or clarify ambiguous references (e.g., team names, tournaments, biographies).  
   - Use canonical Wikipedia-style naming conventions, prioritizing gold-standard or strongly relevant documents.  
   - Avoid overly broad or unrelated titles; focus on those most pertinent to the claim’s entities or events.

Domain-Specific and Niche Considerations:
- Sports domain:  
   - Recognize player roles, tournament titles, defending champions, event years, and participation specifics.  
   - Distinguish between different sports sharing names (e.g., "football" as American football vs. association football).  
   - Understand event structures (e.g., defending champions may or may not participate in title defenses; doubles partners may change).  
   - Identify geographical or nationality nuances (e.g., a player’s origin or national representation).  

- Academic/Professional roles:  
   - Pay close attention to official titles, roles, and timelines (e.g., coaching tenure by season).  
   - Verify exact institutions or locations associated with a person’s work or accomplishments.

- Geographic/Organizational claims:  
   - Clarify country/state/city hierarchical relationships.  
   - Confirm location-based entities like buildings, airports, or headquarters belong to claimed regions.  
   - Be cautious of similarly named places or entities in different countries.

- Video games and technical domains:  
   - Distinguish game genres (e.g., fixed shooter), platforms (e.g., Neo-Geo), and their country of origin.  
   - Recognize that a game may be developed for a platform originating in one country while the game developer or game title could be from another.  
   - Beware of conflating platform names with game names or developer nationalities without clear evidence.

- Art and cultural claims:  
   - Confirm artist attributions, artwork locations, and historical era specificity.  
   - Differentiate among similarly named cathedrals or buildings globally.

Generalized Reasoning Strategy:
- Cross-check each element in the claim with the presented notes, filtering out contradictions or unrelated details.  
- Utilize context to resolve ambiguities in entity names, event dates, or domain-specific terminology.  
- Select or merge note excerpts that best support or refute the claim, condensing multiple overlapping facts into a clear, non-redundant narrative.  
- Extract Wikipedia-style article or entity titles representing core claim-related concepts, people, places, or events necessary for a well-rounded understanding of the claim.  
- Maintain domain precision and avoid overgeneralization or unwarranted assumptions.

Formatting and Clarity:
- Present outputs concisely and factually, avoiding verbosity.  
- For nicknames or specific official names, use quotation marks to clarify meaning.  
- Ensure `new_notes` consist of factual, standalone sentences or statements that directly engage with the claim.  
- List `titles` as canonical article/entity names, formatted consistently, without explanatory text.

This comprehensive approach ensures the assistant can robustly verify multifaceted factual claims across domains such as sports, geography, cultural works, and technical fields.
