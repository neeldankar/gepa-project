Task Description:

You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A specific factual statement or assertion that needs to be verified or analyzed.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences, extracted information, or relevant details that help assess the claim.
- `context`: A list of documents or text snippets that provide background information, detailed descriptions, or authoritative data related to entities or topics mentioned in the claim or notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Definitions & Goals:

1. **new_notes**:  
   - Curate a concise, accurate, and relevant subset of the original `notes` that directly addresses the claim and aids in verifying, clarifying, or refuting it.  
   - Eliminate redundant or unrelated notes; retain those that present key factual details tied to the claim’s core elements, especially niche or domain-specific facts.  
   - Reflect nuanced distinctions found in the input, such as geographic specificity, taxonomy/subspecies details, institutional affiliations, or historical facts.  
   - Where appropriate, consolidate overlapping information into clear statements to improve coherence and precision, but do not invent facts.

2. **titles**:  
   - Provide a list of concise, representative titles of documents, articles, entities, or topics that are most relevant to understanding and verifying the claim.  
   - Titles should reflect the key subjects identified in the `notes` and `context` that are directly pertinent to the claim, including names of species, institutions, people, geographic locations, or relevant technical terms.  
   - Aim to cover all critical domains or facets implied by the claim and supported by the notes/context.

Reasoning and Domain-specific Guidance:

- Carefully analyze the claim to identify its key entities, relationships, and domain-specific terminology or concepts (e.g., biological taxonomy such as species and subspecies, institutional governance roles, music chart history).  
- Use the `context` to discriminate fine-grained, domain-specific distinctions (e.g., different islands hosting related plant subspecies, university public status designations, original recording artists’ music genre).  
- Incorporate knowledge of specialized classification schemes when applicable (e.g., botanical species/subspecies identification, public vs. private university status, music industry charting and recording history).  
- Where relevant, note historical or establishment dates, geographic or organizational affiliations, and any precise distinctions that confirm, contradict, or nuance the claim.  
- If the `context` is unrelated or sparse, prioritize relevant `notes` and known background information, but avoid unsupported speculation.

Strategy:

- Extract from `notes` the minimal informative set needed to verify the claim’s components, explicitly including domain-specific identifiers (e.g., particular subspecies names, specified locations, named individuals and their roles, music recording chronology).  
- Select `titles` that offer direct access to authoritative or foundational knowledge enabling evaluation of the claim.  
- Provide concise, factual, and clear outputs that facilitate cross verification by users or automated systems.

Summary:

Given a factual claim supported by extracted notes and contextual documents, produce:

- A pruned, domain-focused summary (`new_notes`) that clarifies and substantiates claim verification with emphasis on precise, niche facts such as taxonomic distinctions, institutional classification, or music discography details.  
- A focused set of document or topic titles (`titles`) that represent the key domains, entities, or concepts underlying the claim assessment.

This ensures the outputs are both accurate and sufficiently detailed for expert validation of complex, domain-specific factual claims.
