Task Description:
You are given two input fields:
- `claim`: a factual statement or series of statements that need to be verified or explored.
- `notes`: a list of text snippets or evidentiary sentences relevant to the claim, extracted from various documents or sources.

Your goal is to produce a `query` field that is optimized to retrieve supporting or refuting documents about the claim during a fact-checking or document retrieval process.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Information:
1. The `claim` can contain multiple assertions about entities, relationships, attributes, or historical facts.
2. The `notes` provide partial supporting evidence, frequently including names, titles, dates, locations, relationships, and other factual details that help evaluate the claim's truthfulness.
3. The `query` must be precise and comprehensive enough to retrieve all relevant documents necessary to confirm or refute the claim. This often means explicitly naming key entities, temporal data (like dates or years), geographic locations, subspecies or variant names, professional roles, or other specific qualifiers contained in the claim or notes.
4. If the claim involves multiple related subclaims or components (e.g., more than one person, entity, or attribute), the query should be structured to address the verification of each critical element.
5. The query should anticipate which documents remain to be retrieved after initial searches, so it should balance specificity with coverage to maximize retrieval completeness.
6. Queries are typically formulated as one or several clear, direct questions, or an information-seeking phrase that aligns with how a search engine or retrieval system would optimally find corroborating documents.
7. When relevant, the query must differentiate between similar or potentially confusing entities, such as distinguishing between related species subspecies, similar locations on different islands, or different people with partially overlapping roles.
8. The reasoning preceding the query (though not required in output) typically synthesizes the evidence from the notes to identify gaps or ambiguities in available information that the query must clarify.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Thoroughly analyze the claim to identify all factual claims or entities needing verification.
- Cross-reference these with the notes to confirm what is known and what remains uncertain.
- Formulate a query that explicitly asks for confirmation of each key element, including names, titles, dates, specific locations, or taxonomic distinctions critical to the claim.
- Frame the query in a way that is likely to retrieve all related documents, enabling full verification of the claim.
- Avoid overly broad queries that return irrelevant results, and avoid overly narrow queries that miss potentially important documents.

Instruction Summary:
Given the input fields `claim` and `notes`, produce a concise, precise `query` field designed to retrieve all necessary documents to verify or refute the claim. Your query should explicitly cover all key entities and facts from the claim and notes, handling any ambiguous or overlapping information with clear distinctions. Formulate your query as an information-seeking question or statement suitable for document retrieval in fact-checking workflows.
