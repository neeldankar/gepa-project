You are given three fields as input:  
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that relates certain entities, events, or attributes.  
- `notes`: A list of extracted sentences or facts relevant to the claim, drawn from various documents or sources. These notes may contain overlapping, repetitive, or complementary information.  
- `context`: A list of background descriptions or summaries of relevant entities, films, people, species, or concepts referenced in the claim and notes. Context provides detailed encyclopedic or documentary information about the subjects mentioned in claim and notes, useful for verification and title extraction.

Your task is to produce two output fields:  
- `new_notes`: A concise, de-duplicated, and relevant subset or synthesis of the input notes that most directly support, refute, or clarify the claim based on evidence found in notes and context. Focus on extracting key factual information that establishes the claim’s validity or invalidity. When possible, clarify ambiguous or implicit information by integrating relevant context. Avoid including irrelevant, repeated, or unsupported information.  
- `titles`: A list of document or article titles corresponding to key topics, entities, or works referenced in the `new_notes` and necessary for understanding or verifying the claim. Titles should reflect distinct knowledge sources directly related to the claim’s argument. Aim to cover all gold or essential documents implied in the notes/context relevant to the claim.

### Important domain-specific considerations and strategies:  
- Claims often involve relationships among people, films, species, works (e.g., films, documentaries), or concepts and temporal or geographic attributes (e.g., production years, habitats).  
- Notes can contain redundant or partially overlapping information; distill them into a minimized, coherent evidence set.  
- Context may include detailed background on films (titles, release years, composers, cast), individuals (professions, works involved), or species (native ranges, habitats). Use it to confirm, disambiguate, or supplement facts in notes.  
- Extract definitive evidence to support or refute the claim rather than tentative or vague statements.  
- Titles must reflect the principal entities and works essential to the claim’s reasoning (e.g., film titles, personal names, species names).  
- When a claim involves multiple entities, ensure all relevant titles are included, especially those identified in feedback as gold documents.  
- Avoid titles unrelated to the claim even if they appear in the context list.  
- Structurally, provide a logical chain of reasoning implicitly by focusing `new_notes` on key factual linkages, enabling a clear line from claim to evidence.

### Summary of the procedure:  
1. Analyze the claim and identify key entities and relations.  
2. Screen through notes and context to find consistent and relevant factual information addressing the claim.  
3. Remove redundant, unrelated, or non-confirming facts.  
4. Construct `new_notes` as a concise evidence base, referencing critical details from notes and context.  
5. List concise, relevant `titles` representing all major entities and works directly linked to the claim’s proof or disproof.

This approach ensures generation of clear, focused evidence sets and associated key knowledge sources, facilitating accurate factual verification or explanation of the claim.
