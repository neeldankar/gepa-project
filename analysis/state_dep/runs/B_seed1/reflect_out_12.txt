Task Overview:  
You will be given three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Input Details:  
- `claim`: A factual or verification statement involving real-world entities, events, or concepts.  
- `notes`: A list of sentences or facts that are potentially relevant to verifying or reasoning about the claim. These notes often include detailed entity dates, relationships, and historical or geographic data. However, they may contain redundant, overlapping, or extraneous information.  
- `context`: A list of text excerpts from articles or descriptions providing background knowledge or detailed entity information related to the entities in the claim and notes. This context is often detailed and may include biographies, definitions, geographic data, and historical descriptions.

Output Requirements:  
- `new_notes`: A filtered and reformulated subset of the input notes, retaining only key, non-redundant facts critical to supporting or refuting the claim. The new_notes should:  
    - Be concise yet comprehensive enough to cover the claim’s essential facts.  
    - Remove duplicates and near-duplicates.  
    - Clarify relationships, dates, and entities mentioned.  
    - Draw upon contextual knowledge when appropriate to enhance clarity and correctness.

- `titles`: A list of relevant document or entity titles associated with key facts in the `new_notes`. These titles should correspond to those documents or context excerpts which best support or explain the information in `new_notes`. Titles act as references or identifiers for evidence sources relevant to the claim.

Reasoning and Strategy:  
- Understand the claim’s core factual assertion and what needs verification or explanation.  
- Identify from the notes all pertinent facts directly related to the claim.  
- Use the context information to clarify entities, dates, relationships, or events that appear in notes to ensure accurate and precise filtering and summarization.  
- Filter out redundant notes and retain one or a few well-phrased, factually accurate notes that support the claim.  
- Select titles that correspond to key entities, events, or documents involved in the claim that appear both in the notes and context. Aim to include all critical sources for comprehensive coverage, balancing breadth and relevance.  
- Aim for a tight focus on evidence that supports the claim. Excess or unrelated notes/titles reduce precision and should be avoided.  
- Consider temporal, geographic, biographical, or filmographic details as seen in examples to validate timelines or overlaps.  
- When multiple interpretations or relevant documents exist (e.g., multiple operas with similar names by different composers), ensure your notes and titles clearly distinguish these and reflect the claim’s logic.

Domain Specific Notes:  
- Entities often include historical persons (with birth and death years), operas or films (with release years, creators, or contributors), companies (with headquarters and operating locations), and geographical locations (states, countries).  
- Dates and lifespan overlaps are used to verify co-existence claims.  
- Operatic works may differ in composers despite similar titles, based on composition date and style.  
- Geographical claims often rely on hierarchical places (city > state > country).  
- Creator roles (director, composer, editor, producer) and film connections or opera productions need accurate attribution.  
- When multiple people contribute to the same work, emphasize correctly identifying those relevant to the claim.

In summary, the objective is to synthesize a focused, accurate, and evidence-supported set of notes and associated document titles that verify or explain the claim based on the detailed input notes and background context.
