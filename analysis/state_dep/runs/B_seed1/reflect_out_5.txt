Task Description:  
You are given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual statement asserting a specific relationship or attribute involving named entities (e.g., persons, bands, institutions, works, events).  
- `notes`: a set of textual snippets drawn from multiple documents containing factual details relevant to the claim’s core entities and asserted relationships.

Your goal is to analyze the claim and notes to identify the core factual assertions and their uncertainties, then craft a single, concise natural language question (`query`) to facilitate targeted document retrieval for verification.

Requirements for the `query`:  
1. Core Focus: The query must target the essential factual points or relationships expressed in the claim, emphasizing entities and their relationships or attributes. This includes band membership, event locations and dates, album-song associations, professional affiliations, coining of terms, collaboration durations, or similar nuanced domain facts.  
2. Entity Specificity: Explicitly name all key entities mentioned in the claim, including fully disambiguated entities if multiple exist with similar names (e.g., specifying geographic or contextual qualifiers, such as “Scottish band X” vs. “British singer Y”).  
3. Relationship Clarity: Specify the precise relationship or fact to be verified (e.g., membership overlap, event venue and timing, whether a song was performed or covered by an artist in a certain album, or if two people collaborated for a given duration).  
4. Logical Nuance: If the claim contains logical negatives or exclusivity (e.g., “not both members,” “did not collaborate”), the query should reflect these logically exact formulations (e.g., “Did X and Y both collaborate with Z...?” or “Are X and Y both members of the band W?”).  
5. Supported Information Only: Include only facts and entities directly supported by or mentioned in the notes. Avoid introducing assumptions, background information, or unverified entities.  
6. Retrieval Utility: The query should be sufficiently specific and detailed to support retrieval of all relevant documents covering all key entities and their relationships, thus facilitating comprehensive fact-checking and verification.  
7. Domain-specific References: Reflect knowledge of domain-specific nuances observed in the notes, such as:  
   - In music: band names vs. individual musicians, album type (studio, live), song covers, temporal context of fame or release.  
   - In film/military history: director names, film titles, regiment activation vs. combat start dates.  
   - In sports: event names and dates, stadium locations and club tenancy, country/geographic qualifiers.  
   - In science or other domains: coining of terms, author affiliations, corresponding authorship roles.  

General Strategy:  
- Identify all key named entities mentioned in the claim.  
- Correlate these entities with their attributes and relationships as reflected in the notes.  
- Highlight any ambiguity or potential contradiction, especially regarding dates, places, memberships, or other factual assertions.  
- Formulate a clear question that encapsulates the verification need regarding these entities and their relationships, using precise and domain-appropriate language.  
- Ensure the resulting question is concise yet fully descriptive for unambiguous document retrieval.

Example Considerations:  
- When a claim references a collaboration, ask about the duration, participants, and the role of each.  
- For location-based claims, specify the event, location, and associated club or entity.  
- When involving works such as albums or films, specify the work title, entities involved, and temporal context related to fame or release dates.  

Deliverable:  
Produce a single focused natural language question capturing the core fact(s) to be checked, naming all relevant entities and relationships, reflecting logical nuances, grounded only on information supported by the notes, employing domain-specific terminology and distinctions where indicated.
