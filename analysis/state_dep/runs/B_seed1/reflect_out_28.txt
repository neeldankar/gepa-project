Task Description:
You will be provided with three input fields: `claim` (a statement to evaluate or elaborate on), `notes` (a list of factual, domain-specific or contextual snippets related to the claim), and `context` (a set of text excerpts or documents relevant to the entities, events, or subjects referenced in the claim). Using these inputs, your task is to generate two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understanding Inputs:
   - **claim**: A factual or comparative statement involving people, events, cultural products (e.g., shows, films, games), dates, awards, or popularity claims.
   - **notes**: A diverse collection of existing supporting or refuting evidence snippets related to the claim, often including dates, biographies, award nominations, known facts about cultural products, or popularity metrics.
   - **context**: A corpus of article-style text extracts about the main entities, productions, or topics appearing in the claim and notes. These often include biographical details, filmographies, award history, or descriptive information about products or people.

2. Objective:
   - Use the `notes` and `context` to evaluate or clarify the `claim`.
   - Produce `new_notes` that summarize, synthesize, or clarify the most relevant and precise evidence to support or refute the claim while excluding redundant or contradictory information.
   - Extract or generate accurate and concise `titles` representing the key topics, entities, or works central to the claim and supported by the notes/context. These titles should align with known document titles or notable subjects referenced in the context.

3. Approach and Reasoning:
   - Carefully cross-reference the claim against the notes and context facts. Identify correct and incorrect details, focusing on nuanced factual accuracy (e.g., whether a person won an award, role names, birth dates, or nationality).
   - Eliminate extraneous or less relevant notes and merge overlapping facts into clear, focused supporting notes.
   - Highlight any known confusions or common misconceptions (e.g., similar role names or mistaken identity) in the notes if relevant.
   - Titles should reflect:
       * The primary entities (e.g., actor names, director names, designers).
       * Key works or productions (films, shows, books, games).
       * Awards or recognitions relevant to the claim.
       * Sometimes thematic or conceptual entities (such as "Golden Globe Awards" or "Popularity of Chess").
   - Titles should be both informative and specific enough to be useful references for document retrieval or further fact verification.

4. Domain-Specific Knowledge (incorporate into reasoning and notes):
   - Award nominations and wins often account for name variants and specific roles, e.g., Golden Globe nominations can be for specific roles rather than overall career.
   - Some titles or roles may be informal or colloquial, requiring careful confirmation against official records (e.g., "The King of Cocaine" role vs. Pablo Escobar portrayal).
   - Birthdates and nationalities should be verified rigorously as claims often hinge on these details.
   - Popularity claims should be assessed relatively, considering widespread global recognition and historical influence against niche or specialized audiences.
   - The context may include metadata about related or associated people, which can help confirm or negate claims involving multiple subjects.
   - German-style and American-style board games have distinct cultural niches and appeal, important for popularity comparisons.
   - Misattributed or mistaken links between people and works (e.g., Garry Marshall vs. a Ukrainian director) are common, so be vigilant.

5. Output Formatting:
   - `new_notes` should be a concise list of the most decisive and accurate fact statements derived from the notes and context.
   - `titles` should be a list of relevant article/document titles or subject descriptors that would help one locate or identify authoritative references to the claim and its supporting or refuting information.

Summary:
Your core task is to verify, clarify, and synthesize information from the given `notes` and `context` about a `claim`, producing distilled, focused evidence (`new_notes`) and a precise list of relevant subject or document titles (`titles`). This requires careful fact-checking, disambiguation, and the use of domain-specific knowledge about awards, filmography, biography, and cultural context. Maintain clarity, factual accuracy, and thoroughness in evidence summarization and topical identification.
