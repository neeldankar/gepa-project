Task Overview:  
You will be given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual assertion about specific relationships or attributes involving named entities, typically in specialized domains such as music, film, military history, sports, science, or literature.  
- `notes`: a collection of textual snippets drawn from various documents containing factual details relevant to the entities and relationships asserted in the claim.

Your objective is to analyze these inputs and generate a single, concise, natural language verification question (`query`) that precisely targets the factual core of the claim. This question will be used for targeted document retrieval to verify the claim.

Detailed Task Instructions:  

1. **Identify Key Entities and Relationships:**  
   - Extract all named entities explicitly mentioned in the claim. Ensure disambiguation when names could refer to multiple entities (such as geographic qualifiers or domain-specific context: e.g., “Scottish band Franz Ferdinand” vs. “Finnish band Sunrise Avenue”).   
   - Identify the exact relationships or facts asserted between those entities (e.g., membership, collaboration, album release, film direction, event date/location, coining of terms).

2. **Ground Query in Provided Notes Only:**  
   - Base your understanding strictly on the information present in the notes; do not use external or assumed knowledge beyond the given textual snippets.  
   - Use only entities and attributes mentioned in the notes to form your query, avoiding addition of unverifiable or irrelevant details.

3. **Capture Logical Nuances and Negations:**  
   - Reflect any logical structure present in the claim, especially in cases involving negations ("did not", "not both") or exclusivity ("both members", "only one").   
   - Construct the query to embody these logical subtleties explicitly and correctly, ensuring that the question’s logic matches the claim’s.

4. **Make the Query Domain-Specific and Precise:**   
   - Incorporate domain nomenclature and distinctions as appropriate (examples below).  
   - Explicitly mention relevant domain-specific contextual details, like album types (studio, live, cover), film attributes (director, production year, nationality), sports events (dates, locations, teams), scientific contributions (term coining, authorship roles), military units (activation vs. combat start dates), etc.  
   - When entities have overlapping or ambiguous identities, clarify fully by appending disambiguators supported by the notes.

5. **Design for Comprehensive Retrieval:**   
   - Formulate the query with enough specificity to retrieve all documents covering the key entities and asserted relationships relevant to verifying the claim. Your query should be complete, yet concise—capturing all essential verification points to avoid missing relevant evidence.

6. **General Reasoning Strategy:**  
   - Parse the claim carefully and decompose it into core factual assertions (entities + relationships).  
   - Cross-check these assertions against the notes, noting any contradictions, gaps, or ambiguities (e.g., mismatched dates, differing nationalities, uncertain roles).  
   - Identify important domain-specific nuances or facts that influence how the question should be formed (e.g., “albums are by bands not individual artists,” “a song cover usually appears on a specific album type,” “directing a film vs. starring in it,” “formation dates vs. release dates,” “opera acts compared to film narrative acts,” etc.).  
   - Highlight any uncertainty around names, titles, or relationships that the query must explicitly address.

7. **Query Composition:**  
   - Use a natural language interrogative form, formulated as one clear, coherent question.  
   - Name all key entities exactly as they appear in the notes, including qualifiers (e.g., “1994 Hungarian-Swiss-French fantasy film Magic Hunter,” “Finnish rock band Sunrise Avenue,” “operative name Vilyam ‘Willie’ Genrikhovich Fisher”).  
   - Include relationship words that clarify the fact to be checked (e.g., “Did X portray character Y in film Z?”, “Were bands A and B both formed in year N?”, “Does film F have more acts than opera O?”, “Did artist X cover song Y in album Z?”, “Did persons X and Y collaborate continuously for duration T?”).  
   - Explicitly include negative or exclusive queries if claim contains them (e.g., “Did X and Y both collaborate with Z?” or “Is there no record that X portrayed Y in Z?”).

- **Music:** Distinguish between bands and individual musicians; specify album type; confirm release years and formation years; check if songs on albums are original or covers; specify geographical origin of bands.

- **Science:** Specify who coined terms, author affiliations, and corresponding authorship.  
- **Logical Negation and Completeness:** Where claims make exclusionary or contrary assertions, ensure the query mirrors that logic.

Deliverable:
