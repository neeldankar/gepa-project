Detailed Task Instructions for Fact-Based Claim Verification and Evidence Refinement

Overview:
You will be given three input fields—`claim`, `notes`, and `context`—and your task is to produce two output fields—`new_notes` and `titles`. These outputs must be a precise, concise, and accurate summary and reference set that collectively verify or refute the factual claim provided, using the evidence available in the notes and the supplemental context.

Input Details:
- `claim`: A factual statement usually involving real-world persons, organizations, events, artistic works (films, operas, TV shows), geographical locations, or scientific entities. The claim often asserts relationships between entities, events timelines, or attributes that require verification.
- `notes`: A list of fact statements or observations that are potentially relevant to the claim. These often contain overlaps, redundancies, and sometimes extraneous or tangential information.
- `context`: A collection of background excerpts providing authoritative information on entities or events related to the claim and notes. This includes biographies with birth/death years, opera or film details with release years and creators, organizational headquarters, geographical hierarchies, and other well-defined temporal, biographical, or geographic details.

Outputs:
- `new_notes`: A carefully filtered and synthesized subset of notes refined to:
    * Retain only the essential facts definitively relevant to confirming or denying the claim.
    * Exclude any redundant, near-duplicate, or tangential information.
    * Clarify and explicitly state key relationships, dates, and entity identifications, drawing on contextual data to resolve ambiguity or enhance accuracy.
    * Cover the full scope of the claim’s facts concisely yet comprehensively.
- `titles`: A list of relevant document or entity titles that correspond to the notes in `new_notes`. Each title acts as a specific evidence source or authoritative reference from the context or input documents that distinctly supports or explains the facts in `new_notes`.

Domain-Specific Knowledge and Reasoning:
- Entities frequently involved include:
    * Historical persons (with birth and death dates to establish timeframes or life overlaps),
    * Films, operas, TV series (with release dates, directors, producers, composers, actors/voice actors, and genre classification),
    * Companies (including headquarters location, founding dates, and operational areas),
    * Geographic entities (cities, counties, states, countries, along with nested hierarchies and jurisdiction),
    * Scientific and academic figures and institutions (with degrees earned, postdoctoral affiliations, and institutional founding dates).
- Temporal verification is critical: precisely check if events or performances happened before, after, or during overlapping periods relevant to the claim.
- Carefully distinguish similarly named works or persons (e.g., multiple operas with same/similar titles by different composers or years).
- Accurately attribute roles in creative works: differentiate actors, voice actors, directors, composers, producers.
- Clarify nationality of companies or organizations when relevant (American vs Swiss, for example), especially if contradictory claims exist.
- Use hierarchical geographic information to confirm or refute location-based claims (e.g., whether a county lies within a state, or which city is the county seat).
- When examining claims about co-existence or overlap (e.g., two people alive at the same time), use birth and death years or institutional founding dates precisely.
  
Strategy for Task Completion:
1. **Understand the claim’s core factual assertions and what relationships or timelines must be established to confirm or deny it. Identify entities central to the claim.**
2. **From the given notes, extract all facts that directly relate to the claim’s entities, relationships, temporal details, and other relevant attributes.**
3. **Use the context to:**
    - Confirm or correct the details in notes (such as dates, roles, geography).
    - Disambiguate confusing or overlapping entities.
    - Add clarity where notes are vague or ambiguous.
4. **Filter out duplicated or near-duplicated information in notes to avoid redundancy.**
5. **Reformulate the remaining notes for clarity and precision, ensuring that dates, roles, and entity relationships are explicit and easy to interpret.**
6. **Collect the titles of documents or entities from the context that best correspond to and support these refined notes. Titles should cover the key entities and concepts vital to the claim, neither omitting necessary references nor including irrelevant ones.**
7. **Ensure temporal or geographic claims are logically consistent and supported by the evidence (e.g., if verifying postdoctoral training, confirm timelines align with institutional histories).**
8. **When contradictions arise in the input notes, prioritize facts supported by context or authoritative sources and clarify refutations as needed (e.g., when noting the absence of evidence for a claim).**

Additional Notes:
- Strive for precision, avoiding inclusion of extraneous details that do not aid in verifying the claim.
- When the claim involves temporal ordering, detail release, residency, or event dates clearly and in relative terms.
- When multiple entities or documents with similar names appear, clearly delineate which is relevant to avoid conflating facts.
- The output `new_notes` should be suitable as a succinct, clearly articulated evidence summary. The `titles` should act as direct pointers to authoritative sources or contextual excerpts that validate those facts.
- When disproving or partially disproving claim components, new_notes should explicitly note this for clarity.

By following the above instructions, you will synthesize focused, accurate, and thoroughly referenced notes and titles that enable clear verification or refutation of the factual claim presented.
