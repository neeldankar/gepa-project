Task Description:
You are provided with a `claim` — a complex factual statement potentially involving multiple entities, attributes, and relationships — and a set of `notes` containing extracted factual information relevant to the claim. Your goal is to generate a precise, concise search or information retrieval `query` that, when executed, would help verify the truthfulness of the entire claim by retrieving supporting or refuting documents.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A string that states one or more factual assertions linking named entities, events, or concepts. The claim may reference specific locations, people, achievements, or timelines.
- `notes`: A list of strings, each representing factual snippets or knowledge that are relevant to the claim. These notes may overlap or repeat facts and may refer to named entities explicitly.

Output:
- `query`: A natural-language question or concise set of interrogatives explicitly targeting the key factual elements of the claim that need to be verified. The query should aim to retrieve documents or information that confirm or disprove the entities, their relationships, or attributes as stated in the claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Dissect the Claim**: Identify all distinct factual components—entities (people, places, events), attributes (ages, roles, achievements), and relationships (e.g., who defeated whom, affiliations between people and institutions).

2. **Align With Notes**: Use the `notes` to confirm which parts of the claim are already supported or refuted, and which parts lack clear evidence or information.

3. **Focus the Query**:
   - Formulate questions targeting the least supported or most critical parts of the claim to confirm their accuracy.
   - If all parts are supported, questions should still explicitly cover the key factual elements to ensure retrieval of confirmatory evidence.
   - Break compound claims into multiple precise questions if necessary.
   - Use named entities and specific event references to keep queries targeted and unambiguous.

4. **Domain-Specific Considerations**:
   - Sports-related claims: focus on player names, event titles (e.g., tournament names, match years), and roles or actions (e.g., defeated by, started competing at a specific age).
   - Literary or author-based claims: query author names, their works, and biographical details or interests related to the claim.
   - Geographic or institutional claims: clarify the entities involved (e.g., counties, cities, colleges) and the affiliation or activity of people connected to them.
   - Always use formal or canonical names for entities as they appear in the notes.

5. **Refinement Based On Feedback**:
   - Ensure the query covers all gold documents or key knowledge points needed to verify the claim.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries that might miss essential documents.
   - Use direct factual questions rather than open-ended or overly general ones.

Example Strategy:
- From a compound claim like "Person A defeated Person B in Event X, and Person C started competing at age Y," generate a query asking explicitly:
  "Was Person B defeated by Person A in Event X, and did Person C begin competing at age Y?"
  
- For claims where part of the information is missing, ask a query focused solely on that part:
  "Did the author of Work Z engage in or enjoy activity Q?"

By following these instructions, you will produce queries that ensure retrieval of the most relevant supporting documents for claim verification.
