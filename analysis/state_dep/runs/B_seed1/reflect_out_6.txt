Task Description:

You will be given three inputs:
- claim: a factual or composite statement that makes one or more assertions.
- notes: a list of candidate evidential statements extracted from some data, which may confirm, deny, or leave uncertain parts of the claim.
- context: a list of source documents or excerpts, each typically corresponds to support evidence relevant to entities, facts, or events mentioned in the claim.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

1. new_notes:
   - A refined, concise set of factual statements selected or synthesized from the input notes that directly and clearly support or address components of the claim.
   - Avoid redundancy and extraneous or unsupported content.
   - Include only notes that provide evidential support or pertinent information about the claim’s components.
   - If some parts of the claim cannot be verified from the notes/context, explicitly note those as unsupported or unverifiable.

2. titles:
   - A list of titles of documents or topics best associated with the claim, based primarily on the provided context and notes.
   - These titles should correspond to source documents that contain verified or relevant factual information about entities, events, or concepts involved in the claim.
   - Aim to retrieve all gold-standard relevant documents referenced in the context that directly relate to the claim, or to the key entities/subjects involved.
   - Titles should be specific and informative to uniquely identify the relevant documents (e.g., including disambiguation if relevant like dates, regions, or professions).

Key Domain-Specific Information and Guidelines:
- Claims often contain multiple parts or sub-claims (e.g., multiple entities or events). Address each part separately in your reasoning and notes.
- Geographic or comparative information (e.g., country size) should be interpreted precisely using the entities’ geopolitical definitions as given (e.g., England vs UK vs France).
- Names of persons, films, drinks, or concepts should align exactly with the referenced titles in the context documents, including clarifying parentheses (e.g., “Robert Stevenson (director)”, “Solo (Norwegian soft drink)”).
- Music, film release dates, or voice actors’ roles should be referenced accurately with all contextual clarifications (e.g., “Winnie the Pooh (2011 film)” vs “The Jungle Book (1994 film)”).
- When verifying claims about flavors or advertisements (e.g., “soft drink advertised with Krupa (song)”), note that absence of context on a specific element should be explicitly mentioned.
- Avoid including unrelated or partially overlapping topics in titles (e.g., general categories that do not directly support the claim).
- Use the notes to guide which documents to select — notes often contain clear pointers to relevant titles or keywords.
- Summarize the reasoning clearly by confirming truth status of each claim component based on the provided evidence.
- Maintain a style that is factual, precise, and suitable for knowledge base referencing or fact-checking.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Parse the claim into atomic factual components.
- Inspect notes for evidence supporting or refuting each component.
- Cross-reference notes against context documents to identify source titles.
- Generate a concise summary new_notes that focus on validated information or highlight unverifiable parts.
- List document titles that correspond exactly to relevant source entries underpinning those notes.
- Repeat this systematically for each input.

Output Format:
- Provide the fields "new_notes" and "titles" with the corresponding lists as shown in the examples.
- Optionally provide a reasoning section internally for your own logic but only output new_notes and titles.

In summary, your goal is a fact-checking style synthesis of claim and evidence, producing a clear verdict in supported notes and a precise set of relevant source document titles for retrieval and verification.
