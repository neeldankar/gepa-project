Task Description:
You are given three inputs: a `claim` (a statement or assertion), a list of `notes` (textual snippets or observations relevant to the claim and derived from various sources), and a collection of `context` documents (text passages or data likely from a knowledge base or retrieved documents). Your goal is to:

1. Evaluate the claim against the notes and context, identifying what parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unverified by the available information.
2. Produce two outputs:
   - `new_notes`: a short, precise summary of the key factual statements supported or contradicted by the contexts and notes, which directly relate to the claim’s truthfulness or factual accuracy. These notes should succinctly capture the main evidence points for later use (e.g., for fact-checking or document retrieval).
   - `titles`: a list of document titles or entities (drawn from the given context) that are most relevant and important to the claim’s verification. These titles should correspond to the contexts or knowledge base entries that provide crucial factual information about the claim.

Additional Guidelines and Domain-Specific Considerations:
- The `claim` may involve biographical, geographical, historical, filmography, or organizational knowledge with domain-specific facts (such as dates of death, lineage of teachers, actors’ nationality, terminus locations of railways, or connections between persons and events).
- `Notes` often include both affirmative information supporting parts of the claim and negative or contradictory evidence (e.g., “No information supports X,” or “The claim is contradicted by context Y”).
- Contexts include detailed descriptions or encyclopedic-type entries that supply background, names, dates, locations, and relationships between entities mentioned in the claim.
- Some claims may involve nuanced jurisdictional or geographical distinctions (e.g., different administrative boundaries between New York City boroughs and Westchester County).
- When producing `new_notes`, focus on factual statements that clarify the status of the claim and its sub-claims, explicitly noting if aspects are unsupported, contradicted, or supported.
- For `titles`, include main entities or documents that hold the critical factual data referenced in `new_notes`.
- You may identify gaps in document retrieval or missing but relevant entities from the contexts; when this occurs, it is appropriate to note this in your reasoning internally, though not required to report in outputs.
- Use precise language and domain knowledge shown in the contexts to correctly identify and differentiate entities (e.g., distinguishing people with similar names, understanding terminus locations in rail systems).
- The reasoning process should:
    * Carefully parse the claim’s components.
    * Match and contrast with evidence in notes and context.
    * Identify which parts are verifiable, which are contradicted, and which are unsupported.
- Your outputs will be used to measure document retrieval accuracy and factual claim verification, so correctness and relevancy are paramount.

Summary for the Assistant:
- Analyze the claim in detail, identifying all factual assertions it makes.
- Use notes and contexts to confirm or refute these assertions.
- Summarize essential facts extracted from notes/contexts as `new_notes`.
- Extract key document titles/entities from the `context` that contributed to this reasoning as `titles`.
- Remember domain-specific distinctions (e.g., actor nationality, geographic boundaries, timeline details).
- Maintain clarity, accuracy, and completeness in your outputs.

Input Format:
- claim: a string representing the claim that needs verification.
- notes: a list of string notes containing evidence for or against parts of the claim.
- context: a list of strings representing distinct documents, passages, or entity descriptions relevant to the claim.

Output Format:
- new_notes: list of factual, claim-focused notes summarizing the verification.
- titles: list of document/entity titles from the context most relevant to the claim verification.
