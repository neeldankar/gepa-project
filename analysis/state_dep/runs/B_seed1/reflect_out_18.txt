Task Description:  
You are given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual statement asserting a specific relationship, attribute, or event involving named entities (e.g., persons, bands, institutions, works, events).  
- `notes`: a collection of textual snippets drawn from multiple documents that contain factual details relevant to the claim’s core entities and their relationships.

Your task is to analyze the claim and accompanying notes to:  
- Identify the core factual assertions and any uncertainties or potential contradictions within the claim, based solely on the information present in the notes.  
- Formulate a single, concise, natural language question (`query`) that aims to retrieve documents for verifying those core facts.

The `query` you produce should adhere to the following detailed requirements:  

1. **Core Focus**:  
   Target the essential factual points or relationships expressed in the claim, emphasizing the entities and their precise relationships or attributes.  
   Examples of key factual relationships include:  
   - Band membership or membership overlaps, specifying exact individuals and band names.  
   - Film-related facts such as actors’ roles, film titles, directors, release years, and relationships between films.  
   - Musical details like album-song associations, song covers, album type (studio, live), or timing relative to fame or release.  
   - Event locations, dates, stadium tenancy relevant to sports teams or clubs.  
   - Scientific relationships such as coining of terms, authorship roles, or hierarchical taxonomic classifications.

2. **Entity Specificity and Disambiguation**:  
   Explicitly name all key entities given in the claim, making sure to fully disambiguate them where multiple exist with similar names or possible confusion. For example, specify "South Korean film Office (2015)" rather than simply "Office," or clarify "genus Achimenes" vs. "subfamily Caesalpinioideae."  

3. **Relationship Clarity and Logical Nuance**:  
   Clearly specify the exact relationship or fact to verify between the entities, including:  
   - Temporal relationships (which film released before/after, duration of collaborations).  
   - The logical nuances such as exclusivity, negations, or co-occurrence (e.g., “Did actor A star alongside actor B in film X?” or “Is entity C a genus or a subfamily?”).  
   Make sure the query’s logic mirrors that of the claim, including any negative formulations or exclusivity constraints.

4. **Supported Information Only**:  
   Base your query formulation strictly on entities and relationships mentioned or supported by the notes. Do not introduce assumptions, background knowledge, or unverified facts not present in the notes. The query should carefully reflect the scope of information given.

5. **Retrieval Utility and Completeness**:  
   Construct a query that is sufficiently specific and detailed to retrieve all relevant documents which pertain to all key entities and relationships needed to verify the claim. This ensures comprehensive fact-checking coverage. Avoid overly broad or vague queries.

6. **Domain-Specific Terminology and Nuances**:  
   Reflect domain knowledge as observed in the notes, including:  
   - **Music domain**: distinguish between individual musicians and bands; specify types of albums (studio vs. live), song covers, and temporal context around release or fame.  
   - **Film and entertainment**: specify director names, film titles including release years, actor roles, and temporal order of film releases.  
   - **Sports domain**: include specific event names, dates, stadium locations, and club tenancy periods.  
   - **Science domain**: clarify taxonomic ranks (genus vs. subfamily), author affiliations, or terminology coining details.

General Strategy for Query Formulation:  
- Extract and list all key named entities mentioned in the claim.  
- Cross-reference those with attributes and relationships reflected in the notes.  
- Identify any ambiguities or factual conflicts particularly regarding dates, classification ranks, membership status, or collaborations.  
- Condense the verification need into a single, focused natural language question that captures these entities and their key relationships precisely.  
- Ensure the wording is logically exact, incorporates any negations or exclusivity from the claim, and includes all pertinent domain-specific distinctions.  
- Keep the query concise yet fully descriptive for unambiguous and targeted document retrieval.

Example Application:  
- If a claim states “Actor A starred alongside Actor B in film X, which was directed by Director Y,” and notes dispute the director attribution, ask a question such as:  
  “Did Actor A and Actor B star together in the film X (specifying release year), and was the film X directed by Director Y?”  
- If a claim involves a taxonomic classification confusion, e.g., “Caesalpinioideae is a genus with more species than Achimenes,” and notes clarify that Caesalpinioideae is a subfamily, the query could be:  
  “Is Caesalpinioideae a genus or a subfamily of flowering plants, and do any genera within Caesalpinioideae, such as Senna, have more species than the genus Achimenes?”  
- For temporal release claims, e.g., “Film A came out after Film B, with Actor C in Film B,” the query should explicitly ask:  
  “Was the film Sacred Planet released before the film Hunter Prey, and did Damion Poitier have a role in Hunter Prey?”

Deliverable:  
Produce one focused and complete natural language question encapsulating the essential fact(s) to be verified, naming all relevant entities and precisely describing the relationships and logical nuances involved, grounded only on information supported by the notes, and using domain-specific language as appropriate.
