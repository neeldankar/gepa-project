Task Description:  
You will receive two inputs:  
- `claim`: a natural language factual statement asserting a specific relationship, attribute, or event involving one or more named entities. These entities may include persons, bands, institutions, works (such as albums, songs, films), or events. The claim often involves nuanced relationships such as band membership, professional roles, collaborations, event details (dates, locations), precedence or originality of creative works, or assertions about temporal sequences and disputes.  
- `notes`: a collection of textual snippets drawn from various documents, providing factual information relevant to the core entities and relationships mentioned in the claim.

Your task is to analyze the claim and the provided notes thoroughly and generate ONE well-crafted, concise natural language question (`query`). This query is intended for targeted document retrieval to verify the claim by focusing exclusively on the key factual assertions and any uncertainties or contradictions revealed by the notes.

Detailed Requirements:  
1. **Identify Core Entities and Relationships:**  
   - Extract all named entities explicitly mentioned in the claim, disambiguating them fully if possible (e.g., specifying geographic or contextual qualifiers).  
   - Identify the key factual relationship(s) or attribute(s) asserted in the claim involving those entities. This may involve: band membership or overlap, collaboration or co-authorship durations and roles, professional positions, event participation (venue, timing, associated organizations), precedence in recording or authorship, or disputes between individuals.  
   - Avoid introducing entities or facts not present or supported in the notes. The query must rely solely on information directly supported by the notes.

2. **Reflect Logical Nuances Precisely:**  
   - If the claim includes logical complexities such as negations, exclusivity, or conflicts (e.g., "not both members," "disproved by," "longest-serving"), the query should capture those exact logical relationships.  
   - For example, precisely formulate queries asking whether two people were both members, or whether one disproved another’s claim, or whether a particular event or collaboration occurred within specified constraints.

3. **Employ Domain-specific Terminology and Distinctions:**  
   - Use the correct domain-specific language as evidenced in the notes, such as differentiating between band names and individual musicians, specifying album types (studio, live), chart names and rankings, film director names and titles, regiment activation versus combat start, negotiation roles in historical contexts, or scientific terms like corresponding authorship.  
   - When querying musical works, explicitly mention song titles, album names, artist names with genre context, release or chart dates.  
   - When querying historical or military claims, specify precise roles, dates, events, and disputes.  
   - When querying collaborations, specify participant names and exact nature/duration of collaboration.

4. **Ensure Retrieval Utility and Specificity:**  
   - The question must be sufficiently detailed and focused to retrieve all relevant documents covering the entities and relationships involved, enabling comprehensive verification.  
   - Avoid overly broad or ambiguous queries; instead, precisely target the claim’s key factual points to minimize extraneous retrievals and maximize relevant document coverage.

5. **Address Ambiguities or Contradictions:**  
   - If notes reveal contradictions or incomplete information related to the claim, incorporate this uncertainty into the query, e.g., by asking "Did X do Y?" when notes are ambiguous or conflicting about this.  
   - If multiple documents mention overlapping or differing information about the same entities or facts, the query should be designed to retrieve all such documents to assess discrepancies.

6. **General Strategy for Query Formulation:**  
   - Identify the entities and the precise factual assertion(s) in the claim.  
   - Cross-reference these with the notes to detect supporting, contradictory, or missing information.  
   - Model logical relationships explicitly in the query, including temporal or hierarchical constraints if present (e.g., sequencing of events, tenure periods).  
   - Use domain-specific references and terminologies found in the notes to ensure the query is linguistically and semantically aligned with source content.  
   - Phrase the query as a clear, concise, and targeted question that can guide document retrieval systems to all relevant materials for fact-checking.

7. **Examples of Domain-specific Contexts to Consider:**  
   - Music: band membership vs. individual musicians, studio vs. live albums, original vs. cover artists, chart performance and dates.  
   - Film and military history: film directors, film titles, regiment details, activation dates, events and conflicts.  
   - Sports: event names and dates, stadium locations, club tenancies, geographic qualifiers.  
   - Science: terminology origin, author roles, institutional affiliations.

Deliverable:  
A single natural language question (`query`) that:  
- Explicitly names all key entities in the claim, with disambiguation as needed.  
- Clearly specifies the relationship(s) or fact(s) to be verified, reflecting any logical or temporal nuances present in the claim.  
- Is grounded solely in facts and entities supported by the provided notes (no external assumptions).  
- Uses domain-specific terminology or distinctions evident in the notes to ensure precise targeting.  
- Is concise yet comprehensive enough to retrieve all necessary documents covering the core factual assertions for claim verification.

Use this structured and domain-informed approach consistently to produce queries that effectively enable precise and comprehensive fact-checking via targeted document retrieval.
