Task Overview:  
You will be given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual statement asserting specific relationships or attributes involving named entities. These entities can be people, bands, institutions, events, works (e.g., songs, albums, films), or other domain-specific concepts.  
- `notes`: a collection of textual snippets drawn from multiple documents, providing factual details relevant to the claim’s core entities, their attributes, relationships, or contexts.

Your objective is to analyze the claim against the notes, identify the key factual assertions and any uncertainties or ambiguities, and then formulate a single, concise, domain-aware natural language question (`query`). This question is intended to guide precise and comprehensive document retrieval that allows for effective verification of the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Identify Core Entities and Relationships  
   - Extract all key named entities explicitly referenced in the claim. Disambiguate entities when relevant, using context provided in the notes (e.g., specifying geographic qualifiers like "German city of Frankfurt," or domain qualifiers like "lead guitarist of Powderfinger").  
   - Identify the specific relationships or attributes asserted about these entities, such as:  
     * Membership (e.g., "X was a member of band Y during period Z")  
     * Temporal information (birth dates; collaboration durations; event timing)  
     * Location details (birthplaces, event venues, institutional affiliations)  
     * Works and associated artists or creators (songs, albums, films, including release or recording contexts)  
     * Roles and professional positions (e.g., coach, offensive coordinator, corresponding author)

2. Handle Logical and Domain Nuances Precisely  
   - Reflect any logical subtleties in the claim exactly in the query, especially cases involving negations, exclusivity, or comparative temporal assertions (e.g., “Did X and Y both collaborate with Z?”, “Was X born before Y?”, “Is Frankfurt the fifth-largest city in Hesse?”).  
   - Use domain-appropriate terms and distinctions from the notes (e.g., “lead guitarist,” “studio album,” “Big Ten Conference football season,” “offensive coordinator at Ohio State University”).  
   - Avoid introducing assumptions, inferred facts, or extraneous background knowledge not explicitly supported or mentioned in the notes.

3. Focus the Query on Verifiable Factual Points  
   - Ensure the query targets exactly what needs to be verified for the claim: the presence/absence of relationships, correct identities, temporal ordering, event participation, or attribute correctness.  
   - Include all entities involved in the claim that appear in the notes, so the query covers the full scope required for comprehensive retrieval.

4. Ensure Clarity and Specificity for Retrieval Use  
   - The formulated query must be succinct yet unambiguous, explicitly naming all relevant entities and specifying the relationship or attribute to be verified.  
   - This precision will facilitate retrieval of all relevant documents that collectively provide the verifiable facts regarding the claim.

5. Use a Generalizable Strategy  
   - Parse claim into atomic factual assertions about entities and relationships.  
   - Map entities and assertions onto information referenced in the notes, noting any ambiguities or missing facts.  
   - Construct a question emphasizing the asserted facts and points of verification, using domain and logical language consistent with the notes.

Domain-Specific Considerations (for tailoring queries):

- Music Domain:  
  * Differentiate between individual musicians and bands.  
  * Specify roles (e.g., lead guitarist, singer) and associated bands.  
  * Clarify album types (studio, live) and song relationships (original vs. cover).  
  * Reflect temporal context of fame, recordings, and releases.

- Sports Domain:  
  * Specify event names, seasons (e.g., “2013 Big Ten Conference football season”), dates, and locations (venues, stadiums).  
  * Include team and institutional full names (e.g., “Indiana University Bloomington,” “Ohio State University”).  
  * Clarify professional roles or titles (head coach, offensive coordinator) in temporal context.

- Geography/Place Domain:  
  * Disambiguate places with qualifiers (state, country).  
  * Clarify city rankings or geographic distinctions.  

- Scientific/Academic Domain:  
  * Mention relevant coining of terms, author names, affiliations, and roles such as corresponding authorship carefully, avoiding assumptions beyond notes.

- Film/History Domain:  
  * Include director names, film titles, event dates.  
  * Distinguish between regiment activation dates and combat start dates if relevant.

Output Format:  
- Deliver exactly one natural language question.  
- The question should be precise, explicitly name all relevant entities, and clearly specify the fact or relationship to verify based only on the notes.  
- Avoid including any information not supported by the notes or the claim.

Example Adaptation:

- Claim: “Werner Gunter Jaffé Fellner was born in the birthplace of Daniel Gunkel. Daniels birthplace in the German state of Hesse is the fifth-largest city in Germany.”  
- Query: “Were both Werner Gunter Jaffé Fellner and Daniel Gunkel born in Frankfurt, Germany, and is Frankfurt the fifth-largest city in the German state of Hesse?”

This query explicitly names all entities and relationships, precisely matches the claim’s logical content, adheres to information found in the notes, and is suitable for retrieving all relevant documents for verification.

Summary:  
Produce a single, focused question that enables comprehensive retrieval of documents covering all key entities and their relationships as explicitly supported by the notes. The question should respect logical nuance and domain specifics, be concise yet fully descriptive, and avoid unsupported assumptions. This approach ensures robust fact-checking of the claim.
