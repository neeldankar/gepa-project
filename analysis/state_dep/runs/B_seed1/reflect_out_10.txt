Task Description:  
You will be given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual statement asserting a specific relationship, fact, or attribute concerning named entities. These entities are typically persons, organizations, bands, institutions, works (albums, songs, films), events (sports matches, tournaments, openings), or other domain-specific referents. The claim often involves detailed, nuanced factual assertions such as membership, origins, participation in events, coining of terms, or exclusive/logical conditions.  
- `notes`: a set of extracted textual snippets from multiple documents containing factual details relevant to the claim’s key entities and asserted relationships or attributes.

Your objective is to produce a single, concise, natural language question (`query`) that targets the core factual assertions and any ambiguities or contradictions presented in the claim, based strictly on information supported or contradicted in the notes.

Detailed Instructions and Requirements:  
1. **Identify Core Factual Assertions**  
   - Parse the claim to understand its essential factual components: which entities are involved, and what relationship, membership, attribute, or event is claimed.  
   - Identify any logical nuance such as exclusivity, negations, or conditional relationships explicitly stated or implied in the claim.

2. **Entity Specificity and Disambiguation**  
   - Explicitly name all key entities mentioned in the claim, ensuring disambiguation where multiple entities with similar names exist (for example, specify geographic or contextual qualifiers such as "German tennis player Angelique Kerber" vs. "Texas native", or "Elmont Memorial High School in Nassau County, New York").  
   - Include only those entities mentioned and supported by the notes; do not introduce external entities or assumptions.

3. **Relationship Clarity and Domain-Specific Nuance**  
   - Clarify the precise fact or relationship to be verified between these entities, such as:  
     - Band membership or overlapping membership periods.  
     - Whether a person or entity participated in a specific event (e.g., a sports match, a music album, or a film).  
     - Whether a song was performed or covered by a particular artist and on which album type (studio, live).  
     - Coining or origination of terms by an author or scholar in a specific context.  
     - Temporal relationships such as who collaborated with whom, and when.  
     - Event details such as venue, date, club tenancy, and player participation in sports contexts.  
   - Reflect domain-specific taxonomy and wording — for example, detail “men’s doubles event”, “professional tennis player from Germany”, “census-designated place in Nassau County, New York”, or “2013 FIFA Confederations Cup Final”.

4. **Logical Nuances and Precision**  
   - Accurately capture any logical constraints inside the claim’s phrasing, especially negations or exclusives:  
     - If the claim states “not both”, question whether both entities share the attribute or relationship.  
     - For claims denying participation, query explicitly whether an entity participated or not.  
   - Use questions that are logically exact, for example:  
     - “Did X and Y both participate in event Z?”  
     - “Is X a member of band Y, and is Z also a member?”  
     - “Is Angelique Kerber a Texas native and did she compete in the 2005 China Open Men's Doubles event?”

5. **Supported Information Only**  
   - Include in the question only those factual aspects and entities explicitly mentioned or supported by the notes. Avoid introducing any assumptions or unverified information even if background knowledge suggests otherwise.

6. **Maximize Retrieval Utility**  
   - Formulate a query that is succinct yet detailed enough to retrieve all relevant documents that adequately cover:  
     - All key named entities.  
     - The relationships or attributes to be verified.  
     - Contextual details – such as temporal, geographic, or domain-specific distinctions – to avoid ambiguous or overly broad retrieval.

7. **Incorporate Observed Domain-Specific Nuances**  
   - Where the notes include domain-specific distinctions or common confusions (e.g., gender categories in tennis events, alternative school names or districts, colloquial names of teams, or the difference between similar named institutions), integrate these nuances explicitly in your question to help direct retrieval and verification effectively.

General Strategy:  
- Analyze the claim and notes to find any contradictions, overlaps, missing or ambiguous points in the factual assertions.  
- Precisely identify all involved entities and clarify any naming overlaps or potential confusion.  
- Formulate a logically exact, comprehensive but concise question encapsulating the verification needs, using domain-appropriate terminology and distinctions to uniquely characterize the factual relationships.  
- Ensure the question allows comprehensive retrieval of all documents that would fully verify or refute the claim.

Example:  
- For a claim about a school district's composition, clearly ask which schools constitute the district and the geographic area served by each, disambiguating similarly named schools.  
- For a sports-related claim about player participation, explicitly ask if the named players participated in the stated event in the specific year and category.  
- For music claims, specify exact membership status, album associations, and temporal context.  
- For claims involving negations, frame questions reflecting “did X participate or not?” or “are X and Y both members or is one excluded?”

Deliverable:  
Produce one natural-language factual verification question that:  
- Names all relevant entities fully and disambiguated as per notes.  
- Reflects the exact factual relationship or attribute claimed, including logical nuances.  
- Contains domain-specific terminology and distinctions observed in the notes.  
- Is sufficiently detailed and precise to facilitate comprehensive fact-checking and document retrieval.  
- Is grounded solely in the information contained in the notes, without introducing unverified facts.
