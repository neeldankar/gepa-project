Task Description:
You will be provided with three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A factual or evaluative statement about people, films, universities, or other entities.
- `notes`: A list of short extracted factual statements or sentences related to the claim, often with some redundancy or partial overlap.
- `context`: A list of longer encyclopedia-style documents or detailed descriptions that provide background, factual verification, or deeper information related to entities, dates, roles, or relationships in the claim or notes.

Outputs:
- `new_notes`: A concise, cleaned, and semantically relevant subset of the input `notes` that best supports, verifies, or contextualizes the `claim`. Remove redundant or irrelevant notes, keeping only those that clearly relate to and corroborate the claim based on both `notes` and `context`.
- `titles`: A list of document titles from the `context` that are relevant to the claim and the selected `new_notes`. These should be the authoritative or informative sources underlying the claim verification, ideally capturing key entities or concepts mentioned in the claim.

Detailed Guidelines and Domain-Specific Details:
1. Entity Disambiguation and Specificity:
   - Identify proper names (e.g., persons, universities, films, characters) precisely as they appear in the `context`.
   - Distinguish between related entities with similar names (e.g., different universities or films with similar titles) using document titles.
   - Include titles of relevant works (film titles with years, university names, or character names) as they appear in `context` to aid verification.

2. Verification of the Claim:
   - Cross-check the information from `notes` against `context`. If the `context` refutes or nuances the claim, reflect that in the selection of `new_notes`.
   - Highlight supporting facts that establish critical relationships or attributes in the claim (e.g., "Andrew Stanton co-directed A Bug’s Life" supports co-director details).
   - Avoid including irrelevant details in `new_notes` that do not strengthen or clarify the claim.

3. Handling Redundancy:
   - Remove near-duplicate or repeated notes.
   - Summarize overlapping facts into more concise notes if possible.
   - Only retain notes that are reasonably unique, precise, and relevant to the claim.

4. Titles Selection:
   - Titles should come exclusively from the `context`.
   - Include all key documents referenced in supporting notes.
   - If multiple titles relate to different parts of the claim, list them all.
   - Titles may refer to individuals, films, organizations, or general categories (e.g., "Public universities in the United States").

5. Generalizable Strategy:
   - Read the claim carefully to identify core subjects and relationships.
   - Filter the `notes` to those articulating these relationships explicitly.
   - Use the `context` not just as background but as authoritative evidence to confirm or disconfirm factual elements.
   - Produce a minimal, informative, and non-redundant set of notes supporting the claim.
   - Identify and provide relevant `context` titles that are authoritative sources for the information chosen.

6. Domain-Specific Nuances:
   - For film-related claims, pay attention to director/co-director roles, film versions (animated, live-action), release years, and character appearances.
   - For university-related claims, consider roles like trustees, naming origins, status as public or private, and historical timelines.
   - For claims involving characters or franchises, identify associated films, series, or franchises and their genres or characteristics.
   - When institutional structures (e.g., "Board of Trustees") or designations (e.g., "public urban university") are mentioned, verify and include relevant informational notes about those concepts.

By adhering closely to these guidelines, you will help ensure the outputs succinctly support the claim with proper, precise, and well-sourced notes and references, reducing redundancy and improving factual accuracy and traceability.
