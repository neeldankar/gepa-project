Task Description:
You will be given two inputs:
- `claim`: a factual statement containing one or more named entities (people, bands, films, TV shows, albums, events, institutions, etc.) along with specific factual assertions or relationships involving those entities.
- `notes`: a set of textual snippets extracted from multiple documents that provide factual information relevant to the entities and relationships mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to analyze the claim and the notes to extract the core factual assertions and any uncertainties or possible contradictions within them, and then to formulate a single, concise, and precise natural language question (`query`). This question should be designed to enable highly targeted retrieval of documents for verifying the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Core Focus and Content:
   - Identify all **key named entities** explicitly mentioned in the claim.
     - If entities are ambiguous or shared names exist, disambiguate clearly using relevant context from the notes (e.g., nationality, profession, geographic qualifiers, album types).
   - Identify the **precise relationships or attributes** asserted by the claim between these entities, such as:
     - Band membership or overlap between members.
     - Film or album participation by actors, directors, musicians.
     - Song appearances or covers, especially in relation to album titles or TV/film episodes.
     - Event occurrences, including dates, locations, programming blocks, or network broadcasts.
     - Professional accolades or roles (e.g., Nobel Prize winners, poet-diplomats).
     - Collaboration durations or exclusions (e.g., “not both members”).
   - Take note of any **logical nuances or negations** in the claim (e.g., “did not both star,” “does not belong to,” “excludes a particular entity”) and incorporate these verbatim or in logically-equivalent form.

2. Entity Specificity and Disambiguation:
   - Use full descriptive names and qualifiers for entities as provided or supported by the notes to avoid confusion.
   - For entities mentioned in multiple contexts or with synonyms, choose the wording that best aligns with the wording or domain-specific terminology found in the notes.
   - Avoid introducing any entities or contextual details **not supported** by the notes.

3. Reflect Domain-Specific Knowledge and Terminology:
   - Music domain:
     - Distinguish between bands and their members.
     - Specify album types (studio, live, compilation) where relevant.
     - Identify whether songs are originals or covers and their appearance on albums or TV shows.
     - Include temporal context like release dates or periods of fame.
   - Film and television domain:
     - Distinguish clearly among film titles, TV shows, episodes, and programming blocks.
     - Include director/producer names and production studios if relevant to the claim.
     - Reference specific episode titles and numbering when verifying song appearances or airings.
   - Sports domain:
     - Use event names, dates, stadium names, and club or country affiliations with proper geographic qualifiers.
   - Science and literature:
     - Accurately attribute coining terms, affiliations, authorship roles, laureateship, and professional titles (e.g., Nobel Prize winners, poet-diplomats).
   - History / Military:
     - Distinguish between regiment activation dates and combat start dates.
     - Include specific roles, deployments, or relevant events and their dates.

4. Query Construction Requirements:
   - The query must be a **single, focused natural language question** encapsulating all the core factual points from the claim.
   - Name every relevant entity clearly and completely as supported by notes.
   - State explicitly the **relationship(s) or attribute(s)** to be verified.
   - Preserve logical complexities, negations, or exclusivity as present in the claim.
   - Ensure that no assumptions, general background knowledge, or entities unrelated to the notes appear in the query.
   - Craft the question so that retrieval includes **all documents containing relevant facts about all enumerated entities and their relationships**, enabling comprehensive verification.

5. Reasoning Strategy to Develop the Query:
   - Parse the claim to identify key factual components (entities, relationships, specific assertions).
   - Cross-reference entities and relationships with the content and details provided in the notes.
   - Detect any contradictions, uncertainties, or logical nuances suggested by the notes.
   - Formulate a concise but sufficiently detailed question reflecting all this to capture the verification scope.
   - Avoid redundancies or overly broad phrasing that would reduce retrieval precision.
   - Prioritize specificity of entities, dates, and relationships, and domain-specific terms.

6. Avoid common pitfalls:
   - Do not introduce or assume facts not present in the notes.
   - Do not omit entities or relationships mentioned explicitly in the claim and supported by notes.
   - Do not generalize or oversimplify complex relationships or logical negations.
   - Do not make the query too vague to be effective for document retrieval.

Examples (informal):
- For a claim about film casting: “Did English actor Gary Oldman star alongside Demi Moore and Meryl Streep in the 1995 American romantic drama film produced by Cinergi Pictures?”
- For a claim about literary or academic affiliation: “Is there an Austrian poet-diplomat who won the Nobel Prize in Literature and is recognized as a writer, and is Alan Sillitoe a British writer known for his novels and short stories?”
- For a claim about music appearances: “Did a TV show named ‘Lost In Space’ or ‘La La Land’ air on the KidsClick programming block on a Sunday, and was a song from Wax Fang’s album ‘La La Land’ featured on an American Dad! episode sharing the same name as that TV show?”

By following these instructions, you will produce high-quality, focused queries that enable efficient and comprehensive verification of factual claims grounded strictly in the given notes, respecting domain-specific nuances and logical precision.
