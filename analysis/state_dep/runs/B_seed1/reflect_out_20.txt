Task Description:  
You will be given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual statement that asserts a specific relationship, attribute, or event involving one or more named entities (such as persons, bands, institutions, works like albums or films, events, or geographic places).  
- `notes`: a set of textual snippets extracted from multiple documents, each containing factual details relevant to the claim’s core entities and relationships.

Your objective is to produce a **single, focused, and concise natural language question** (`query`) crafted to support targeted document retrieval for verifying the claim. This query should precisely capture the core factual assertions and any uncertainties or logical nuances present in the claim, grounded only on the entities and facts mentioned in the notes.

Detailed Instructions and Requirements:

1. **Core Focus and Entity Identification**  
   - Identify all key named entities explicitly mentioned in the claim.  
   - These entities may include individuals, collective entities (such as bands or councils), works (songs, albums, films), events (concerts, military engagements), locations, or institutions.  
   - Use fully disambiguated entity names where applicable (e.g., specifying “British author Michael Moorcock” vs. just “Michael Moorcock,” or “nu metal band Otep” vs. “Otep Shamaya”).

2. **Targeted Factual Relationships**  
   - The query should target the central factual relationships asserted by the claim. Examples include:  
     - Authorship or songwriting credits (including performer roles vs. writing roles).  
     - Band membership or overlapping membership periods.  
     - Roles or parts played in films or events (e.g., whether an actress starred in a specific film and her prior notable roles).  
     - Event details: date, location, and related club or institution associations.  
     - Collaborative relationships: existence, duration, participants, and role delineation.  
     - Administrative or governance relationships (e.g., which council administers a certain town).  
   - If the claim asserts exclusion or negation (e.g., “not a member,” “did not collaborate”), formulate the query to capture these logical nuances precisely (e.g., “Is X credited as a writer, or excluded from writing credits?”).

3. **Logical Nuance and Ambiguity**  
   - Reflect any inherent logical structures such as exclusivity, negation, or conditional relationships from the claim into the query.  
   - If the claim contains compound facts or multiple parts, the query may incorporate them in a single, well-structured question but remain concise.

4. **Grounding and Supported Information Only**  
   - Use only information directly supported or mentioned in the notes. Do not introduce assumptions, external background knowledge, or unverified details.  
   - If a fact is ambiguous, incomplete, or lacking direct support in the notes, include that uncertainty in the query by phrasing it to prompt retrieval covering all possibilities.

5. **Entity and Domain-Specific Terminology**  
   - Apply domain-specific distinctions and terminology where relevant, as observed in the notes:

6. **Query Specificity and Retrieval Utility**  
   - Formulate the query to be sufficiently detailed for retrieval systems to access all relevant documents linking the key entities and factual relationships.  
   - Ensure the query’s coverage encompasses all core aspects needed to verify the claim fully, avoiding overly broad or vague phrasing.  
   - Use clear, domain-appropriate language that minimizes ambiguity for automated or manual retrieval and fact-checking.

7. **General Strategy**  
   - Analyze the claim carefully to extract core factual assertions.  
   - Cross-reference these entities and assertions against the notes to identify supported facts and highlight potential contradictions or missing information.  
   - Distill this analysis into a single, coherent question that comprehensively captures what is to be verified, including potential uncertainties or negations.
