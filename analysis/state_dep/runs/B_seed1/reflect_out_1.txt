You are given two text fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a precise and clear `query` (or set of queries) to be used in external information retrieval systems (such as databases, knowledge bases, or search engines) to verify the factual accuracy of the `claim`.

Detailed Task Description:
- The `claim` is a statement that makes one or more factual assertions involving named entities (people, publications, songs, movies, roles, professions, events, dates, frequencies, etc.).
- The `notes` are extracted facts or evidences related to elements mentioned in the `claim`. These notes may support, contradict, or leave unverified parts of the claim.
- Your goal is to formulate a query that:
  - Targets key factual components and relationships expressed in the claim.
  - Incorporates relevant specific details from the `claim` (e.g., names, titles, roles, dates).
  - Accounts for ambiguities or implicit connections present in the claim that need verification.
  - If the claim involves cross-entity relationships (e.g., an actor appearing in multiple films, or a musician collaborating with others), the query should reflect these conjunctive criteria.
  - If the claim includes comparative or temporal facts (e.g., "published more often than", "inducted in early 1980s"), the query should explicitly include these conditions.
- The query language should be natural, focused, and targeted towards retrieving precise evidence that can confirm or refute the claim.
- Avoid overly broad or vague queries; instead, explicitly mention all key entities and their interrelations necessary to fact-check the claim.
- When multiple distinct factual checks are necessary (e.g., verifying both publication frequency and Hall of Fame induction), incorporate these clearly in a single compound query or split logically if appropriate.
- Use domain-specific terminology and context as inferred from the claim and notes to increase precision (e.g., "authors", "actors", "musicians", "publication frequency", "Hall of Fame induction date").

Generalizable Strategy:
- Identify all key entities and their attributes or relations described in the claim.
- Use the notes to highlight known facts and gaps.
- Formulate queries that focus on confirming the presence or absence of these facts in an authoritative source.
- Maintain clarity and specificity in the queries to maximize retrieval of relevant supporting documents or records.

Example:
If the claim is about an actor starring in multiple works and best known for a particular role, the query should explicitly ask: 
"Which actors starred in [Film A] and [Film B], and among them, who is best known for [Role/Show]?"

If the claim involves professions shared by individuals who collaborated on a work, the query should explicitly verify:
"Do [Person A] (associated with [Band A]), who co-wrote [Song] with [Person B] of [Band B], and [Person C] all share the profession of musician?"

If the claim involves comparing publication frequencies and Hall of Fame inductions:
"Which publication inducted [Person] into its Hall of Fame in [timeframe], and is published more frequently than [Other Publication]? What are the publication frequencies of both?"

In summary, your queries must be carefully crafted to focus on exactly what needs to be verified, balancing specificity and coverage, grounded in the entities and facts extracted from the claim and notes.
