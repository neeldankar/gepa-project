Task Description:  
You are tasked with generating a single, precise natural language question (`query`) that enables targeted document retrieval for verification of a factual claim. The input consists of:  
- `claim`: a factual statement asserting a specific relationship or attribute involving named entities (persons, bands, institutions, works, events).  
- `notes`: a collection of textual snippets drawn from multiple documents containing factual information relevant to the core entities and relationships in the claim.

Your goal is to analyze the claim and notes to identify the essential factual assertions and any uncertainties or ambiguities, then craft a focused question that clearly targets these core facts to facilitate comprehensive and accurate retrieval of supporting or contradicting documents.

Detailed Requirements for the Generated Query:

1. **Core Focus on Entities and Relationships:**  
   Identify all key named entities mentioned in the claim as explicitly as possible, fully disambiguating entities when there are potential conflicts (e.g., specify geographic, temporal, or contextual qualifiers to avoid ambiguity—such as "the 2014 Pakistani film Sultanat," or "the South Korean girl group Girls' Generation").

2. **Explicit Relationships or Attributes to Verify:**  
   Clearly specify the exact fact, attribute, or relationship to be verified. These can include, but are not limited to:  
   - Band membership or subgroup participation.  
   - Participation in film or television productions (including roles, years active, and show/film titles).  
   - Album-song associations (e.g., which album an artist released a song on or covered).  
   - Actor or artist family relationships linked to roles.  
   - Event dates and locations, along with associated entities (e.g., sports stadium tenancy, film production companies).  
   - Collaboration durations, coining of terms, professional or author affiliations, and roles such as corresponding authorship.  
   - Logical negations or exclusivity (e.g., "Did X and Y both collaborate with Z?" or "Is X not a member of band Y?").

3. **Entity Specificity Supported Solely by Notes:**  
   Use only entities and information that appear in the notes. Do not introduce external assumptions or background information. If the notes include multiple similarly named entities or temporal distinctions, reflect those distinctions precisely.

4. **Logical and Nuanced Formulation:**  
   Respect logical nuances from the claim, including negatives, exclusives, or conditional statements, and replicate these exactly in the query.

5. **Domain-Specific Terminology and Nuance:**  
   Leverage domain-specific knowledge as observed in the notes to guide query formulation. Examples include:  
   - **Music domain:** distinguish between individual musicians and bands; specify album types (live, studio); identify covers vs. original songs; clarify temporal context for band membership or releases.  
   - **Film/TV domain:** include director names, film production company names, lead actors, production years, and distinguish similarly titled works.  
   - **Sports domain:** specify event name, date, venue, associated clubs or teams, and geographical qualifiers.  
   - **Science domain:** reflect detailed academic roles like corresponding authorship, coining of terms, and institutional affiliations.

6. **Conciseness with Fully Descriptive Queries:**  
   While being as concise as possible, the query must contain all relevant entities and relationships needed to retrieve all pertinent documents for full verification.

7. **General Strategy for Complex Claims:**  
   - Map all named entities mentioned in the claim to their disambiguated forms based on notes.  
   - Resolve any ambiguity or conflicting details (such as multiple similarly named films, artists, or dates) by incorporating exact qualifiers.  
   - Highlight and explicitly include all relationships or factual elements that require verification, including temporal spans, role designations, or relational overlaps.  
   - Formulate a single question that encompasses all aspects of the claim’s fact pattern, ensuring retrieval comprehensiveness and logical precision.

Example Guideline:  
- For a claim about collaborations, ask: "Did [Person A] and [Person B] both collaborate with [Person C] during [timeframe]?"  
- For verifying band membership: "Are [Person X] and [Person Y] both members of [Band Z] during [years]?"  
- For film appearances and relationships: "Did [Actor X], the star of [Film A (year, country)], appear in [TV Series B] during [year] and [Film C] in [year]?"  
- For familial relationships linked to roles: "Did the father of the star of [Film A (year, country)] act in [Film B (year, production company)]?"

Deliverable:  
Provide a single, concise, unambiguous natural language question that names all relevant entities with full disambiguation (as supported by notes), specifies the factual relationship(s) or attribute(s) being verified with precise wording, logically reflects any negatives or exclusivities in the claim, and is sufficiency detailed to guide retrieval systems toward all documents containing evidence pertinent to full verification.

Notes:  
- Avoid vague phrasing; be explicit about time frames, roles, production distinctions, and qualifiers.  
- Use exact titles, production years, or attributes as mentioned in the notes.  
- Ensure that the question, when used for document retrieval, would retrieve all documents covering the core factual components identified in the notes.

By adhering carefully to these instructions, the query will be optimized to support robust, domain-aware verification of the claim based on the supplied notes.
