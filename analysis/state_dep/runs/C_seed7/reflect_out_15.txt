Task Description:

You are provided with two inputs: a `claim` (a statement that requires verification or fact-checking), and a set of `notes` (textual evidence, facts, or snippets related to the claim). Your goal is to produce a precise `query` that aims to retrieve supporting information or evidence to verify the claim.

Key Points and Domain-Specific Details:

1. Inputs:
   - `claim`: A natural language statement involving named entities, events, dates, and relationships that may or may not be factual.
   - `notes`: A list of sentences or facts, often containing domain-specific data such as political figures, dates, organizations, films, persons, events, and their relationships.

2. Goal:
   - Create a query that efficiently and effectively targets the core entities, dates, relationships, or facts crucial to confirm or refute the claim.
   - The query should be expressed in natural language or in structured query language (e.g., SPARQL) that captures the essential information needed to verify the claim.
   - The query should focus on verifying the most relevant entities or facts mentioned in the claim with respect to the supporting notes.
   - The produced query should include all integral components from the claim (e.g., named persons, dates, roles, relationships, titles), as identified and supported by the notes.

3. Reasoning and Generalizable Strategy:
   - Carefully extract and isolate core facts and entities from the claim.
   - Compare and cross-check these extracted facts against the notes to identify which parts are supported, contradicted, or require further evidence.
   - Formulate a focused query that explicitly requests verification for these core facts, ensuring that it covers both the entities and specific claims related to their attributes or actions.
   - Where applicable, incorporate temporal constraints (e.g., dates, years), relationships (e.g., "first served as President in 1940", "directed by founder of Soul Temple Records"), and entity disambiguation (e.g., clarifying nationality, role, or organizational affiliation).
   - Use domain knowledge embedded in the notes to reflect correct relationships and terminology (e.g., political offices, film industry roles, airline headquarters).

4. Output Format:
   - Include a short reasoning or explanation text outline (optional for clarity and internal logic demonstration).
   - Produce a clear `query` that a knowledge retrieval or database system could use to locate definitive evidence for the claim.
   - Ensure the query is comprehensive enough to retrieve all "gold documents" or key evidence entities to confirm or deny the claim.

Example Elements to Include in Instruction:
- Identification and confirmation of person roles (e.g., "Who was President of Cuba in 1940?")
- Verification of claims about creative works and their creators (e.g., "Did Eli Roth write a 2012 film directed by the founder of Soul Temple Records?")
- Clarification of organizational or company attributes (e.g., "Was ValuJet Airlines a Swiss or American company headquartered in Clayton County, Georgia?")
- Attention to multiple entities with overlapping roles or conflicting claims and resolving contradictions (e.g., experts at odds with each other, political propaganda).

Summary Instruction:

Given a `claim` and supporting `notes`, extract the key factual elements and formulate a precise verification query focused on retrieving authoritative evidence about the entities, relationships, dates, and attributes mentioned in the claim. Your query should be designed to comprehensively validate or invalidate the claim by retrieving all relevant supporting documents or data points, guided by detailed cross-referencing between the claim and the supplied notes.
