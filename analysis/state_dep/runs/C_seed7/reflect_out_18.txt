Task Overview:

You will be presented with three inputs:
- `claim`: a factual statement that asserts something specific, usually involving niche or domain-specific information (e.g., movie details, scientific taxonomy, biographies, or music albums).
- `notes`: a list of extracted factual observations or statements related to the claim, which may be overlapping, redundant, or incomplete.
- `context`: a list of document texts or excerpts that contain background information, evidence, or relevant facts related to the claim and notes.

Your goal is to process these inputs and produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`:  
   Create a refined, concise, and factual summary list that:
   - Synthesizes key evidence from both the provided `notes` and relevant `context` documents.
   - Focuses strictly on facts that directly support, clarify, or refute the claim.
   - Avoids redundancy by selecting only the most pertinent and unique facts.
   - When applicable, incorporates specific entities such as names, dates, roles, titles, classifications, and other explicit details to improve clarity and factual precision.
   - Reflects the logical relation between the evidence and the claim, clearly indicating whether the evidence supports or contradicts the claim.
   - Prepares an accurate factual basis to enable verification, reasoning, or judgement about the claim’s truth value.

2. `titles`:  
   Compile a concise list of the most relevant document or article titles from the `context` that:
   - Include primary source documents, authoritative biographical entries, encyclopedic articles, or official records directly related to the main entities, topics, or subjects of the claim.
   - Are key references to verify, deepen understanding, or provide comprehensive background for the claim.
   - Cover all central named entities (people, albums, scientific taxa, films, dog breeds, etc.) mentioned or implied in the claim and in the `new_notes`.

Detailed Guidance and Domain Context:

- Claims frequently involve specialized knowledge such as movie credits (director, writer, actor roles), dog breed registries and rare breeds, plant taxonomy (distinguishing subfamily vs genus, species counts), music album production details, evolution terminology, or literary professions.

- Scrutinize the `notes` for repeated or contradictory statements; use the `context` documents to resolve these conflicts with authoritative evidence.

- Use precise domain terminology:  
  For example,  
  - Film and music claims: specify exact roles (director, writer, actor), album release dates, label founders.  
  - Biology and taxonomy claims: differentiate levels like genus vs subfamily, count species accurately, name known species or genera.  
  - Biographical claims: distinguish professions clearly, name institutions correctly.  
  - Voice acting and character credits: confirm named roles and actors precisely.

- Evaluate the claim critically:  
  - If parts of the claim are true and others false, your `new_notes` should reflect both the correct and incorrect parts clearly.  
  - Do not assume or generalize; rely only on evidence from `notes` and `context`.

- Maintain correctness in spelling, naming conventions, and terminology as found in the `context`.

- The relationship between claim and evidence is key: explicitly clarify if the evidence confirms, refutes, or partially supports the claim.

- The `new_notes` and `titles` together create a focused, factually grounded knowledge base essential for downstream claim verification workflows.

Expected Output Structure:

- `new_notes`: a short paragraph or bullet-point list of distilled factual statements directly tied to the claim verification.  
- `titles`: a list of 3 to 5 relevant document titles carefully selected from the `context` that provide the backbone for verification.

By following these instructions, your output will serve as a robust, accurate, and domain-aware factual summary that supports precise truth assessment of the claim and guides further investigation effectively.
