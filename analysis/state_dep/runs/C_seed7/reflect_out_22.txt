Task Description:

You are given two fields: `claim` and `notes`.

- The `claim` field contains a factual assertion or statement that may involve one or more entities, events, or relationships.
- The `notes` field is a collection of relevant documents, statements, or retrieved facts that may or may not confirm or refute elements of the claim. These notes include domain-specific factual information such as names, dates, roles, relationships, and detailed descriptions related to the entities or topics in the claim.

Your goal is to produce a `query` field that formulates concise, clear, and specific information retrieval questions aimed at validating, verifying, or investigating the elements of the given claim. The queries should be designed to retrieve the most relevant documents or facts needed to confirm or refute the claim based on the domain-specific knowledge embedded in the notes.

Guidelines and Domain-Specific Details:

1. Understand the Claim Structure:
   - Identify key entities (e.g., people, films, musical works, shows).
   - Identify relationships or attributes claimed (e.g., "X acted in Y," "X released before Y," "X is a judge on Z," "X composed Y").
   - Identify any temporal elements (dates, release order).
   - Identify any claims of inclusion/exclusion (e.g., "not Johann Christoph Pepusch," "Junaid Khan was not a judge").

2. Leverage the Notes:
   - Notes often include specific information such as release years of films, cast and crew details, composer nationalities and attributions, show cast lists, and relationships between multiple individuals.
   - Duplicate or closely related notes may appear; synthesize them to understand the consensus or conflicts.
   - Extract and recognize factual nuggets that help verify parts of the claim.

3. Query Construction Strategy:
   - Formulate queries that directly ask for confirmations or disambiguations implied by the claim.
   - Include requests for dates, roles, or associations that determine the truthfulness of the claim (e.g., "What films featured Damion Poitier and their release dates?", "Who are judges on Music Icons?", "Did composer Béla Bartók write 'Rhapsody, Op. 1'?").
   - Where exclusion is claimed, explicitly query for the presence or absence (e.g., "Is Junaid Khan a judge on Music Icons?").
   - If multiple entities are involved, query about each and their relations to the claim.

4. Aim for Completeness and Precision:
   - Queries should aim to retrieve all crucial missing documents or facts needed to completely assess the claim.
   - If the claim involves multiple parts or entities, include multiple queries or combined queries covering all.
   - Ensure queries are specific enough to differentiate between similar entities or works (e.g., distinguishing "Hunter Prey" from "Sacred Planet", or Béla Bartók's compositions from those of Franz Liszt).

5. Example Query Patterns:
   - "What are the release dates and casts of [Movie A] and [Movie B]?"
   - "Who served as judges on [Show Name] including [Person A], [Person B], or others?"
   - "Who composed [Musical Work]? Was it written by [Composer A] or [Composer B]?"
   - "Did [Person A] have a role in [Film or Show]?"
   - "Are there any connections between [Person/Entity] and [Work/Event]?"

By following these instructions, your generated queries will be well-targeted, domain-aware, and effective at retrieving the key information necessary to verify or refute the claims based on the notes provided.
