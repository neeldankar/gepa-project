Task Description:
You will be provided with two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to generate a `query` field that represents a precise and focused question or set of questions designed to verify or clarify the claim by leveraging the information (or lack thereof) contained in the notes.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that may require verification or further details.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets extracted from various documents related to the claim. These notes may confirm, deny, or be silent about various aspects of the claim, and may also reference related entities or contextual information.

Output:
- `query`: A concise, yet comprehensive question or series of questions, formulated so that retrieving answers to it would allow verification or disambiguation of the claim. Ideally, the query should:
  1. Directly reference the key entities, events, or details in the claim.
  2. Address any ambiguity or uncertainty evident from the notes.
  3. Target missing information or unresolved points highlighted by the notes.
  4. Include multi-part questions if the claim is compound or involves multiple facts.
  5. Avoid overly broad or vague questions; be as specific as possible to guide document retrieval or fact-checking.

Domain-Specific Considerations and Strategies:
- Claims often involve named entities (people, movies, awards, songs, products, flavors, dates). The query should clearly specify these entities and their relationships as mentioned in the claim.
- Notes may include partial confirmations, negations, temporal details (e.g., release dates), or relationship clarifications. Use these clues to narrow the query to missing or uncertain facts.
- In cases where notes suggest a discrepancy or absence of information, the query should seek authoritative verification (e.g., "Which actor from X was nominated for Y award for playing role Z?" or "Is the main flavor of product X orange?").
- For claims involving temporal comparisons (e.g., release dates), the query should explicitly request dates or chronological ordering.
- For claims involving multiple elements (e.g., multiple drinks or actors), consider multi-part queries addressing each component distinctly.
- The query should always be phrased as an information-seeking question(s), not as a judgment or conclusion.
- Use clear, unambiguous language avoiding pronouns without clear antecedents to maximize retrieval relevance.
- If notes mention related but not fully relevant information (e.g., related actors or different roles), mention these in the reasoning but the query should focus on directly verifying the claim.

General Methodology:
- Read the claim carefully, extracting all key components (persons, roles, events, dates, products, songs, awards).
- Analyze notes for confirmations, contradictions, gaps, or relevant related facts.
- Identify what critical piece of information is needed to verify or understand the claim fully.
- Craft a precise query that will most efficiently lead to retrieval of evidence resolving the claim.
- When multiple facts are claimed, or multiple entities involved, structure the query in logically ordered parts.

The output `query` is intended for use in a fact-checking or information retrieval system to obtain evidence documents or data relevant to validating or disproving the claim.
