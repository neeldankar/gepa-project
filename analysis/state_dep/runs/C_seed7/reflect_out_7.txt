Task Description:
You will be given two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a `query` field that contains precise, concise, and focused questions aimed at verifying or fact-checking the claim based on the information contained in the notes.

The `claim` usually includes one or multiple factual assertions, occasionally with multiple components or sub-claims.

The `notes` provide context, relevant facts, or evidence that either support, refute, or leave uncertain parts of the claim. These notes often include domain-specific or niche facts, such as names, dates, titles of works, participation details, or detailed relationships between entities.

Your goal is to:

1. Understand the entire claim, breaking it down into its key components or assertions.
2. Analyze the notes to identify which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unverified.
3. Generate one or more clear, targeted questions that precisely seek to verify the crucial aspects of the claim, especially those that are ambiguous or unsupported by the notes.
4. Use specific domain terms or entities from both claim and notes to form queries that will help retrieve or check the exact relevant information.
5. Ensure that each query aims to:
   - Confirm identities (e.g., who exactly did something or who possesses a certain attribute).
   - Verify relationships or events (e.g., did this event happen on a certain date or in a certain context).
   - Check for existence or non-existence of described facts (e.g., does a show episode exist with a certain title; did a person start competing at a given age).
   - Resolve contradictions or missing evidence.

Additional Domain-Specific Details and Considerations:
- Claims may concern entertainment media (e.g., TV shows, episodes, songs, artists, albums), sports (e.g., players, matches, tournaments), or other niche knowledge domains.
- Frequently involved entities include titles of albums, episodes, names of artists, sports players, shows, dates of events, and their participation roles.
- Verify the exactness of names and titles mentioned (e.g., episode titles, album names) as these are often critical.
- Distinguish between similar or related entities (e.g., different people with similar names, titles of shows vs. episodes).
- When a claim mentions events or facts about specific dates or timelines (e.g., release or airing dates, career start ages), queries should explicitly target those.
- Construct queries that allow retrieval systems or fact-checkers to find the most relevant documents, thus aiming to cover all distinct parts of the claim that require validation.

Generalizable Strategy to Apply:
- Parse and break down the claim logically.
- Cross-check the notes to identify supporting or contradicting evidence.
- Identify gaps or missing confirmations.
- Formulate explicit "verification questions" that precisely target these gaps or contradictions.
- Make queries as direct and focused as possible to aid precise document retrieval, avoiding broad or ambiguous phrasing.
