Task Description:
You will be given three fields as input: `claim` (a factual statement or assertion combining elements/entities), `notes` (a list of supporting or related factual statements), and `context` (a list of longer text snippets or document extracts relevant to entities mentioned in the claim). Your goal is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Detailed Instructions:
1. Understand the Claim:
   - The claim typically relates to a factual assertion involving multiple entities, events, or items (e.g., whether two items are from different countries, whether two individuals both won a prize, or if two films were produced in the same year).
   - The claim may contain minor inaccuracies like wrong titles or paraphrasing, so identify and correct such mistakes where necessary for clarity (e.g., correct a film title if clearly misstated).

2. Analyze Notes and Context:
   - Notes provide multiple pieces of factual information related to entities or themes in the claim; some may be repetitive or overlapping.
   - The context provides important background, detailed descriptions, or additional facts from encyclopedic or reference-like sources.
   - Use both notes and context to verify and corroborate facts, clarify ambiguities in the claim, and supply accurate, precise, and concise factual statements relevant to the claim.
   - Focus on factual accuracy and completeness, and identify the relevant key documents or entities that establish or relate to the claim.

3. Create `new_notes`:
   - Synthesize concise, non-repetitive, factual statements from both `notes` and `context` that clearly support or explain the claim.
   - Ensure that new notes include corrections or clarifications if the claim has errors (e.g., correct film titles).
   - Each note should be a single distinct factual point, directly relevant to understanding or verifying the claim.
   - Avoid redundancy and keep notes focused on essential information that supports or clarifies the claim.

4. Generate `titles`:
   - Produce a list of the key document, entity, or concept titles that the `new_notes` are derived from or that are essential for comprehending the claim.
   - Titles should be specific and representative of the main entities or works referenced (e.g., the correct film title, author names, relevant companies, or events).
   - Include all core titles required to understand the claim and verify the information, even if not all titles were initially retrieved.
   - Avoid overly general or irrelevant titles.

5. Addressing Domain-Specific Nuances:
   - Titles of creative works (films, novels, games) should be precise and verified, as these entities are often confused or misnamed.
   - When claims involve prizes (e.g., Nobel Prize), ensure clarity on which individuals won and which did not.
   - When claims mention years or countries of origin, clarify exact dates and nationalities using authoritative references from the context.
   - For technical or specialized items (e.g., specific platforms or software), identify their origin, developer, or country accurately.
   - If notes or context contain overlapping or repetitive facts, consolidate them into singular clear points.
   - Recognize and include the gold-standard or canonical titles/entities referenced in the claim to maximize retrieval completeness.

6. Reasoning and Verification:
   - Internally reason about the relationships or factual assertions in the claim, referencing correct entities and timelines.
   - Ensure your output clearly supports or refutes the claim with factual backing.
   - Your produced `new_notes` and `titles` should enable a clear understanding of the claim’s truthfulness and the main sources underlying it.

Summary:
Given a claim and supporting information, produce a distilled set of factual notes and an accurate list of key entity/document titles that verify or explain the claim. Correct errors in the claim if needed, consolidate overlapping facts, emphasize precise domain-specific knowledge (film titles, authors, years, countries), and include all relevant core titles to fully capture the information required to assess the claim.
