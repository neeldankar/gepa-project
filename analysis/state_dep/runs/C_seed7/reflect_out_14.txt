Given two input fields — `claim` (a factual statement or assertion to be verified) and `notes` (a list of relevant supporting or refuting evidence sentences or facts associated with the claim) — your task is to generate a `query` field that would best retrieve the critical information needed to verify or investigate the given claim.

The `query` should be a well-formed question or search prompt that:

1. Precisely targets the key factual elements or entities involved in the claim.
2. Reflects the logical reasoning steps necessary to confirm or refute the claim based on the `notes`.
3. Explicitly connects multiple relevant facts or attributes from `notes` that support or contradict the claim.
4. Includes specific details, such as dates, names, numbers, or domain-specific terms, needed to retrieve authoritative documents or sources.
5. Functions as a focused, information-seeking query that helps retrieve all necessary "gold" documents or evidence required for claim verification.

Your generation process should consider:
- Identifying and unifying core entities, relationships, and attributes mentioned in the claim and corroborated or challenged in the notes.
- Including context from the `notes` to disambiguate or clarify ambiguous terms in the claim (e.g., correct names, nicknames, classifications).
- Formulating the query as if you are preparing a precise search or fact-checking question that covers all contentious or confirmatory elements of the claim.
- Avoiding vague or overly broad queries; aim for clarity and specificity to maximize retrieval of relevant evidence.
- If the claim involves temporal data, affiliations, or categorical relationships (e.g., leader of a party on a certain date, genus species counts, club nicknames), ensure those aspects are present in the query.

Example of the general approach:  
- Analyze the claim for factual claims (e.g., "who led a team," "which genus has more species," "who is leader of a party on a given date").  
- Cross-check these with `notes` to identify the critical details that confirm or contradict the claim’s components (e.g., player names, dates, birthdates, species counts, nicknames, affiliations).  
- Formulate a concise, specific question encapsulating these key elements that a document retrieval system can use to find supporting evidence.

This task requires domain-sensitive fact extraction and query synthesis to facilitate effective evidence retrieval for claim verification across diverse topical areas such as sports history, political leadership, and biological taxonomy.
