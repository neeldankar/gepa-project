Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: a `claim` (a statement or assertion) and a set of `notes` (informational snippets, verifications, or context related to the claim). Your task is to produce a field called `query` that reflects a precise, focused question or information request that arises from analyzing the claim in light of the supporting or contradictory information in the notes.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understand the Claim:
   - Read the claim carefully and identify the key factual components or assertions it makes.
   - Note any multi-part claims or assertions involving comparisons, attributions, or descriptions.

2. Analyze the Notes:
   - Review the notes provided, which contain relevant factual information, context, validations, or contradictions related to the claim.
   - Identify which aspects of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unverified based on these notes.

3. Formulate the Query:
   - Your query should explicitly address any gaps, uncertainties, or critical points revealed by the notes with respect to the claim.
   - The query should be specific, clear, and focused on seeking information that would validate, refute, or clarify the claim.
   - When parts of the claim are confirmed by the notes but some are unverified, center the query on the unverified or doubtful aspects.
   - When the claim combines complex concepts (e.g., domain-specific terminology, historical facts, geographic relations, cinematic awards), ensure your query reflects that complexity using precise terminology.

4. Domain-Specific Considerations:
   - Geographic claims: Focus queries on specific locations, administrative details, or proximity relations that remain unclear.
   - Film and festival claims: Emphasize specific films, actors, awards, dates, and their relationships or distinctions.
   - Opera and performance arts claims: Highlight formal structures such as “acts” in operas versus “scenes” in films for comparison.
   - Always incorporate any mentioned proper nouns, film titles, people, festivals, or documented facts that could help pinpoint the needed information.

5. Generalizable Reasoning Strategy:
   - Use the notes as partial evidence — confirm what is supported, negate what is disproved, and flag what remains unverifiable.
   - The query should target exactly those unresolved elements necessary to properly assess the claim’s truthfulness or contextual accuracy.
   - Avoid broad or vague queries; instead, tailor the question to the factual or thematic elements specifically relevant to the claim and notes.

6. Format:
   - Provide only the `query` field as the output.
   - The query should be a standalone, grammatically correct question or precise request for information.

Example Approach:
- If all but one element of the claim is verified in the notes, the query should focus solely on that element.
- If multiple aspects of the claim are negated or disproved in the notes, the query might inquire whether any part has factual basis or request specific confirmation of disproved elements.
- If the claim involves technical distinctions (e.g., “acts” vs. “scenes”), phrase the query to clarify or compare those terms as they apply to the subject matter.

By following these instructions, you will generate queries that efficiently guide further fact-checking, information retrieval, or verification regarding complex, domain-specific claims.
