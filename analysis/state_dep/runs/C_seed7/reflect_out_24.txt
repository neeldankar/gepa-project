Task Description:  

You are given two inputs:  
- **claim**: a factual statement or set of related factual statements that need to be verified or explored. The claim may encompass multiple distinct factual components, including entities, relationships, dates, locations, or attributes.  
- **notes**: a list of evidence snippets, extracted or retrieved, relevant or partially relevant to the claim. These notes may confirm, contradict, or be silent on parts of the claim, and may be complete or partial.

Your task is to produce an output field called **query** — a highly precise, clear, and targeted natural language question or set of questions designed to guide additional document retrieval or fact-checking research to fully verify or falsify the original claim and every factual sub-component within it.

---

Detailed Instruction Guidelines:

1. **Decompose the Claim into Atomic Factual Components:**  
   - Identify and isolate every distinct factual assertion made in the claim.  
   - Extract named entities (people, authors, places, institutions, events, species, titles) explicitly or implicitly referred to.  
   - Specify the relationships or properties assigned to entities (e.g., "X is author of Y," "X completed training at institution Z," "X was longest-serving commandant at location Y").  
   - Include categorical distinctions where applicable, for example: distinguishing an individual dog vs. a breed, author vs. writer vs. owner, grandfather vs. descendant.  
   - Extract temporal qualifiers (dates, intervals, chronological order) and spatial qualifiers (locations, institutions within regions, relevant geopolitical boundaries).

2. **Analyze the Provided Notes for Supporting or Missing Information:**  
   - Use notes as preliminary evidence confirming some claim components. Identify which facts are explicitly confirmed, which are contradicted, and which remain unspecified, ambiguous, or unsupported.  
   - Highlight gaps preventing full verification—e.g., missing establishment date of an institution, missing author identification, missing familial links, or historical event roles.  
   - Identify any partial or equivocal evidence in notes that require clarifying questions (e.g., negotiation roles attributed to different individuals, ambiguous identity matches, missing temporal sequencing).

3. **Formulate Queries to Target Retrieval of Missing or Ambiguous Evidence:**  
   - For each unresolved factual sub-claim, formulate a specific, focused question designed to yield retrieval of authoritative documents or evidence, ideally covering every component necessary to fully verify the overall claim.  
   - Ensure each question in the query unambiguously addresses a single fact or tightly connected facts (e.g., "Did X complete postdoctoral training at Y?" "Was Y established before Z?" "Is X the grandfather of Y?").  
   - Where multiple related facts exist, produce multiple connected sub-questions if needed, rather than broad or vague queries.  
   - Reference all relevant entities and attributes explicitly in natural language form, using domain-appropriate terminology (e.g., "postdoctoral training," "resident-commandant," "author of The Hundred Secret Senses," "Institution establishment date," "negotiations with Zyklon B owners").  
   - Avoid queries that are too general or unfocused; the goal is to obtain gold-standard evidence pinpointing the truth of the claim's components.

4. **Incorporate Domain-Specific Knowledge and Nuances:**  
   - Where the claim hinges upon domain-specific distinctions (e.g., dog breeds vs. individual dogs, authorial identities, genealogical links, institutional history), use terminology precisely.  
   - Temporal facts (e.g., dates of death, establishment years) should be explicitly queried to settle chronological relations mentioned or implied.  
   - For historical or biographical claims, include questions about relationships, identities, professional roles, and contextual historical facts.  
   - Queries should reflect the expected types of authoritative evidence for the domain (biographies, institutional histories, official reports, scientific taxonomies, etc.).

5. **Purpose and Usage Orientation:**  
   - The queries you generate will be used by retrieval systems or human researchers to find additional sources or evidence documents necessary to fully verify the claim.  
   - Your queries must be scoped to retrieve precise documents that fill in missing knowledge gaps or clarify ambiguous facts identified from notes.

---

Examples to Guide Query Construction:

- For distinct factual assertions:  
  "Did Norman Alfred Norman Lindsay enjoy boxing?"  
  "Who is the author of The Hundred Secret Senses, and did they have any documented interest in boxing?"

- For temporal and institutional relationships:  
  "Was the University of Washington established before Texas Christian University?"  
  "Did Theodore Garland, Jr. complete postdoctoral training at the University of Washington?"

- For familial and historical role verification:  
  "Is Rudolf Höss the grandfather of Rainer Hoss?"  
  "Was Rudolf Höss the longest-serving resident-commandant of Auschwitz concentration camp?"  
  "Did Rudolf Höss conduct negotiations with the owners of Zyklon B, or were these negotiations conducted by another individual?"

---

Summary:  
Your final **query** output should be a well-structured, multi-part natural language question or series of questions that comprehensively cover every sub-claim within the original claim not fully verified by the notes. It should clearly reference all relevant entities, specify the relationships and attributes under scrutiny, integrate temporal or spatial qualifiers as needed, and leverage appropriate domain terminology to maximize effective retrieval of authoritative evidence.

Always think critically about what evidence is missing or uncertain from the notes and formulate queries that precisely seek to close those gaps, thereby enabling comprehensive and accurate claim verification.
