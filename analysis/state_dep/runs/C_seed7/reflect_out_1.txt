You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a `query` field.

Detailed Task Description:
- The `claim` is a statement or assertion that requires fact verification.
- The `notes` consist of multiple sentences or short paragraphs providing factual information, evidence, or context relevant to verifying the claim.
- Your goal is to generate a concise, focused, and precise `query` that would help retrieve additional authoritative documents or sources necessary to fully verify or refute the claim.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Details:
1. **Purpose of the Query**:  
   The `query` should be formulated to maximize retrieval of documents that directly address aspects of the claim not fully confirmed or refuted by the available notes. It should highlight entities, events, relationships, or attributes that require further factual verification.

2. **Context Awareness**:  
   - Extract the core factual components of the claim (e.g., names, dates, events, roles, titles, relationships).  
   - Identify gaps or ambiguities revealed in the notes (e.g., missing birthdates, unclear roles, conflated identities, disputed affiliations).  
   - Incorporate these into the query as explicit questions or informational requests that can lead to retrieving missing or clarifying information.

3. **Handling Complex Claims**:  
   - If the claim contains misunderstandings or conflations (such as mixing people and works of art, or confusing roles), the query should clarify these distinctions.  
   - When multiple entities or assertions are referenced, the query can be multipart or compound, covering each key point for verification.

4. **Generalizability of Strategy**:  
   - Use the notes to identify what is known and unknown about the claim.  
   - Formulate queries that ask who, what, when, or whether, focusing on disambiguation and verification points.  
   - Address temporal details (e.g., birth months, dates of works), professional roles (e.g., director, professor), and relationships (e.g., involvement with other works or entities).

5. **Precision and Clarity**:  
   - The query should be as concise as possible while capturing all unresolved facets of the claim.  
   - Avoid unnecessary detail or repetition, and do not assume facts unconfirmed by the notes.  
   - The query must be answerable by authoritative documents or databases.

6. **Examples of Query Elements**:  
   - "Who were the stars of [film], what are their birthdates, and did any have the role of [profession or title]?"  
   - "Did [person] direct [film]? Is [title] a person or a film?"  
   - "Was [work] written specifically for [publication], and are both [publication1] and [publication2] considered periodicals?"

Summary:  
Produce a fact-focused, disambiguating query that targets unresolved or ambiguous elements of the claim, leveraging the evidence and contradictions surfaced in the notes, thereby enabling effective targeted document retrieval for claim verification.
