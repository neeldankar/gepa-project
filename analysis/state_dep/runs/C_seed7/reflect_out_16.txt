You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

- The `claim` is a factual statement that may be accurate, inaccurate, ambiguous, or partially true.
- The `notes` are an unordered and sometimes redundant or contradictory collection of factual statements, clarifications, corrections, and evidence that relate to the claim.
- The `context` contains supplemental information such as excerpts or summarized descriptions of relevant entities, topics, or documents connected to the claim, which can be used to clarify, confirm, or supplement the notes.

Your goal is to process these inputs to:

1. **Synthesize and distill** the information from `notes` and, as needed, the `context` into a concise yet comprehensive and factually precise list called `new_notes`. This list should:

   - Explicitly address the claim by confirming, refuting, or clarifying its factual accuracy with reasoned points.
   - Correct any factual inaccuracies or taxonomic misrepresentations in the claim (e.g., correct incorrect taxonomic ranks such as mistaking subfamilies for genera).
   - Remove redundant, repeated, or contradictory statements from the notes to improve clarity and coherence.
   - Emphasize critical domain-specific facts relevant to verifying the claim, such as:

     - **Taxonomy:** Include taxonomic ranks (genus, subfamily, tribe, family), species counts, hierarchical relationships, and clarifications about classification terms when relevant.
     - **Institutional histories:** Note founding or establishment dates, institutional affiliations, and connections between individuals and institutions (e.g., educational backgrounds).
     - **Organizational data:** Clarify memberships, sponsorships, league participations, and correct organization or team names, especially when the claim concerns sports leagues or clubs.
     - **Geography and administrative boundaries:** Clarify spatial or administrative separations relevant to the claim.
     - **Discographies or artistic outputs:** Specify counts of works like albums or publications when comparing output across artists or groups.
     - **Professions or fields:** Clearly identify professions of individuals and distinguish when they differ, especially in biographical or historical claims.

   - Use the `context` to supplement gaps in the notes, ensuring completeness and precision.
   - Summarize overlapping, complex, or verbose notes into clear, non-redundant factual statements.
   - Ensure the `new_notes` enable a human researcher or fact-checker to independently verify or refute the original claim.

2. **Produce a list of concise and descriptive `titles`** representing:

   - The key entities, concepts, or documents referenced or necessary to understand or verify the claim and the content of `new_notes`.
   - Coverage should include all significant subjects mentioned in your `new_notes`, such as specific people, organizations, taxonomic groups, documented events, or locations.
   - Titles should follow standard naming conventions typical of encyclopedic articles or well-known knowledge domains (e.g., "Stephen Jay Gould", "West Cheshire Association Football League", "Caesalpinioideae").
   - Avoid extraneous or irrelevant titles; focus on those essential for claim understanding and verification.

**General strategies and best practices:**

- Carefully distinguish taxonomic ranks, as taxon names can be confused (e.g., subfamily Caesalpinioideae vs. genus Caesalpinia).
- When the claim involves numerical or comparative data (e.g., album counts, founding years), explicitly highlight these data points.
- When an individual is linked to an institution by profession or education, clearly state that connection.
- Address geography with precision; administrative boundaries and city/county distinctions often invalidate geographic claims.
- If the claim includes entities in roles of sponsor, member, or participant (e.g., in sports leagues), verify and clearly state the validity or lack thereof.
- If the notes lack direct evidence for parts of the claim, draw on related factual context as necessary to fill gaps.
- Avoid duplication in new_notes; consolidate repetitive information into single, clear points.
- In cases of ambiguous or unverifiable claims due to missing data, explicitly state the insufficiency of evidence.

**Output format (exact):**

- `new_notes`: a list of concise, well-structured factual notes directly responsive to the claim, integrating context and eliminating redundancy or contradiction.
- `titles`: a list of succinct, appropriate article/entity/concept titles representing the main topics covered.

This focused and domain-aware synthesis ensures your outputs provide a clear, reliable evidence summary with associated knowledge topics suitable for rigorous claim verification and fact-checking.
