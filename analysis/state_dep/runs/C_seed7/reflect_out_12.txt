Given an input consisting of the fields `claim` (a statement or assertion about one or more entities or events) and `notes` (a list of textual evidences, facts, and contextual information related to the claim), produce the field `query`.

The purpose of the `query` is to formulate a precise, focused, and answerable information retrieval or fact verification question that can be used to gather authoritative sources or evidence to confirm or refute the claim.

The input `notes` contain detailed but sometimes partial or ambiguous information about the claim’s subjects, often referencing specific entities (e.g., film titles, actors, places, events) and factual attributes (e.g., production origin, genres, burial locations), with nuanced domain-specific distinctions (e.g., knowing that a Disney film featuring Cruella de Vil is "One Hundred and One Dalmatians" (1961), or that Golden Gate National Cemetery and Golden Gate National Recreation Area are distinct entities). The notes also often point out missing or unclear evidence regarding certain parts of the claim.

When producing the `query`, follow these guidelines:

1. **Identify the key assertions within the claim that require verification**, especially those parts where the notes show incomplete, contradictory, or insufficient evidence.

2. **Formulate the query to explicitly target those critical facts or entity attributes** that need to be confirmed or disconfirmed to resolve the claim’s truthfulness. This may include:
   - Verifying specific attributes of named entities (e.g., film genres, actor nationalities and roles, burial locations and their administrative boundaries).
   - Detecting if certain entities (people, places, films) co-occur or are linked as asserted.
   - Clarifying potential confusions or distinctions among similarly named entities (e.g., different cemeteries or similarly titled films).

3. **Use natural, clear language that directly asks for the factual information needed**, often in the form of “Is [entity] ...?”, “What is the [property] of [entity]?”, “Did [person] appear in [work]?”, or “Is [location] part of [larger entity]?”

4. **Where appropriate, request specific names or confirmation that resolve ambiguity** (e.g., “Please provide a list of matching actors if any,” or “If possible, specify the name of the cemetery”).

5. **Leverage domain-specific knowledge evident in the notes**, such as:
   - Recognizing that “Cruella de Vil” is tied to the 1961 Disney film “One Hundred and One Dalmatians.”
   - Understanding that the Golden Gate National Cemetery is different from the Golden Gate National Recreation Area.
   - Noting actor nationalities and film production origins (to verify claims about English/American actors or film genres).
   - Recognizing that verifying linked appearances (an actor in a show and a film) requires cross-referencing both entities.

6. **The query should be actionable**, i.e., formulated in a way that a database or knowledge source can answer definitively, aiding in retrieval of the relevant authoritative documents necessary to validate or refute the claim.

7. **Do not restate the entire claim or details from the notes**, but focus the query narrowly on the missing or uncertain pieces of evidence highlighted by the notes.

Overall, the task is to convert complex claims and detailed but partial evidence into a precise and focused information-seeking query that would enable an effective factual verification process by guiding retrieval to the most relevant, authoritative information sources.
