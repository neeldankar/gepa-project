Given the inputs `claim` (a factual statement or set of statements that need verification or exploration) and `notes` (a list of extracted or retrieved evidence snippets relevant to the claim), produce the output field `query`.

The `query` should be a precise, clear, and focused natural language question or set of questions that would guide further document retrieval or fact-checking research to validate the claim. The goal of the query is to retrieve documents or evidence that together can comprehensively verify every factual component or sub-claim within the original claim.

When generating the `query`, follow these detailed guidelines:

1. **Understand the Claim in Depth:**  
   - Identify each distinct factual assertion or entity relationship in the claim. For example, if the claim involves multiple entities (people, places, species, events), or complex relationships (e.g., "X is a breed" vs. "X is an individual dog"), break these down and address them explicitly in the query.  
   - Capture temporal, spatial, or categorical qualifiers (e.g., dates, locations, types) mentioned or implied in the claim.

2. **Leverage the Provided Notes to Identify Supporting and Missing Information:**  
   - Use the notes as a preliminary knowledge base to confirm which parts of the claim are already supported or contradicted.  
   - Highlight specific knowledge gaps or unresolved sub-claims—i.e., any key fact that is not sufficiently addressed in the notes and requires further verification or retrieval.  
   - If the notes reveal partial evidence or ambiguous results, the query should be designed to resolve those uncertainties with targeted, clarifying questions.

3. **Formulate the Query Precisely:**  
   - The query must be scoped so that its answers will directly support verification of the claim or its critical components.  
   - Include relevant named entities (persons, locations, species, events, organizations, or documents) and attributes that appear in the claim or notes.  
   - Use terminology consistent with the domain implied by the claim (e.g., dog breeds and individual dogs in the first example, composer lifespans and teachers in the second, national parks and plant species distribution in the third).  
   - Avoid overly broad queries; instead, aim for multiple specific sub-questions if the claim contains multiple factually distinct components.

4. **Incorporate Domain-Specific Nuances:**  
   - If the claim contrasts categories (e.g., individual vs. breed, teacher-student relationships, geographic ranges within archipelagos), explicitly incorporate these distinctions in the query.  
   - If temporal facts are relevant (e.g., dates of death or establishment), include these as part of the query especially when the claim’s temporal assertions need confirmation.

5. **Purpose and Use of the Query:**  
   - The generated `query` will inform document retrieval modules or human researchers on what to look for next. It should thus be tailored to help retrieve missing gold-standard evidence documents or knowledge sources necessary to fully verify all components of the claim.

Example template for generating queries:
- Single entity verification: "Did [Entity] have [Property/Relationship]?"  
- Multiple entities or relationships: "Are [Entity1] and [Entity2] both [Category], or is only one classified as such?"  
- Location and temporal confirmation: "Is [Place] located in [Region], and was it established in [Year]? Are [Entities] found there or elsewhere?"

By following these guidelines, your generated queries will enable precise and comprehensive retrieval of necessary evidence to verify complex factual claims with domain-specific details and relationships accounted for.
