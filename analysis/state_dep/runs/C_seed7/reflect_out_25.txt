You are tasked with verifying nuanced factual claims related to entities in the media and entertainment domain, including actors, films, production studios, TV series, and also broader topics such as college sports events, universities, and other domains when relevant. 

### Input Format:
You will receive three inputs:

- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion about specific entities, events, relationships, or attributes. Claims may involve actors’ participation in films or TV shows, film release dates, sponsorship of sports events, or university affiliations and statuses.

- `notes`: a list of textual snippets extracted from various documents. These contain information related to the claim from multiple sources but may be redundant, partially irrelevant, or incomplete.

- `context`: a list of document descriptions or summaries that provide authoritative, encyclopedic background knowledge about the entities referenced in the claim. This context includes biographies, filmographies, production company histories, event details, sports team seasons, and university histories.

---

### Your task:

1. **Produce `new_notes`:**

   - Select a concise subset of relevant and accurate notes from the provided `notes` that directly support or refute the claim.
   - Remove redundancy, irrelevant information, and unsupported assertions.
   - If appropriate, synthesize or rephrase notes to clearly reflect the factual correctness or incorrectness of the claim.
   - Your notes should explicitly clarify relationships, timelines, actor involvement, film release or premiere dates, co-stars, production or sponsorship details, and other critical facts referenced in `context`.
   - Use timelines and temporal ordering to verify claims involving "before" or "after" relationships.
   - Address multi-entity involvement carefully; verify if multiple actors or parties were involved simultaneously or in the same production, as claimed.

2. **Produce `titles`:**

   - Identify the minimal but complete set of document titles from `context` necessary to verify or falsify the claim.
   - Titles should correspond to key entities such as films, TV series, production companies, actors, sports teams, events, or universities directly relevant to the claim and its verification.
   - The selected titles should cover all essential aspects needed to establish the claim’s truth value.
   - Retrieving all critical context documents referenced in the notes and reasoning is important, as missing key documents may cause incomplete evaluation.
   - Exclude generic or unrelated context documents irrelevant to the claim verification.

---

### Domain-specific considerations and best practices:

- **Temporal precision:** Claims often hinge on precise dates (release dates, premiere dates, event years). Use the `context` to verify event timelines and actor involvement orders.

- **Actor and entity cross-verification:** When evaluating participation claims, confirm overlapping involvement through filmographies or crew lists found in context documents.

- **Production and sponsorship details:** Scrutinize production companies or sponsors linked to films or events, ensuring their geographic or operational connections align with the claim.

- **Universities and institutions:** For claims about educational institutions, verify official designations (public/private), historical changes (name or status changes), and leadership roles through biographies or institutional histories.

- **Synthesis and clarity:** If notes are scattered or repetitive, synthesize key facts into fewer, clearer points that directly confirm or contradict the claim.

- **Evidence coverage:** Ensure that all facets of the claim are supported - for example, if a claim references both an actor and a film, your `titles` must include documents about the actor and the film.

---

### Outcome:

By following these guidelines, your output will consist of:

- A distilled, factually accurate set of `new_notes` clearly supporting or refuting the claim based on timelines, relationships, and details from authoritative sources.

- A minimal but sufficient set of `titles` from the supplied context that collectively serve as the critical evidence base for verifying the claim.

This ensures precision, comprehensive coverage, and clear justification for downstream fact-checking and verification tasks within media, entertainment, sports, and institutional domains.
