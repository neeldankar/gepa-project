Given input fields `claim` and `notes`, produce an output field `query` with a precise information retrieval or knowledge base query that can verify or refute the claim based on the evidence found in `notes`.

This task involves:

1. **Understanding the claim**: Break down the claim into its implicit sub-claims or components, especially if it contains multiple parts or comparisons.

2. **Using the notes**: The `notes` provide partial evidence, clarifications, and background necessary to frame the query. They often contain domain-specific entities (e.g., person names, band names, song titles) and key concepts like musical genres, chart positions, collaborations, or biographical details. Use these to:

   - Identify relevant entities, attributes, or relationships mentioned or implied in the claim.
   - Extract relevant data fields (e.g., genre classifications, collaborations, chart positions) to confirm or dispute the claim.
   - Understand the context and possible confusions in the claim (e.g., similar names, genre overlaps).

3. **Constructing the query**: The query should be:

   - Focused and targeted, reflecting the key elements of the claim.
   - Formatted as a concise natural language question or a structured database-like query (SQL or similar) that would retrieve authoritative, domain-specific evidence.
   - Capable of retrieving the documents or factual information necessary to support or disprove the entire claim or its components.
   - Inclusive of entity disambiguation where relevant (for example, distinguishing “Jacob Gentry” from “Troy Gentry”).
   - Able to handle complex, compound claims by framing queries that retrieve multiple pieces of related evidence, often requiring joins or multiple entity checks (such as original artist, genre, chart peak).

4. **Domain considerations**: The following domain-specific knowledge may be useful or necessary:

   - Music domain: Artists, bands, collaborations, song discographies, chart positions (e.g., Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks), music genres (rock, country, progressive rock, pop rock, electronic/alternative rock).
   - Literature and authorship: Authors of specific works (e.g., Amy Tan as the author of *The Hundred Secret Senses*), hobbies or interests linked to individuals (e.g., boxing).
   - Biographical clarifications and name disambiguations (e.g., multiple artists or persons with similar names).
   - Recognizing that claims often involve verifying both positive and negative statements (e.g., "X enjoyed boxing," "Y did not enjoy boxing").

5. **Generalizable approach**:

   - Identify the key entities and concepts in the claim.
   - Use the notes to identify evidence or lack thereof supporting parts of the claim.
   - Translate these into a query that will extract precise information about these entities and concepts.
   - The query should enable retrieval of all relevant gold-standard supporting documents or knowledge base entries required for fully verifying or refuting the claim.

Overall, the goal is to convert a human-readable claim and supporting notes into an automated, structured query that can retrieve authoritative information about the claim’s truthfulness by checking verifiable attributes, timelines, collaborations, classifications, and other domain-specific facts implied by the claim.
