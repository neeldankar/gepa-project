You will be given two inputs:

- `claim`: A factual statement containing one or more assertions about entities, events, dates, locations, or relationships that require verification.
- `notes`: A list of evidential sentences or contextual excerpts retrieved from relevant documents relating to the claim.

Your task is to produce a `query` field that formulates precise, clear, and focused fact-checking questions designed to verify each key factual element in the claim, especially those unsupported, contradicted, or insufficiently corroborated by the notes.

---

### Detailed Task Description and Guidelines

1. **Decompose the Claim into Atomic Factual Sub-Claims:**
   - Identify **all discrete factual elements** or assertions that require verification. These often include:
     - Names of people (e.g., actors, directors, authors)
     - Names of places (e.g., cities, venues, census-designated places)
     - Organizations or bodies (e.g., school districts, production companies)
     - Works or productions (e.g., films, plays, sports events)
     - Dates or time periods (e.g., formation year, debut year, event dates)
     - Relationships or affiliations (e.g., actor starring alongside another, a film directed by a particular person)
     - Specific factual details about entities (e.g., geographic locations a team is based, a school's catchment area)

2. **Leverage and Analyze the Notes to Identify Gaps or Contradictions:**
   - Examine the notes carefully to uncover:
     - Which assertions are **already confirmed** or well-supported
     - Which assertions are **refuted, contradicted, or unsupported** by the notes
     - Entities mentioned in the claim but **absent or unclear** in the notes
     - Any **specific ambiguities or discrepancies** in the notes relevant to the claim’s assertions
   - Target your queries primarily on these **uncertain, missing, or contradictory points**.

3. **Formulate Precise, Unambiguous, and Verifiable Fact-Checking Queries:**
   - Queries should be **concise yet sufficiently detailed** to avoid ambiguity:
     - Include full names, titles, places, and other disambiguating details.
     - Specify contexts such as production year, venue, or geographic location when necessary.
   - When multiple sub-claims require verification, combine related queries **without losing focus**.
   - Queries should aim to be answerable by factual/documentary evidence (e.g., official records, cast lists, location descriptions).
   
4. **Domain-Specific Considerations:**

   - **Film/Entertainment:**
     - Confirm actors’ identities, nationalities, and roles.
     - Confirm film titles, director names, release years, and venues of performances.
     - Verify relationships such as an actor starring in multiple productions.
   
   - **Military:**
     - Verify unit or regiment names, formation dates.
     - Confirm historical events and locations.
   
   - **Education:**
     - Confirm composition of school districts (list of schools).
     - Verify locations (e.g., census-designated places served).
   
   - **Sports:**
     - Verify home venues and event locations.
     - Confirm clubs’ bases or geographic affiliations.
   
   - **Historical Events/Timelines:**
     - Confirm dates of inception, events happening at specific times/places.

5. **General Strategy:**

   - Begin by clearly isolating each factual sub-claim.
   - Cross-reference these with the content of the notes to determine which should be queried.
   - Frame queries in a way that enables fact-checkers or retrieval systems to **efficiently locate authoritative evidence** addressing each point.
   - When notes directly contradict or negate a sub-claim, highlight queries that seek to clarify or resolve such contradictions.

6. **Output Format:**

   - Provide a single `query` string or a series of focused questions clearly separated (e.g., by punctuation or numbered points).
   - Ensure queries explicitly name all relevant entities and attributes from the claim to avoid ambiguity.
   - Avoid overly broad or speculative questions.

---

### Summary

Your outputs should be **structured fact-checking queries that:**

- Extract and verify *every critical factual element* from the claim.
- Focus on *elements poorly supported or contradicted by the notes*.
- Use *full names, dates, and contextual information* for clarity.
- Be *domain-aware*, refining queries to the type of entities involved (films, schools, actors, events, etc.).
- Enable efficient retrieval of precise, factual documents to confirm or refute the claim.

By adhering to these instructions, you will produce high-quality, targeted fact verification queries that facilitate thorough and accurate verification workflows.
