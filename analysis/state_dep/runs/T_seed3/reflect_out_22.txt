Given the input fields:

- `claim`: a complex factual statement often involving multiple entities, events, or attributes that need verification.
- `notes`: a list of evidential sentences or facts related to the claim, which may include details about people, events, dates, nationalities, sponsorships, authorship, and interests, among others.

Your task is to generate the output field:

- `query`: one or more precise, fact-checking questions derived from the `claim` and supported by the relevant information in the `notes`.

Detailed instructions:

1. **Understand and Decompose the Claim:**  
   Carefully parse the `claim` to identify its key factual components. Claims often involve multiple entities or assertions (e.g., two persons’ professions, participation in an event, or an individual’s hobby). Decompose these distinct assertions to ensure your query can address each as needed.

2. **Utilize Relevant Details from the Notes:**  
   The `notes` contain domain-specific factual information that helps confirm or refute parts of the claim. These are drawn from diverse contexts such as:
   - Film directors’ identities and nationalities (e.g., director of a film and their nationality).
   - College football teams’ participation in bowl games and sponsorship details (e.g., sponsor company’s location, the year of the bowl).
   - Authors of literary works and their personal interests or biographies.
   
   Use this information to ground your query in facts that directly target verifying the claim’s components.

3. **Formulate Fact-Checking Queries:**  
   Compose your query as clear, concise, and direct questions that could be answered by referencing authoritative sources. These questions should be designed to:
   - Confirm the truthfulness of each claim segment (e.g., “Who directed X?”, “Is X a Y?”, “Did team Z participate in bowl game A?”).
   - Disambiguate entities when needed (e.g., confirming full names, nationalities, or event dates).
   - Capture negative evidence appropriately, especially when disproving a claim (e.g., “Is there any evidence that person X enjoyed hobby Y?”).

4. **Include Multiple Linked Questions if Required:**  
   If the claim contains multiple factual assertions, your query should address all parts to cover the full scope of the fact-checking task. This might mean combining questions with conjunctions (“and”, “or”) or listing them sequentially to ensure all points are verified.

5. **Domain-Specific Considerations and Terminology:**
   - For films: verify directors, nationalities, and film titles with release years.
   - For sports: verify team participation in specific bowl games, year discrepancies (e.g., season vs. calendar year), and sponsors with locations.
   - For literature: author identification and personal traits or hobbies if relevant to the claim.
   - Be aware of nuances such as season year vs. calendar year for sports tournaments, full proper names of people, and distinctions between “no evidence of dislike” vs. “positive evidence of dislike”.

6. **Goal:**  
   The generated query should retrieve all gold evidence documents necessary to fully fact-check the claim or else highlight what documents remain unretrieved if partial. The query acts as a retrieval prompt to get all supporting or refuting documents for claim verification.

By following this approach, you ensure the query is tightly aligned with both the claim's verification requirements and the factual context provided in the notes, maximizing document retrieval relevance and completeness for downstream fact-checking.
