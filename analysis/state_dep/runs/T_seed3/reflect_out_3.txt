Task Description: 

You are given two fields as input:
- `claim`: A statement or assertion containing specific factual information or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of textual evidence snippets, contexts, or supporting statements related to the claim.

Your goal is to produce a single field output:
- `query`: A clear, specific, and focused natural language question aimed at retrieving, verifying, or clarifying the factual truth or validity of the claim, based solely on the provided notes.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understand the Claim:
   - Parse the `claim` to identify key entities, relationships, or factual assertions that require verification.
   - Look for compound or multi-part claims that may need to be broken down or rephrased logically into one or more verification questions.
   
2. Analyze the Notes:
   - Carefully review the `notes` for direct or indirect evidence supporting, partially supporting, or contradicting parts of the claim.
   - Pay attention to domain-specific references, names, places, dates, and nuanced facts within the notes that relate to the claim.
   - Identify any ambiguities, gaps, or common confusions (e.g., similarly named places or people) that could affect claim verification.

3. Formulate the Query:
   - The `query` should explicitly target the uncertain, unsupported, or pivotal aspect(s) of the claim.
   - Frame the query as a factual question that, if answered, would resolve the truthfulness or accuracy of the claim.
   - The question should be precise, concise, and unambiguous, referencing key entities or facts found in both the claim and notes.
   - Avoid overly broad or general questions; focus on what is necessary to confirm or refute the claim given the evidence.
   
4. Domain-Specific Considerations:
   - Locations with similar or confusing names (e.g., "Higgin's Creek" vs "Higgins Lake State Park") require queries that disambiguate.
   - For named individuals (e.g., Craig Heyer), queries should clarify their relationship to locations or institutions.
   - When operatic works or similar cultural artifacts are involved, queries should focus on composers, specific productions or versions, and historical context to verify claims about authorship or performance.
   - Address multi-faceted claims by focusing on the component that remains unsupported or controversial after reviewing notes.
   
5. Reasoning Strategy:
   - Your query derives from identifying gaps or uncertainties in the notes relative to the claim.
   - You should aim to generate a question that directly seeks the missing evidence or confirmation.
   - If multiple clarifications are possible, focus on the most critical one that impacts claim validity.

Example of the approach:
- If the claim says a town is administered by X and near Y and Z, and notes confirm the administration and proximity to Z but not Y (which might be confused with another place), the query should ask specifically whether there is evidence linking the town to Y.
- If a claim involves multiple colleges and a person linked to them, and notes confirm some but not all connections, query the college or connection that is missing from evidence.
- For claims comparing composers or historical events, ask about the composers of each work referenced and whether they are the same or differ.

Ultimately, your `query` should prompt retrieval of evidence that resolves remaining uncertainties in the claim using the given factual notes.
