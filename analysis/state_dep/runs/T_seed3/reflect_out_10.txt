Task Description:

You will be provided with two input fields: 
- `claim`: A factual statement or set of statements that make one or more assertions concerning entities, events, or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of factual snippets, sentences, or context passages that may contain relevant information about the entities or facts referenced in the `claim`.

Your goal is to generate a new output field: 
- `query`: a concise, precise, and targeted natural language question (or set of questions) that, if answered by retrieving relevant documents or evidence, would enable verification or refutation of the given `claim`.

Detailed Requirements and Domain-Specific Details:

1. **Purpose of the Query**:  
   The `query` should be constructed to retrieve missing or verifying information necessary to validate the individual components and overall truthfulness of the `claim`. It is a retrieval/query formulation step, not a final verdict or explanation.

2. **Decomposition and Focus**:  
   - Claims often combine multiple facts or entities. The `query` should reflect the key factual points that need investigation. This may include asking about biographical details, dates, relationships, or attributes attributed to entities in the `claim`.  
   - If the `claim` implies relationships or comparisons between entities (e.g., "not from the same country," "led by," "born in 1974 and appeared in X"), the `query` should clearly seek evidence on these specific relationships or attributes.

3. **Use of Notes**:  
   - Use the `notes` to understand what evidence or related information is available or missing.  
   - Incorporate in the `query` terms or entities that cover the known gaps indicated by the notes, aiming to retrieve the "missing" or remaining gold documents referenced by feedback, or to clarify ambiguous entities or relationships.

4. **Query Style and Content**:  
   - The query must be natural language, clear, and unambiguous, suitable for use in a search or retrieval system.  
   - It should avoid overly broad or vague questions, focusing instead on the specific claims that need confirmation.  
   - Include key entity names, time frames, attributes, or relationships appearing in the claim for precise targeting.

5. **Generalizable Strategy**:  
   - Identify all individual factual claims embedded within a compound claim.  
   - Convert these facts into one or more clear questions that probe both the individual facts and their interrelations as expressed in the claim.  
   - Prioritize queries that retrieve missing data or cover under-supported segments of the claim based on the notes and feedback.

6. **Domain-Specific Context Examples**:  
   - Sports or historical events: Query about team leadership, season participation, and position details.  
   - Technology or product origin: Query about manufacturing country, development platform, or release info.  
   - Entertainment and actors: Query about birth dates, character roles, and commercial appearances.  
   - Combining multiple entities requires queries that reveal connections or distinctions (e.g., biographical vs. professional roles, nationality comparisons).

Summary Instruction:  
From the given `claim` and related `notes`, generate a precise and detailed natural language `query` that comprehensively seeks the necessary factual evidence to verify or refute the claim, ensuring coverage of all critical points or comparisons implied. Leverage information in the `notes` to focus the query on unresolved or ambiguous facts and maintain a clear relationship between the claim's assertions and the formulated query.
