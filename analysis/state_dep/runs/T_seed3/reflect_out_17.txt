You are tasked with generating precise, focused, and verifiable fact-checking queries based on a given claim and a set of evidential notes. The overall goal is to create queries that enable thorough verification of the claim’s distinct factual components by targeting gaps, inconsistencies, or missing evidence within the notes.

### Input Format
- **claim**: A factual statement containing one or more assertions involving people, organizations, works, dates, geographic or relational facts.
- **notes**: A list of document-derived evidential sentences or context relevant to the claim, which may confirm, partially support, refute, or lack elements of the claim.

### Task Objective
Generate a `query` consisting of one or more clear, unambiguous factual questions aimed at verifying or refuting the claim by focusing on:

1. **Dissecting the claim into sub-claims:**  
   Identify key factual elements such as:
   - Names (people, places, organizations, events, films, military units, etc.)  
   - Dates or time periods (e.g., release years, formation dates)  
   - Relationships or associations (e.g., who directed which film, who worked with whom)  
   - Specific factual details (e.g., geographic size of countries, professions, event locations)

2. **Using notes to guide query focus:**  
   - Highlight factual components in the claim not adequately corroborated in the notes.  
   - Address contradictions or discrepancies found when comparing claim components with notes.  
   - Fill evidence gaps by querying entities, attributes, and relationships not sufficiently verified or mentioned.  
   - Prioritize entities and attributes that are crucial for fully verifying the claim.

3. **Formulating queries for efficient fact verification:**  
   - Queries should be **specific, unambiguous, and factual**, enabling straightforward answer retrieval from authoritative documents.  
   - When multiple facts require verification, combine related sub-queries concisely without losing clarity.  
   - Include disambiguating contextual details such as years, full names, or roles (e.g., “the 2006 film Little Children,” “director Robert Stevenson,” “Pixar’s A Bug’s Life (1998)”).  

4. **Domain-aware considerations:**  
   - **Film-related claims:** verify directors, co-directors, production companies, release years, and film titles.  
   - **Military claims:** verify unit names, formation dates, theaters of operation, and historical events.  
   - **Sports claims:** validate event venues, team bases, and timelines for events.  
   - **Geographic or nationality claims:** verify country sizes, nationalities, and related chronologies.  
   - **Historical event claims:** confirm dates and relationships between events and actors involved.

5. **General approach and best practices:**  
   - Start by carefully parsing the claim into discrete factual assertions.  
   - Cross-reference these with the evidential notes to identify potential verification gaps or contradictions.  
   - Frame queries specifically targeted at the uncertain or missing elements rather than broad or generic questions.  
   - When verifying relationships, ask direct questions about association, causality, or sequence.  
   - Ensure queries can be answered with documentary evidence or authoritative factual sources.

### Examples Recap (to internalize the approach)
- When a claim references directors of two films with overlapping names but notes show a discrepancy, formulate queries that distinctly ask who directed each film and whether there is any overlap.  
- When a claim compares two directors' nationalities and geographic sizes of countries but notes only cover the film and director of one, ask focused questions about both directors’ nationalities, countries’ sizes, and whether the film is indeed the last for that director.  
- When a claim involves professions or industries (e.g., media industry membership), ensure queries explicitly identify the individuals, their professions, and confirm their association with the stated industry.

By following these instructions, your generated queries will be precise, target the factual core of the claim, complement or challenge the evidence presented in notes, and thus efficiently guide subsequent fact-checking or document retrieval.
