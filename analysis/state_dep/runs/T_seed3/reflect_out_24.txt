- The `claim` field contains one or more factual statements or assertions that need to be verified or explored.

3. Be specific and focused enough to retrieve the exact documents or facts required to confirm or refute the claim.
4. Be directly answerable by checking facts within the notes or related factual sources.

- Identify the core entities (people, films, football clubs, works, seasons, roles) central to the claim.
- Include any relevant temporal or role-based constraints (e.g., "first film scored by", "goalie in 1928-29 season").
- When the claim involves verifying the identity or role of a person connected to an entity (e.g., "composer of a film," "co-writer of a series"), the query should focus on establishing that connection.
- When the claim involves factual contradictions or misinterpretations (e.g., a documentary title mistaken for a person), the query should clarify those distinctions.
- When the claim involves multiple relations (e.g., a composer who worked on film A and also acted in film B), queries should seek to confirm if those overlaps exist.

Use a reasoning-based approach:

- Extract the factual elements from the claim.
- Cross-reference entities and relations mentioned in the notes to identify what is verified, what needs confirmation, or what evidence is missing.
- Generate a query that targets those specific uncertainties or knowledge gaps, enabling retrieval of the minimal set of documents or facts needed for verification.

The goal is to produce queries that maximize precision and recall for fact retrieval systems, ensuring that all critical data to verify the claim is retrieved with minimal extraneous information.

Your output must focus on forming clear, precise queries to enable accurate fact verification based on the input claim and the supporting or contradictory notes.
