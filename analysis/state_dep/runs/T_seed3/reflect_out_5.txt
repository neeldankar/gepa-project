Task Description:  
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a statement making factual assertions that often involve multiple entities, relationships, dates, events, or attributes. The `notes` are a collection of sentences or short passages drawn from documents that may provide evidence supporting or contradicting parts of the claim.  

Your goal is to produce a `query` (or set of queries) that, if answered, would allow verification of the claim’s truthfulness. You do this by:  
1. Carefully analyzing the claim to identify all key factual components and assertions that need verification. This might include:  
   - Identifying specific people, works (e.g., films, clubs), dates, roles, or other entities and their relationships.  
   - Recognizing contradictions or questionable facts embedded in the claim that require confirmation or refutation.  
2. Reviewing the notes to understand the context and factual snippets related to the claim’s components, such as:  
   - Names of people involved, film titles, release dates, directors, clubs and their leagues, sponsors, etc.  
   - Clarifying facts that confirm or contradict parts of the claim (e.g., whether a director’s name matches, whether two entities belong to the same league).  
3. Formulating a clear, concise, and focused natural language query (or queries) that explicitly targets the crucial verification points of the claim, based on the entities and relations identified in the claim and supported by the notes. Queries typically ask about:  
   - Whether a particular person acted in a specific film.  
   - The director of a film.  
   - Membership of clubs in leagues.  
   - Sponsorship information of leagues.  
   - Release years of films.  
4. The query should combine multiple related verification points into a single well-formed question wherever reasonable, to efficiently facilitate fact-checking. For example, “Did Actor X appear in Film Y, and was Film Y directed by Director Z?”  
5. Avoid overly broad or vague queries; tailor the query to the exact facts that underpin the claim.  

Additional Domain-Specific Details:  
- Films are often identified with release years and type descriptions, e.g., “2015 South Korean slasher-thriller” or “1935 propaganda documentary.”  
- Directors of films and starring actors are key relationships to verify.  
- Football clubs’ membership in leagues and the sponsorship details of those leagues are common factual points to verify.  
- Multiple notes may contain duplicate or overlapping information; synthesize rather than repeat.  
- The queries should reflect the need for retrieval of key documents relevant to all major components of the claim for complete verification.  

Generalizable Strategy:  
- Parse the claim to extract entities and their relationships or claims about them.  
- Map those to the relevant factual snippets in the notes to identify what must be confirmed.  
- Distill this into minimal but comprehensive verification questions that cover all critical factual points.  
- Phrase queries as direct, natural-language yes/no or fact-finding questions that aim to confirm or refute specific aspects of the claim.  

Output Specifications:  
- Produce only the field `query` as a single natural language question (or coordinated questions joined by 'and' / 'or') targeting the core fact-checking requirements implied by the claim and notes.  
- Do not produce explanations or reasoning in the final answer; those are for internal process only.
