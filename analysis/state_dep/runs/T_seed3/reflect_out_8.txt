Task Description:

You are given two inputs: 
- `claim`: a statement that asserts some factual information involving entities and relationships in a specific domain (e.g., sports, academia, TV series). 
- `notes`: a list of supporting or related factual sentences about the entities, timeline, or context relevant to the claim. These notes may include repeated or overlapping information and mention several related entities and dates.

Your goal is to produce a single field `query` that contains a carefully constructed, precise, and fact-checking question or set of questions to verify the claim, based on the `claim` text and the information found or implied in the `notes`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Understand the claim carefully:**  
   Identify all the factual points in the claim that require verification, including but not limited to:  
   - Named entities (people, organizations, events, dates).  
   - Relationships between entities (e.g., who won what, when did someone join a team, when was an institution founded).  
   - Temporal, geographical, or categorical constraints (e.g., "X was before Y", "driver nationality", "character played by an actor in a specific series", "postdoctoral institution established before another").

2. **Analyze the notes:**  
   The notes provide crucial context to help determine what facts are already supported and which require further verification. They often explicitly mention:  
   - Entities’ roles, positions, or actions (e.g., who won a championship, when an actor joined a series).  
   - Dates or time periods linked to entities or events.  
   - Potential contradictions or clarifications of the claim.  
   - Overlapping facts repeated multiple times, which may reinforce or clarify important aspects.

3. **Identify key queries needed to verify the claim:**  
   Your generated query should:  
   - Cover all critical factual components relevant to the claim, ensuring completeness.  
   - Explicitly include questions aimed at resolving any ambiguity or contradiction within the claim or between the claim and the notes.  
   - Include questions that verify key context facts needed for the claim (such as establishment dates, participation periods, winner identity and nationality, premiere dates).  
   - Reference entities mentioned in the claim as well as any additional entities suggested by the notes to verify parts of the claim that rely on external comparisons or timelines.

4. **Formulate the query:**  
   - Compose the query as natural language questions (one or multiple questions) that can be used to retrieve or verify the factual evidence.  
   - Be specific and precise, avoiding vague or overly broad questions.  
   - Ensure the questions are directly answerable by facts (e.g., “Did X happen in year Y?”, “Who won...?”, “What is the founding year of institution Z?”).  
   - Avoid redundant questions if multiple notes already confirm the fact, but include questions for facts not confirmed in notes or that contradict the claim.

5. **Generalizable strategy:**  
   - Start by breaking down the claim into individual factual statements.  
   - Cross-reference these with the notes to identify which parts require fact-checking queries.  
   - Construct a minimal but comprehensive set of verification questions that collectively cover all facets of the claim, especially focusing on possible contradictions or ambiguities.  
   - When possible, include multiple queries covering all entities and timelines relevant to settle the claim fully.

Domain-Specific Considerations:  
- In sports-related claims (e.g., World Rally Championship), verify winners, nationalities, and participation years carefully.  
- For academic claims, verify affiliations, training institutions, and founding dates of institutions.  
- For TV series or entertainment claims, verify premiere dates, character-actor relationships, and actor joining dates.  
- Always consider temporal ordering (e.g., whether one institution was established before another).

Output format:

``` 
### query
<One or more natural language questions to verify the claim based on claim and notes> 
```
  
Example queries provided above illustrate the style and granularity expected.

By following this approach, you ensure the generated queries enable retrieval of all necessary documents to support or refute the claim with factual precision and completeness.
