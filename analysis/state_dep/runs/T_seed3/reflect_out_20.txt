Given the input fields `claim` (a statement or assertion to verify or explore) and `notes` (a list of textual evidences or context to help verify, expand upon, or explore the claim), produce the output field `query` — a precise, concise, and well-structured set of natural language questions designed to retrieve all necessary factual information and sources that validate or refute the claim.

Key points to consider when formulating the `query`:

1. **Identify key entities, events, dates, and relationships** within the `claim` that need fact-checking or further clarification.

2. **Explore implicit assumptions or knowledge gaps** in the `claim` that are crucial to confirm its truthfulness, especially when the `claim` combines multiple facts or requires temporal/spatial relations.

3. **Leverage information from the `notes`** to:
   - Confirm known facts that ground the query in relevant context,
   - Detect when deeper or broader questions are necessary (e.g., formation dates vs. combat start dates, jurisdiction relationships, born-before comparisons).

4. **Compose queries that are open-ended but focused:**
   - Ensure they request specific, verifiable facts (formation dates, jurisdiction of a place, birth dates of individuals, band membership and roles).
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions; queries should directly target resolving key ambiguities or verifying components necessary to support or contradict the claim.

5. **Include multiple related questions if necessary**, to capture all relevant facets of the claim, particularly when relationships between multiple entities must be established.

6. **Maintain clarity and brevity** — the query should be easy to parse by retrieval systems or knowledge bases, minimizing unnecessary verbosity while preserving completeness.

7. If `notes` are empty or insufficient, rely on well-formulated questions to discover the missing critical details (e.g., identifying an unknown entity, its attributes, and relevant temporal information).

Generalizable strategy:

- Parse the claim into constituent factual elements.
- Cross-check and validate these elements against provided notes to identify what is known, what is ambiguous, and what is missing.
- Formulate targeted, evidence-seeking questions that would return precise supporting documents or data to confirm or deny the claim.
- Reference all relevant entities explicitly to avoid ambiguity in the query.
- When temporal or relational precision is needed (e.g., was event A before event B, jurisdiction overlaps, membership details), clearly articulate these comparisons.

This method ensures that information retrieval or fact verification tasks downstream can efficiently access all necessary evidence, improving accuracy and completeness when validating complex claims.
