Task Description:
You will be given two input fields:
- `claim`: a factual statement or set of statements that make one or more assertions,
- `notes`: a collection of relevant supporting or contradicting sentences derived from various documents or sources related to the claim.

Your objective is to produce a single, focused `query` that can be used to verify or falsify the factual accuracy of the `claim` based on the information in the `notes`. The query should capture the essential verifiable components of the claim and reflect any nuanced or potentially misleading elements present in it.

Detailed Guidelines and Domain-Specific Knowledge:

1. **Claim Analysis**:
   - Extract the key factual assertions contained within the claim. These may include:
     - Identification and relationships of people, places, or entities (e.g., musicians, sports teams, historical figures).
     - Specific events or achievements (e.g., a medal win, a sports season, an album release date).
     - Roles, titles, or nicknames (e.g., "trainer", "coach", "goalkeeper", "the Red Devils").
     - Temporal references or qualitative descriptions (e.g., "first in his home country," “the greatest football player of all time,” “studio album,” “the lead of Santana,”).
   - Look for potential conflations, inaccuracies, or ambiguous attributions within the claim (for example, mixing roles or identities).

2. **Utilization of Notes**:
   - The notes provide evidence that either supports, contradicts, or adds context to parts of the claim.
   - Identify:
     - Confirmed facts that verify claim elements,
     - Contradictory information that refutes claim components,
     - Lack of information or evidence on certain claim aspects.
   - These notes typically come from a variety of sources, including encyclopedic entries, historical records, sports archives, music discographies, and biographical data.

3. **Query Construction**:
   - Formulate a clear, concise, and specific question or set of questions aimed at verifying the validity of the claim, based on the factual components and contradictions identified.
   - Queries should:
     - Target verifiable entities or assertions (e.g., “Did [person] [perform action or hold role]?”, “Was [album] released as a studio album by [artist/band]?”, “Did [player] participate in [team/season]?”).
     - Include relevant qualifiers to avoid ambiguity (e.g., exact dates, roles, titles).
     - Address any potentially misleading or conflated information inside the claim by explicitly questioning those specifics.
   - Avoid yes/no formats when possible; prefer elaborated questions that encourage retrieval of detailed evidence.
   - Ensure that the query can serve as a direct input to a knowledge retrieval or verification system to obtain documents relevant for fact-checking.

4. **Generalizable Strategy**:
   - Start by decomposing the claim into individual factual units.
   - Compare each unit against the notes, identifying where the notes confirm, contradict, or do not address these units.
   - Reformulate the core contentious or unclear portions of the claim into a structured query (or queries) that explicitly and efficiently requests verification from the dataset.
   - Incorporate domain-specific nuances, such as recognizing sports roles vs. players (e.g., “trainer” vs. “coach” vs. “player”), music industry terms (“cover song,” “studio album”), or event-specific achievements (e.g., “first Native American to win Olympic medal in home country”).

5. **Examples of Domain-Specific Facts to Keep in Mind**:
   - Carlos Santana leads the band Santana; “Inner Secrets” is a 1978 studio album containing the cover “Stormy.”
   - Jim Thorpe: Native American athlete, coach of the Oorang Indians football team, first Native American Olympic medalist in his home country, but not necessarily referred to as “trainer.”
   - Eddie Mahan is a football player named by Jim Thorpe as “the greatest football player of all time,” but not a coach or trainer.
   - Manchester United F.C. is nicknamed “the Red Devils” and verification of player transfers or roles should consider typical positional functions (goalkeepers usually don’t lead strike forces).
   - Historical and sports records should be referenced by season or event year.

By adhering to these detailed guidelines when generating your `query`, you will enable efficient retrieval of relevant documents required for fact-checking the claim and clarifying ambiguous or contested assertions accurately.
