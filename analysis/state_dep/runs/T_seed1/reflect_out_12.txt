Task Description:
Your task is to generate a precise and focused query designed to verify or explore a factual claim based on relevant background or supporting information (notes). The query should be a concise, well-formed question or information request explicitly referencing the key entities, relationships, timelines, and attributes from both the claim and the notes.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual statement involving named entities such as people, organizations, films, events, or dates, often describing relationships or attributes between these entities (e.g., participation in events, roles, timelines).
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or fragments containing related, supporting, contradictory, or contextual information about the claim, entities, or relationships mentioned therein.

Output:
- `query`: A clear, specific, and unambiguous question or information request that captures all critical elements of the claim and uses the notes for guidance, targeting retrieval of evidence or documents that can validate or refute the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Entity and Relationship Extraction:
   - Identify and explicitly include all key entities (people, institutions, films, events, dates, awards) mentioned in the claim.
   - Identify important relationships between these entities, such as roles (e.g., actor, director), affiliations (sponsorship, membership), temporal relations (event sequences, years), or attributes (nicknames, veteran status).
   - Incorporate any relevant disambiguation or specificity (e.g., full film titles with release years, full event names and editions).

2. Use Notes to Guide Query Construction:
   - Extract definitive facts in the notes that support, contradict, or contextualize parts of the claim, including exact names, event outcomes, dates, and participant identities.
   - Recognize contradictions or missing information in the notes that indicate points requiring further confirmation.
   - Respect domain-specific conventions: for example, "strike force" relates to forwards in football (soccer) and not goalkeepers; the "Golden Bear" is the top award at the Berlin International Film Festival tied to specific editions.
   - Identify temporal markers to clarify the correct timing and sequence of events or participation (e.g., football seasons vs. event years, film festival editions).

3. Query Formulation Strategy:
   - Formulate queries that explicitly address verification points raised in the claim, integrating all relevant entities, relationships, and temporal details.
   - If notes disagree with or lack confirmation of a claim element, include these elements in the query to prompt retrieval of missing or contradictory evidence.
   - Queries should be detailed enough to avoid ambiguity—use full names, event editions, year qualifiers (e.g., "50th Berlin International Film Festival" vs. just "Berlin International Film Festival").
   - Queries often focus on one or more of these aspects:
        * Confirmation of specific facts about entities (founding years, award winners, nicknames).
        * Verification of roles or relationships (an actor's participation in a film, sponsorship by a company).
        * Temporal ordering or co-occurrence (did event A happen before event B? Did a person participate in event X after joining organization Y?).
        * Overlap or intersection of participations across entities and timelines.

4. Coverage and Completeness:
   - Ensure the query comprehensively covers all critical factual elements in the claim necessary for adjudicating its truth.
   - Incorporate entities or relationships mentioned but absent or contradicted in the notes, to explicitly seek missing evidence.
   - Combine multiple verification points into a single clear and concise question where appropriate to optimize retrieval focus.

5. Clarity, Specificity, and Domain Nuance:
   - Queries must be clear, specific, and free from vague generalities.
   - Use proper disambiguation: include years for films and events, full organization names, and clarify ambiguous terms (e.g., "goalie" vs. "forward").
   - Reflect domain knowledge and conventions when forming queries (e.g., "strike force" means forwards, award names tied to specific festivals and years).
   - Avoid overly broad or open-ended questions; target the core fact(s) needing verification.

6. Generalizable Approach:
   - Carefully analyze the claim for its core entities and relations.
   - Reconcile clues and contradictions in the notes to identify verification focus.
   - Formulate a single, integrated query that explicitly includes all pertinent entities, relationships, and temporal markers necessary to retrieve decisive evidence.
   - Anticipate missing elements in notes and construct queries to explicitly retrieve these.

Summary:
Produce fact-driven, integrated, and domain-informed queries that tightly connect entities, attributes, relationships, and contextual timelines drawn from the claim and notes. The goal is to generate targeted questions that facilitate precise retrieval of evidence verifying the extent to which the claim is supported, contradicted, or unsubstantiated by available information.
