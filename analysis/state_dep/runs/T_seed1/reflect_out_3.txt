Your task is to generate a precise and factually relevant query based on the given `claim` and a corresponding set of detailed `notes`. 

Input:
- `claim`: A factual or historical statement containing specific entities, dates, events, or relationships that might be verified or challenged.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences, excerpts, or key information pieces extracted from various documents that provide evidence supporting, contradicting, or clarifying aspects of the claim.

Output:
- `query`: A focused, clear, and well-structured question designed to verify or investigate the key factual elements of the `claim`, explicitly referencing relevant entities, events, dates, or relationships that appear in the claim and notes. The query should be suitable for information retrieval systems or human experts to retrieve documents and information that will confirm or refute the claim.

Detailed guidance and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Identify the core factual points in the claim**  
   Extract key entities (e.g., regiment names, film titles, sports teams), temporal facts (years, release dates), and relationships (e.g., "voiced Piglet," "hosted UEFA Euro 2012 Final") mentioned in the claim.

2. **Leverage the notes to refine and contextualize the query**  
   The notes often contain nuanced, domain-specific details such as:
   - Exact formation years of military units vs. their notable events.
   - The linkage between films and real historical units or actors.
   - Precise locations and affiliations of sports teams and venues by country or city.
   - Verification of voice actors in specific movie releases and chronology of films.
   
   Use these to clarify ambiguities in the claim or to cast the query in a way that covers the specific factual dispute (e.g., whether a regiment started in 1944 or earlier).

3. **Ensure the query explicitly references key entities and correct terminology**  
   - Name films with production year and director when relevant to differentiate versions or adaptations.  
   - Use full official names of military units, sports clubs, or venues found in the notes.  
   - Clearly mention geographic locations (e.g., city and country) when place confusion is part of the claim.

4. **Adopt a verification focus**  
   Frame the query to confirm the accuracy of the claim by including contrasting possibilities when appropriate (e.g., "did it begin in 1944 or earlier?").

5. **Avoid assumptions or vague language**  
   The query should not presuppose facts beyond both the claim and notes. It should remain open to confirming or denying the claim based on evidence.

6. **Generalize approach**  
   Use this method for any domain where a claim references complex or niche factual assertions, and notes provide supporting or contradictory details:
   - Extract and isolate claim fact patterns.
   - Compare them against provided notes.
   - Generate fact-checking queries encapsulating potential ambiguities or contradictions for retrieval.

Example structure for queries based on the samples:
- When focusing on time and origin of entities:  
  "When was [entity] originally formed? Specifically, did it begin in [year] or earlier/later?"  
- When location and affiliation are confused:  
  "Which multi-use sports and recreation facility in [location] currently houses [team], and has it ever hosted [event]?"  
- When confirming roles and chronology in media:  
  "Did [person] provide the voice of [character] in the [year] [movie title], and was [another movie] released before that?"

In summary, your instruction is to produce a clarifying, fact-checking question that directly addresses the key factual claims and uncertainties illuminated by the notes, maximizing retrieval of relevant documents for verification or further research.
