You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim` is a factual assertion or statement whose truth or accuracy needs to be assessed or elaborated upon based on contextual background.
- `notes` are a list of fact-focused observations, supporting or undermining evidence, clarifications, or hypotheses related to the claim, extracted or inferred from the given context. They often consolidate details about persons, places, events, or entities mentioned in the claim and from the context.
- `context` is a collection of text snippets or documents containing factual information relevant to the claim, providing background, explanations, or specifics about the entities or subjects involved.

Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. **`new_notes`** should be a concise, focused list of key, factual points derived from examining both `notes` and `context`. These notes should:

   - Summarize the essential findings regarding the validity or support of the claim.
   - Include domain-specific facts about entities (e.g., geographic locations, people, organizations, events, or works such as films and their casts) that clarify or refute the claim.
   - Address nuances such as geographic boundaries, actor nationalities or filmographies, historical facts, or organizational relationships as relevant.
   - Reduce redundancy from the input `notes`, eliminate speculative or uncertain phrasing, and emphasize confirmed facts or strong absence of evidence.
   - Represent a well-reasoned synthesis that can stand alone to clarify whether the claim is supported or not based on the evidence.

2. **`titles`** should be a list of the most relevant, specific, and authoritative document or entity titles selected from the `context` or logically inferred that are critical to understanding and verifying the claim. These typically correspond to:

   - Named entities explicitly mentioned in the `context` or `claim` (e.g., specific cemeteries, films, railway stations, actors).
   - Geographic or administrative areas that are important for disambiguation (e.g., counties, cities, neighborhoods).
   - Persons involved, particularly when verifying biographical or filmographic details.
   - Organizations or works (e.g., films, parks, companies) playing a central role in the claim.

When selecting `titles`:

   - Ensure coverage of all key entities necessary to assess the claim.
   - Avoid titles unrelated or tangential to the core fact-checking, even if present in the context.

**Additional guidance based on domain-specific observations from examples:**

- For geographic claims, carefully note the jurisdictional boundaries and relationships, distinguishing e.g., cities vs. counties, including sub-regions or boroughs, and confirm that terminologies such as "part of" or "within" are supported by authoritative context.
- For claims about persons (e.g., actors), identify nationality facts and filmographies clearly, especially when addressing claims involving multiple titles and actor participation.
- For transportation-related claims, note historical treminuses/stations and their correct geographic placement.
- Avoid including speculative or unsupported claims in `new_notes`; focus on documented facts or well-supported negations.
- When reasoning about claims, clearly highlight any factual contradictions, geographic misconceptions, or absence of linkage in the evidence.
- Synthesize multiple notes into clear, distilled points rather than reproducing them verbatim.
- Ensure the output facilitates retrieval or verification of key documents (as in `titles`) and provides a distilled fact-checking summary (`new_notes`) useful for transparency and downstream tasks.

**Summary:**

Analyze `claim`, `notes`, and `context` together to:

  - Generate a factual, concise, and non-redundant set of supporting or contradicting notes (`new_notes`) that evaluate the claim accurately.
  - Select a high-precision list of relevant document or entity titles (`titles`) that are essential to substantiate or refute the claim, reflecting gold-standard coverage for retrieval.

This task combines fact assessment, entity disambiguation, geographic and biographical domain knowledge, and information synthesis for claim verification.
