Task Description:
Given two inputs, `claim` and `notes`, your goal is to produce a precise and focused `query` that can be used to verify or explore the claim based on the information provided in the notes.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual claim or statement that involves named entities, their relationships, events, timelines, or attributes.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or fragments containing supporting, contradicting, or background information relevant to the entities and relationships in the claim.

Output:
- `query`: A concise, well-formed question or information request that explicitly references the key elements and relationships from both the claim and the supporting information in the notes. The query should be designed to retrieve additional evidence or documents that address the core factual points needed to verify the claim.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Information:

1. Entity and Relationship Identification:
   - Identify all key entities in the claim such as people, institutions, films, events, dates.
   - Identify their relationships as expressed in the claim (e.g., "X completed postdoctoral training at Y," "Actor A performed in Movie B," "Movie B was directed by Person C," "Event D happened before Event E").

2. Use Notes to Guide Query Construction:
   - Extract definitive facts from the notes that support or provide context to parts of the claim, such as exact names of institutions, founding dates, actor names, film release years, and relationship status (e.g., whether actor X appeared in movie Y).
   - Capture temporal or causal relationships explicitly stated or implied in the notes (e.g., timelines of events, sequence of performances).

3. Query Formulation:
   - Formulate queries that explicitly incorporate dates, entities, and relationships critical to verifying the claim.
   - Queries should generally focus on:
     * Confirmation of entity attributes (e.g., founding years, release dates).
     * Overlaps or intersections between entities (e.g., actors common to two films).
     * Temporal order or sequence of events (e.g., did event A occur before event B?).
     * Roles or capacities in which entities participated (e.g., director vs. actor).
   - If certain elements mentioned in the claim are lacking in notes (such as no info about a director or film), still incorporate these elements into the query to prompt further evidence retrieval.

4. Coverage & Completeness:
   - Aim for a query that covers all main aspects of the claim as supported by notes or flagged as missing from them.
   - The query should attempt to target retrieval of all critical documents or facts necessary to adjudicate the truth of the claim (e.g., documents related to all key entities, films, dates, directors).

5. Clarity and Specificity:
   - Make queries clear, unambiguous, and as specific as possible to aid precise document retrieval.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions.
   - Use proper names with disambiguation where necessary (e.g., "Wonder Woman (2017 film)" rather than just "Wonder Woman").

6. Examples of Typical Query Types:
   - Confirm temporal facts: "Was the University of Washington founded before Texas Christian University?"
   - Verify career overlap and timing: "Which actors appeared in Wonder Woman (2017) and also performed in Anomalisa after the premiere of Fargo (TV series)?"
   - Confirm roles and participants: "Did the actor starring alongside Go Ah-sung in Office also appear in The Deal, and was The Deal directed by Choi Woo-shik?"

Summary:
Your task is to produce focused, fact-driven queries that explicitly extract and integrate all relevant entities, relationships, and temporal sequences from the claim and notes to enable comprehensive evidence retrieval. The queries should explicitly address verification points raised by the claim, informed by the facts and gaps found in the notes.
