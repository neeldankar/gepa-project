Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` field that effectively captures the key factual questions or verifications required to validate or refute the claim using the information summarized in `notes`. The `query` should focus on retrieving or checking specific pieces of evidence or facts relevant to the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A statement that makes one or several factual assertions.
- `notes`: A list of text snippets extracted or summarized from multiple documents or sources. These snippets contain relevant facts, confirmations, contradictions, or contextual information relating to the claim.

Output Format:
- `query`: A clear, concise, and precise question or set of questions that directly targets the verification of the claim's key elements. This query should be suitable for searching or retrieving factual documents or evidence to confirm or deny the claim.

Detailed Task Instructions:
1. Parse the provided claim and decompose it into its essential factual components—entities (people, places, works), relationships, dates, attributes, or events that require validation.
2. Review the `notes` for relevant context:
   - Identify the entities, dates, and relationships documented within.
   - Pay particular attention to named entities (e.g., films, people, locations), roles (e.g., actor, composer), time-related information (release years, chronological order), and hierarchical or jurisdictional relationships (e.g., administrative governance).
3. Formulate the `query` by:
   - Turning the key elements of the claim into explicit verification or retrieval questions.
   - Including all critical facets of the claim that need to be confirmed, such as:
     - Identification of persons linked to roles, appearances in works, or notable fame.
     - Temporal relationships between events or releases (e.g., which work was released first).
     - Geographic or administrative hierarchy validations.
   - Structuring the query so that it targets the minimal yet sufficient set of documents or facts needed to verify the claim fully.
4. The query should be syntactically clear and grammatically correct, often phrased as natural language questions.
5. When the claim contains compound assertions, the query should reflect the connections between facts, e.g., confirming an actor's involvement in multiple films and their known roles.
6. Avoid extraneous or overly broad queries; focus on the precise intersection of facts necessary for validation.
7. The goal is to produce a query that, if used to retrieve documents, will collect all necessary evidence (and only those) to confirm or deny the claim, optimizing for relevance and completeness.

Domain-Specific Knowledge and Strategies Observed:
- Recognize that works (films, television shows) can be linked to people by roles or appearances, and those links often serve as verification paths.
- Time-based validation often involves comparing release years or event dates to establish sequencing or precedence.
- Geopolitical claims may require understanding administrative hierarchies (e.g., counties under cities, cities within provinces, provinces within countries).
- The term “sequel-in-part” may be ambiguous and requires distinguishing between unrelated works even if terminology is similar.
- The queries should explicitly request verification of these linkages (e.g., “Who starred in X?”, “Is person A best known for role Y?”, “Which city governs location Z?”, “Are two places in the same country?”, “What year was work X released?”).
- The reasoning quality depends on accurately extracting and connecting multiple facts from the notes, sometimes involving disambiguation among entities sharing similar attributes.

Ultimately, your output `query` should mirror the central factual verification lines uncovered through your reasoning about the claim and notes.
