Task Description:
You will be given two inputs:
- `claim`: A factual statement involving named entities (people, organizations, films, events, places, dates, etc.) and their relationships, attributes, or temporal sequences.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or fragments that provide supporting, contradicting, or contextual information relevant to the claim’s entities and relationships.

Your goal is to produce a precise, explicit, and focused `query` — a well-formed question or information request — that can be used to verify, expand, or investigate the truth of the claim by retrieving additional pertinent evidence or documents.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A natural language factual claim involving entities and their relationships, attributes, or chronology.
- `notes`: A list (array) of factual statements that help confirm, contradict, or provide relevant context for parts of the claim.

Output:
- `query`: A concise, clear, and fact-driven query that explicitly references all key entities, relationships, and temporal markers from both the claim and notes, designed to target the retrieval of documents or evidence that adjudicate the claim's validity.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Guidance:

1. Entity and Relationship Extraction:
   - Identify all critical named entities from the claim: people (with full names), organizations/institutions, films/movies (include release years as disambiguation), events, locations (cities, counties, countries), dates, and other relevant attributes.
   - Identify their relationships as described in the claim (e.g., membership in a band, actor roles in a movie, company headquarters location, timeline of events).

2. Integrate Notes to Guide Query Focus:
   - Analyze the notes to extract exact names, dates, attributes, and relationships that support, partially support, contradict, or contextualize the claim.
   - If notes suggest clarifications or contradictions (e.g., company nationality differs from claim), reflect those in your reasoning to guide query scope.
   - Identify missing but relevant entities or relationships in notes (e.g., missing director info for a film mentioned in claim) and include these in your query to prompt retrieval of needed details.

3. Constructing the Query:
   - Incorporate all critical entities and temporal or causal relationships needed to verify the claim fully.
   - Ensure the query explicitly asks about:
     * Confirmation of entity attributes (e.g., "Was X founded in year Y?")
     * Roles and participation (e.g., "Did actor A appear in Film B directed by Director C?")
     * Temporal sequence (e.g., "Did Event X occur before Event Y?")
     * Geographic base or national affiliation (e.g., "Was the company headquartered in Location L and registered in Country C?")
     * Intersection or overlaps (e.g., "Which actors appeared in both Film A and Film B after Date D?")
   - When multiple related entities occur (e.g., several bands involving the same member), address this plurality explicitly ("Which band(s) featuring Member M are based in County C?").

4. Coverage and Completeness:
   - Your query must cover all main aspects of the claim and relevant facts found or missing in the notes.
   - The query should be comprehensive enough to target retrieval of all crucial documents necessary to evaluate the entire claim.
   - Avoid narrow queries that miss key entities or relationships indicated by the claim or notes.

5. Clarity and Specificity:
   - Use precise and unambiguous entity names, disambiguated where possible (e.g., "Wonder Woman (2017 film)").
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions that could retrieve irrelevant information.
   - Phrase queries clearly with explicit reference to the core factual points needed for verification.

6. Generalizable Strategy:
   - Carefully integrate all available information from notes with the claim’s components, highlighting support or contradictions.
   - Identify gaps in the notes and incorporate those missing elements into the query to stimulate retrieval of evidence needed for full adjudication.
   - Explicitly include temporal and spatial qualifiers and role-based relationships to disambiguate entities and their involvement.

7. Typical Query Types:
   - Verifying founding dates or locations (e.g., "Was X founded before Y and headquartered where?")
   - Checking whether specific people fulfilled roles or participated in particular events or productions.
   - Confirming temporal orders between events or performances.
   - Identifying overlaps in membership or participation across different groups or productions.

Summary:
Your output query should be a focused, fact-based question or information request incorporating all relevant entities, dates, relationships, and contextual details from the claim and notes, designed to retrieve definitive evidence or documentation that verifies or refutes the core factual assertions of the claim. Prioritize completeness, clarity, and specificity while capturing any contradictions or gaps noted.
