- `context`: A collection of textual excerpts or documents about the entities or topics referenced in the claim.    - Avoid repeating redundant statements and maintain precision.  
   - Reflect domain-specific factual nuances, such as distinguishing between related but separate data (e.g., album release dates vs band formation, number of acts in an opera vs film structure, exact locations vs administrative regions).  
   - When the claim involves multiple parts or compound conditions (e.g., part A and part B), address the verifiability of each component separately and then altogether.  
   - If a claim includes ambiguous references (e.g., slightly incorrect titles), clarify the most probable intended entity or fact and note any uncertainty.  
   - State clearly whether the claim is supported, contradicted, or unverifiable with the data given.  

2. `titles`: A list of precise document or entity titles that best represent the authoritative sources necessary to verify the claim. For this:  
   - Include all primary and secondary entities, works, or institutions directly referenced by the claim and highlighted in the `new_notes`.  
   - Choose titles that use disambiguation when applicable (e.g., including entity type or contextual clarifiers like “(film)”, “(opera)”, “(Franz Ferdinand album)”).  
   - Cover all key subjects involved, such as individual people, companies, institutions, geographic locations, films or novels, awards, or biological species.  
   - Avoid including peripheral or unrelated titles even if present in broader context documents.

Additional guidelines and domain specific considerations:

In summary, your goal is to:  
- Analyze domain-specific factual information from input notes and context, synthesizing essential verification points clearly and accurately.  
- Reflect explicit support, contradiction, or insufficiency of evidence regarding the claim, identifying missing key facts.  
- Provide a targeted list of authoritative titles representing the core evidence base for the claim verification.

Your outputs will help humans evaluate the claim’s correctness and efficiently access foundational resources for confirmation or refutation. Keep the output factual, neutral, and well-structured to enable straightforward interpretation.
