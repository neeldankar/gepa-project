Given the input fields:
- `claim`: a complex factual assertion often involving named entities, historical or cultural facts, biographical details, or domain-specific events;
- `notes`: a list of factual statements or extracted evidences relevant to the claim, which may include confirmations, contradictions, or clarifications;
- `context`: a list of summarized or partial background documents or encyclopedia-like excerpts providing detailed information about entities, events, or topics related to the claim;

Your task is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: a concise, filtered, and logically organized subset of the input `notes` that best supports a reasoned evaluation of the claim. This output should:
   - Emphasize directly relevant facts that confirm or disprove components of the claim,
   - Remove redundancies and less relevant information,
   - Incorporate clarifications about the factual context (such as correcting misattributed roles, distinguishing verified from unverified individuals, or explaining historical or administrative structures),
   - Reflect a balanced view from the evidence if the claim is partially true or unverified,
   - Aim to produce a compact and coherent set of statements that can support reasoning or verdicts about the claim's accuracy.

2. `titles`: a list of document titles or entity names that represent the key contextual sources or topics that underpin the reasoning. These should:
   - Include titles clearly linked to the main entities or events in the claim (such as biographies, specific historical sites, literary works, or film titles),
   - Prioritize documents that provide unique or authoritative explanatory background,
   - Strive to match or exceed the set of gold or expected documents that would be used to verify or refute the claim,
   - Avoid overly generic or unrelated titles unless they provide necessary background context (e.g., genre definitions when relevant).

In forming your outputs, follow these domain-specific and task-specific insights:

- Claims may blend multiple facts (dates, roles, identity, works) that must be separately verified and addressed.
- Verification requires matching entities (people, films, events) in the notes and context to those in the claim, noting variant name spellings or aliases.
- Distinguish official titles and roles from common misunderstandings (e.g., Auschwitz was run by the SS, not private owners; Neo-western is distinct from western genre).
- Avoid introducing information not present in the supplied notes or context.
- When a part of the claim is unverified due to lack of information in context, acknowledge that explicitly in the notes.
- Your outputs should enable subsequent fact-checking, providing both precise evidence (`new_notes`) and source anchors (`titles`).

As a high-level strategy, analyze the claim’s components, identify relevant evidence within notes and context, summarize the key points supporting or refuting the claim in `new_notes`, and select authoritative titles from the context that a researcher would consult to assess the claim’s accuracy.
