Your goal is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: A refined and concise subset of the input `notes` that are the most relevant to assessing the factual accuracy of the claim, ideally emphasizing statements that clarify whether the claim is supported, contradicted, or lacks verification based on the provided `context`. These notes should focus strictly on definitive or evidential information about the claim and its key entities or events.

2. `titles`: A list of the most relevant document titles from the `context` that are critical to understanding or verifying the claim. The titles should correspond closely to the main subjects, entities, or events referenced in the claim and supported or contradicted in the notes/context. Retrieve titles of those documents that are likely authoritative or central to the claim’s factual verification, prioritizing exact matches or direct relevance over peripheral or loosely related topics. Include known proper nouns, notable units, named individuals, or historical events central to the claim.

- Analyze the claim carefully to identify the key entities, dates, events, or subjects it references.
- Examine the `notes` for statements that address the accuracy of the claim, especially those clarifying dates of formation, key persons involved, or direct contradictions/validations.
- Cross-reference the `notes` with the provided `context` entries to identify which documents relate closely to the claim’s core subjects. Note that context entries may have detailed background or historical information relevant for claim verification.
- Select a concise set of `new_notes` that best summarize the evidence for or against the claim, omitting less relevant or unsupported statements.
- Extract from `context` those document titles that provide foundational or authoritative knowledge about the claim’s key elements (e.g. notable historical military units, specific films with named directors and actors, specific publications or people, or named works and biographies).
- If the claim is unsupported by the context, the `new_notes` and `titles` can be empty lists, but still provide a short reasoning inference explaining the lack of evidence.
- Use domain-specific knowledge gleaned from the examples, such as:
  * Military unit formation years relative to WWII, especially units like the 100th Infantry Battalion/442nd Regimental Combat Team.
  * Films centered on historical military units by named directors (e.g. Lane Nishikawa).

  * Actors and films related to portrayals of Marilyn Monroe linked to named novels or plays.
- Avoid including unrelated or generic context entries; focus on those directly related to the claim subject and entities.
- When multiple documents are relevant, prioritize titles that explicitly match or have direct genealogical or functional ties to the claim (e.g., military regiments, named filmmakers, film titles).
- Your response is not required to include the `reasoning` text explicitly, but make sure the `new_notes` and `titles` reflect clear evidence or lack thereof to support or contradict the claim.

Summary:
Effectively, your task is to synthesize the supporting or contradicting facts from the input notes and identify the foundational documents that underpin those facts, ensuring that the outputs provide clear, evidence-based refinement of the claim’s truthfulness and its key sources.
