Task Description:  
You will be given two inputs: a `claim` and a set of `notes`. Your goal is to generate a single, precise natural language `query` (or a tight set of linked queries) designed specifically to verify or investigate the claim by leveraging the information available in the notes. The query should be targeted and unambiguous, formulated to efficiently retrieve the authoritative evidence needed to support or refute the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A concise factual or relational statement involving one or more entities, their attributes, or relationships, which requires verification.  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets containing factual background, clarifications, partial information, or potential contradictions about the claim entities or their relationships.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A focused, well-structured natural language question or connected questions that explicitly target the critical elements of the claim, refined and disambiguated using the notes. The query must facilitate retrieval of relevant and authoritative documents or facts necessary for verification.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Entity and Relationship Extraction  
   - Identify all key named entities mentioned in the claim (e.g., persons, locations, organizations, events, works such as books, songs, films).  
   - Extract the claim’s core factual relationships or assertions (e.g., authorship, membership, native place, event participation, temporal overlap, genre classification).  

2. Use Notes for Contextualization and Disambiguation  
   - Analyze the notes for supplemental data — dates, places, roles, definitions, or known associations — that clarify or constrain the claim’s scope.  
   - Detect and resolve ambiguities such as:  
     • Multiple entities with similar or identical names,  
     • Geographic subdivisions or jurisdictions,  
     • Temporal details (e.g., birth dates, event dates),  
     • Classification clarifications (e.g., genre, type of publication, band membership).  
   - Pay particular attention to contradictions or confirmations in the notes about the claim’s key assertions.

3. Query Content and Focus  
   - Develop queries explicitly to verify the claim’s main factual components:  
     • Presence or absence of the stated relationship(s),  
     • Specific attributes or temporal facts necessary to support/refute the claim,  
     • Jurisdictional, membership, or classification aspects relevant to the entities.  
   - When multiple entities or facts are involved, connect queries logically to encompass comparative or combined verifications, for example:  
     • Do two entities share a geographic or temporal context?  
     • Does one entity have more members than another?  
     • Is a work attributed to the specified creator at a given date?  
   - Where contradictions or ambiguous points are apparent from the notes, the query should explicitly seek resolution of these, often by questioning both sides or alternative possibilities.

4. Query Quality and Efficiency  
   - Compose queries to be as concise, clear, and narrowly targeted as possible, avoiding overly broad or vague questions.  
   - Use natural-language phrasing that retrieval systems can effectively interpret to identify relevant authoritative evidence.  
   - Always include key entities by name and relevant qualifiers (e.g., birth year, band genre, event date) to disambiguate and focus the retrieval.

5. Domain-Specific and Niche Knowledge Considerations  
   - For claims about authorship, publication, or creative attributions: verify authorship, publication year, work type (e.g., magazine as a periodical), and proper entity disambiguation.  
   - For natural history or biology claims: focus queries on native habitat classification and geographic distribution (e.g., desert versus temperate regions).  
   - For temporal overlap or lifespan claims: clarify birth and death years or career periods to verify conditions like coexistence or active involvement in an event.  
   - For entertainment industry claims (e.g., actors, musicians): verify roles, participation dates, and membership status or associations.  
   - For geographic or political jurisdiction claims: query administration levels and verify if multiple entities lie within the same regions or jurisdictions.  
   - Recognize “periodical” scope includes magazines; clarify classification to aid precise retrieval.  

6. Generalizable Strategy  
   - Prioritize verification of key factual claims embedded in the input claim.  
   - Carefully leverage notes to provide disambiguation, conflicting details, or confirmation.  
   - Identify all pertinent entities and relationships, accounting for possible ambiguity or contradiction.  
   - Formulate a minimal but comprehensive set of linked, precise natural language questions covering all critical aspects, thus maximizing retrieval of the authoritative "gold" documents or facts to verify or refute the claim.

Summary:  
Produce a single, explicit, and focused natural language query (or tightly linked questions) tailored to effectively verify the claim using the notes. The query should distinctly mention the main entities with relevant attributes or temporal markers and explicitly address any ambiguities or contradictions identified in the notes. This will enable retrieval of core authoritative evidence needed for fact-checking or investigation.
