You will be given three inputs:

1. **claim**: A statement asserting a factual relationship or fact that needs verification or clarification.

2. **notes**: A list of extracted snippets, statements, or paraphrased facts relevant to the claim. These may contain redundancies, partial contradictions, or incomplete support.

3. **context**: A list of background documents or article summaries associated with the claim topic. Each document contains authoritative, detailed, or supporting factual information that may back or refute the notes or claim.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

### Outputs

1. **new_notes**:  
   A concise, factual, and non-redundant set of notes synthesized primarily from the provided notes and supported by the context. This set should:  
   - Directly support, clarify, or refute the claim by focusing on *relevant, precise facts only*.  
   - Exclude unsupported information, contradictions, or extraneous details.  
   - Be factually correct and consistent with the context documents.  
   - Summarize or merge duplicate or overlapping notes into single clear statements.  
   - Use standalone sentences—each note should independently convey a fact related to the claim’s verification or contradiction.  
   - Remove any misinformation or incorrect details present in the original notes by cross-referencing with the context.  
   - Address domain-specific considerations where relevant, such as:  
     - Distinguishing between persons/entities with similar names or roles (disambiguation).  
     - Clarifying organizational/geographical affiliations (e.g., band bases, county vs. city locations, school and district distinctions).  
     - Verifying biographies, filmographies, historical dates, or attribution of works accurately.  
   - Avoid introducing any information not present in the notes or context or based on unsupported assumptions or speculation.

2. **titles**:  
   A short list of document or article titles from the provided context that *best represent the authoritative sources* containing the facts used in the new_notes. Selection of titles should aim for:  
   - Completeness: Cover all key facts underlying the new_notes.  
   - Appropriateness: Titles closely align with the entities or subjects pertinent to the claim (technical or domain-specific specificity preferred, e.g., use "ValuJet Airlines" for airline-related facts rather than generic aviation articles).  
   - Minimality: Include the smallest sufficient set of titles that together cover the evidence.  
   - Accuracy: Titles correspond exactly or as closely as possible to the context documents that provide definitive factual support.

### Domain-Specific and General Best Practices

- Claims often hinge on verifying specific factual relationships such as:  
  - The composition or membership of organizations or bands.  
  - Geographical bases, headquarters, or county/city affiliations.  
  - Biographical data (birth dates, nationalities).  
  - Attribution of creative works (films, albums, roles).  
  - Historical facts and timelines.  

- Notes and context may contain redundancies, contradictions, or misinformation; critical evaluation against the context is essential.

- When multiple similar entities exist (e.g., people with similar names or related bands), disambiguate carefully to avoid conflating facts.

- Reasoning should synthesize and clarify why particular notes are included or excluded based on their factual support by the context.

- Summarize detailed background from multiple notes into clear, singular statements without loss of meaning.

- Do not invent details or include speculative reasoning beyond the information in the notes and context.

- Each new_notes entry must stand on its own as a factual statement relevant to verifying or invalidating the claim.

- When assessing and selecting titles, ensure no critical documents from the context are overlooked that substantiate the new_notes.

### Summary

Your overall goal is to produce an evidence-based synthesis from the notes and context that *validates or refutes the claim* with accurate, precise, and non-redundant factual statements, accompanied by the minimal but comprehensive set of source titles that definitively underpin the conclusions.
