Task Title: Construction of Precise Verification Queries for Fact-Checking Claims Using Contextual Notes

Task Objective:  
Given a factual claim and a set of background notes related to that claim, your task is to generate a concise, well-structured, natural language query or a logically connected set of queries that effectively focus on verifying or investigating the truthfulness of the claim. The query must tightly incorporate and disambiguate all key entities and relationships using the supplemental information from the notes — such as dates, classifications, roles, and geopolitical or domain-specific context — to maximize retrieval of authoritative and relevant evidence that can confirm or refute the core assertions efficiently.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A short factual or relational statement involving named entities (persons, locations, organizations, creative works, biological species, etc.) and their attributes or relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets providing clarifications, contextual background, partial evidence, contradictory or confirming facts, or domain-specific details regarding the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A focused, precise natural language question or minimal linked set of questions, explicitly targeting the verification of the main factual points, refined by contextual clues and potential ambiguities highlighted in the notes.

Detailed Task Instructions:

1. **Entity and Relation Extraction**  
   - Identify all key named entities in the claim: persons (including roles like directors, actors), places (cities, countries, regions), organizations (labels, studios), creative works (films, books, albums), biological species, or other relevant entities.  
   - Extract the factual or relational claims made between these entities, such as authorship, membership, directorship, native habitat, time period overlaps, and label affiliations.

2. **Disambiguation and Context Utilization**  
   - Use the notes to:  
     - Clarify ambiguous names, overlapping identities, or unclear references (e.g., a band name vs. a label of the same name).  
     - Distinguish entity types (e.g., differentiating labels from bands or actors from characters).  
     - Resolve contradictions in the claim or corroborate details with multiple note snippets.  
     - Incorporate domain-specific knowledge such as industry terminology, classification of works, or geopolitical jurisdictions.  
     - Use temporal and geographic details in notes to refine or narrow the focus of verification.

3. **Domain-Specific Verification Considerations**  

   a. **Film and Media**  
     - Verify identities and roles of directors, actors, screenwriters for specific works with attention to dates and sequels.  
     - Check if set periods (e.g., “worked for 3 years”) are supported by evidence.  
     - Resolve whether multiple entities referenced (e.g., bands, labels) share origin or collaboration history accurately.  
   
   b. **Book and Publication Claims**  
     - Confirm author attribution and publication mediums (e.g., magazine vs. periodical).  
     - Verify publication dates and classification of the publishing outlet.  
   
   c. **Biological and Natural History Claims**  
     - Confirm habitat classification and regional specificity for species or natural entities.  
   
   d. **Geographical and Political Jurisdiction**  
     - Validate locations’ administrative relationships (e.g., city within province, or organizations’ origin within countries).  
   
   e. **Temporal Overlaps**  
     - Verify timeline overlaps such as active years, lifespan, or concurrent participation in events or projects by entities.

4. **Query Formulation Principles**  
   - Explicitly mention the key entities and the precise relationships/facts to verify.  
   - Use concise, focused phrasing to target retrieval at definitive evidence sources.  
   - Where the notes suggest ambiguity, contradictions, or gaps, phrase queries to directly resolve those points.  
   - Separate multiple core sub-claims into linked, coherent questions if necessary, to cover all critical factual elements thoroughly without redundancy.  
   - Avoid vagueness or broad queries that introduce irrelevant information.  
   - Ensure natural language formulation that retrieval systems can effectively interpret to return authoritative documents confirming or refuting the claim.

5. **Generalizable Strategy for Verification Query Construction**  
   - Begin by extracting and prioritizing the core factual components of the claim (entities and key relationships).  
   - Analyze notes to disambiguate or contextualize ambiguous elements and identify contradictions or confirmations.  
   - Translate these insights into explicit verification questions, addressing each critical component and any disputed or ambiguous aspect.  
   - Incorporate temporal, geographic, role-specific, or domain-classification details from notes to narrow query scope.  
   - Produce a single, well-structured query or minimal set of logically linked queries that collectively cover all essential verification points with maximal clarity and precision.

Summary:  
Your output should be a single, tightly focused natural language query or a minimal cluster of linked queries that leverages all relevant background information from the notes to verify the claim’s validity. The queries must explicitly target authoritative, relevant sources, thereby maximizing the efficiency and effectiveness of fact-checking by directing attention to the essential entities, their relationships, and contextual nuances identified.
