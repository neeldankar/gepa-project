1. `new_notes`:

   - Extract or synthesize these from the provided `notes` prioritized by strong support from the `context`.
   - Each note should be a standalone factual statement with no speculative or unsupported content.
   - Remove duplicate or contradictory information.
   - Explicitly exclude misinformation or inaccuracies present in the claim based on evidence.
   - When multiple similar facts appear, merge or select the clearest and most context-supported version.
   - Clarify any ambiguity or disambiguate entities/facts if multiple similar entities exist (e.g., distinguishing solo albums versus band albums, specific airports versus similarly named airports, nationality versus ethnic background).
   - Focus on relationships or factual assertions relevant to verifying the claim, such as:
       * Organizational headquarters or locations,
       * Biographical data (e.g., name, nationality, career span),
       * Specific event outcomes or timelines (e.g., winner of a season, date ranges),
       * Roles and professions (e.g., confirming if a person is a film director or actor associated with a film),
       * Clarification of entity attributes or involvement (e.g., who directed a particular film),
       * Avoid introducing new facts not supported by notes/context.

2. `titles`:
   - Provide a minimal, short list of document or article titles from the `context` that most authoritatively cover the facts used in `new_notes`.
   - Titles should closely correspond to the specific subjects/entities central to the claim and notes—not generic or tangential sources.
   - If multiple documents cover key facts, include all relevant titles to ensure completeness.
   - The goal is to identify the key sources justifying the factual synthesis in `new_notes`, to enable verification of the output.
   - Avoid inferring or fabricating document titles; use titles as given or clearly derivable from the context data.

Domain-specific considerations and best practices:

- Claims often focus on verifying precise factual relationships. Pay attention to details such as:
    * Geographical location hierarchies (city, state, country),
    * Temporal ranges (e.g., years active),
    * Correct identity matching in cases of similar names,
    * Attribution of creative works (film directors, album credits),
    * Sporting event outcomes including season numbering and competitor participation.

- Use the `context` to validate and disambiguate the information in `notes`. Discard facts in notes unsupported or contradicted by context.

- Carefully avoid carrying forward misinformation from the claim text. If a claim element is unsupported or contradicted, explicitly exclude or correct it in `new_notes`.

- Where notes contain redundant or repeated facts, distill them into the most clear, accurate, and supported statements.

- Summarize your reasoning implicitly in the structure and content of `new_notes` by choosing and phrasing evidence carefully.

- Never introduce personal opinions, guesswork, or unverifiable information.

- Each output `new_notes` entry should be brief yet sufficiently informative for verifying the claim's validity.

- The quality of your output is judged on factual accuracy, clarity, non-redundancy, and appropriate source referencing through `titles`.

Example general strategy:

- Identify key factual elements of the claim requiring verification.

- Locate corresponding facts in notes that address these elements.

- Confirm these facts against the detailed `context` documents.

- Remove duplicates and contradictions from notes, prioritizing contextual support.

- Write clear, standalone factual sentences for `new_notes`.

- Select document titles from the `context` best representing those facts as `titles`.

By following this methodology and the above considerations, produce a factual, precise, and well-documented validation or refutation of the claim using the given notes and context.
