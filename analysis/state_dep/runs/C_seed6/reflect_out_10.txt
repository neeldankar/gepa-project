You will be given three inputs: 

1. **claim** – A statement or assertion that potentially involves one or more entities or facts.
2. **notes** – A list of factual sentences or observations relevant to the claim. These notes summarize or extract information from source documents and contain important facts that support or refute aspects of the claim.
3. **context** – Full textual descriptions or extended summaries of multiple entities or topics related to the claim. These context passages often come from encyclopedia-style or detailed reference sources and provide background, definitions, timelines, and supporting information.

Your task is to process these inputs and produce two outputs:

- **new_notes**: A refined, concise set of factual sentences distilled from the input *notes* that are directly relevant to verifying or understanding the claim, while removing redundant, contradictory, or irrelevant details. Emphasize clarity, factual correctness, and alignment with the evidence presented in the context. The new notes should focus on the main factual points supporting or contradicting the claim and clarify temporal, causal, or definitional relationships crucial for evaluating the claim.

- **titles**: A list of the names or titles of the key entities, topics, or documents referenced both in the notes and context that are essential for understanding or providing evidence for the claim. This list should be comprehensive with respect to the main factual components and should prioritize retrieval of gold-standard supporting documents or key concepts mentioned explicitly or implicitly in the claim and notes.

When forming your response, use the following reasoning strategy learned from the examples:

- Analyze the *claim* carefully and identify the key entities, relationships, and claims needing verification.
- Examine the *notes* to find factual data points—such as release dates, roles, institutional statuses, or popularity metrics—that bear directly on the claim.
- Reference the *context* to confirm details, clarify ambiguities, and supplement or validate the notes.
- Detect and resolve any contradictions or inaccuracies emerging from notes or context, especially regarding timelines, roles, or definitions.
- Summarize or consolidate the notes to reflect the accurate timeline or facts that confirm or refute the claim, avoiding unnecessary repetition.
- Extract and output the minimal set of key titles/entities that genuinely explain or support the reasoning.

Additional domain-specific details you should consider during the task:

- For claims about films, actors, or media, verify release years, casting, and the order of releases.
- For academic or institutional claims, confirm founding figures, university statuses (e.g., public or private), and historical name changes.
- For games and designers, distinguish between niche popularity, fanbase scope, and global recognition levels.
- Use temporal order and contextual knowledge to check claims about sequences (e.g., which movie released first).
- When any conflicting note suggests one thing while the reliable context clarifies otherwise, rely primarily on the authoritative context.
- Always output notes and titles that directly address the claim; do not include tangential or loosely related information.
- Ensure to cover all significant entities that appear in the claim and evidence, as failure to retrieve even one relevant title may result in incomplete or incorrect conclusions.

In sum, your output must be a carefully curated evidence summary (new_notes) and an exhaustive, relevant entity list (titles) that together provide a firm factual foundation for assessing the input claim.
