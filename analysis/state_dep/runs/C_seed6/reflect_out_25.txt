Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a single `query` field consisting of a precise, focused natural-language question or logically linked questions specifically designed to verify or investigate the factual accuracy of the claim using the information provided in the notes. The query is intended to guide efficient retrieval of authoritative evidence that can directly support or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise factual or relational statement involving one or more named entities (e.g., persons, works, organizations, locations, events) and their attributes or relationships requiring validation.
- `notes`: A list of relevant text snippets, facts, or contextual excerpts that provide supplemental, clarifying, or partial information about the claim’s key entities or relationships.

Output Format:
- `query`: A natural language question or set of logically connected questions explicitly targeting the verification of the claim’s core factual components, refined through disambiguation and contextual clues from the notes. The query should maximize retrieval of relevant documents or facts that are authoritative and directly applicable for confirming or refuting the claim.

Detailed Instructions and Considerations:

1. Entity and Relationship Identification
   - Identify the main named entities in the claim (e.g., individuals, films, songs, organizations, locations).
   - Determine the key factual relationships or claims to be verified (e.g., authorship, original recording artist, company nationality and location, participation or role in events or works, native habitat, temporal aspects like dates or lifespan overlap).
   - Distinguish attributes such as genre, chart rankings, headquarters location, filmography credits, or temporal facts relevant to the claim.

2. Utilizing Notes for Context, Disambiguation, and Constraints
   - Analyze notes to supplement, clarify, or restrict the scope of verification: e.g., correct disambiguation of locations, multiple individuals with similar names, temporal anchoring, or classification of entities (e.g., distinguishing a magazine from a periodical).
   - Identify any contradictions or confirmatory facts in the notes pertinent to the claim.
   - Use notes to recognize subtleties such as: 
     • Whether a named artist’s song actually reached the claimed chart position.
     • Identification of the first recorded artist versus later performers.
     • Corporate nationality vs. headquarters location.
     • Overlapping involvement of persons in works or roles.
     • Classification nuances (e.g., magazines as periodicals).
     • Exact dates and sequencing (e.g., first composition credits).
   
3. Query Content and Scope
   - Formulate queries that explicitly verify each key factual component of the claim:
     • Confirm existence and identity of entities and relationships.
     • Clarify relevant temporal facts (e.g., which came first, overlap in dates).
     • Establish jurisdictional or geographic facts (e.g., company headquarters locations versus nationality).
     • Confirm classification or genre when relevant (e.g., music genre, publication type).
   - Where multiple entities or relational claims exist, logically connect questions to jointly verify combined facts.
   - Address any potential contradictions or ambiguities by phrasing queries that seek to resolve them clearly.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions; maintain focus on critical verification targets.

4. Query Quality and Precision
   - Keep queries concise, clear, and specific to the claim’s factual core.
   - Use natural-language phrasing suitable for retrieval/search systems to locate key evidence or documents efficiently.
   - Explicitly include the key entities and relevant temporal or attribute qualifiers to refine retrieval.

5. Domain-Specific and Niche Knowledge Integrated from Examples
   - For music or discography-related claims: verify chart positions, original recording artist identity, genre classification, and authorship dates.
   - For film and entertainment industry claims: verify composer credits, acting roles, sequence of works, and collaborative roles.
   - For aviation or corporate claims: verify people’s roles (e.g., captain), airline/company nationality, headquarters location, and possible contradictions in nationality versus location.
   - For geographic or political claims: verify administrative divisions, geographic jurisdiction, and compare geographic locations of entities.
   - Recognize periodicals as including magazines and clarify classification when relevant.
   - Understand temporal sequencing, such as first works or overlapping lifespans, to check plausibility of relationships.

6. Generalizable Strategy
   - Extract and prioritize verification of the claim’s critical facts.
   - Use the notes to confirm, disambiguate, or provide necessary qualifiers.
   - Formulate a minimal, precise, and linked set of natural language questions that cover all relevant factual facets needed for verification.
   - Target the query design to maximize the likelihood of retrieving gold-standard or key authoritative documents.

Summary:
Produce a focused, well-formed natural language query or linked queries that explicitly seek to confirm or refute the factual claims in the input `claim` by leveraging contextual and disambiguating details found in the `notes`. The query must be narrowly tailored to retrieve the essential evidence for verification, incorporating domain-specific nuances and adhering to precision and clarity best practices demonstrated in the example feedback.
