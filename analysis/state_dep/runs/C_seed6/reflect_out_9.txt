Task Description:  
You will receive two inputs: a `claim` and a set of `notes`. Your task is to generate a single concise, focused `query` — a natural language question or logically linked set of questions — designed specifically to verify or investigate the factual accuracy of the claim by leveraging the information contained in the notes. The query should guide retrieval of authoritative, relevant evidence that can either support or refute the claim as efficiently and precisely as possible.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A brief, factual or relational statement involving one or more entities (people, places, organizations, works, events, etc.), their attributes, or their relationships that require verification.  
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets providing background details, contextual information, partial facts, or clarifications about the entities or relationships mentioned in the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A targeted, natural language question or set of clearly linked questions explicitly addressing the key entities and factual claims in the claim, refined and disambiguated using the contextual information in the notes. The query must be optimized to retrieve critical documents or facts for claimant verification.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. Entity and Relation Identification  
   - Extract the main named entities from the claim (persons, bands, companies, places, events, publications, etc.).  
   - Identify the specific relationships or factual assertions made (e.g., authorship, membership, sponsorship, geographic location, temporal overlap, participation, classification).  

2. Use of the Notes for Context and Disambiguation  
   - Carefully analyze the notes to gather supplemental details such as specific dates, locations, organizational affiliations, definitions, or clarifications to disambiguate entities or relationships.  
   - Use the notes to identify and resolve ambiguous or conflicting information (e.g., multiple entities with the same or similar names, geographic subdivisions like cities vs. counties, time frames for events or lifespans).  
   - Recognize confirmations or contradictions within the notes regarding the claim’s components, and use these to sharpen the focus of the query.

3. Query Content and Scope  
   - Formulate queries that explicitly target verification of the core factual elements of the claim, including:  
     • Existence and nature of relationships (e.g., was a person a member of a band? Did a company sponsor a specific event?)  
     • Precise attributes or temporal facts (e.g., dates related to authorship, tenure, event occurrence, or geographic jurisdiction)  
     • Classification or category confirmation (e.g., is a source a magazine, a periodical, or another media type).  
   - When multiple entities or related claims exist, link queries logically to cover relevant comparative or relational facts (e.g., did two persons serve simultaneously? Are two entities located within the same geographic area?).  
   - Where contradictory or ambiguous points arise in the notes, explicitly include questions aimed at resolving those uncertainties.

4. Query Quality and Precision  
   - Keep queries concise, clear, and narrowly focused solely on retrieving evidence required for verifying the claim. Avoid overly broad, vague, or multi-topic questions.  
   - Utilize natural-language phrasing optimized for retrieval systems to find authoritative and relevant documents or facts.  
   - Mention key entities with relevant attributes, dates, or locations whenever possible to maximize precision in retrieval.

5. Niche and Domain-Specific Knowledge & Examples  
   - For claims about authorship or publication: focus on verifying exact authorship attribution, publication years, and the classification/type of publication (e.g., understanding magazines as periodicals).  
   - For natural history or biology-oriented claims: focus on verifying native habitat with precise ecological terms (arid, semi-desert, temperate).  
   - For temporal overlap claims: clarify exact lifespans or periods of activity of persons vs. event or creation dates to check feasibility.  
   - For entertainment industry or sports claims: verify specific persons’ participation or membership, temporal spans of involvement, and relationships to events or roles (e.g., sports team rosters, competition categories).  
   - For geographic and political jurisdiction claims: verify administrative divisions to confirm if entities or events belong within defined geographic or political borders (e.g., city within county, country).  
   - Recognize synonyms or overlapping categories (e.g., magazines as periodicals) to enhance retrieval coverage.

6. Generalizable Strategy  
   - Extract and prioritize the claim’s key factual assertions for verification.  
   - Use the notes to disambiguate all entities and relationships, resolve conflicts and provide refining context.  
   - Construct a minimal set of precise, logically connected natural language questions that explicitly target key verification points.  
   - This approach maximizes retrieval of ‘gold’ or core documents essential for evidence-based verification or refutation.

Summary:  
Generate a well-structured, fact-focused natural language query or set of linked queries tailored explicitly to verify the claim by leveraging all relevant and clarifying information in the notes. Ensure queries are concise, precise, and structured so that retrieval systems can efficiently find necessary authoritative evidence to confirm or deny the claim’s factual components.
