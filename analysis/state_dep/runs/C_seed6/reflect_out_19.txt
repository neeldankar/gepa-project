Task Description:

You are given three input fields: 
- `claim`: a factual or semi-factual statement that typically mentions entities (people, places, organizations, media titles, etc.) and relationships or events between them.
- `notes`: a list of evidential or explanatory sentences derived from or about the claim, providing affirmations, contradictions, clarifications, or contextual information about the claim’s components.
- `context`: a list of background reference documents or summaries related to entities and topics relevant to the claim and notes.

Your objective is to produce two output fields:  
- `new_notes`: a concise, accurate, and well-justified synthesis of the key evidential points from the `notes` that relate directly to verifying or refuting parts of the claim. The `new_notes` should remove redundancy, discard irrelevant or unverified details, highlight any contradictions or affirmations discovered, and emphasize whether certain claim components are correct, incorrect, or unverifiable within the given context.  
- `titles`: a list of document or entity titles from the `context` that are critical to supporting or refuting the claim, or that best represent the main topics or entities involved in your reasoning.

Key points and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Fact Verification via Cross-Referencing**: The task requires rigorous cross-referencing of claim contents against notes and the context to evaluate factual correctness, entity associations, timelines, and relationships. When an element of the claim does not match the context or notes, denote this clearly in the `new_notes`.

2. **Entity Disambiguation and Identification**: Claims often mention entities such as actors, schools, films, or organizations, along with specifics like dates, genres, or locations. Use the context to confirm these entity identities, affiliations, and attributes accurately. For example:
   - Confirm exact film titles, release years, cast, and production/distribution companies.
   - Confirm membership and composition of organizations or districts.
   - Confirm correct timelines of events related to individuals/entities.

3. **Temporal Reasoning**: When claims involve sequences or timing (e.g., a person performing an action after a certain event), verify the chronology based on the years or dates given in the context and notes.

4. **Handling Negative Evidence and Non-Confirmations**: Explicitly state when information in the claim cannot be verified or is negated by the context or notes, e.g., “Meryl Streep did not star in the referenced film,” or “No confirmation that the film was produced by that company in the given documents.”

5. **Concise, Relevant Output**: The `new_notes` should succinctly represent the verified core facts, corrections, and unresolved points; avoid verbatim repetition of input notes unless clarifying or summarizing key evidence. The `titles` output should include main related documents or entities that support the reasoning and help retrieval or further validation.

6. **Domain Examples and Terms**: Be aware of domain-specific references such as:
   - Film industry terms: film titles (including year), cast, director, production companies.
   - Television terminology: series and season titles, premiere dates.
   - Educational institutions: school district composition, school names, locations, and demographics.
   - Geographic terms: census-designated places, hamlets, counties.
   Use these to enhance precision in verification and output notes.

7. **Generalizable Reasoning Approach**:
   - Parse the claim to identify all entities and relations.
   - Extract and group relevant notes and context snippets referencing these entities.
   - Identify consistencies and contradictions.
   - Note any missing or unverifiable claims.
   - Summarize evidence to confirm or refute claim components.
   - Select titles that relate directly to key entities and factual points.

Deliverable:

- For each input claim and supporting inputs, produce:
  - `new_notes`: a focused, verified, and synthesized set of notes clarifying the factual status of the claim parts.
  - `titles`: a list of key context article or document titles essential for supporting or disputing the claim.

This structured approach ensures factual validation, precise entity matching, and clear summarization of evidence to support accurate claim assessment.
