Task Description:
You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a single `query` field that formulates a precise, concise, and focused natural language question or linked set of questions aimed specifically at verifying or investigating the factual accuracy of the claim using the contextual and factual information found in the notes. This query will be used to retrieve relevant evidence that can support or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise factual or relational statement involving specific entities, relationships, or attributes whose truthfulness or details need to be checked.
- `notes`: A list of text snippets, facts, or excerpts that contain relevant factual information, clarifications, or contextual background about the entities, relationships, or events mentioned in the claim. The notes may include domain-specific details such as scientific classifications, geopolitical jurisdictions, film credits, musical collaborations, or other specialized knowledge about entity roles or attributes.

Output Format:
- `query`: A natural language question or linked questions explicitly targeting the key entities and relationships highlighted or implied by the claim and refined by the notes. The query should be formulated to retrieve authoritative or “gold standard” documents or evidence relevant to verifying the claim’s truthfulness.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Entity and Relationship Identification**  
   - Extract the key factual components from the claim, including entities (people, places, organizations, species, works of art, bands, shows) and relationships (jurisdiction, membership, professional roles, coining terminology, number of species, award comparisons, film or music credits).
   
2. **Contextual Disambiguation Using Notes**  
   - Use the notes to clarify, disambiguate, or augment the identities and relationships in the claim. For example:  
     - Confirm the exact scientific names or genus associated with common names (e.g., “Saule” is French for “willow” in genus Salix).  
     - Verify jurisdictional or geopolitical relationships (e.g., administrative boundaries).  
     - Disambiguate film and music credits based on time, familial relationships, or production company distinctions.  
     - Identify specific individuals in colloquial or nickname references (e.g., “German Frank Sinatra” refers to Udo Jürgens).  
     - Clarify professional roles and collaborations to test shared professions or memberships.  
     - Validate award counts or comparative prominence where noted or implied.  

3. **Verification of Critical Facts and Contradictions**  
   - Frame queries that explicitly target disputed, confirmatory, or critical elements of the claim, such as:  
     - Does a specific entity hold the claimed attribute or profession?  
     - Is a named person a member or judge on a particular panel or show?  
     - Are two entities related via a particular relationship (e.g., “coined term with,” “colleague,” “band member”)?  
     - Numeric or comparative relationships like counts of species or awards.  

4. **Combining Multiple Entities or Relations**  
   - When the claim involves multiple interrelated facts (e.g., two cities’ jurisdiction and country membership, two people sharing a profession, or multiple band members’ involvements), structure the query to encompass all relevant verifications together in logically connected questions to maximize retrieval relevance.  

5. **Query Style and Scope**  
   - Keep queries clear, direct, and specific so that retrieved documents are likely to provide definitive verification.  
   - Avoid vague, overly broad, or generalized questions unrelated to the entities and relationships at hand.  
   - Include temporal context if relevant to disambiguate (e.g., the year of a film, or the era of a music collaboration).  
   - Reflect known contradictions or confirmations in the notes by phrasing the query to test those critical points explicitly.  

6. **Domain Examples**  
   - Taxonomy: Query genus-species comparisons and confirm associations between common names and scientific genera.  
   - Entertainment: Query filmography, judge panels of shows, band membership, and professional affiliations.  
   - Awards: Query comparative award counts or recognitions associated with nicknames or stage personas.  
   - Academic: Query professional titles and collaborations (e.g., researchers who coined terms together).  

Generalizable Strategy:

- Carefully parse the claim to extract each discrete factual assertion or relationship.  
- Cross-reference the notes to gain clarity, resolve ambiguity, establish relevant background, and identify contradictions or confirmations related to these assertions.  
- Formulate one or more natural language questions that explicitly mention the entities and relationships involved, refined by facts or contradictions evident in the notes, designed to retrieve key and authoritative documents or information sources to verify or refute the claim.

Summary:

Produce a focused, entity- and relationship-specific, natural language query or linked queries that explicitly address the main factual points and discrepancies in the claim, utilizing the contextual information from the notes to refine and clarify the search intent. The final query should direct retrieval toward the most relevant evidence needed to confirm or dispute the claim’s truth.
