Task Title: Formulation of Precise Verification Queries from Claims and Contextual Notes

Task Overview:  
You will be provided with a factual claim and a set of background notes related to that claim. Your objective is to construct a single, targeted natural language query (or a logically linked set of queries) designed specifically to verify or investigate the truthfulness of the claim, using the contextual details provided in the notes to disambiguate entities, resolve contradictions, and focus on key factual elements. The query should maximize retrieval of authoritative, relevant evidence that can confirm or refute the claim efficiently.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A concise statement putting forward a factual or relational assertion involving one or more entities (persons, locations, organizations, works of art or media, biological species, etc.), their attributes, or relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets or facts related to the claim, providing background, clarifications, partial evidence, or possible contradictions.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A well-formed, precise, and concise natural language question or set of linked questions. These queries should explicitly target the verification of core factual components of the claim, tightened and refined based on the information and contextual cues drawn from the notes.

Detailed Task Instructions:

1. **Entity and Relation Identification**  
   - Identify all key named entities from the claim, such as persons, geographic locations, organizations, events, creative works (films, books, TV shows), biological species, and roles or titles.  
   - Extract the factual or relational claims made between those entities, for example: authorship, direction, acting roles, membership, native habitat, jurisdictional boundaries, date/time overlaps, production or publication details.

2. **Utilizing Notes for Disambiguation and Context**  
   - Analyze notes for supplemental facts, including dates (years, time periods), relationships, clarifications about identities or titles, geographic or political jurisdictions, or classifications (e.g., distinguishing types of publications—magazines vs. periodicals).  
   - Detect any disambiguation needs arising from ambiguous names, multiple entities with similar names, or overlapping timelines or roles.  
   - Identify contradictions, confirmations, or insufficient evidence highlighted in the notes about the claim’s specifics.  
   - Use the notes to apply domain-specific knowledge for narrowing scope and clarifying ambiguous points.

3. **Domain-Specific Verification Focus Areas**  
   a. **Film and Media Claims:**  
      - Confirm directors, writers, actors, and their roles in specific works and years.  
      - Clarify overlapping roles (e.g., co-writing vs. sole authorship).  
      - Check temporal details to confirm contemporaneity or timeline accuracy.  
      - Verify professional background and media industry affiliation.  
   
   b. **Book Authorship and Publication Claims:**  
      - Verify author attribution, publication medium type, and publication dates.  
      - Confirm classification of the publication (e.g., magazine considered a periodical).  
   
   c. **Biological/Natural History Claims:**  
      - Confirm native habitat classifications (specific biomes or regions such as arid, semi-desert, temperate).  
   
   d. **Geographical and Political Jurisdiction Claims:**  
      - Verify administrative divisions (e.g., city within a province or country).  
      - Check if multiple entities share jurisdiction or classification.  
   
   e. **Temporal Overlap Claims:**  
      - Check lifespans, periods active, or timelines of events and entity participation to confirm concurrency or sequence.

4. **Query Formulation Principles**  
   - Queries should explicitly refer to the key entities and relationships identified.  
   - Formulate concise, logically connected questions that collectively cover all major factual elements of the claim.  
   - Prioritize clarity and focus on retrieving definitive evidence to support or refute each factual component.  
   - Where notes suggest ambiguity or challenge parts of the claim, phrase queries to explicitly seek resolution.  
   - Avoid queries that are overly broad, vague, or encompassing irrelevant details.  
   - Use natural language phrasing that can be effectively interpreted by retrieval systems to target authoritative documents or facts.

5. **Generalizable Strategy**  
   - Extract and prioritize verification of the claim’s core factual points.  
   - Disambiguate and contextualize all key entities and relations using the notes.  
   - Explicitly separate multiple sub-claims into linked queries for clear retrieval targets.  
   - Incorporate any temporal, geographic, role-based, or classification detail provided in the notes to refine and narrow the query scope.  
   - Identify if the notes confirm, contradict, or lack sufficient information, and focus queries accordingly to verify or disprove the claim.

Summary:  
Produce a single, well-structured, focused natural-language query or minimal set of linked questions that explicitly address the essential entities and relationships presented in the claim, enriched and disambiguated by the background notes. The query should maximize the effectiveness of retrieval systems in locating authoritative evidence that directly confirms or refutes the claim’s validity, leveraging all relevant domain knowledge and contextual insights from the notes.
