Task Overview:  
You will receive two types of inputs: a `claim` and a set of `notes`. Your role is to generate a single `query` field consisting of one or more tightly linked natural language question(s) that explicitly aim to verify or investigate the factual content of the claim by extracting and using critical contextual details found in the notes. The purpose of the query is to facilitate efficient retrieval of authoritative evidence that can support, refute, or clarify the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A concise factual or relational statement involving one or more entities (persons, works, locations, events, etc.) and the factual relationships or attributes asserted about them (like authorship, event outcomes, native habitat, genre classification, etc.).  
- `notes`: A list of text excerpts, facts, or partial information providing background, clarifications, temporal or geographic details, or confirmations/contradictions relating to the entities and relations mentioned in the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A focused, precise natural language question or set of logically connected questions explicitly focused on verifying the claim’s factual substance, as refined and disambiguated by the notes.

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Entity and Relationship Extraction**  
   - Identify all key named entities in the claim, including persons, places, organizations, creative works (films, books, performances), species, events, or other proper nouns.  
   - Extract the primary factual claims or relationships described involving these entities, such as authorship, participation, defeat/competition results, genre classification, native habitats, temporal overlaps, or administrative jurisdictions.

2. **Leveraging Notes for Context and Disambiguation**  
   - Use the notes to gather supplementary information relevant for scope refinement, disambiguation, or temporal/geographical contextualization. For example:  
     • Identifying exact individuals referenced (in case of multiple entities with similar names)  
     • Determining production years, event dates, or lifespan dates  
     • Clarifying definitions or classifications (e.g., whether a publication counts as a periodical, or genre classifications of films)  
     • Noting if any contradictions or confirmations exist that affect how the claim should be queried  
     • Incorporating known affiliations or locations to refine geographic or jurisdictional questions.

3. **Focus and Content of the Query**  
   - Construct questions that directly target the main factual elements necessary to verify the claim, such as:  
     • The identity of a person or entity involved in a described event (e.g., who was defeated by whom, who starred in what)  
     • Attributes or temporal facts relevant to the entities (e.g., when someone started competing, exact event dates, etc.)  
     • Classification or genre designation of works or entities (e.g., is a film a family comedy adventure, is a magazine considered a periodical)  
     • Relationships between entities (e.g., are two entities both sportsmen, do two places lie in the same administrative division)  
   - Where multiple factual claims or entities are included, formulate questions that systematically cover all key aspects, using linked or logically connected phrasing (e.g., "Did X and Y both star in Z? Did X begin sports at age A?").

4. **Quality and Precision**  
   - Keep queries clear, concise, and narrowly targeted to maximize retrieval of relevant and authoritative supporting evidence. Avoid vague or overly broad questions.  
   - Use natural language phrasing easily interpretable by search or retrieval systems.  
   - Explicitly mention key entities and relevant attributes or temporal factors uncovered in the notes to minimize ambiguity.  
   - If the notes reveal potential conflicting evidence or ambiguities, formulate queries to explicitly resolve these (e.g., "Who exactly starred in X?" where multiple possible persons are mentioned).

5. **Domain-Specific and Niche Knowledge to Consider**  
   - For claims about sports or competitions: verify identities of competitors, match results (e.g., who defeated whom), starting ages, and professional status.  
   - For claims about creative or entertainment works (films, performances, books): verify involvement of named actors or creators, production or release dates, genre classifications, and location of performances/events.  
   - For claims about publications: confirm authorship, publication medium, publication year, and whether a publication is a periodical or magazine. Note that magazines are considered a type of periodical.  
   - For biology or natural history claims: verify native habitats with attention to terrain type (arid, semi-desert, temperate, etc.) and confirm species-related facts.  
   - For geographic or political claims: verify administrative jurisdictions, subdivisions, and whether multiple entities share the same geographic or political area.  
   - For temporal overlap claims: verify lifespans or active periods to confirm whether persons/entities overlapped in time relevant to the claim (e.g., whether someone was alive when a work was created).  
   
6. **Generalizable Strategy**  
   - Prioritize extraction and verification of the core factual elements from the claim.  
   - Use the notes exhaustively to clarify, disambiguate, and contextualize entities and relationships before formulating queries.  
   - Formulate a minimal yet complete set of linked natural language questions covering all relevant verification points, structured to lead to retrieval of ‘gold-standard’ authoritative documents or facts that decisively address the claim.

Example Patterns:  
- "Who did [Person A] defeat in the [Event, Year]? Are both [Person A's opponent] and [Person B] recognized as sportsmen? Did [Person B] begin competing at age [X]?"  
- "Did English actor [Person] star in the stage performance '[Performance]' at [Venue] and also star in the film '[Film Title]' (Year/genre)?"  
- "Are both the Disney animated film featuring character [Character Name] and the film [Film Title] American-produced family comedy action adventure movies? In which film does [Character Name] appear?"  
- "Is [Publication] considered a periodical or magazine? Did [Author] author [Work] published by [Publisher] in [Year]?"

Remember: The goal is to produce precise, focused, and well-formed natural language queries explicitly designed to maximize retrieval of core evidence needed to verify or refute the factual claim, incorporating all relevant disambiguation and contextual details from the notes.
