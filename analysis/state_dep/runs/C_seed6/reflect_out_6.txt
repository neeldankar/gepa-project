Task Description:
You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single `query` field that formulates a precise, focused natural language question or linked questions designed specifically to verify or investigate the claim by using the information contained in the notes. The query should be constructed to guide retrieval of relevant evidence that can support or refute the claim efficiently.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise factual or relational statement describing one or more entities, their attributes, or relationships that need verification.
- `notes`: A list of text snippets, facts, or excerpts providing background knowledge, contextual details, or partial information about the entities or relationships mentioned in the claim.

Output Format:
- `query`: A targeted natural language question or set of logically connected questions that explicitly focus on the key entities and relationships present in the claim, refined and disambiguated using the information found in the notes. The query should be composed to maximize retrieval of relevant, authoritative documents or facts.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:
1. Entity and Relation Identification  
   - Extract the key named entities from the claim (e.g., persons, locations, organizations, events, works such as books or films).  
   - Identify the relationships or factual claims being made (e.g., authorship, native habitat, time-overlapping lifespans, membership, jurisdiction, participation in an event).  

2. Use of the Notes for Context and Disambiguation  
   - Analyze the notes to gather supplemental facts such as dates, places, definitions, or known associations that clarify or restrict the scope of verification.  
   - Use notes to determine if any terms or entities require disambiguation (e.g., multiple entities with similar names, geographic subdivisions, temporal context).  
   - Identify if the notes reveal confirmations, contradictions, or insufficient evidence regarding key aspects of the claim.

3. Query Content and Scope  
   - Formulate a query that explicitly targets verification of the claim’s core factual components focusing on:  
     • Existence of relationships (e.g., was Person A a creator of Film B?)  
     • Specific attributes or temporal facts (e.g., dates of authorship or lifespan overlap)  
     • Jurisdiction or classification details (e.g., is a city within a given province, are two magazines both periodicals).  
   - Where multiple entities or relationships are involved, structure the query to cover combined or comparative facts, for example:  
     • Are both entities geographically related?  
     • Did an individual and another related individual serve simultaneously in a role?  
   - Incorporate any contradictions or ambiguous points noted, by phrasing the query so that it explicitly seeks resolution of those points.

4. Query Quality and Precision  
   - Keep queries concise, clear, and focused solely on retrieving the critical evidence necessary for claim verification. Avoid vague or overly broad questions.  
   - Use natural-language phrasing that a search or retrieval system can effectively interpret to find relevant documents.  
   - Ensure queries mention key entities with relevant attributes or temporal markers wherever applicable.  

5. Generalizable Strategy  
   - Extract and prioritize verification of key factual claims from the input claim.  
   - Review the notes carefully for background and possible contradictions or confirmations.  
   - Identify and disambiguate entities and relationships using the notes.  
   - Formulate a minimal set of precise, clearly linked natural language questions covering all relevant aspects of the claim to aid retrieval of authoritative evidence.

Niche and Domain-Specific Knowledge Captured from Examples:
- For claims relating to book authorship, publication medium, and dates: verify authorship attribution, publication year, and the classification/type of publication (e.g., magazine versus periodical).  
- For natural history or biology claims involving species and native habitats: verify native habitat specifically, focusing on whether it is arid, semi-desert, temperate, or otherwise.  
- For temporal overlap claims concerning individuals (e.g., creators and historical figures), clarify exact lifespans or alive periods and cross-check with creation/release dates of works or events.  
- For entertainment industry claims about film creators or judges on a show: identify exact persons associated with a work or role, their temporal involvement, and membership status if relevant.  
- For geographic or political jurisdiction claims: verify administrative divisions and whether two or more entities lie within the same jurisdiction or country.  
- Recognize periodicals as including magazines, so queries should clarify if entities classified as periodicals are indeed periodicals by definition.

Summary:
Generate a well-formed, fact-focused natural language query or set of linked queries explicitly tailored to verify the factual claims in the input by leveraging all relevant contextual and disambiguating details found in the notes. Structure queries to maximize the chance of retrieving the ‘gold’ or key documents essential for verifying or refuting the claim, according to the examples and feedback.
