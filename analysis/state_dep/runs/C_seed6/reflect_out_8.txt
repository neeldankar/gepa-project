Task Description:
You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to create a precise, fact-focused `query` field that is a natural language question or set of linked questions aimed at verifying or investigating the truthfulness, details, or relationships within the `claim` by using the context provided in the `notes`.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise factual or relational statement involving one or more named entities (such as people, places, organizations, works, events) and their attributes or relationships. The claim may involve checking membership, jurisdiction, authorship, chronology, or other defined relationships.
- `notes`: A collection of text snippets, factual statements, or excerpts relevant to the entities and facts mentioned in the claim. These notes provide additional context, background, or potential contradictions to help formulate a focused retrieval query.

Output Format:
- `query`: One or more clear, concise natural language questions that explicitly reference the key entities and relationships in the claim and are designed to retrieve relevant evidence to support or refute the claim. The query should be narrowly targeted to the elements most critical for verifying the claim’s truthfulness.

Detailed Task Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:
1. Identify key factual components in the claim:
   - Extract named entities (e.g., places, people, organizations, works, dates).
   - Determine key relationships or attributes to verify (e.g., membership in a group, administrative jurisdiction, awards won, role in a work).
2. Use the notes to understand the relevant domain context, including:
   - Geopolitical hierarchies or jurisdictional boundaries (e.g., towns within shires, cities within provinces and countries).
   - Temporal sequencing of events or releases (e.g., membership status relative to DVD or album release dates).
   - Specific roles of individuals in creative works (e.g., actor roles, producer/singer credits, jury membership on TV shows).
   - Disambiguate entities with similar names or resolve potential contradictions.
3. Reflect in the query any conflicting or missing information in the notes that are crucial to affirm or deny parts of the claim. Queries should explicitly:
   - Confirm if an individual/entity held a particular role or membership at a specified time.
   - Verify if one location is administratively governed by another location or governing body.
   - Verify provenance or authorship and associated awards if relevant.
4. When multiple entities or claims are involved, structure the query to check combined relationships:
   - E.g., Is a person both a member of a group and a contributor to a work?
   - Are two places in the same administrative or national boundaries?
   - Are all listed judges or participants officially part of a panel, including disputed or additional members?
5. The query should be narrowly scoped but inclusive enough to fetch all key documents relevant to the claim’s verification, avoiding broad or vague phrasing.
6. Utilize temporal qualifiers or disambiguation where necessary to separate entities or events occurring at different times or with overlapping names.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Parse the claim into its core factual components focusing on entities and relationships.
- Analyze the notes to capture context, confirm or question temporal sequences, membership statuses, geographic jurisdictions, or role assignments.
- Construct concise, answerable natural language questions explicitly mentioning the critical entities and relationships in the claim.
- Integrate relevant disambiguation and temporal or jurisdictional context from the notes to refine the query.
- Formulate the query to explicitly target the primary documents or facts that would provide a definitive answer to the claim, facilitating retrieval of the ‘gold’ or key documents indicated in feedback examples.

Examples (illustrative only):
- For a claim about membership and release timing: "Did [person] leave [group] before the release of [work]? When did [person] leave [group], and when was [work] released?"
- For a claim about administrative location and proximity: "Is [town] administered by [local council]? Is [town] located near [geographic feature] and [national park]? What is the spatial relationship among them?"
- For claims about award recipients and authorship: "Who is the [nationality] poet-diplomat who won the Nobel Prize in Literature? Is this person recognized as a writer? Is [other person] also recognized as a writer?"

Summary:
Your output should be well-formed, focused queries designed to retrieve documents or information that best verify the presence, status, or relationship of key entities and facts in the claim, guided by the contextual information in the notes. These queries enable evidence-based verification and should be customized for each task according to domain-specific nuances like geopolitical hierarchy, timeline, membership, and role identity.
