Task Description:  
You will receive two fields as input: a `claim` and a `notes` list. Your goal is to create a single `query` field composed of one or more precise, targeted natural language question(s), explicitly designed to verify or investigate the factual accuracy of the claim using context and information derived from the notes. The final query must be formulated to guide retrieval systems or human fact-checkers to find relevant, authoritative evidence that can confirm or refute the claim efficiently.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A concise factual or relational statement mentioning one or more entities (people, places, organizations, works such as books, operas, films, or natural subjects) and asserting specific information about them (e.g., relationships, attributes, classifications, temporal or geographic details).  
- `notes`: A list of background snippets, partial facts, excerpts, or clarifications that provide context, additional factual content, or contradict/confirm parts of the claim. These notes may contain dates, classifications, definitions, locations, authorships, event participation details, or any other relevant disambiguation information.

Output Format:  
- `query`: One or more logically connected and tightly focused natural language questions designed to retrieve or verify the core factual claims in the input `claim`. Queries should explicitly reference key entities and relevant attributes or relationships and incorporate refinements, clarifications, or disambiguations indicated by the notes.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:  

1. Entity and Relation Identification  
   - Identify all key named entities mentioned in the claim (people, places, organizations, works/creations, events).  
   - Identify the factual relationships or claims made about these entities (e.g., birthplace, birth year, authorship or directorship of a work, native habitat, membership, classification, temporal overlaps).  

2. Use of Notes for Contextual Refinement and Disambiguation  
   - Leverage notes to clarify ambiguous or disputed terms such as:  
     • Nationalities or ethnicities (e.g., whether the actor is Greek or otherwise).  
     • Exact birth dates or years.  
     • Geographic hierarchies and classifications (e.g., whether a city is in a given state or jurisdiction, its ranking by population).  
     • Classification of works (opera vs. film, number of acts, influence or inspiration).  
     • Temporal details (publication dates, lifespan periods, release dates).  
     • Confirmations or contradictions of claim elements.  
   - Use notes to correct or challenge the claim’s assertions (e.g., correcting a film’s co-production countries, confirming if a film is divided into acts like an opera).  
   - Disambiguate homonymous or closely related entities (e.g., multiple places with similar names, distinguishing between different works with overlapping influences).

3. Query Content and Scope  
   - Formulate queries tightly focused on the core factual claims needing verification:  
     • Explicitly ask about the existence of relationships or events (e.g., “Did Isaiah Mustafa portray a CIA operative in the ‘Chuck Versus the A-Team’ episode?”).  
     • Ask for key temporal or numeric attributes (birth year, number of acts, ranking of a city).  
     • Confirm or clarify classifications and definitions (e.g., “Is ‘Magic Hunter’ a Hungarian-Swiss-French or Hungarian-Swiss-Italian film?”, “Is it structured in acts like an opera?”).  
     • Combine related queries logically when multiple entities or facts are connected (e.g., “Did both individuals share the same birthplace?”, “Is the birthplace in the specified German state and is it the fifth-largest city in Germany?”).  
     • Explicitly address contradictions or unclear points found in notes, phrased as queries seeking definitive resolution.

4. Query Quality and Style  
   - Keep queries concise, unambiguous, and clearly focused on retrieving the minimal set of critical facts needed to verify the claim.  
   - Use natural, fluent language with explicit references to key entities, works, dates, or descriptors as indicated in claim and notes.  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague questions that could retrieve irrelevant information.  
   - When multiple linked questions are necessary, structure them clearly and logically, ensuring each targets a distinct verification aspect.

5. Domain and Niche Knowledge Integration  
   - For entertainment-related claims: verify specific roles (e.g., acting in a particular episode), birth dates, national or ethnic identity claims, and commercial appearances.  
   - For works of art (opera, film, literature): verify authorship, directors, number of acts/scenes, inspiration sources, and co-production nationalities.  
   - For geographic and biographical claims: verify exact birthplaces, city rankings, state or national jurisdiction relationships.  
   - For natural history or biological claims: focus queries on native habitats with specified ecological classifications (arid, temperate, etc.).  
   - Recognize the inclusion or exclusion criteria for classifications such as ‘periodical’ includes magazines.

6. Generalizable Strategy  
   - Extract main entities and claims from the input.  
   - Review notes for additional, clarifying, contradictory, or disambiguating information.  
   - Reformulate the core verification questions incorporating that additional context.  
   - Create the minimal complete set of concise, focused natural language questions that cover all necessary aspects for efficient information retrieval or fact-checking.

Summary:  
Produce a well-structured, fact-focused, and contextually refined natural language query or set of queries that explicitly target verification of the claim by leveraging all relevant details and clarifications available in the notes. The query should maximize the probability of retrieving the key evidence or ‘gold’ documents necessary to confirm or reject the claim with high precision and minimal ambiguity.
