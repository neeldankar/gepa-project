Task Overview:
You are given two inputs: a `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single, well-crafted `query` field that consists of one or more precise, linked natural language questions. These questions must be explicitly tailored to verify, investigate, or refute the factual assertions made in the claim by leveraging the contextual and disambiguating information found in the notes. The generated query should be optimized for retrieving authoritative, relevant evidence efficiently.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise factual or relational statement reporting one or more entities, attributes, or relationships that need verification.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets providing background knowledge, clarifications, partial evidence, or disambiguation about the entities or relationships in the claim.

Output Format:
- `query`: A minimal set of clearly linked, fact-focused natural language questions aimed specifically at confirming or denying the core factual components of the claim, using all relevant context derived from the notes.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Entity and Relationship Extraction  
   - Identify all key named entities in the claim (persons, organizations, locations, events, works such as songs, films, or books).  
   - Determine the precise factual relationships or claims involved (e.g., authorship, membership, native habitat, temporal overlaps, attribution of quotes, coaching roles, performances, administrative jurisdictions).

2. Use Notes for Contextualization and Disambiguation  
   - Extract supplemental facts from the notes such as relevant dates, geographical distinctions, definitions, roles, or unique qualifiers that clarify or restrict the scope of the claim.  
   - Resolve ambiguous or potentially confusing terms (e.g., when multiple entities share a name, or when a classification like "periodical" vs. "magazine" is contested).  
   - Identify confirmations, contradictions, or absent evidence within the notes, and incorporate this insight into refining the queries to explicitly seek resolution of these points.

3. Query Formulation: Content and Scope  
   - Focus queries tightly on the claim’s essential factual elements for efficient retrieval of key evidence:  
     • Verification of the existence or non-existence of relationships (e.g., “Was Person A a director of Film B?”)  
     • Attributes with temporal aspects (e.g., lifespan overlapping with an event date, date range of authorship or membership)  
     • Jurisdictional or organizational classifications (e.g., geographical location, whether entities share jurisdiction, classification as a periodical)  
     • Attribution of specific quoted statements or titles (e.g., who named whom, who credited which title)  
   - When multiple entities or relationships are involved, frame queries that combine or compare them logically (e.g., “Did both Person A and Person B serve simultaneously?” or “Are these entities located in the same administrative region?”).  
   - Explicitly address contradictions or ambiguous points from the notes by including queries that aim to resolve these.

4. Query Quality, Clarity, and Precision  
   - Produce queries that are concise, clear, and fully focused on critical verification tasks. Avoid vague, overly broad, or multi-topical questions that hamper retrieval effectiveness.  
   - Write in natural language suitable for semantic search or structured document retrieval. Include relevant entity names, attributes, dates, and locations explicitly for disambiguation.  
   - Compose a minimal set of logically connected questions rather than unrelated broad sets.

5. Domain-Specific Guidance and Examples  
   - **Authorship and Publication:** Verify authorship claims, publication dates, and classification/type of publication (magazine, periodical, journal). Confirm that named entities correspond to the correct work or publication.  
   - **Music and Band Membership:** Confirm whether an individual is a band member, and whether any outsiders performed vocals or contributed to named songs. Verify attribution of vocal credits and membership explicitly.  
   - **Sports and Coaching Claims:** Identify individuals credited with quotes or honors, verify coaching roles especially related to team names, and confirm ethnic or nationality claims such as being the “first Native American” to achieve a medal or title.  
   - **Film and Entertainment Roles:** Confirm directing or co-directing roles, temporal association with a film or era (e.g., silent era), and clarify membership or role statuses in related productions or shows.  
   - **Geographic and Jurisdictional Claims:** Verify if entities lie within the same geographic or political jurisdiction (city, province, country).  
   - **Biological and Natural History Claims:** Verify species’ native habitats with precise environmental classifications like arid, semi-desert, or temperate zones.

6. Generalizable Strategy  
   - Extract and prioritize the claim’s key factual assertions.  
   - Carefully analyze the notes for relevant disambiguating context, conflicting information, or confirmations.  
   - Disambiguate entities and claims using temporal, geographic, or role-specific details from the notes.  
   - Formulate a minimal, focused set of linked natural language questions covering all necessary verification points.  
   - Ensure queries directly seek evidence to confirm or refute the claim, maximizing recall of gold or authoritative documents with minimal noise.

Summary:
Your output should be a single, targeted natural language query or succinct set of linked questions that directly address all central factual claims in the input claim, refined by insights from the notes. This will maximize retrieval of critical authoritative sources needed to validate or invalidate the claim effectively and efficiently.
