Task Description:

You are provided with three input fields: 
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion, often involving relationships or properties of entities (e.g., locations, people, events, temporal sequences).
- `notes`: A list of extracted factual sentences that may support, refute, or provide context about the claim.
- `context`: A list of longer, detailed passages or descriptions from various sources related to entities or topics mentioned in the claim and notes.

Your goal is to process these inputs and produce two outputs: 
- `new_notes`: A concise, relevant subset of the `notes` that directly supports or refutes the claim, synthesizing key factual points without repetition. The `new_notes` should be coherent, focused, and sufficient to understand the verification or falsification of the claim.
- `titles`: A list of document or entity titles corresponding to the main sources or topics referenced in the `new_notes`. These titles should accurately represent the principal entities or documents needed to verify the claim. The titles are often proper nouns such as names of people, organizations, places, or works (films, TV shows, cathedrals, etc.).

Detailed Instructions and Considerations:

1. Understand the claim thoroughly: Identify the entities involved, their relationships, and any temporal, geographic, or hierarchical information implied.

2. Analyze the `notes`: Select and consolidate relevant information that directly pertains to the claim. Remove redundant or duplicate facts unless repetition reinforces a different nuance. Prioritize notes that clearly establish facts supporting or contradicting the claim.

3. Consult the `context` for domain-specific or niche factual details to verify, clarify, or add nuance to the notes, especially for place names, temporal sequences, or roles of people/entities. The context often contains precise dates, locations, or hierarchical geographic information (e.g., states within countries, cities within states).

4. In `new_notes`, strive for clarity and sufficiency but brevity. Keep only facts necessary to confirm or refute the claim, restate key facts succinctly, and combine related facts where appropriate.

5. For the `titles` field:
   - Identify primary and authoritative sources or entities relevant to the claim’s validation.
   - Titles should be specific and reflect entities or documents that offer the foundational information (e.g., a company name, a film title, an airport, a cathedral).
   - Avoid overly broad or unrelated titles. Choose titles that are likely the main sources a fact-checker or reader would consult.

6. Account for temporal sequences (dates, premieres, releases) when verifying chronological claims.

7. Respect domain-specific conventions: 
   - Geographic entities should be represented with proper hierarchies (e.g., city, state, country).
   - Titles of films, TV seasons, organizations, cathedrals, and people’s names should be formatted correctly as they appear in the context.
   - Include relevant details such as city and state for places and dates for events or releases, when they help clarify the claim.

8. Always ensure that the `new_notes` and `titles` together provide a clear path for understanding the validity of the claim using the input data.

Summary:

Given `claim`, `notes`, and `context`, produce:
- `new_notes`: A refined and focused subset of notes that directly address and support or refute the claim with accurate, unambiguous information.
- `titles`: A list of key entity or document titles that represent the main sources from which the new notes are derived or could be verified.

This task involves careful synthesis, elimination of redundancy, domain-specific factual verification, and clear presentation of both evidence and source attribution.
