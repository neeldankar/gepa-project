Task Description:  
You will receive two inputs: a `claim` and a `notes` list. Your goal is to generate a single `query` field that is a precisely formulated, natural language question or set of closely linked questions. The query should be explicitly designed to verify or investigate the factual accuracy of the claim by focusing on the information and context contained in the notes. The main purpose of the query is to guide retrieval of authoritative, relevant documents or facts that can confirm or refute the claim efficiently and unambiguously.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A concise statement asserting one or more factual or relational facts involving key named entities (people, works, places, events, organizations, etc.), their attributes, or relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets or facts providing background knowledge, clarifications, temporal markers, or partial information relevant to the entities and relationships mentioned in the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A concise, clear, and focused natural language question or linked set of questions that target the key entities, relationships, and claims. The query must be carefully scoped and disambiguated using the notes for maximal precision and efficiency in retrieving evidence.

Detailed Instruction and Domain-Specific Considerations:  

1. Entity and Relation Identification  
   - Extract all key named entities from the claim (persons, geographic locations, organizations, cultural works such as operas, films, books, etc.).  
   - Identify the claim’s factual relationships such as authorship/composition, birth/death dates, roles or memberships, historical overlap or timing, or classification (e.g., is a publication a periodical).  
   - When multiple entities or relationships are involved, parse whether the claim posits equivalences, differences, temporal overlaps, geographic jurisdiction, or composite conditions.

2. Use of Notes for Context and Disambiguation  
   - Analyze notes to obtain critical clarifying facts such as precise dates (e.g., composition years, death dates), entity definitions (e.g., identifying "Castor et Pollux" as the specific 1755 opera), or disambiguations (noting multiple adaptations of "Manon Lescaut").  
   - Use notes to confirm or reveal contradictions or insufficiencies in the claim’s information, and adjust the query accordingly to focus on resolving ambiguities or verifying contested points.  
   - Identify if entities or terms require explicit disambiguation due to multiple possible referents or variants.

3. Query Construction  
   - Formulate queries that explicitly address the core factual claims to be verified:  
     • Confirm existence or non-existence of relationships (e.g., who composed which opera?).  
     • Confirm specific temporal facts (e.g., precise death dates or composition years).  
     • Confirm attributes and classifications (e.g., magazine vs. periodical, jurisdiction of a city).  
   - Where multiple entities are involved, link queries logically to verify combined facts (e.g., were two people both judges on the same show at the same time?).  
   - If notes reveal partial evidence or contradictions, phrase queries to explicitly seek clarity on these points.  
   - Include all significant entity names with relevant contextual markers (dates, places, roles) to ensure retrieval systems can locate relevant authoritative sources.

4. Query Quality and Precision  
   - Keep the query concise, carefully scoped, and clearly focused solely on the elements needed to verify the claim’s factual core.  
   - Avoid broad or vague questions; prefer direct, natural-language formulations that a search/retrieval system can interpret effectively.  
   - Where appropriate, form compound questions that explicitly connect the entities and relationships involved for maximal disambiguation and document retrieval.

5. Generalizable Strategy  
   - Extract and prioritize the most critical factual components from the claim for verification.  
   - Use notes to enrich, disambiguate, and clarify these components, noting any contradictions or confirmations in the data.  
   - Formulate a minimal, precise, linked set of natural-language questions that direct retrieval systems to authoritative evidence essential for claim verification or refutation.  
   - In complex claims, explicitly separate verification of each factual sub-claim (e.g., verification of death date, verification of teacher identity, etc.) within the query if needed, maintaining clear logical linkage.

Niche and Domain-Specific Knowledge Incorporated:  
- For authorship claims in operas, books, and periodicals: verify exact composers/authors, composition/publication years, and publication types (magazine vs. broader class 'periodical').  
- For natural history or biology claims: verify specific habitat types (arid, semi-desert, temperate) when relating species to habitat.  
- For temporal claims regarding individuals: verify death and birth dates, lifespan overlaps, or service periods for roles or offices carefully as per notes.  
- For entertainment industry claims on actors and awards: confirm main cast or role attributions, nomination or award details, and timing. Disambiguate roles such as “The King of Cocaine” and cross-check associated actors and awards.  
- For geographic or political jurisdiction claims: verify exact jurisdiction hierarchies (e.g., whether a city lies within a specified province or country).  
- Understand that periodicals can include magazines; verify classification when relevant.  

Summary:  
Produce a well-formed, targeted, and disambiguated natural-language query or tightly linked question set that explicitly addresses all key factual components of the claim, guided by relevant context and clarifications found in the notes. Structure questions to maximize retrieval of authoritative documents or data essential for confirming or refuting the claim’s truthfulness, reflecting all domain-specific subtleties and known ambiguities.
