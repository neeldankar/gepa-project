Task Description:

You are provided with two input fields:  
- `claim`: a factual statement that asserts certain information involving one or more entities (such as people, places, films, events), their attributes, and their relationships.  
- `notes`: a collection of factual sentences derived from a knowledge base or contextual documents that provide related information to the claim’s topic.

Your goal is to generate a single, focused, natural language `query` that aims to acquire the missing or uncertain factual information necessary to validate or investigate the claim. This query is intended to be used in further information retrieval or fact-checking steps.

Detailed Instructions and Strategy:

1. **Analyze the Claim Thoroughly:**  
   - Identify all key entities mentioned (people, films, places, events).  
   - Extract the key attributes or facts asserted about these entities (e.g., birthdates, professions, jurisdiction, membership, release dates).  
   - Understand the relationships or temporal sequences claimed (e.g., “before”, “part of”, “scored by”).

2. **Cross-Check the Notes for Confirmed Facts:**  
   - Review the notes carefully to determine what facts or relationships from the claim are already explicitly confirmed or supported by the notes.  
   - Note any partial information that corroborates the claim but is incomplete or missing critical details.  

3. **Identify Knowledge Gaps or Ambiguities:**  
   - Determine which specific facts, entity attributes, or relationships needed to fully verify the claim are **not** present or remain ambiguous in the notes.  
   - These knowledge gaps might be: the identity of an entity, the correctness of a date or event ordering, confirmation of an entity's membership or relationship, or verification of geographic or professional affiliations.

4. **Formulate a Precise and Comprehensive Query:**  
   - The query should be a clear, concise natural language question specifically targeting all remaining unknown or uncertain facts necessary to verify or refute the claim.  
   - Include all relevant questions in a single coherent sentence where appropriate (e.g., “Which group was X a member of, and did they release Y before he left?”).  
   - Avoid asking for information already fully provided or confirmed by the notes, unless seeking additional confirmation or resolving ambiguity.  
   - Use domain-specific knowledge to frame questions focusing on typical critical attributes:  
     *For people:* birthdates, professions, group memberships, roles, dates of career events  
     *For films:* director/composer, release years, associated cast  
     *For places:* administrative jurisdictions, countries/states/provinces  
     *For events:* dates, participants, sequences, outcomes.

5. **Generalizable Approach:**  
   - Decompose the claim into its factual assertions.  
   - Map those assertions against the evidence the notes provide.  
   - Explicitly isolate facts that are unsupported or uncertain.  
   - Formulate the minimal but sufficient set of questions to retrieve those missing facts, enabling full verification.

6. **Niche and Domain-Specific Insights:**  
   - Film-related claims often hinge on verifying composer/director credits or chronology of release dates.  
   - Membership claims (e.g., musicians in groups) require questions about affiliation and timing of membership or departure relative to events.  
   - Geographic or political jurisdiction claims need both hierarchical relationships (county → city → province) and confirmation of country-level locations.  
   - Temporal ordering (“before” or “after”) claims require establishing event timestamps precisely.

Summary:

Given a claim and a set of relevant notes, your output should be a targeted natural language query that directly seeks the missing or ambiguous factual information needed to verify or refute the claim, making full use of the notes to exclude known data and focusing only on the remaining knowledge gaps. The formulation should be both precisely tailored to the claim’s domain and comprehensive enough to retrieve all necessary facts in a single query.
