- `notes`: a list of factual sentences or snippets related to the claim that provide contextual or background knowledge relevant to verifying the claim.

Your task is to generate the output field:

- `query`: a precise information retrieval query or set of queries designed to retrieve supporting evidence or authoritative knowledge about the claim. The query should focus explicitly on obtaining facts necessary to verify the key aspects of the claim.

Detailed guidelines and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Understand the claim fully, and break it down into key factual components that need verification.**

   - Entities involved (e.g., people, companies, locations, bands, publications).  
   - Attributes or properties (e.g., ownership, publication frequency, award counts, locations, time frames).

   - They also often clarify dates, locations, and clarifications that are central in verifying a temporal or geographical claim.  
   - Use the notes to pinpoint what exact facts are still uncertain or missing for complete verification.

3. **Formulate one or more precise queries that target:**

   - The key entities and their associated properties or relationships (using logical relations such as "owned by," "member of," "award received by," "published frequency," "location within administrative entity," etc.).  
   - The verification points relevant to the claim (for example, if a claim involves comparing award counts, retrieve award data for both entities).  
   - Use structured query language (e.g., SPARQL) or natural language queries crafted to retrieve the minimal but sufficient set of facts needed.

4. **In multi-part claims, queries should fetch data addressing each component needed to fully verify the claim.**  
   For example, for claims combining geographic facts plus organizational relationships, the queries should retrieve linked administrative entities and company terminus locations.

5. **In queries, leverage known entity identifiers or labels from `notes` and common linked data properties, such as:**

6. **Aim to retrieve authoritative data sufficient to evaluate all parts of the claim, thus enabling a definitive verification.**

7. **Where appropriate, queries can be composed in a single or multiple parts that complement each other to cover all claim aspects.**

The overall goal is to produce queries specifically designed to retrieve all evidence needed to confirm or refute the claim given the domain context and complexities found in the notes. The queries should be accurate, precise, and guided by linking the claim’s assertions to factual properties evident or implied in the notes.
