Task Description:

You are given two input fields:  
- `claim`: a factual statement that may mention one or more entities, events, relationships, or attributes.  
- `notes`: a list of relevant factual sentences or pieces of information from a knowledge base or context related to the entities or topics mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to produce the output field `query`, a focused, precise, natural language question that would help retrieve missing or verifying information necessary to validate or further examine the claim.

Key details and constraints for generating the `query`:

1. **Purpose:** The main aim of the `query` is to identify the specific unknown or uncertain facts needed to assess the truth or validity of the `claim` given the `notes`.

2. **Analyze the claim:**  
   - Identify the claim’s core components: the entities involved (people, films, dates, places), their attributes, and the relationships asserted.  
   - Determine what critical fact(s) are missing or uncertain from the `notes` to verify the claim.

3. **Use the notes carefully:**  
   - Extract relevant information or evidence from the `notes` that partially address aspects of the claim.  
   - Identify the remaining knowledge gaps or entities not sufficiently covered by the notes.

4. **Formulating the query:**  
   - The query should specifically ask for the missing attribute(s), fact(s), or entity-related information needed to fully confirm or refute the claim.  
   - Use clear, concise, and precise natural language.  
   - If multiple facts are missing, include all relevant questions within a single coherent query, phrased naturally (e.g., “Who is X? Does Y have Z attribute?”).  
   - Focus the query on retrieving knowledge about the entities or facts that remain unknown or ambiguous after considering the notes.  
   - Avoid restating facts already present and verified in the notes, unless asking for further detail or confirmation about disputed points.

5. **Generalizable approach:**  
   - Parse the claim to identify what it asserts.  
   - Cross-check against the notes to see what corroborates the claim and what is still unknown.  
   - Formulate a minimal but comprehensive question that can extract the missing data needed to confirm or deny the claim.

6. **Domain-specific considerations:**  
   - Common entities include people (with attributes like birthdates, professions, nationality), films (with directors, release years), and industry roles (e.g., film director, musician).  
   - Familiarity with typical biographical facts is useful, e.g., birthdates for age comparisons, nationality for verifying origins, profession or film credits for attribution claims.  
   - Queries must often seek specific missing entity attributes such as birthdates, professions, or relationships between people and works.

Summary: Given a claim and supporting notes, generate a precise natural language query that targets the missing factual information required to verify the claim, leveraging all evidence available to exclude known facts and focus on gaps.
