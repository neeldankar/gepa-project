Task Description:
You are given two input fields:
1. `claim` - a factual statement or assertion that may contain multiple factual components.
2. `notes` - a list of factual statements, evidence, or background information that relate to the claim, drawn from various sources. These notes include specific names, dates, titles, affiliations, and other domain-specific details relevant to the claim.

Your task is to produce the output field:
- `query` - a concise, clear, and focused natural language question (or set of related questions) that captures the key factual aspects of the claim that need to be verified or researched further.

Detailed Requirements and Domain-specific Information:
- The `claim` often involves verifying complex factual relationships involving entities such as individuals (e.g., politicians, musicians), organizations (e.g., political parties, youth wings, TV shows), dates (e.g., birthdates, event dates), works (e.g., albums, songs), roles (e.g., judges, leaders), and affiliations.
- The `notes` provide authoritative or contradictory evidence regarding the claim, often including names, dates, events, titles, or affiliations. Examples include:
  - Names of judges on a TV show or panel.
  - Leadership succession in political parties, along with birthdates of individuals.
  - Details regarding music albums, release years, and artist biographies.
  - Affiliations between organizations (e.g., youth wings linked to political parties).
- The goal of the `query` is to explicitly target the main points requiring verification inferred from the claim and supported or contradicted by the notes.
- The `query` should be framed in natural, grammatically correct English, and it should be designed so it can serve effectively as a search or research query to retrieve verifiable evidence or documents.
- When multiple factual elements appear in the claim (e.g., names, roles, dates, affiliations), the `query` should cover all those elements clearly, breaking them into connected questions if needed but retaining conciseness.
- Avoid including speculative or uncertain language; focus on factual verification based on the claim's content.
- Ensure the query references the key named entities or concepts explicitly (e.g., “Who are the judges on the show Music Icons?”, “Did Jagmeet Singh become leader on October 1, 2017?”, “Did the album Inner Secrets include the cover of Stormy?”).
- Use aliases or disambiguation where necessary for clarification (e.g., “New Democratic Party (NDP)”, “federal vs. provincial affiliations”, or the exact show name as in the notes).

Generalizable Strategy:
- Analyze the claim to extract key factual assertions (names, dates, roles, affiliations).
- Map these assertions to corresponding evidence in the notes.
- Formulate a natural language question or questions that directly target verification of these assertions.
- When more than one fact is present, combine them into a cohesive query that queries the full set.
- Ensure the query is suitable for retrieving solid, authoritative documents or corroborating evidence.
  
This approach generalizes across domains such as entertainment, politics, music, and others, where claims involve verifying precise factual details supported or disputed by provided notes.
