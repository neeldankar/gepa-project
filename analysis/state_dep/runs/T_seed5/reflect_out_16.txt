Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: 
- `claim`: A factual statement or set of statements that assert specific information.
- `notes`: A list of factual evidentiary snippets or documents that provide information related to the claim.

Your goal is to produce a single field `query` that synthesizes a precise, targeted question (or set of closely related questions) which directly tests the validity or accuracy of the `claim` by referencing the key factual elements present in the claim and the notes.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Considerations:
1. The claim may contain multiple factual assertions that require verification.
2. The notes often include domain-specific entities such as:
   - Names of persons (e.g., Reverend Lyttleton Morgan, Travis Oates)
   - Names of institutions or organizations (e.g., Morgan State University, Dynamo Kyiv)
   - Names of events or cultural artifacts with dates (e.g., UEFA Euro 2012 Final, movies with release years)
   - Geographical locations (e.g., England, Kyiv, Ukraine)
   - Roles or titles (e.g., chairman of a board of trustees, voice actor)
3. The underlying factual relationships may involve:
   - Verifying roles or positions held by individuals at specific institutions
   - Confirming whether entities share categories or attributes (e.g., whether universities are public)
   - Temporal sequencing or date comparisons (e.g., one movie released before another)
   - Geographic residency or location claims about facilities or clubs
4. Notes can be noisy or redundant, but the query should be focused on the core facts needed to verify the claim.
5. The query should explicitly reference the named entities and relations central to the claim.
6. The query should be formulated to capture all the main factual points in the claim that need verification—e.g., referencing all institutions, events, people, or chronological facts cited.
7. You can form a multi-part question if the claim involves multiple fact points, but keep it concise and clear.
8. The query is intended to retrieve or confirm the relevant evidentiary documents that prove or disprove the claim, so ensure the question wording guides toward retrieving all components of the claim.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Identify the key entities, attributes, and relationships in the claim.
- Check the notes for which entities and facts appear or are corroborated.
- Formulate a direct question or set of questions targeting those entities and facts explicitly.
- Ensure the query is comprehensive enough to encompass all critical claim elements to avoid missing supporting documents.
- Phrase temporal comparisons with clear reference points (e.g., release dates).
- Phrase location or role-related queries in a way that will retrieve clarifying evidence about geography and affiliations.
- Avoid ambiguity in the query: relate specific names, titles, dates, and categories exactly as mentioned in the claim and notes.

Example:
If the claim states a person held a position at a university and some universities are public, the query should explicitly ask about that person’s position at the specific university and whether that university is public, referencing any comparison university if mentioned.

Summary Instruction:
Given the claim (factual assertions) and the notes (sets of evidentiary facts), create a clear, explicit, and comprehensive query that targets key named entities, factual relationships, and attributes in the claim, so the query can retrieve or test all the relevant evidence necessary to confirm or refute the claim fully. Ensure the query covers every factual component, including multiple entities and temporal or locational claims if present.
