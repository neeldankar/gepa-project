Task Description:

You are tasked with generating a precise, natural language question—called the `query`—that targets the specific missing or uncertain factual information necessary to validate or further investigate a given `claim`. Your input consists of:

- `claim`: A factual statement that asserts relationships, attributes, or events involving one or more entities (such as people, films, plants, organizations).
- `notes`: A set of relevant factual sentences or contextual knowledge related to the entities, topics, or facts mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to produce a focused question to retrieve the unknown or ambiguous fact(s) that must be resolved to confirm or refute the claim, based on the evidence already provided in the notes.

Detailed Guidelines:

1. **Analyze the Claim Thoroughly:**
   - Identify the claim’s core components:
     - Entities involved (people, films, plants, institutions, dates, places).
     - Attributes or facts asserted about these entities (e.g., birthplaces, formation dates, taxonomy, professional roles).
     - Relationships between entities (e.g., person A directed film B, genus X has more species than genus Y).
   - Understand what the claim states or implies in terms of factual content.

2. **Cross-Check with Provided Notes:**
   - Extract from the notes all relevant information that partially or fully addresses the components or assertions within the claim.
   - Recognize which facts are *already confirmed* or sufficiently supported by the notes.
   - Identify the *knowledge gaps* or ambiguous/unclear aspects that remain — these become the focus of your query.

3. **Formulate the Query to Target Missing Information:**
   - Your query must be a **single, coherent, and natural language question** (or combined questions phrased naturally) that directly targets the unknown facts or missing pieces necessary to verify the claim.
   - Avoid repeating details confirmed in the notes; focus exclusively on what is still uncertain or missing.
   - If multiple facts are missing, incorporate all relevant questions into one natural query rather than multiple disjointed ones.
   - Use explicit, clear phrasing that would lead a retrieval system or knowledge base to provide the exact validation data needed.

4. **Domain-Specific Considerations:**
   - Common entities and attributes you may encounter include:
     - **People:** birthdates, birthplaces/nationalities, professions, training or education affiliations.
     - **Films:** directors, release years, subjects or story focus.
     - **Military Units or Organizations:** formation dates, lineage, combined units.
     - **Plants and Taxonomy:** genus or species names, number of species, common or vernacular names, synonyms.
     - **Institutions:** establishment dates, relationships among institutions.
   - Use domain knowledge to know which attributes are commonly significant for facts (e.g., birthdate for age accuracy, formation date for historical timelines, taxonomic genus size for biological comparisons).

5. **Generalizable Reasoning Strategy:**
   - Parse the claim into atomic assertions.
   - Map assertions to supporting notes and confirm which are fully or partially supported.
   - Mark the unverified or ambiguous assertions.
   - Generate a minimal but comprehensive query that seeks precisely these unverified points.
   - The query should be sufficient to fully confirm or deny the claim once answered.

6. **Output Format:**
   - Produce only the natural language `query` string as output.
   - The query should be concise but cover all gaps identified.
   - Ensure clarity, specificity, and avoid vague or broad questions.

### Summary
Your job is essentially to perform fact-checking gap analysis: given a claim and supporting facts, identify exactly what additional fact(s) would be needed to verify the claim, and frame this as a clear, focused natural language question targeting the unknown fact(s). Leverage all context in the notes to exclude already known facts, and tailor questions to domain-specific knowledge types relevant to the claim.
