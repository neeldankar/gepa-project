Given two input fields: `claim` (a statement or assertion combining multiple facts or entities) and `notes` (a list of text snippets containing partial or related factual information), produce an output field `query`.

The `query` should be a clear, concise, and precise search or verification question aimed at collecting the minimal set of additional factual documents or evidence needed to fully verify or refute the `claim`.

Instructions and domain-specific details:

1. **Purpose of the query:**  
   The query should target the key pieces of information embedded in the claim that remain unsupported or ambiguous given the provided `notes`. It should efficiently focus on missing entities, relationships, or facts needed to confirm or deny the claim.

2. **Claim decomposition:**  
   Break down the claim into its core factual components (e.g., entities, events, attributes, relations). If the claim combines multiple facts (e.g., "X did Y and Z"), the query should reflect all these linked sub-claims clearly.

3. **Leverage notes analysis:**  
   Analyze the `notes` to determine which parts of the claim are already partially supported or contradicted, and which require further evidence. Use this to guide the construction of the query so as to retrieve only the missing information.

4. **Domain-specific detail and factual grounding:**

   - When the claim involves **cultural or entertainment entities** (e.g., films, bands, festivals, awards), queries should:
     - Specify exact events or years where relevant (e.g., film festival editions, release years).
     - Include associated persons (actors, directors, band members) and their biographical or collaborative details.
     - Clarify genre or category distinctions (e.g., musical film, rock band, indie rock).
     - Cross-verify awards and their recipients (e.g., Golden Bear winner of a particular Berlin International Film Festival edition).

   - When the claim involves **sports teams or players** (e.g., football clubs, seasons, player roles):
     - Mention specific seasons or years.
     - Specify player positions relevant to the claim (goalkeepers, forwards).
     - Cross-link players’ affiliations or transfers between clubs.
     - Confirm known nicknames or designations for teams where they are part of the claim.

5. **Query formulation style:**  
   - Pose factual yes/no or wh-questions that target the knowledge gaps uncovered.  
   - Combine multiple related questions if the claim hinges on the conjunction of multiple facts.  
   - Queries should be specific enough to retrieve targeted documents/entities rather than broad or vague.

6. **Avoid redundancy and ambiguity:**  
   If a piece of information is clearly established in the notes, do not include it unnecessarily in the query. Instead, focus on those parts not found or clearly refuted in the notes.

7. **Example logic applied:**

   - Example 1 (film & award): Query structured as "Which film won the Golden Bear at the 50th Berlin International Film Festival, was it a musical, and did any actor in that film serve as a U.S. Navy Combat veteran in World War II?"  
   - Example 2 (bands): Query asking if a band is considered rock, and if the band a particular person collaborated with is also classified as rock.  
   - Example 3 (sports): Query asking if any goalie led the strike force of a given season, and whether that goalie played for another specified club.

By following this approach, the assistant can generate focused queries that help retrieve missing or verifying documents, enabling subsequent fact verification or claim validation tasks.
