Task Description:

You are given two input fields for each instance:  
- `claim`: a factual statement that may require verification or fact-checking  
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets or document passages relevant to the entities or topics mentioned in the claim, providing contextual background information  

Your goal is to produce a single output field:  
- `query`: a concise, targeted information retrieval or fact-checking query designed to check the factual accuracy of the `claim` using the information in `notes`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understand the Claim Context and Entities  
   Carefully analyze the `claim` to identify the explicit or implicit factual assertions it makes, including any named entities (people, organizations, locations, dates), relationships, or roles.

2. Use the Notes to Guide Reasoning  
   Examine the `notes` which contain relevant facts, often with repeated or overlapping information. Use these to:  
   - Identify key entities and their attributes mentioned in the claim.  
   - Detect potential ambiguities, confusions, or points of contention that should be investigated.  
   - Note which parts of the claim are supported or contradicted by given facts.

3. Formulate a Verification Query  
   The `query` should:  
   - Target the core factual components necessary to verify the claim’s truthfulness.  
   - Include questions about entities’ specific attributes (e.g., location, roles, dates, relationships) that the claim asserts.  
   - Be specific enough to guide information retrieval towards verifying the claim fully, e.g., “Who led team X in year Y?”, “What country is location Z in?”, “Did person A hold position B during time period C?”  
   - When the claim involves comparisons or multiple entities, ask about the corresponding facts for each to enable direct comparison.

4. Include Reasoning (optional but recommended in intermediate steps)  
   - Consider providing explicit reasoning that explains why the query is formed as it is (e.g., “The claim compares locations of two entities, so we need their country of location to verify if they are in the same country.”)  
   - Highlight any ambiguities or name confusions that need resolution.

Domain-Specific and Task-Specific Notes:  
- The claims often involve detailed factual information about entities in domains such as aerospace companies, airports, films and their actors, sports teams and coaches, and time-specific roles or events.  
- In some cases, entities may have similar or confusingly similar names (e.g., Kevin Wilson vs. Kevin Reece Wilson). The query should clarify distinctions.  
- The notes might include multiple redundancies or repeated information; focus on relevant facts that clarify or support the claim’s truth conditions.  
- Queries should be framed in plain language, directly addressing the fact(s) to be checked.

Generalizable Strategy Reflected:  
- Extract key factual components from the claim.  
- Use notes to identify relevant verification points and potential ambiguities.  
- Construct focused, disambiguating questions aimed at retrieving exactly the information required to accept or refute the claim.

Example Pattern:  
If the claim states: “Entity A and Entity B share property P,” the query should be: “What is the property P of Entity A and Entity B respectively?”  

If the claim involves a person’s role and identity confusion, e.g., “Person X did action Y in time Z,” the query should be: “Did Person X perform action Y during time Z, or was it another person with a similar name?”

By following this structured approach, the produced `query` will be a precise, fact-checking question that maximizes the likelihood of effective evidence retrieval to validate or invalidate the claim.
