Task Description:
You will be provided with two inputs:
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving named entities (people, films, bands, roles, dates, nationalities, professions, works, etc.) that may require verification or further fact-checking.
- `notes`: A set of textual snippets containing partial or related information about the entities or facts mentioned in the claim. These notes may confirm, contradict, or lack some key details related to the claim.

Your core objective is to generate the output field `query` — a precise, targeted question (or set of questions) designed to retrieve the critical missing, contradictory, or uncertain factual information needed to verify the claim fully.

Key Domain-Specific and Task-Specific Knowledge:
- Named entities often involve persons (actors, directors, musicians), their roles (e.g., director, bassist, voice actor), works (films, albums, songs), dates (birthdays, premiere dates, release dates), nationalities, and professional relationships.
- Important relationships and attributes include:
  * Identity and nationality of persons associated with films, bands, or works.
  * Birthdates, ages, or timelines where age or order of events matter.
  * Director, actor, or musician roles linked to named works.
  * Premiere or performance dates to establish chronological order.
  * Verifying if a certain person fits a claimed role or relationship (e.g., "bass player and lead vocalist who created Cobra Starship" = Gabe Saporta).
  * Confirmations about locations (airports, company headquarters) belonging to specific countries or regions.
- Notes might partially confirm parts of the claim but often lack specific details necessary for verification (e.g., birthdate of a lesser-known director, premiere dates of works, or nationality of a director).

Generalizable Strategy for Formulating Queries:
1. Carefully analyze the factual assertions within the claim, especially those involving named entities and relationships.
2. Review the notes thoroughly to determine which facts are:
   - Supported and confirmed.
   - Missing or absent.
   - Contradictory or ambiguous.
3. Identify the minimal yet critical gaps in factual information that prevent fully verifying the claim.
4. Formulate clear, concise queries aimed specifically at retrieving missing or uncertain data about:
   - The identity of key persons or works when unclear.
   - Dates (birthdates, release dates, premieres) crucial for timeline-based claims.
   - Roles or relationships linking individuals and works.
   - Locations and nationalities relevant to the claim’s assertions.
5. Avoid broad or generic questions; tailor your query narrowly to resolve the precise factual uncertainty.
6. When multiple gaps exist, efficiently combine them into a concise query or a logically connected set of questions.

Examples:
- If only one person’s birthdate is known and the claim relates to relative age, ask:  
  “What is the birthdate or age of [Person with missing info]?”
- If the claim involves verifying a director’s identity or nationality linked to a named work, ask:  
  “Who directed [Film Name]? What is this director’s nationality and date of birth?”
- If verifying location claims, ask:  
  “What country is [Place or company headquarters] located in?”

Additional Notes to Maintain High Query Quality:
- Ensure queries reflect domain knowledge — e.g., recognize that “Cobra Starship’s lead vocalist and bass player” refers to Gabe Saporta.
- Pay attention to premiere dates or performance timelines to verify chronological assertions.
- When multiple entities appear, ensure you target the specific entity not confirmed in notes rather than redundant queries.
- The goal is to retrieve authoritative documents or facts that close specific knowledge gaps evident from the notes.

Your well-crafted queries will directly contribute to issuing efficient fact-check retrievals and verifying complex claims effectively.
