Task Description:

You will be given three input fields:

- `claim`: A textual statement comprising one or more factual assertions that need to be verified or explained.
- `notes`: A list of short textual observations, facts, or inferences directly related to the claim and the context. These notes may partially confirm, contradict, clarify, or expand on aspects of the claim or the entities/events involved.
- `context`: A list of textual excerpts, document summaries, or entity descriptions containing potentially relevant and verifiable information about the claim's elements.

Your objective is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: A concise, distilled, and well-organized synthesis of the input notes that meets the following criteria:
   - Summarizes the key evidentiary points relevant to understanding, verifying, or explaining each part of the claim.
   - Removes redundancies, repeated information, and irrelevant details.
   - Clearly emphasizes verified factual information including domain-specific distinctions (e.g., taxonomic classification hierarchies such as genus vs. subfamily in biology, precise event details like official event names with sponsors and dates, or artistic credit and work cataloguing in music).
   - Explicitly highlights inaccuracies or misconceptions in the claim (e.g., mistaken botanical classifications, wrongly ascribed authorship).
   - Includes information about missing evidence or aspects that cannot be verified from the provided data.
   - Separately addresses multi-part claims, ensuring each distinct factual assertion is covered individually.
   - Employs clear, factual, and logically reasoned language suitable for supporting downstream fact verification.

2. `titles`: A list of document or entity titles essential for verifying or understanding the claim. These titles must:
   - Be precise and accurate names of relevant entities, works, events, or documents (e.g., specify "St Paul's Cathedral, London" rather than just "St Paul's Cathedral" when multiple exist).
   - Include all major relevant named entities explicitly mentioned or implied by the claim and notes (e.g., full names of awards, composers, species, films, event sponsorship titles).
   - Be domain-appropriate and contextually useful for a downstream retrieval or verification system to identify critical sources.
   - Reflect disambiguation where necessary (e.g., multiple cathedrals named the same but in different countries).

Additional Guidance:

- The claim and notes often contain domain-specific terminology and factual relationships requiring careful interpretation. Examples include:
  - Botanical taxonomy (distinguish genus, subfamily, family levels explicitly).
  - Music composition metadata (opus numbers, catalog numbers, composer nationality, and gender).
  - Film and TV productions (film titles, actors’ roles, original novel sources, production dates, and sponsorship names).
  - Art and architecture (correct building names and locations, sculptor contributions).
- For claims with multiple components, treat each fact or assertion discretely to ensure fine-grained verification.
- When a claim contains factual inaccuracies (e.g., calling "Caesalpinioideae" a genus instead of a subfamily), explicitly note this correction.
- If evidence for parts or all of the claim is missing or uncertain in the provided data, explicitly state that verification is not possible or that there is no supporting evidence.
- Use corroborated domain knowledge to support clarity and factual accuracy (e.g., recognized facts about Nobel laureates, historically notable events, or well-documented scientific classifications).
- Avoid inferred or unsupported conclusions; base the synthesis strictly on the notes and context provided.
- Organize the synthesis logically, maintaining clarity and brevity suitable for efficient fact verification.

By following these detailed instructions, you ensure your outputs are precise, factually sound, pragmatically useful, and fit robustly into automated or human-assisted fact-checking pipelines that require domain-sensitive understanding.
