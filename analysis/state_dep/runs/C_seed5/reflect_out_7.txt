Task Description:
You will be provided with two inputs:
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that requires verification or factual support.
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets containing partial information relevant to verifying the claim. These notes may confirm, contradict, or partially cover entities, attributes, or relationships mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to produce an output field called `query`: a precise, focused research question (or concise set of questions) designed to identify and retrieve the critical missing or ambiguous factual information necessary to verify the claim. The `query` should specifically target the unresolved facts, entities, attributes, or relationships not sufficiently addressed or confirmed by the notes.

Key Domain Knowledge and Task Requirements:
- The claims typically involve named entities such as people (e.g., musicians, actors), creative works (films, songs, albums, publications), roles and contributions (e.g., director, producer, vocalist, band member), biographical details (birthdates, nationalities), awards and honors (including Hall of Fame inductions), and professional or creative relationships.
- Notes may only partially address these entities or contain contradictions, omissions, or insufficient detail.
- The `query` must aim to close such knowledge gaps by precisely asking for authoritative, domain-specific factual information directly related to the claim’s key uncertain points.
- The `query` should be targeted enough to retrieve documents specifically about the named entities or facts that remain unclear or unsubstantiated, avoiding broad, vague, or generic questions.
- Domain-specific facts to consider and address include:
   * Individuals’ roles and credits (e.g., "bass player and lead vocalist who created Cobra Starship" identifies Gabe Saporta).
   * Nationalities or origins of persons associated with works (e.g., film directors’ nationalities for verifying production claims).
   * Dates of birth or relative ages if the claim involves temporal or comparative biographical details.
   * Award histories, recognitions, or Hall of Fame inductions, including the timing of such honors.
   * Associations between creative works and their contributors (actors, producers, musicians, etc.).
   * Publication frequencies or schedules of magazines, journals, or other periodicals if relevant to claims involving comparative frequencies.
- When multiple factual gaps exist, combine them in the query efficiently by grouping questions about the same entity or closely related facts.

Generalizable Strategy:
1. Carefully analyze the claim to extract all factual assertions requiring verification.
2. Thoroughly review the notes to identify which specific components of the claim are confirmed, contradicted, missing, or ambiguous.
3. Isolate the minimal set of missing or uncertain data—entities, attributes, relationships—that prevents full verification of the claim.
4. Formulate a concise query (or set of queries) targeting these exact pieces of information. For example:
   - If a birthdate is missing for a comparative age claim, ask: "What is the birthdate of [Person]?"
   - If a producer is unconfirmed in claims about film credits, ask: "Who produced [Film A] and [Film B]? Is there any producer common to both?"
   - If award comparisons are made without award data, ask about the awards and recognitions received by the named individuals.
   - If a publication’s frequency and Hall of Fame inductions are ambiguous, ask about the publication schedules and Hall of Fame histories relevant to the involved personalities.
5. Avoid queries that are overly broad or vague—your goal is to retrieve specific authoritative documents that will close the identified factual gaps.
6. Use exact entity names and contextually relevant attributes or relationships in your queries to enable precise retrieval.

Examples:
- For comparing awards of two people when only one person's awards are known:
   "What awards has [Person B] received?"
- For verifying claim about an actress starring in a film and roles played in another film:
   "Who stars in [Film]? Did this actress also appear in [Other Film] and in what role?"
- For verifying production credits and shared producers between two films:
   "Who produced [Film A] and [Film B]? Are any producers common to both films?"
- For verifying publication frequency and related Hall of Fame inductions:
   "What is the publication frequency of [Publication]? Did it induct [Person] into its Hall of Fame in the early 1980s?"

By adhering to this approach, you will ensure that the queries you generate efficiently target the critical unknown or uncertain facts, enabling retrieval of authoritative evidence necessary for claim verification.

Format for Output:
Your output should be a concise, clear set of one or more direct questions, phrased as natural, fact-finding queries targeting the missing or ambiguous information in the claim, based on the current notes.

Avoid:
- Vague or generic questions
- Overly broad topics not tightly connected to the claim’s verification needs
- Repetition or unnecessarily expansive questioning

In short: your queries must be targeted, minimal, and factually oriented toward closing the precise knowledge gaps identified from the claim and notes combination.
