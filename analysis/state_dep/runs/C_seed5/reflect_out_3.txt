Task Description:
You will be given two input fields:

- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving entities such as people (e.g., actors, musicians, directors), works (e.g., films, songs, albums, soft drinks), dates (e.g., birthdates or release years), nationalities, professions, roles (e.g., director, composer, bassist), or relationships/associations between these entities. The claim requires verification or further factual substantiation.

- `notes`: A list of textual snippets that contain partial, sometimes contradictory, and often incomplete information related to the entities or facts in the claim. These notes reflect what is already known or confirmed from the given context, including some details that might contradict or only partially address the claim.

Your Goal:
To produce an output field called `query` — a precise, focused, and minimal set of question(s) to retrieve the missing or unconfirmed key factual details required to verify the claim completely. This query should directly target specific knowledge gaps evident from the notes.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Knowledge:

1. **Domain Entities and Attributes**:  
   - Recognize and focus on named entities such as persons (including their roles and professions), films, songs, albums, beverages, and their key characteristics (e.g., flavor, release year, nationality).  
   - Understand domain-specific roles and contributions (e.g., a "bass player and lead vocalist who created Cobra Starship" is Gabe Saporta).  
   - Identify relevant attributes like birthdates, first works, film directorship, soundtrack composers, actor-film associations, and beverage flavor profiles.

2. **Verification Challenges**:  
   - Claims may involve confirming multiple related facts, such as who directed a film, who acted in which film, who composed the soundtrack, or a product's main flavor.  
   - Notes often contain partial overlap or contradictions, so carefully distinguish verified information from what remains uncertain or absent.  
   - When the claim involves relative information—e.g., ages or order of works—identify the missing piece (e.g., birthdate of the second person, first work of the composer, director of a particular film).

3. **Formulating the Query**:  
   - The query must be concise but sufficiently comprehensive to retrieve authoritative documents or facts covering all ambiguous or missing parts required to verify the claim.  
   - Avoid overly broad or generic queries; tailor queries to the specific named entities and the precise information gaps evident from the notes.  
   - Combine multiple factual questions efficiently when the claim involves multiple unknowns, ensuring completeness without unnecessary verbosity.

Generalizable Strategy:

1. Analyze the claim to extract all factual assertions and their components (entities, roles, attributes, relationships).  
2. Review the notes carefully to determine which parts of the claim are already supported, contradicted, or missing.  
3. Identify the minimal set of precise questions that target only the uncertain or missing facts needed to confirm or dispute the claim fully.  
4. When possible, include named entities in the query for specificity (e.g., "Who directed The Deal (2015 South Korean film)?", not just "Who directed The Deal?").  
5. Where relative factual information is necessary (e.g., comparing ages or order of projects), focus queries on the entity with missing or uncertain information.

Examples of Effective Queries:

- If a claim requires confirming a composer's first film and the composer's involvement in a related movie, ask:  
  "What film did [Composer Name] first compose the soundtrack for? Did [Composer Name] score [Film Name]?"

- If a claim involves unclear beverage flavors:  
  "What is the main flavor of [Soft Drink Name]? Which soft drink is advertised with the song '[Song Name]' and what is its main flavor?"

- If a claim involves contradictory director or actor information:  
  "Who directed [Film Name] (Year)? Did [Person Name] act in or direct [Film Names]?"

- If the claim involves relative ages or birthdates:  
  "What is the birthdate of [Person with missing birthdate]?"

Outcome:
Your precisely formulated query will enable retrieval of authoritative documents, allowing the claim to be verified accurately and conclusively.
