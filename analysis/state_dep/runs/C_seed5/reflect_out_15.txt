Task Description:
You will be given two input fields:

- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving named entities, events, or relationships (e.g., actors, directors, films, bands, songs, years, roles, nationalities, or professions) that requires verification or fact-checking.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets providing partial information related to verifying the claim. These notes may include confirmatory facts, contradictory details, or missing key information about the entities or relationships mentioned in the claim.

Your objective is to produce an output field `query` — a precise, focused, and concise question or set of questions designed explicitly to retrieve the minimal yet sufficient factual evidence required to confirm or refute the claim.

----

Key Domain-Specific Knowledge and Considerations:

1. **Named Entities & Attributes**:  
   - People: including full names, birthdates, nationalities, professions (e.g., actor, director, musician), and roles/characters played.  
   - Works: films, TV shows, songs, albums, or bands, including their release or premiere dates, genre, and notable contributors.  
   - Roles & Contributions: Specific contributions by individuals (e.g., director, lead actor, screenwriter, voice actor, bassist, vocalist).  
   - Relationships: Connections between individuals and works (e.g., an actor starring in multiple productions, a director’s nationality affecting claim verification).

2. **Temporal Information**:  
   - Release/premiere dates of films, TV shows, or songs.  
   - Relative timing of appearances or performances (e.g., did an actor perform in a certain work before/after another).

3. **Verification Focus**:  
   Identify the exact gaps or ambiguities in the facts relevant to confirming the claim. For example:  
   - If one birthdate is known but the claim compares ages, request the unknown birthdate.  
   - If the director's nationality is in question, specifically query the director’s identity and nationality.  
   - If a claim involves multiple works or actors, identify missing links or overlaps between those entities.

----

Generalizable Strategy to Formulate Queries:

1. **Analyze the Claim for Factual Assertions**  
   Extract all discrete facts or relationships implied or explicitly stated—for example, "Actor X starred in film Y," or "Director Z is American."

2. **Cross-Reference with Notes**  
   Review the provided notes carefully to determine which facts/relationships are confirmed, contradicted, or missing.

3. **Identify Key Missing or Ambiguous Elements**  
   Pinpoint what essential data is necessary to verify the claim but is unavailable or unclear in the notes.

4. **Formulate Concise, Targeted Natural Language Queries**  
   - The query should request the minimal but sufficient information to resolve key uncertainties.  
   - Avoid broad or vague questions; be specific about entities, attributes, or relationships.  
   - When multiple pieces of information are needed, combine them efficiently into a focused multi-part query.

5. **Focus on Authoritative, Fact-Retrieval Targets**  
   Queries should naturally elicit references to authoritative documents or databases containing biographical, filmographic, discographic, or professional information about named persons and works.

----

Examples for Query Construction:

- If the claim compares ages and one date of birth is unknown:  
  *"What is the birthdate of [Person missing info]?"*

- If verifying an actor’s participation in multiple productions:  
  *"Who starred in [Film A]? Did this actor appear in [Show B] in [year]?*"

- For director nationality and debut film questions:  
  *"Who directed [Film]? What is the director's nationality? For [Actress], what was her debut film and who directed it?"*

----

Important Notes:

- The queries must be self-contained and explicit enough to retrieve relevant, verifiable facts without requiring the user to interpret vague or partial information.  
- Do not restate reasoning in the output; only provide the final, actionable query or queries.  
- Ensure the queries target the precise gap in knowledge that prevents claim verification based on the notes.  
- Assume notes may include contradictory or partial data—queries should seek definitive clarifications.

By following this instruction, you will help retrieve essential, authoritative factual information needed to verify complex claims about people, works, roles, dates, and relationships with precision and efficiency.
