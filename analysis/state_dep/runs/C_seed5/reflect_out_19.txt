Task Overview:  
You will be provided with two inputs:  
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving named entities (people, films, bands, roles, dates, nationalities, professions, works, etc.) that requires verification or factual support.  
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets that contain partial, confirmatory, contradictory, or insufficient information related to the claim. These notes may mention entities, relationships, or facts, but often lack key details needed to verify the claim conclusively.

Your primary goal is to generate a precise, factual query or set of queries - the output field `query` - aimed at retrieving exactly the missing or uncertain critical information necessary to confirm or refute the claim. The queries should be targeted and minimally sufficient to close the knowledge gaps evident from the notes.

Domain-Specific Knowledge & Key Considerations:  
1. Named entities in claims generally include:  
    - People: Actors, directors, musicians, managers, characters, authors, etc.  
    - Works: Films, operas, songs, bands, stage performances, cultural products with known structural elements (e.g., number of acts or parts).  
    - Attributes: Birthdates, nationalities, roles/contributions (e.g., lead vocalist, director), significant years (founding dates, years of activity), occupations, filmographies, managerial careers.  
2. Entities often have multiple relevant attributes that serve as verification anchors, e.g., who starred in a work, who managed a team at a particular time, who created or inspired a cultural work, or the structural elements of operas and films (such as number of acts).  
3. The temporal dimension matters for verification, especially regarding birthdates, activity periods, and overlapping timelines.  
4. Nationality and origin of persons and works may be critical for verifying claims linking entities to specific countries or cultural contexts.  
5. Claims sometimes fuse multiple assertions that require cross-validation, e.g., connection between a person’s playing career and subsequent managerial career, or the structural comparison between an opera and a loosely inspired film.

Generalizable Strategy:  
1. Carefully analyze the claim and identify all factual assertions and entities it involves.  
2. Review the notes to determine exactly which components of the claim are supported, contradicted, missing, or ambiguous.  
3. Identify the minimal but sufficient factual gaps that need to be resolved to verify the claim fully. These typically include:  
    - Missing entity attributes (e.g., birthdates, nationalities, roles).  
    - Clarification of relationships or involvement (e.g., who starred where, who managed which team when).  
    - Structural or numerical facts (e.g., number of acts, founding dates).  
4. Formulate precise, concise queries targeted to retrieve authoritative information on these missing factual points. Avoid overly broad or generic questions. Instead, ask narrow, specific questions explicitly connected to the knowledge gaps identified.  
5. When multiple key facts are missing, combine them logically in a single query to maximize retrieval efficiency but maintain clarity and focus.

Examples of domain-specific fact focus areas:  
- Exact birthdates or ages of named persons where age comparisons are relevant.  
- Confirmation of nationality or professional roles relevant to the entities in the claim.  
- Specific involvement in works or events (e.g., whether a named actor played a lead role in a certain film or stage production).  
- Structural details of works such as number of acts or divisions in operas or films.  
- Chronology and career relationships in sports or entertainment scenes (e.g., playing career followed by management).  
- Founding years and key player histories of sports teams in claims about sports franchises.  
- Attribution or inspiration source of cultural works (which opera a film is inspired by).

By carefully honing in on these targeted factual gaps, your query output will facilitate the retrieval of key documents that enable unambiguous verification or refutation of complex factual claims.

Your output:  
- Provide a well-structured, focused query (or queries) phrased as clear questions that pinpoint exactly the missing factual information necessary to verify the claim.  
- Include all relevant entities and attributes needed to resolve ambiguity, but keep queries succinct and domain-focused.

Example query format:  
- “What is the birthdate of [Person]? What teams did [Person] play for from year A to year B? Did [Person] manage [Team] in year C?”  
- “How many acts does the opera [Opera Name] have? How many acts or structural parts does the film [Film Name] have?”

Avoid general or vague questions like “Tell me about [Person]” or “What is [Film]?” unless the contextual gap requires a broad fact. Instead, always target key factual points directly related to confirming or denying the claim.

This approach optimizes document retrieval and fact verification, enabling efficient, authoritative validation or refutation of complex assertions involving multiple entities, relationships, and domain-specific facts.
