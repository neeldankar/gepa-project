Task Description:
You will be given two input fields:
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that may require verification or further factual support.
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets containing partial information relevant to verifying the claim. These notes might include facts, contradictions, or missing information about entities or topics in the claim.

Your goal is to produce the output field `query` — a precise and focused question (or set of questions) aimed at retrieving the key missing or uncertain factual information needed to verify the claim. The `query` should target specific entities, attributes, relationships, or facts that have not been confirmed or sufficiently covered in the provided notes.

Key Points and Domain-Specific Knowledge:
- The claim involves named entities such as people, films, bands, roles (e.g., director, bassist), birthdates, nationalities, professions, and works (e.g., films, songs).
- Notes often contain partial or confirmatory information about these entities, but sometimes lack critical data required to verify the claim fully.
- Your query should be designed to retrieve authoritative documents or data specifically covering the missing or ambiguous elements mentioned or implied by the claim.
- The query should reflect recognition of domain-specific facts, such as:
   * Individuals’ roles and contributions (e.g., “bass player and lead vocalist who created Cobra Starship” refers to Gabe Saporta).
   * Nationality or origin of persons associated with cultural works (e.g., nationality of film directors related to 'Cannibal Holocaust').
   * Dates of birth or ages when claims relate to relative age.
   * Professional associations or occupations that confirm or refute the claim.
   * Connections between named works and their creators or contributors.

Generalizable Strategy:
1. Analyze the claim and identify the factual assertions that require verification.
2. Review the notes to establish which parts of the claim are already supported and which parts are missing, contradictory, or uncertain.
3. Formulate concise, targeted queries that seek the minimal yet sufficient data required to resolve remaining ambiguities.
4. When multiple pieces of missing information exist, combine them efficiently in the query, balancing completeness and precision.
5. Focus on retrieving documents or facts about:
   - Specific named entities not covered or unclear in the notes.
   - Attributes or relationships critical to verifying the claim.
6. Avoid overly broad or generic questions; tailor queries to close specific knowledge gaps evident from the notes.

Example:
If the claim is about the relative age of two persons, and only one birthdate is known, the query should be:  
   "What is the birthdate or age of [Person with missing info]?"

If the claim involves multiple nationalities or roles, and only partial info is available, the query should ask specifically about the identities, nationalities, or professions of the key persons involved.

By following this approach, you will help retrieve the essential documents needed to verify complex claims effectively.
