Task Description:

You will receive three input fields:

- `claim`: A textual statement containing one or more factual assertions or composite facts that need to be verified, corrected, clarified, or explained.
- `notes`: A list of short textual observations, facts, or inferences directly related to components of the claim and its context. These notes may provide partial confirmation, contradiction, clarification, or elaboration on specific elements of the claim or involved entities/events.
- `context`: A collection of textual excerpts, document summaries, or entity descriptions containing verifiable and relevant information about the entities, events, or facts referenced in the claim.

Your goal is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`:  
   A clear, concise, and well-organized synthesis of the input notes, designed specifically to support accurate fact verification of the claim. Your synthesis must:
   - Summarize all key evidentiary points strictly relevant to verifying, explaining, or correcting each distinct factual element in the claim.
   - Remove redundancies, repeated information, and unrelated or irrelevant details.
   - Explicitly highlight verified facts with precise domain-specific details when applicable, including:
     - Scientific and taxonomic distinctions (e.g., genus vs. subfamily vs. family in botanical taxonomy).
     - Correct authorship, composition metadata, or cataloguing details in music (e.g., opus numbers, composer nationality and gender).
     - Accurate film and TV production information (e.g., director names, production dates, original source material).
     - Precise art, architecture, and geography terminology (e.g., full building names with location disambiguation when necessary).
   - Explicitly identify and correct any errors, inaccuracies, or misconceptions present in the claim. For example, if a claim incorrectly classifies "Caesalpinioideae" as a genus instead of a subfamily, state this clearly.
   - Identify and emphasize when evidence supporting any part of the claim is missing or insufficient, making clear what cannot be verified.
   - Break down multi-part claims into separate factual assertions, addressing and verifying each one distinctly.
   - Use clear, factual, and logically reasoned language suitable for automated or human-assisted fact-checking.

2. `titles`:  
   Provide a list of precise and accurate document or entity titles essential for verifying or understanding the claim. These must:
   - Identify exact relevant named entities, domains, or works referenced or implied in the claim and notes.
   - Use disambiguation where necessary (e.g., specify "St Paul's Cathedral, London" rather than just "St Paul's Cathedral" if multiple exist).
   - Include major relevant named awards, films, albums, scientific taxa, events (including sponsor names and official titles), or notable persons as pertinent.
   - Be suited for retrieval systems to locate critical verification sources efficiently.

Additional Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:

- Taxonomy:  
  Clearly distinguish between hierarchical botanical ranks (e.g., genus, subfamily, family). Avoid conflating ranks. When botanical classifications are referenced, include correct scientific taxonomy to clarify or correct claims.

- Music Metadata:  
  Accurately mention compositional metadata such as opus or catalog numbers, composer nationality, gender (if relevant to claim), and recognized authoritative identifiers for compositions.

- Film and TV Production:  
  Verify film titles, director names, production years, and casting roles precisely. For multi-lingual or multinational productions, note country of origin and language details when relevant.

- Architecture and Art:  
  Provide exact names and locations of buildings or structures, including city and country if multiple entities share a name.

- Multi-Part Claims:  
  Decompose claims with multiple factual assertions and address verification or correction per assertion.

- Where the claim contains potentially conflated or incorrect facts (e.g., wrong album artist, incorrect city ranking, or unverified actor-film associations), explicitly note these issues and clarify the correct facts.

- If the notes or context do not contain evidence sufficient to verify any part of the claim, explicitly state the lack of verification possible.

- Avoid speculation or inference beyond what is strictly supported by the given notes and context.

- Organize synthesized output logically, ensuring clarity and brevity to assist downstream fact verification processes efficiently.

By adhering to this detailed guidance, your output will be precise, domain-aware, and optimally structured for downstream fact-checking workflows that demand fine-grained, domain-specific factual reasoning.

Format:

Provide outputs exactly as:
- `new_notes`: List of concise, factual statements as per the above instructions.
- `titles`: List of precise document/entity titles critical for claim verification.
