You will be given three inputs: a `claim` (a statement to be verified or analyzed), a list of `notes` (extracted relevant factual statements or evidence related to entities and concepts mentioned in the claim), and a `context` (a list of background documents or descriptions potentially relevant to the claim).

Your task is to analyze these inputs and produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. **`new_notes`**:  
   - Synthesize, consolidate, and filter the input `notes` into a concise set of key factual points that directly address or evaluate the truthfulness or implications of the `claim`.  
   - These new notes should summarize relevant facts, clarifying ambiguous or conflated information in the claim, and explicitly connect claim components with verifiable evidence.  
   - Combine redundant or repeated notes, and exclude irrelevant or unsupported information.  
   - Incorporate relevant knowledge inferred from the `context` if it improves clarity or factual grounding.  
   - The result should be a clear, factual basis that supports an explicit reasoning about the claim’s validity.

2. **`titles`**:  
   - Identify and list the titles or names of the key authoritative documents or sources from the `context` that support the assessment or fact-checking of the claim.  
   - Include all relevant documents that provide definitive or authoritative information about the entities, events, or concepts involved in the claim.  
   - Strive to cover all major supporting or contradicting sources referenced in `new_notes`.

**Additional domain-specific and procedural considerations:**  
- The task is essentially fact verification and evidence synthesis:  
  - Distinguish between closely related entities and clarify misunderstandings (e.g., differentiate writing a book *for* a magazine from writing a book separately than contributing to a magazine).  
  - Confirm the status or definitions of terms mentioned (e.g., clarify what qualifies as a periodical, establish founding dates for institutions for chronological claims).  
  - Consider general domain knowledge only if supported by contextual documents or notes but avoid assuming unsupported facts.  
  - Ensure the reasoning aligns with the strict definitions or widely accepted expert knowledge provided in the `context`.

- Use internal logical consistency checks:  
  - Identify contradictions in claims versus evidence.  
  - Address possible conflations or vague references in the claim by explicitly clarifying relationships among the entities mentioned.

- The strategy to solve the task includes:  
  - Parsing and interpreting each element of the claim carefully.  
  - Mapping claim elements to corresponding notes and context documents.  
  - Extracting or corroborating facts from multiple overlapping notes to form a logically sound summary.  
  - Selecting relevant documents from `context` that provide concrete evidence or definitions that underlie the claim or its key terms.

Deliver the final outputs—`new_notes` as a cleaned, well-argued collection of factual summaries, and `titles` as a set of contextual document titles supporting these facts—ready to aid ultimate verification or refutation of the claim.
