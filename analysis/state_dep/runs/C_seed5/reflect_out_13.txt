Task Description:

You will be given three fields as input:

- `claim`: A textual statement that makes one or more factual assertions.
- `notes`: A list of short textual observations, facts, or inferences related to the claim and the provided context. These notes may include partial evidence supporting or contradicting parts of the claim, clarifications, or explanations about terminology or entities mentioned in the claim.
- `context`: A list of textual excerpts or documents that contain facts and descriptions potentially relevant to the verification of the claim.

Your goal is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: A concise, refined list of notes that summarizes and synthesizes the key evidentiary points from the input `notes`, focusing on the information most critical to understanding, verifying, or explaining the claim. These new notes should:
   - Remove redundancies or repeated information.
   - Emphasize verified facts and relevant domain-specific distinctions (e.g., taxonomic classifications like genus vs. subfamily, event details such as sponsorship and dates).
   - Be clear and factual, reflecting both confirmations and uncertainties.
   - Highlight missing evidence or unknowns relevant to the claim.
   - Address different parts of the claim separately if applicable.

2. `titles`: A list of document or entity titles that correspond to highly relevant information for verifying or understanding the claim. These should be the key topics, entities, or documents that the claim depends on or that would be most important to retrieve for further verification. They should:
   - Reflect correct and precise article or entity names (e.g., specific event names, notable person names, scientific taxa).
   - Include all major relevant entities mentioned or implied by the claim and notes.
   - Be domain-appropriate and contextually useful for a downstream retrieval or verification system.

Additional Guidance:

- The claim may involve complex domain-specific terminology or nuanced factual relationships (e.g., botanical taxonomy distinctions, award winners and their credentials, sponsorship and event participation). Your notes should reflect careful attention to such domain knowledge.
- When the claim includes multiple parts (e.g., a two-part claim), treat them distinctly in your synthesis.
- If the claim contains inaccuracies (e.g., incorrect categorization like "Caesalpinioideae" as a genus when it is a subfamily), explicitly note these in your new notes.
- If the claim references entities or events not explicitly confirmed in the context, note that evidence is lacking or that verification is not possible.
- Use background and domain-specific facts (for example, the Nobel Prize status of Nelly Sachs, or that certain sports events are named with sponsors, or botanical classifications) as appropriate to support your synthesis.
- Your output should be logically reasoned, factually accurate, and pragmatically useful for further fact verification tasks.
- Titles should aim to cover all relevant entities needed to check the claim's veracity.

By following these instructions, you will produce precise and informative summaries and a focused list of key documents/entities that align with the factual verification needs of the claim.
