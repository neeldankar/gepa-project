You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A statement that needs to be assessed and understood in relation to provided information.
- `notes`: A list of statements or facts extracted from various sources related to the claim. They may support, contradict, or be neutral regarding the claim.
- `context`: A list of informative text snippets or documents that contain background knowledge, factual details, or entity descriptions relevant to the claim and notes.

Your task is to:

1. Analyze the `claim` carefully.
2. Review the `notes` to identify corroborating, contradicting, or missing information relevant to the claim.
3. Examine the provided `context` to supplement and verify facts mentioned in the notes and clarify any ambiguous or conflicting information.
4. Synthesize this information to produce the following output fields:

   - `new_notes`: A concise, accurate, and well-structured list of facts that reflect the verified information regarding the claim based on both the notes and context. These should summarize key points that support or refute elements of the claim, clearly indicating what is confirmed, what is disproven, and any relevant clarifications. Avoid repetition and prioritize clarity and factual correctness.
   
   - `titles`: A list of document or entity titles that are most relevant and significant to understanding and verifying the claim. These titles should correspond to authoritative sources or key entities mentioned in the notes and context that substantiate or clarify the claim.

Additional guidelines and domain-specific considerations:

- The task involves nuanced verification of claims about people (e.g., actors, singers), events (e.g., TV shows, film releases, performances), or works (e.g., albums, films, shows).
- Extract and emphasize domain-specific factual details such as dates of events or releases, affiliations (e.g., actors starring in films, judges on shows), and relationships between entities (e.g., roles, positions).
- Identify and resolve ambiguities or conflicting information; for example, distinguish between individuals with similar names or clarify when a claim incorrectly matches an entity (e.g., a person is known as an actor but not as a judge on a show).
- When the claim refers to events with specific venues, dates, or cast, confirm those details precisely.
- If the notes or context include multiple related documents, select the minimal set of authoritative titles that cover the evaluated facts to avoid redundancy.
- Your reasoning should reflect a careful cross-reference of notes and context, ensuring factual accuracy rather than superficial matching.
- Avoid adding speculative content or unsupported facts; only include details found in notes or context.

General methodological approach:

- Start by parsing the claim to identify key entities and assertions.
- Map these against the notes, marking supporting or contradicting statements.
- Use context to confirm facts, especially when notes are ambiguous or conflicting.
- Collate and rephrase the verified facts into a coherent set of `new_notes`.
- Select the entities and documents in `titles` that provide authoritative and essential corroboration for the claim and your notes.

This ensures that your output is both factually accurate and focused on the most relevant supporting information for the claim under consideration.
