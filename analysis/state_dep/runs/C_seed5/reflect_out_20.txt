Task Description:

You will be provided with three inputs:

- `claim`: A textual statement that includes one or more factual assertions which require verification or explanation.
- `notes`: A list of textual observations, facts, or logical inferences drawn directly from the provided context or related materials. These notes may corroborate, contradict, clarify, or expand upon different elements of the claim.
- `context`: A list of textual excerpts, document summaries, or detailed entity descriptions that contain potentially relevant, factual information about the entities, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim.

Your objective is to produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: A clear, concise, and well-organized synthesis of the input `notes` that adheres to the following:

   - **Verification and Distillation:** Summarize the key evidentiary points relevant to each factual element of the claim, supporting verification, explanation, or contradiction.
   - **Factually Precise with Domain Awareness:** Emphasize verified factual information with explicit attention to domain-specific distinctions relevant to taxonomy (biological classification at genus, subfamily, family levels), music metadata (e.g., opus/catalog numbers, composer nationality and gender, credited authorship), film and TV production (specific film titles, director names, actors, production dates, original work authorship, sponsorship details), art and architecture (precise building names and locations, artists/sculptors credited).
   - **Correct Identification of Errors:** Explicitly identify any factual inaccuracies or misconceptions contained in the claim, supplying accurate corrections grounded in the context (for example, correcting botanical misclassifications, misattributed authorship, or erroneous event dates).
   - **Separately Address Multiplicity:** If the claim contains multiple factual assertions, treat each as a discrete fact to verify or explain. Organize the notes correspondingly to prevent conflation or ambiguity.
   - **Eliminate Redundancy and Irrelevance:** Remove any redundant information, repeated facts, or notes irrelevant to the verification of the claim.
   - **Call Out Missing Evidence:** When the context or notes do not allow verification or explanation of certain claim components, explicitly state that no evidence is available to verify those claim aspects.
   - **Logical, Factual Language:** Use clear and objective language expressly suitable for supporting downstream fact verification pipelines, human or automated. Avoid speculation or unsupported inferences; only synthesize from provided materials and well-corroborated domain knowledge.

2. `titles`: A precise list of the names of documents, entities, or works essential to verifying, understanding, or contextualizing the claim. Your titles must:

   - **Precise Naming:** Use exact, official names including full designations to avoid ambiguity (e.g., "St Paul's Cathedral, London" vs. generic "St Paul's Cathedral"; include "100th Infantry Battalion (United States)" rather than simply "100th Infantry Battalion").
   - **Domain-Appropriate Scope:** Include all major relevant named entities or works explicitly referenced or implied in the claim and notes, such as recognized historical events, notable scientific taxa, well-documented artistic works, films, or institutional titles.
   - **Disambiguate Where Necessary:** If multiple entities share similar names (e.g., multiple universities or cathedrals), include disambiguators like location or institutional qualifiers to ensure clarity.
   - **Suitability for Retrieval:** Ensure the titles serve as precise keyphrases for downstream retrieval or fact-checking systems to identify critical corroborating documents.

Additional Guidance and Strategies:

- Pay close attention to **domain-specific terminology and hierarchical classifications** in the contexts of biology, musicology, film, and architecture, as claims often hinge on precise distinctions (e.g., subfamily vs. genus, credited composer vs. performer, lead actor vs. director roles, proper architectural nomenclature).
- When a claim juxtaposes chronological or biographical facts (e.g., birth dates, formation dates of units or bands), verify the availability of dates for each party and explicitly state when there is insufficient data.
- In cases involving multiple entities or claim components, **address them separately** to allow fine-grained factual confirmation or refutation.
- Do not infer facts beyond what the notes and context explicitly support. Avoid introducing outside knowledge unless it is widely accepted and corroborated in the provided information.
- Your synthesis should be succinct but comprehensive enough to enable fast fact verification without losing necessary detail or nuance.
- In situations with no relevant information or missing data (e.g., lack of birth dates, no record of a referenced band or album), state this transparently to avoid unsupported conclusions.
- Always **highlight factual corrections** to any inaccuracy found in the claim, referencing the domain-specific context clearly.
- Maintain consistent, factual tone and structure suitable for automated or human-assisted fact-checking pipelines.

By following these instructions, your outputs will be precise, factually accurate, contextually informative, and domain-sensitive, enabling robust downstream verification or explanation tasks across scientific, cultural, historical, and biographical domains.
