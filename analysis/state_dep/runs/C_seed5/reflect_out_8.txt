Task Overview:  
You will be given:  
- **claim**: a factual statement or assertion involving named entities (people, works, events) and their attributes or relationships that may require verification or additional factual support.  
- **notes**: a list of textual snippets containing partial, confirmatory, contradictory, or missing information relevant to verifying the claim’s key facts.

Your goal is to produce a **query** — a concise, focused question designed to retrieve the minimum necessary authoritative information to close the factual gaps and ambiguities preventing verification of the claim.

---

Detailed Task Description:  

1. **Identify factual assertions in the claim that need verification.** Examples:  
   - Identity, birthplace, or nationality of persons named.  
   - Birthdates or ages when relative timing or age comparisons are stated.  
   - Roles, professions, or occupations of individuals (e.g., musician, director, athlete).  
   - Attributions of works to particular authors, artists, or creators.  
   - The occurrence or non-occurrence of an event or participation (e.g., tournament attendance, authorship).  
   - Popularity or cultural significance comparisons between named items (e.g., games, sports).

2. **Analyze notes carefully to determine which parts of the claim are:  
   - Supported/confirmed, partially covered, contradictory, or entirely missing.**  
   Notes may contain relevant facts scattered across multiple snippets and may mention entities with variations. Identify:  
   - Missing key facts needed to verify relationships or attributes in the claim.  
   - Ambiguities such as unclear references to entities (e.g., “a game designed by Leo Colovini” without naming the specific game).  
   - Conflicting or insufficient evidence about roles, interests, or claims (e.g., whether a person is a "Texas native").  
   
3. **Formulate a precise, minimal set of targeted question(s) as your output query.** The query should:  
   - Ask for specific entities or attributes not confirmed in the notes.  
   - Combine related missing facts efficiently without overgeneralizing to avoid unnecessary broadness.  
   - Use domain-specific factual recognition (e.g., "Who was the defending champion of the 2005 China Open – Men's Doubles?", "What is the birthplace of Angelique Kerber?", "What is the global popularity of Leo Colovini’s most popular game?").  
   - Address ambiguous pronouns or vague references by specifying the entity clearly (“the author of The Hundred Secret Senses” instead of just “the author”).  
   - Particularly focus on critical attributes such as:  
     * Nationality, origin, or birthplace of persons.  
     * Roles and contributions (e.g., who created or performed a work).  
     * Dates of birth or age data when relevant to relative age claims.  
     * Participation or attendance in specific events or competitions.  
     * Confirmation or denial of interests or hobbies when relevant to the claim.  
     * Identification of individuals when the claim involves multiple persons.  

4. **Avoid overly broad or generic queries.** Tailor the query narrowly to the minimum information required to verify the claim and to resolve uncertainties evident from the notes.  

---

Domain-Specific Knowledge to Leverage:  

- Individuals’ roles and contributions within specific cultural or professional contexts (e.g., musicians who created certain bands or songs, authors of novels).  
- Naming conventions and variants of persons and works to resolve ambiguous references.  
- Typical attributes important for verification: nationality, birthplace, date of birth, professions or occupations, participation in specific events, and authorship/creation of particular works.  
- Popularity or cultural influence comparisons require targeted queries about relative reach or recognition.  
- Recognition that some events or claims may involve different categories (e.g., men’s versus women’s sports events) and should be specified to avoid conflation.  

---

Generalizable Strategy Summary:  

- Step 1: Parse the claim into atomic factual assertions.  
- Step 2: Map each assertion against the notes for confirmation, contradiction, or missing information.  
- Step 3: Identify the minimal critical knowledge gaps preventing verification.  
- Step 4: Formulate focused, specific query(ies) targeting those knowledge gaps, naming precise entities or attributes.  
- Step 5: Combine queries efficiently when multiple missing facts relate to the same entities or closely linked facts.  
- Step 6: Avoid vague questions or overbroad information requests; keep queries authoritative and direct.

---

Example Patterns:  

- When birthdate or age is unknown but relevant:  
  * “What is the birthdate or age of [Person]?”  

- When authorship or creation needs verification:  
  * “Who is the author of [Work]?” or “Did [Person] write/create [Work]?”  

- When clarifying participation in an event:  
  * “Did [Person] compete in [Event] in [Year]?”  

- When verifying identity or status of a competition champion:  
  * “Who was the defending champion of [Event] in [Year]?”  

- When comparing popularity or influence of works:  
  * “What is the global popularity or cultural reach of [Work] compared to [Other Work]?”  

---

By following these instructions, you will help retrieve the essential factual documents and data needed to accurately verify complex claims involving multiple entities and nuanced relationships.
