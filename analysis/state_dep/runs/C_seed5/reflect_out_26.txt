Task Description:  
You will be given two inputs:  
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving one or more named entities (persons, films, bands, roles, professions, nationalities, works, events, etc.) that may or may not be fully verified. The claim may involve comparisons, attributions, or categorizations that require confirming specific factual details.  
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets containing partial, confirmatory, contradictory, or missing information relevant to verifying the claim. These snippets may reference some, but often not all, elements needed to fully verify the claim.  

Your goal is to produce one or more precise, focused natural language `query` questions. These queries are intended to retrieve the minimal but sufficient factual information needed to fully and authoritatively verify the claim.

---  

Key Domain-Specific Knowledge and Considerations:  
1. **Entities & Roles:**  
   - Individuals are often referenced by name and role (e.g., "former Midtown bassist and lead vocalist who created Cobra Starship" refers to Gabe Saporta).  
   - Films and works may be referenced via character names, release dates, or productions (e.g., "Disney animated film with Cruella de Vil" refers to "One Hundred and One Dalmatians" (1961)) or nicknames/titles ("G-Force").  
   - Entities often have attributes critical for verification such as birthdates, nationalities, professional roles, or creation credits.  

2. **Relationships & Attributes:**  
   - Verify who performed which role (e.g., director, captain, lead vocalist).  
   - Confirm dates such as birthdates or release years to establish relative age or chronology.  
   - Confirm nationalities or geographic headquarters relevant to organizational claims (e.g., whether an airline is American or Swiss, where it is headquartered).  
   - Confirm genre classifications for films or other cultural products (e.g., family, comedy, action, adventure).  

3. **Verification Focus:**  
   - Identify facts missing or ambiguous in the notes, especially those essential to confirming or refuting the claim.  
   - Avoid overly broad or generic queries; tailor queries to the specific knowledge gap(s).  
   - When multiple related facts are missing, combine them efficiently into a single concise query, balancing completeness and precision.  

4. **Common Types of Verification Queries:**  
   - Birthdates or ages of named individuals when relative age is asserted.  
   - Nationality, city or country headquarters of named companies or organizations.  
   - Association of characters with specific films or works.  
   - Genre or category of films when claimed to fall under specific genres.  
   - Confirmation of professional roles or involvement in creation of works.  

---  

Generalizable Strategy for Query Generation:  
1. **Analyze the claim** thoroughly to parse factual assertions. Break down into discrete facts needing verification.  
2. **Compare with notes** to determine which facts are supported, which are missing, and which may be contradicted or ambiguous.  
3. **Identify minimal necessary missing information** to verify the claim fully.  
4. **Formulate crisp, targeted queries** addressing only that missing information — e.g., “What is the birthdate of [Person]?”, “What is the nationality and genre classification of [Film]?”, or “Which company operated the plane captained by [Captain’s Name]?”  
5. **When multiple interconnected facts are missing, combine in a logically structured query** to minimize number of retrievals needed but avoid excess breadth.  

---  

Examples of Effective Queries Given Claims and Notes:  
- For relative age claims where one birthdate is known, query only for the missing birthdate:  
  “What is the birthdate of [Person with unknown birthdate]?”  
- For nationality and headquarters verification of a company alleged to be from a specific country and location, query:  
  “Which country and city is [Company Name] headquartered in?” and/or “Did [Company Name] operate [Specific Plane/Flight]?”  
- For verifying association of characters to films and genre, query:  
  “What is the nationality and genre classification of the Disney animated film featuring [Character Name]?” plus “What is the nationality and genre classification of [Other Film Name]?”  

By following these guidelines, your queries will consistently retrieve the essential precise documents or facts needed to verify complex claims involving multiple entities and attributes.
