Task Description:
You will be given two input fields:
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion involving named entities (people, places, works, roles, dates, professions, nationalities, etc.) that needs verification or confirmation.
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets containing partial, corroborating, contradictory, or incomplete information related to verifying the claim. These snippets cover facts about the entities, their attributes, relationships, and contexts, but often leave gaps or ambiguities.

Your goal is to produce one or more precisely targeted queries in the output field `query`. Each query should ask for exactly the missing key factual information needed to definitively verify all parts of the claim. The queries must be:

- Focused on retrieving authoritative and specific data to close knowledge gaps,
- Efficient and balanced between completeness (covering all uncertain aspects) and precision (avoiding unnecessary or overly broad questions),
- Tailored to domain-specific factual points relevant to the claim’s entities and attributes.

Key Domain-Specific Knowledge and Considerations:
- Named entities encompass persons, organizations, works (films, songs, books), places, events, and biological taxa.
- Verification often hinges on:
  * Individuals’ professions, roles, contributions, and collaborations (e.g., coining terms, band memberships, directing films).
  * Dates of birth, establishment, or release to establish timelines or relative age assertions.
  * Geographical locations and jurisdictions (e.g., county vs city, island vs archipelago).
  * Relationships between entities (e.g., who collaborated with whom, who did what role in a production).
  * Specificity in naming (e.g., which subspecies is found in which park exactly).
  * Confirmations or contradictions about where and how an entity or fact is situated.
- Partial notes often provide overlapping information but do not conclusively cover all necessary attributes or relationships; queries should prioritize missing or ambiguous data.
- Avoid redundancy by combining related missing facts into concise questions.

Generalizable Strategy:
1. Dissect the claim into atomic factual assertions requiring confirmation (e.g., existence, location, date, profession, relationship).
2. Cross-reference the notes to identify what is confirmed, contradicted, or absent.
3. Identify precisely which entities or facts remain unclear or unverified.
4. Formulate queries that ask specifically for those entities’ key attributes or relationships:
   - If the claim contains multiple unverified facts, structure queries so they encompass them together without ambiguity.
   - When the claim involves a comparison (e.g., relative ages, shared professions), ensure queries retrieve the missing data for all parties.
5. Include in the query the minimal disambiguating context (e.g., full names, exact works, or locations) to avoid vague or overly broad searches.
6. Do not ask for information already clearly confirmed by notes unless needed for relative comparison.

Examples of query focus:
- For verifying locations: "Which colleges are located in Clark County, Nevada besides UNLV?" or "Are both Teide National Park and Caldera de Taburiente National Park located in the Canary Islands, and on which islands?"
- For verifying professions or roles: "What are the professions of Stephen Jay Gould and Ann Beattie?" or "Who was the director and what is the nationality of the film 'Cannibal Holocaust'?"
- For verifying birth dates or ages: "What is the birthdate of [Person]?" when verifying relative age claims.
- For verifying contributions or collaborations: "Who coined the word 'exaptation' with Elisabeth Vrba?" or "Who is the bass player and lead vocalist who created Cobra Starship?"

Formatting:
- Output one or more succinct, clear, and directly relevant questions in the `query` field.
- Avoid redundant or overly broad questions that would retrieve unnecessary or unrelated information.
- Combine missing facts efficiently when possible without sacrificing clarity.

This approach ensures retrieval of precise authoritative information to fully verify or refute the claim given partial notes, across diverse domains including biography, geography, arts, sciences, and taxonomy.
