Task Description:
You will be provided with two inputs:  
- `claim`: A factual assertion involving named entities such as people (actors, musicians, directors), artistic works (songs, films, commercials), their roles (e.g., vocalist, director, actor), birthdates, nationalities, and other related attributes. The claim may combine multiple facts, relative comparisons (e.g., birth order), or associations between entities and works.  
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets containing partial, sometimes overlapping, sometimes contradictory or incomplete information relevant to verifying the claim. The notes may confirm certain facts, deny others, or leave key details ambiguous or missing.

Your task is to output a single field, `query` — a precise, concise, and focused question or set of questions, aimed specifically at retrieving the smallest and most essential set of missing or unconfirmed facts required to verify the entire claim fully.  

The `query` must:  
- Identify the exact factual gaps in the notes relative to the claim (e.g., unknown birthdates, unclear identities, nationality ambiguities, unknown associations or roles).  
- Target very specific named entities (e.g., identifying a particular "other musician" or confirming the nationality of a director).  
- Focus on critical attributes or relationships tied to those entities that are central to verifying the claim (e.g., role in a band or film, birthdate, nationality).  
- When multiple missing facts are involved, combine them efficiently in a single query without being overly broad or generic.  

Domain-Specific Knowledge to Leverage:  
- Named entities often include musicians, actors, film directors, and their works (films, songs, commercials).  
- Verification often hinges on "who did what" — e.g., who sang a particular song, who directed a film, who acted in which role.  
- Birthdates and nationalities are commonly key to verifying relative age or identity claims.  
- Recognizing common roles and associations: e.g.,  
    * Knowing that "bass player and lead vocalist who created Cobra Starship" refers to Gabe Saporta.  
    * Understanding that certain works (films, TV episodes, commercials) have well-documented casts and crews.  
- Being aware that sometimes the claim references incorrect or confused information (e.g., misattributed nationality or role), so queries should focus sharply on confirming or refuting these specific points.

Generalizable Strategy:  
1. Analyze the claim and break it down into discrete factual assertions requiring verification.  
2. Cross-check these points against the notes to determine which facts are fully confirmed, which are contradicted, and which remain unaddressed or ambiguous.  
3. Formulate concise queries narrowly aimed to retrieve the minimal authoritative information that closes these gaps.  
4. Combine multiple related fact queries logically when needed, but avoid overly broad or generic questions that might return irrelevant data.  
5. Ensure the queries explicitly request critical missing data such as:  
    - "Who is [ambiguous entity]?"  
    - "What is the birthdate or nationality of [person]?"  
    - "Who performed/sang/acted/directed [specific work]?"  
    - "What is the role of [person] in [work or group]?"  
6. Structure questions with clarity to maximize retrieval of authoritative documents covering the precise verification point.

Key Points Variant Examples:  
- If verifying relative ages but only one birthdate is known:  
   Query: "What is the birthdate or age of [person with missing info]?"  
- If verifying association with a band or group:  
   Query: "Is [person] a member of [band], and what role do they perform?"  
- If verifying contradictory information about a nationality or identity:  
   Query: "What is the nationality of [person], and what is their profession?"  
- If multiple open points exist, integrate them concisely:  
   Query: "Who is [ambiguous person], what is their birthdate, and what is their role in [work or group]?"

Avoid:  
- Generic open-ended questions like “Tell me everything about [entity].”  
- Vague questions that do not directly address the specific factual gaps.  
- Overly long or multi-part questions that could cause dilution of focus.

By adhering to these principles, your queries will efficiently guide document retrieval towards the minimal necessary authoritative information to fully verify or falsify the factual claims provided.
