Task Description:
You will be provided with two inputs:
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that requires verification, often including named entities (people, films, bands, roles, dates, professions, works, etc.).
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets containing partial, confirmatory, contradictory, or missing information about some or all entities or facts mentioned in the claim.

Your role is to generate a well-formulated, concise, and focused output named `query` — that asks for the minimal yet crucial missing or ambiguous factual information necessary to verify the claim.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Knowledge to Consider:
- Claims often mention named entities with domain-specific roles or attributes, such as film directors, actors, musicians, birthdates, professions, nationalities, or specific works (films, songs, shows).
- Notes may confirm some elements but often leave gaps — for example, missing birthdates, unknown release years, or unclear linkages between persons and roles.
- Queries should:
  * Target specific entities or attributes lacking confirmation.
  * Focus on essential details like birthdates, professions, official titles of roles or characters, collaborators, release or production years, and nationalities.
  * Resolve ambiguities such as different names for the same role (e.g., "The King of Cocaine" vs. actual character name).
  * Avoid overly broad or general questions by tailoring to what is strictly missing for verification.

Generalizable Strategy for Query Construction:
1. Identify all discrete factual components of the claim (persons, works, dates, roles, titles).
2. Compare these against the information in the notes to find confirmed vs. unconfirmed facts.
3. Distill the minimal set of precise information needed to fill gaps preventing full verification.
4. Formulate direct, clear questions aimed at retrieving authoritative facts on those gaps.
5. When multiple gaps exist, combine queries efficiently but avoid vagueness.
6. Ensure queries specifically mention the relevant entities or works to yield targeted, retrievable facts.
7. If the claim involves relationships (e.g., shared professions, collaborations), explicitly query about these relationships.
8. If the claim involves comparing dates (e.g., relative birthdates, production years), ensure queries ask for the unknown dates.

Examples and Domain Specifics:
- If birthdate of one person is missing to verify who is older, query:  
  "What is the birthdate or age of [PersonName]?"
- If a claim refers to professional roles or collaborations (such as coining a term), query:  
  "What is the profession of [PersonName]?" or  
  "Did [PersonA] collaborate with [PersonB]? What is [PersonB]'s profession?"
- If a film’s production year is unknown, query:  
  "What is the production or release year of the film [FilmTitle]?"
- If an actor’s nomination status or role title is unclear, query:  
  "Did [ActorName] receive a [award name, e.g., Golden Globe] nomination for playing [CharacterName or role description]?"
- Clarify ambiguous role titles or official character names if notes mention informal or alternate titles.

By following these instructions, you will generate effective, precise queries that are key to retrieving just the missing critical details needed for fact verification of complex claims involving multiple domain-specific elements.
