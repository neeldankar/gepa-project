Task Description:
You will be provided with two inputs:
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that may require validation, correction, or additional evidence.
- `notes`: a collection of textual snippets containing partial, confirmatory, contradictory, or missing information relevant to verifying the claim. These notes often refer to entities, dates, roles, relationships, or works mentioned in the claim.

Your task is to generate an output field called `query` — a well-defined, focused, and concise question (or set of related questions) specifically designed to retrieve the crucial missing or uncertain factual information needed to verify the claim. 

The key objective is to identify what precise data or authoritative references are lacking or ambiguous from the notes, and then to formulate queries that target these gaps directly, minimizing unnecessary breadth or vagueness.

Domain-Specific and Niche Knowledge to Incorporate:
- The entities involved often include specific people (with relationships such as teachers, roles such as musicians, directors), locations (counties, cities, provinces, countries), dates (birth, death, chart peaks), cultural works (songs, films, discographies), professions, nationalities, and their interrelations.
- Important relational facts include:
  * Jurisdictional relationships between places (e.g., which city governs which county).
  * Exact birthdates or death dates where relative age or chronology matters.
  * Attribution of roles and contributions in creative works (e.g., who originally recorded a song, who is the director of a film).
  * Confirmation of connections between entities (e.g., a shared teacher between two people).
  * Chart histories, original performers vs. cover artists, and genre affiliations.
- Recognize that some relevant terms or roles may be domain-specific, such as “bass player and lead vocalist who created [band name]” or “Hit song on Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks.”

Generalizable Strategy for Query Generation:
1. Break down the claim into discrete factual assertions requiring validation.
2. Evaluate the notes to determine which assertions are fully supported, which have contradictory data, and which are missing key information.
3. Identify the minimal critical pieces of information or authoritative references that remain unknown or ambiguous.
4. Frame sharp, specific queries targeted at the named entities, attributes, or relationships that need resolving.
5. When multiple factual gaps exist, combine related questions efficiently while maintaining clarity and focus.
6. Avoid broad or generic questions; aim for precision that directs retrieval towards key missing documents or facts.
7. Incorporate domain-aware phrasing reflecting the nature of the entities or data sought.

Examples of Query Focus:
- If a claim involves the jurisdictional relationship of a county, but the governing city is unknown:  
  “What city exercises jurisdiction over [County Name] in [Province or Country]?”
- If a claim concerns relative birthdates but only one birthdate is known:  
  “What is the birthdate or age of [Person B]?”
- If a claim names multiple songs and their recording histories are unclear:  
  “Which artist first recorded the song [Song Title]? Was this artist a [genre] musician?”
- If a claim links two people via a shared teacher but the teacher’s identity is missing:  
  “Who was the science teacher of [Person A]? Did this teacher also teach [Person B]?”
- If a claim asserts chart performance tied to original artists, verify completeness:  
  “Are there any #1 Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks songs by [Artist] beyond the known four? For those, who originally recorded them, and what is the original artist’s genre?”

By applying these detailed guidelines, you will craft precise, information-seeking queries that enable retrieval of exactly those authoritative documents needed to confirm or refute the claim, optimizing verification accuracy and efficiency.
