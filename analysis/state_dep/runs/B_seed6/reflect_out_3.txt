Task Description:

You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a factual statement requiring verification or fact-checking. The `notes` contain extracted or summarized information related to the claim, including potential evidence that supports, contradicts, or is neutral toward the claim. The goal is to generate an effective *search query* (output field `query`) that can be used to retrieve authoritative documents or data to verify the truthfulness of the claim.

Your task is to carefully analyze both the claim and the notes, then compose a concise, targeted search query designed to maximize relevant retrieval and minimize irrelevant results. This search query will guide downstream document retrieval systems to find evidence that confirms or refutes the claim.

Detailed instructions for generating the query:

1. **Analyze the Claim:**
   - Identify the key entities (people, organizations, events, places, championships, films, songs).
   - Pinpoint the main factual assertions (dates, awards, titles, roles, competition results, historical facts).
   - Understand the relationships between entities (e.g., who acted in which movie, who won which championship, founding dates of sports teams).
   - Note any temporal bounds (e.g., specific seasons, years, decades).
   - Include nicknames, alternate names, or common epithets if relevant (e.g., "The King of Cocaine" = Pablo Escobar).

2. **Review the Notes:**
   - Extract domain-specific context from the notes that refine or clarify the claim.
   - Identify corrections, contradictions, or confirmations embedded within the notes.
   - Identify relevant domain-specific facts that may help narrow or specify the search query. For example:
     - Baseball franchises: founding years, franchise relocations, managers, player tenure.
     - Film domain: actors’ filmographies, familial relations, awards received.
     - Motorsports: seasons, driver participation years, winners, nationality.
     - Geographic details: place names, administrative divisions, jurisdictions.
   - Recognize when some elements in the claim might be false or partially true from the notes, and incorporate key terms to check those facts explicitly.

3. **Formulating the Query:**
   - The query should be a natural language question or a targeted keyword phrase (or combination) that directly asks about the core elements necessary to verify the claim.
   - Include all critical named entities, dates, and unique identifiers that anchor the claim in a factual context.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad search terms; prefer specificity to guide retrieval towards authoritative sources.
   - When applicable, combine search terms with logical connectors (e.g., AND, OR) or phrase them as concise fact-check questions (e.g., “Who won X in year Y?”, “Did person A receive award B for role C?”, “Which songs by artist D reached number one and who originally recorded them?”).
   - Include alternative names or aliases if mentioned or deducible (e.g., “Pablo Escobar” and “The King of Cocaine”).

4. **Domain-Specific Nuances:**
   - **Music domain:** Focus on chart positions such as "Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks," original recording artists, and genre classifications.
   - **Film and TV domain:** Confirm main cast members’ roles and their awards (e.g., Golden Globe nominations), also note familial or biographical relationships when relevant.
   - **Sports (especially motorsports and baseball):** Check start and end years of participation, season numbering equivalences (e.g., "28th season = 2000"), nationality of competitors, and record of winners.
   - **Geography:** Clarify administrative divisions and their hierarchical relationships where claims involve location-based facts.

5. **Implementation Strategies:**
   - Use the notes to rule out impossibilities or prior refutations in the query construction by explicitly including key facts the claim contradicts.
   - Construct factual verification queries rather than open-ended broad questions.
   - Prioritize queries that can retrieve multiple relevant documents (e.g., one query that simultaneously addresses claim aspects and related entities).
   - When the notes identify misalignments (e.g., claimed facts are false), incorporate those exact elements for checking.

6. **Desired Output:**
   - Output only the field `query` with a single refined search query string.
   - The query should be concise yet comprehensive enough to yield authoritative evidence for or against the claim.
   - Avoid extraneous explanation or analysis in the final output.

By following these instructions, you will generate effective search queries tailored to domain-specific factual claims across varied topics such as music chart history, filmographies and awards, sports seasons and participants, and geographic or political facts. The query will enable precise and productive evidence retrieval for accurate claim verification.
