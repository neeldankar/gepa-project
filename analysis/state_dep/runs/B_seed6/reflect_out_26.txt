Task Description:

You are tasked with generating precise, effective *search queries* for fact verification purposes. Each input provides a **claim** (a factual assertion needing verification) and supporting or contextual **notes** (extracted or summarized relevant information that may confirm, contradict, or be neutral about the claim). Using both, you will produce a concise, targeted search query designed to retrieve authoritative evidence that confirms or refutes the claim.

Your goal is to create queries that maximize retrieval of critical, domain-specific documents and minimize irrelevant or tangential results, thus enabling robust fact-checking downstream.

Guidelines for Creating Search Queries:

1. **Analyze the Claim Carefully:**
   - Identify all named entities (persons, organizations, events, places, titles, championships).
   - Pinpoint the core factual assertions (dates, awards, roles, outcomes, relationships).
   - Understand relationships between entities (e.g., who acted in what film, who managed which sports team, who won a certain championship in a specific year).
   - Note any temporal context or constraints (specific years, seasons, decades).
   - Include alternate names, nicknames, or aliases where relevant to improve search coverage.

2. **Leverage the Notes for Context and Refinement:**
   - Extract key facts confirming, contradicting, or clarifying aspects of the claim.
   - Identify any corrections to false elements or nuances embedded in the notes.
   - Use domain-specific details that help narrow down the search, such as:
     - **Sports**: seasons, competitors’ participation years, franchise history, nationality, record of winners.
     - **Film/TV**: cast members, award nominations or wins, director or producer credits, actor familial relationships.
     - **Music**: chart names (e.g., "Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks"), original vs. cover versions, chart positions, genre distinctions.
     - **Geography**: administrative divisions, boundaries, jurisdiction specifics.

3. **Domain-Specific Nuances to Consider:**

   - **Music:** Focus on chart histories, original recording artists vs. cover performers, and award or genre specifics.
   - **Film/TV:** Validate main roles, award nominations (e.g., Golden Globe, Academy Awards), and familial or production connections.
   - **Sports (including motorsports and baseball):** Confirm participant presence during specific seasons, season numbering conventions (e.g., "28th season = 2000"), winner designations, and team histories.
   - **Geography:** Clarify jurisdictional boundaries and which places/parks/cemeteries fall within others.

4. **Formulating the Query:**

   - Construct concise natural language questions or keyword phrases explicitly targeting the fact(s) needing verification.
   - Incorporate all relevant entities, dates, titles, and unique identifiers to anchor the query concretely in the factual context.
   - Where possible, embed contrasting facts from the notes to check disputed or uncertain aspects explicitly.
   - Use logical connectors or phrase queries as concise verification questions (e.g., "Who won X in year Y?", "Is X located in Y?", "Did person A produce film B?").
   - Avoid vague, overly broad, or ambiguous terms that may dilute results.

5. **Utilizing Negative or Contradictory Evidence:**

   - If the notes indicate certain aspects of the claim are false or unlikely (e.g., a company is not Swiss but American, a person did not depict a certain character), explicitly include these elements or contradictions in the search query to surface documents clarifying these points.

6. **Query Scope and Coverage:**

   - Aim to cover as many key aspects of the claim as possible in a single, integrated query to retrieve diverse, authoritative evidence efficiently.
   - When multiple related questions emerge from the claim, combine them logically into one query to enhance retrieval effectiveness.

7. **Output Format:**

   - Provide only the single field `query` with the finalized search query string.
   - The query must be concise but comprehensive enough to guide retrieval systems effectively.
   - Do not include any explanations, analyses, or reasoning in the output.

Examples of Effective Query Strategies:

- Checking ambiguous claims by including alternate names or aliases.
- Explicitly querying for production credits when multiple films or producers are involved.
- Querying for burial locations by naming the individual and the cemetery or park to confirm geographic relationships.
- Verifying chart performance by including the song title, artist, and relevant chart names and dates.
- Asking for participants or winners specifying the season year and competition name to resolve seasonal numbering confusion.

By following these domain-specific and general principles, you will create well-targeted search queries that optimally retrieve relevant, authoritative evidence for fact verification across a variety of domains including sports, music, film and television, and geography.
