Task Description:

You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`.

- `claim`: A factual statement that requires verification or fact-checking.
- `notes`: Extracted or summarized information related to the claim. This may include evidence that supports, contradicts, or is neutral toward the claim.

Your goal is to generate a highly effective *search query* (output field `query`) that a document retrieval system can use to find authoritative sources confirming or refuting the claim.

---

Detailed Instructions for Generating the Query:

1. **Analyze the `claim`:**
   - Identify key named entities such as people, organizations, teams, brands, products, events, locations, championships, films, albums, songs, awards, or historical facts.
   - Isolate the core factual assertions: dates, results, titles, roles, and relationships between these entities (e.g., who scored in which match, who acted in which film, founding dates of organizations).
   - Determine any temporal constraints (specific years, seasons, decades).
   - Include alternate names, aliases, nicknames, or epithets if relevant.
   - Break down complex claims into their essential components (multiple facts or comparisons).

2. **Process the `notes`:**
   - Extract and integrate domain-specific context to refine or clarify the claim.
   - Identify contradictions, corrections, confirmations, or lack of evidence about claim elements.
   - Detect domain-specific facts that help narrow search scope. For example:
     - **Sports (especially football/soccer, baseball, motorsports):** verify player participation years, event winners, competition final results, and nicknames of teams or players; recognize season numbering or calendar year equivalences.
     - **Music:** focus on chart histories like "Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks," original vs. cover recordings, song release years, and genre categories.
     - **Film & TV:** confirm cast members, their roles, familial relationships, and awards like Golden Globe nominations.
     - **Technology/Computers/Gaming:** verify platform origins, manufacturer country, and product-release relationships.
     - **Geography/Administrative Divisions:** understand geopolitical hierarchies and jurisdictional facts relevant to claims.
   - Note any elements in the claim that are demonstrably false or unsubstantiated from the notes; explicitly target those in the query to test accuracy.

3. **Formulate the Search Query:**
   - Construct a concise, focused natural language question or targeted keyword phrase addressing the critical fact(s) to verify.
   - Include all main entities, precise dates, and unique identifiers anchoring the claim.
   - When relevant, add logical connectors (AND, OR) or phrase as specific fact-check questions (e.g., "Who scored in X final 2013?", "Is Y a nickname for Z team?", "Which platform was Proof of Destruction developed for?").
   - Avoid general or ambiguous terms; prioritize specificity to retrieve authoritative, relevant documents.
   - Incorporate alternative names or aliases to widen relevant retrieval without diluting focus.
   - Address multiple claim components simultaneously, but always keep the query concise and sharply targeted.

4. **Domain-Specific Nuances and Examples:**
   - **Music:** Emphasize chart positions, original recording artists versus cover artists, specific album and song titles, and genre indicators.
   - **Film/TV:** Confirm roles alongside award nominations or wins. Include familial ties if pertinent.
   - **Sports:** Include exact season numbering or year equivalences, players’ nationalities, team nicknames, final match participants and results.
   - **Technology/Gaming:** Clearly differentiate platform origins by manufacturers/countries; verify platform-game associations.
   - **Geography:** Clarify administrative hierarchies (e.g., counties, states, municipalities).

5. **Utilize the Notes to Eliminate False Leads:**
   - If the notes refute components of the claim, explicitly include those components in the query to validate their falsehood.
   - Prioritize queries that increase the chances of retrieving multiple relevant documents covering all significant facets.

6. **Final Output Requirements:**
   - Output only one field: `query`.
   - The `query` must be a single string representing the optimized search string or question.
   - Do not include explanations, reasoning, or any other text besides the query.

---

**General Strategy:**

- Dissect the claim into core factual components and key entities.
- Use evidence from the notes to confirm which parts of the claim are credible and which need explicit verification.
- Build a precise search query combining all necessary elements (entities, dates, alternate names) tailored to the domain.
- Formulate queries as targeted fact-check questions or highly specific keyword combinations.
- Ensure the query is comprehensive enough to retrieve multiple authoritative sources but narrow enough to avoid irrelevant noise.

By following this approach and observing domain-specific constraints and nuances, the resulting queries will efficiently guide evidence retrieval systems to verify the truthfulness of diverse factual claims.
