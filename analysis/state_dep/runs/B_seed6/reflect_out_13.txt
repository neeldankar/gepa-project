Task Description:

You will receive two fields: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a factual assertion that requires verification. The `notes` provide extracted or summarized domain-relevant information about entities and facts related to the claim. Your core task is to generate a precise, concise, and effective *search query* (output field: `query`) to enable retrieval of authoritative sources that can confirm or refute the claim.

Your responsibilities and best practices are:

1. Deeply Analyze the Claim:  
   - Extract critical named entities (people, places, organizations, events, awards, titles).  
   - Identify the exact factual assertions (dates, roles, relationships, outcomes, awards, competitions, authorial attributions).  
   - Note relationships and dependencies between entities (e.g., familial relationships, award recipients, tenure periods).  
   - Identify relevant temporal bounds (years, seasons, establishing or founding dates).  
   - Include alternate names, common epithets, or known aliases if applicable to increase search coverage.

2. Scrutinize the Notes for Domain Context and Contradictions:  
   - Extract confirming or contradictory facts to clarify ambiguities in the claim.  
   - Note corrections or factual refinements in the notes that challenge or qualify parts of the claim.  
   - Use domain knowledge that appears in the notes to disambiguate terms or specify details that narrow the focus. For example:  
     - Music domain: Chart types (e.g., Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks), original vs. cover artist, genre distinctions.  
     - Film & TV domain: Cast roles, award nominations (Golden Globes, Oscars), familial connections, filmography years.  
     - Sports domain (motorsports, baseball): Competition years, season numbering equivalence (e.g., 28th season corresponding to the year 2000), team founding or relocation dates, player tenures, nationalities.  
     - Geographic and historical claims: Administrative divisions, hierarchical place relationships.

3. Formulate the Search Query to Target Verification:  
   - Frame the query as a natural language question or as a focused keyword phrase combining all salient elements required to judge claim truthfulness.  
   - Include all key entities and unique identifiers that pinpoint the factual core (e.g., "Did Rudolf Höss serve as longest commandant of Auschwitz?", "Who conducted negotiations for Auschwitz operations: Rudolf Höss or Kurt Gerstein?", "Did Theodore Garland, Jr. complete postdoctoral training at University of Washington established before Texas Christian University?").  
   - Incorporate temporal bounds to avoid broad ambiguous retrievals.  
   - When notes highlight partial truths or direct refutations, explicitly include those points to check in the query to ensure retrieval of contrary evidence.  
   - Use logical connectors (AND, OR) when grouping multiple fact-check components into a single query to aid comprehensive evidence retrieval.  
   - Avoid vague or generic terms that invite irrelevant results.

4. Leverage Domain-Specific Nuances for Precision:  
   - Music: Emphasize chart history, chart names, genre-specific hits, original vs. cover versions, artist aliases.  
   - Film/TV: Verify cast roles, awards, nominations, familial relations among actors, film credits year.  
   - Sports: Match season numbers with years, confirm participation periods, driver or player nationalities, founding histories of franchises or teams.  
   - Geography: Clarify territorial or administrative relationships when locations are involved in the claim.

5. Query Construction Strategies:  
   - Prioritize creating queries that can retrieve multiple relevant documents at once, reducing the number of separate queries needed for full verification.  
   - Convert the claim’s factual elements into direct verification questions or precise keyword combinations.  
   - Use information from the notes to exclude or check claim elements that have been contradicted or corrected.  
   - Ensure queries target authoritative, fact-rich documents rather than broad, general text.

6. Final Output Requirements:  
   - Output only the field `query` with a single search query string.  
   - The query must balance conciseness and comprehensiveness to maximize retrieval relevance.  
   - Avoid explanations or additional commentary in the final output.

By following these instructions you will produce domain-aware, fact-specific, and highly focused search queries optimized for retrieving authoritative evidence efficiently and fully verifying factual claims across domains such as music chart histories, filmographies and awards, sports participation and records, and geographical or historical facts.
