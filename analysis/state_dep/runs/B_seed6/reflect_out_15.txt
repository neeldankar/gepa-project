You are given three fields as input:

1. `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that may or may not be fully accurate.
2. `notes`: A list of textual pieces of evidence or observations, often extracted from supporting documents, which discuss or relate to the entities, events, or assertions in the claim. These notes may include direct fact checks, corrections, or contextual information.
3. `context`: A list of detailed background information or excerpts from relevant knowledge sources such as encyclopedic entries, biographies, filmographies, discographies, company histories, or other domain-specific summaries relevant to the entities mentioned in the claim.

Your task is to:

- Analyze the `claim` carefully and compare it against the information available in both the `notes` and `context`.
- Identify which parts of the claim are supported, partially correct, or contradicted, based on the combined evidence.
- Produce two output fields:

  1. `new_notes`: A concise, coherent summary of the key factual findings derived from the `notes` and `context` as they relate to verifying the claim. This should highlight direct confirmations, clarifications, or refutations of the claim’s components. Avoid redundant or overly detailed information; prioritize clarity and relevance to the claim’s correctness.
  
  2. `titles`: A list of the most relevant titles (document or topic headings) from the `context` that underpin your assessment. These should include key entities such as people, films, music groups, songs, companies, or other notable topics directly relevant to the claim and your reasoning.

Additional requirements and domain-specific details to consider:

- Claims often involve verifying relationships between named entities (e.g., actor appearances in films, musicians’ collaborations, group membership and timing, production companies connected to films).
- Notes and context may mention detailed domain information such as film production companies, cast lists, album and single release dates, membership changes in musical groups, songwriting credits, and professional roles of individuals.
- Pay attention to the precision of the claim’s components: e.g., exact film titles, years of release, involved personnel, company names, release dates, and correct associations.
- Correct factual resolution often requires identifying contradictions or modifications, such as an entity not belonging to a certain group at a certain time or a misattributed collaborator.
- The `titles` should be comprehensive enough to give an overview of the primary supporting documents but focused on the most relevant and authoritative sources related to the claim components.
- When possible, aim to cover all critical aspects in the `new_notes` that validate or refute the claim, leaving no key mismatches unexplained.

Use the following reasoning strategy:

- Parse the claim into atomic factual assertions.
- Cross-reference each assertion with evidence in `notes` and `context`.
- Formulate an overall judgment reflecting partial or complete correctness.
- Construct `new_notes` that summarize your diagnostic reasoning.
- Identify and select `titles` that are crucial to your verification.

Keep your outputs factual, clear, and succinct to assist downstream use for claim verification or fact-checking purposes.
