Task Overview:

You will receive two textual inputs: a factual claim (`claim`) and a collection of related context information (`notes`). Your goal is to produce a single, precise, and well-targeted *search query* (`query`) that can be used to find authoritative evidence to verify or refute the claim.

This task involves deep analysis and synthesis of both the claim and the notes to identify the key factual points and domain-specific nuances before constructing a query optimized for retrieval of relevant documents or data.

---

Detailed Instructions for Query Generation:

1. **Analyze the Claim Thoroughly:**

   - Identify **key named entities** such as people, organizations, locations, events, competitions, awards, titles, and works (books, films, songs).
   - Pinpoint the **main factual assertions** embedded in the claim, including dates, quantities, rankings, affiliations, roles, and outcomes.
   - Note explicit or implicit **temporal bounds** (specific years, events, festival editions, sports seasons).
   - Detect **relationships between entities**, such as who acted in a particular film, who won a championship, or which organization is associated with a given event.
   - Incorporate **alternate names, nicknames, or aliases** where appropriate to increase recall (e.g., "The King of Cocaine" for Pablo Escobar).

2. **Extract Critical Context from the Notes:**

   - Identify any **domain-specific clarifications or corrections** to the claim, such as the actual taxonomy rank (genus vs subfamily), veracity of associated facts, or noted contradictions.
   - Extract **supportive, contradictory, or neutral facts**, especially those related to temporal, relational, or categorical details.
   - Recognize when the notes reveal **partial truths or errors** within the claim, and include these as explicit checks in the query.
   - Collect **domain-specific data points** that help disambiguate or refine the claim, e.g.:
     - **Music domain:** Chart names (e.g., Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks), original recording artists, song covers, genre.
     - **Film and TV domain:** Award events and specific wins or nominations, main cast and their historical biographical facts (e.g., military service), film genre and screening details.
     - **Sports domain:** Specific seasons with numbering equivalences (e.g., 28th season = year 2000), participant career spans, nationality, winners and links to competitions.
     - **Geography domain:** Administrative divisions and hierarchy, place name disambiguations, jurisdictions.

3. **Formulate a Focused and Clear Search Query:**

   - Construct a **natural language question or keyword phrase** that directly targets the core verification points.
   - Combine **all critical entities, dates, and unique identifiers** into the query to maximize retrieval relevance.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad terms; prefer **specific, well-scoped queries** that imply fact-checking rather than open-ended investigation.
   - When relevant, use logical connectors (AND, OR) to combine concepts succinctly or phrase the query as a purpose-driven question:
     - Examples:
       - “Who won [award] at [event] in [year]?”
       - “Was [person] a [role/occupation/award winner] in [work]?”
       - “Is [term] a genus or subfamily and how many species does it contain compared to [other term]?”
       - “Did any musical film win the Golden Bear at the Berlin International Film Festival in [year], and was any lead actor a U.S. Navy Combat veteran in WWII?”
   - Include **alternative names or aliases** if the claim or notes indicate their relevance.

4. **Domain-Specific Nuances and Requirements:**

   - **Music:** Emphasize chart history (Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks and equivalents), original vs cover versions, and artist relations.
   - **Film and TV:** Focus on confirming lead actor roles, awards (Golden Globe, Golden Bear, etc.), relationships or biographical facts (e.g., military service), and festival details (edition number, screening years).
   - **Sports (Motorsports, Baseball):** Prioritize exact season years, participant tenure dates, champion identities, and nationality; note that some season numbers correspond to specific years and may not be intuitive.
   - **Geography:** Clarify administrative hierarchies, place naming variants, and jurisdictional relationships.

5. **Use Notes Strategically to Refine the Query:**

   - Use the notes to **rule out impossible or false claims** by including those contested elements explicitly in the query.
   - When the claim is partially false or imprecise, construct the query to **verify those precise points** rather than the entire claim broadly.
   - Incorporate any **missing or uncertain facts** flagged in the notes, such as unconfirmed publication years, lack of known veteran status, or unverified periodical status.

6. **Query Output Requirements:**

   - Output **only** the field `query` with a **single concise but comprehensive** search query string.
   - The query must be compact but contain enough context to retrieve multiple relevant authoritative documents.
   - Do **not** include commentary, explanations, or reasoning in the output.
   - Strive for an effective balance between **specificity to reduce noise** and **comprehensiveness to avoid missing key facts**.

---

Generalizable Strategy Recap:

- **Dissect Complex Claims:** Break multiple assertions down to their atomic factual points.
- **Leverage Notes Fully:** Extract domain-specific context and contradictions to frame the fact-check question precisely.
- **Incorporate Entities and Temporal Anchors:** Use all key names, titles, dates, and aliases.
- **Phrase Queries as Directed Fact-Checking Questions:** Target the essential verification needs crisply.
- **Domain Awareness:** Tailor queries to the nuances of music, film, sports, or geography domains.
- **Exclude Overly Broad Queries:** Avoid vague terms that dilute retrieval focus.
- **Output Precision:** Produce a single, clean query without extra text.

---

By integrating these elements, you will generate effective, domain-aware queries that guide retrieval systems to precise evidence sets for claim verification across diverse factual domains.
