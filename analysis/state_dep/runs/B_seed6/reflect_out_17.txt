Given three input fields:

- `claim`: A statement or assertion about a specific factual or historical scenario.
- `notes`: A list of textual excerpts or factoids relevant to the claim, potentially containing repeated or partially overlapping information.
- `context`: A list of more detailed, encyclopedic-style text passages (e.g., sections or summaries from articles) providing background knowledge and more comprehensive details related to the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

- `new_notes`: A concise, logically organized set of factual sentences drawn and synthesized from the input `notes` and `context` that directly support or clarify the claim. The `new_notes` should:
  - Combine multiple facts into clear, non-redundant statements.
  - Include key domain-specific facts that corroborate or explain the claim.
  - Summarize essential entities (people, places, events, organizations) involved with accurate temporal, geographical, or relational details.
  - Extract and emphasize relevant connections such as dates, place names, roles or offices held, affiliations, relationships between individuals, or sponsorship links, depending on the domain of the claim.

- `titles`: A list of relevant document or knowledge source titles (topics or entity names) that are the most pertinent evidence bases for validating the claim. The titles should:
  - Reflect the key entities or topics featured in the synthesized `new_notes`.
  - Cover all aspects of the claim to provide a comprehensive basis for verification.
  - Include official names of people, organizations, events, locations, or entities mentioned in the claim or its supporting facts.
  - Be diverse enough to cover all major angles of the claim but no more than necessary.

Additional domain-specific details and guidance:

1. **Entity identification and disambiguation**: Recognize and emphasize the main entities relevant to the claim—for example, a sports team and a bowl game and its sponsor; a political figure, their rival, and associated historical movements; a musician and the bands and geographical locations associated with them.

2. **Temporal and geographical precision**: Incorporate dates (e.g., years, seasons, game dates, offices held) and locations (cities, counties, countries) explicitly as they are often critical for validating claims in sports, history, or music domains.

3. **Relation extraction**: Capture relationships such as sponsorship (e.g., company sponsoring an event), membership in bands or teams, rivalry or antagonism between people, or the timing/order of political offices held.

4. **Summarization and pruning**: Avoid redundancy and superfluous detail from the input notes/context. Focus on relevance to the claim and ensuring that the final `new_notes` directly support or clarify the claim’s components.

5. **Title selection strategy**: Prioritize documents that specifically address:
   - The central entities named in the claim.
   - The nature of their relationships as per the claim (e.g., sponsorship, membership, rivalry).
   - The temporal or event-related context that ties these entities together.

6. **General approach**:
   - Thoroughly cross-check details in `notes` and `context`.
   - Identify which facts support each part of the claim.
   - Synthesize a coherent, evidence-based narrative in `new_notes`.
   - Select corresponding titles reflecting this narrative for `titles`.

The goal is to produce a factually rich, precise, and focused summary that can be used to verify or refute the claim, along with a list of key knowledge sources underpinning that summary.
