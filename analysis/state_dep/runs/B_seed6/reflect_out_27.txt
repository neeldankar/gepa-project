You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- The `claim` field contains a statement whose factual accuracy or relationship between entities needs to be examined.
- The `notes` field is a list of extracted sentences or facts from various sources related to the claim. These notes can contain confirming, contradicting, or neutral information about the claim components.
- The `context` field is a list of longer text passages or document summaries that provide background information or detailed facts relevant to the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`:  
   A concise, carefully curated list of notes derived from the given `notes` and `context` that are most relevant to verifying or refuting the claim. These new notes should:
   - Focus on verifiable factual information that directly supports, contradicts, or clarifies the claim.
   - Include essential domain-specific facts such as dates, relationships between entities (people, works, events), characteristics (e.g., ethnicity, number of acts in an opera vs. a film), or any other precise data points mentioned.
   - Remove redundancy by merging or selecting distinct, relevant points.
   - Correct or clarify erroneous assumptions found in the claim (e.g., wrong ethnicity, incorrect association, misattributed inspiration).
   - Aim to cover all facets of the claim that affect its truth evaluation.

2. `titles`:  
   A short list of key document or topic titles referenced in the selected `new_notes`. This list should:
   - Reflect the main sources or subjects critical to understanding or verifying the claim.
   - Include specific entities (people, works, events) or thematic topics (e.g., "Ethnicity of Isaiah Mustafa," "Opera acts," "Discography of One Unique Signal").
   - Focus on precision and relevance, not quantity.

### Important detailed guidelines and domain-specific knowledge to apply:

- When claims about personal attributes (e.g., ethnicity, birth year) or roles in media are made, verify these precisely with referenced biographical information.
- Recognize and distinguish between closely related but distinct works (e.g., different operas, films, or TV episodes) and avoid conflating inspirations or influences unless explicitly stated.
- Understand structural differences between artistic forms (such as operas having defined acts vs. films usually lacking a formal act count).
- If album counts or specific quantitative data about musical groups are missing, explicitly state this absence and avoid unsupported assumptions.
- Verify the accuracy of associations between actors and roles, especially in TV or commercial contexts.
- When no information is available to confirm or deny the claim portions, acknowledge this clearly to avoid misleading or fabricated conclusions.
- Use reasoning that directly connects claim elements to the evidence found in notes and context, summarizing contradictions and confirmations in the new_notes.
- The reasoning process itself need not be output, only the distilled facts and relevant titled topics.

### Summary:

Produce a factually accurate, concise summary of key notes supporting/refuting the claim, enriched by contextual information, and identify the main relevant documents or topics. Ensure clarity, precision, and comprehensive coverage of the claim's factual components using all available information, including the nuances about artistic works, individuals, and their attributes.
