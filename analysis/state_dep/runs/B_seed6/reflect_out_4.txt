- `claim` is a factual assertion that may require verification, comparison, or explanation based on available information.
- `notes` is a list of extracted facts or snippets from relevant sources that relate to the claim.
- `context` is a list of full documents or article excerpts from which the notes were derived, containing detailed background information and authoritative content relevant to the claim.

Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. **new_notes**: Summarize and consolidate the most relevant, accurate, and informative notes from the provided `notes` field that directly support analysis or verification of the claim. This should:
   - Retain critical and unique information from `notes` that contribute to understanding or evaluating the claim.
   - Remove redundancy or repeated facts while preserving diversity of relevant evidence.
   - Highlight any explicit data points (e.g., dates, names, ownership, relationships) crucial to resolving the claim.
   - Include clarifications about missing or unavailable information if the claim cannot be conclusively resolved (such as lack of birth date data).
   - Be concise but sufficiently detailed to enable an informed evaluation.

2. **titles**: Identify and list the titles (names) of key documents, entities, or topics from the `context` that are most relevant and necessary to understand or verify the claim. Specifically:
   - Include all entities explicitly referenced or central to the claim (e.g., persons, organizations, works, locations).
   - Include notable related concepts or articles that provide background or essential facts for the claim.
   - Ensure you cover all principal subjects mentioned in the claim and those critically needed for claim contextualization.
   - Avoid unrelated or tangential titles.

**Additional Guidelines and Domain Knowledge:**

- Claims often involve comparing personal data (e.g., ages, roles), verifying historical facts (e.g., dates of productions), or confirming ownership and location relationships (e.g., venue ownership).
- Names may appear with variations or partial information; use context to correctly identify the corresponding entities.
- Some entities may not have complete data in the context (e.g., birthdates missing for a person), explicitly acknowledge when verification is impossible.
- Ownership or relationship explanations frequently involve corporate entities and property names; note and link related entities as needed.
- Be mindful that some "notes" may contain duplicates or slightly differing phrasing; synthesize these into a coherent, non-redundant set.
- Titles should correspond to proper Wikipedia-style article titles or well-recognized entity names as seen in the `context`.
- The overall goal is to create a minimal but comprehensive subset of notes and document titles adequate to support or refute the claim and provide clear evidence references.

**Reasoning and output structure:**

Although not requested for output, internally reason clearly about the factual match between `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Extract and prioritize the strongest supporting evidence. When unable to verify, summarize the limitation.

Output only the two fields as specified:

- `new_notes`: a list of concise, non-redundant, relevant factual notes supporting claim evaluation.
- `titles`: a list of key document or entity titles identified from the `context` that are necessary to understand and verify the claim.

This structured approach ensures precise, verifiable, and contextually grounded outputs for factual claim assessment tasks involving linked document retrieval and evidence synthesis.
