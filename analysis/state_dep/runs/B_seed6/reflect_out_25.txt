Task Description:

You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a specific factual statement that needs verification or fact-checking. The `notes` field contains extracted or summarized information related to the claim, including potential confirming, contradicting, or neutral evidence.

Your primary goal is to generate a well-crafted *search query* (output field: `query`) that can be used by downstream systems to retrieve authoritative, relevant documents or data to verify the truthfulness of the claim.

---

Detailed Instructions for Generating the Search Query:

1. **Analyze the Claim Thoroughly:**
   - Identify key entities such as people (with nationalities or roles), organizations, specific events, film titles, song titles, sports teams, championships, or geographic places.
   - Pinpoint the main factual assertions: dates, awards, titles, roles, participation or competition results, founding or release dates, chart rankings, or other numerical facts.
   - Understand relationships and roles between entities (e.g., "X directed Y," "Z covered the song," "team A is composed of players B and C," or "a school is part of district D").
   - Note any temporal constraints (specific years, decades, season numbering equivalents, or timeframes).
   - Include any relevant alternate names, aliases, or nicknames if known or deducible.

2. **Extract and Integrate Domain-Specific Context from the Notes:**
   - Determine if notes confirm, contradict, or clarify part or all of the claim.
   - Look for corrections or disconfirmations that invalidate elements of the claim.
   - Identify domain-specific factual details that narrow, specify, or contextually position the claim (e.g., film release year and main director, original recording artist and cover versions for songs, sports season numbers and winners, school district compositions, geographic jurisdictions).
   - Use such details to refine or constrain the query to verify potential inaccuracies or commonly confused facts in the claim.

3. **Formulating the Search Query:**
   - Construct the query as a concise, targeted natural language question or a precise keyword phrase aimed at retrieving documents that verify the core factual assertions.
   - Include all crucial named entities, roles, dates, and unique identifiers anchoring the claim.
   - When relevant, include alternative names or aliases to cover possible variations in sources.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries. Be specific enough to filter out unrelated documents while broad enough to maximize recall of evidence for or against the claim.
   - When appropriate, combine terms logically (AND/OR) or phrase as direct fact-check questions (e.g., “Who directed [film] in [year]?”, “Did [artist] cover [song] originally recorded by [artist]?”, “Which schools belong to [district] in [location]?”, “Who won [sports event] in [season/year]?”).

4. **Domain-Specific Considerations and Nuances:**
   - **Music:** Focus on charting information (e.g., Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks), original and cover artists, album type (studio, live), release years, and artist fame period.
   - **Film & TV:** Confirm director and main cast members, roles, and any related awards or nominations (Golden Globes, Oscars). Check details about familial or biographical connections if mentioned.
   - **Sports (Motorsports, Baseball):** Clarify participation seasons and their numbering (e.g., the 28th season corresponds to the year 2000), nationalities of competitors, and records of winners. Identify team compositions and franchise relocation if relevant.
   - **Geography:** Clarify administrative divisions — which towns/census-designated places belong to which school districts or counties. Confirm correct place names and hierarchical jurisdiction (e.g., Nassau County, New York).

5. **Utilize the Notes as Correcting References:**
   - If notes indicate elements of the claim are false or misrepresented, include these in the search query explicitly to help retrieval systems find evidence addressing these contradictions.
   - Use the notes to exclude common confusions (e.g., correcting a school’s identity or excluding non-related geographic entities).

6. **Final Query Composition and Output:**
   - Output only the `query` field as a concise, natural language or keyword-based search phrase.
   - The query should directly target the claim’s factual core, incorporating all relevant entities, dates, and corrections from the notes.
   - Avoid explanations, analysis, or extraneous information in the output.

---

Summary of Generalizable Strategy:

- Begin by isolating all key factual assertions within the claim.
- Use notes to confirm, clarify, or refute these assertions.
- Develop a focused and precise query that targets those factual elements, incorporating corrections or clarifications where necessary.
- Tailor the query to the domain: music, film, sports, or geography, incorporating domain-centric data points like chart rankings, season numbers, film directors, school district compositions, and places served.
- Phrase the query to maximize retrieval of authoritative documents and minimize irrelevant results, often posing it as a direct concept check (e.g., “Who directed X?”, “Which schools are in Y district?”, “Did artist A cover song B?”).

By rigorously applying these principles and integrating domain-specific details from the notes, you will produce effective, retrieval-optimized queries suitable for precise fact verification.
