Task Description:

You will be provided with two inputs: 

- `claim`: a factual statement that requires verification or fact-checking.
- `notes`: extracted or summarized information pertinent to the claim, potentially including evidence that supports, contradicts, or is neutral toward it.

Your goal is to produce a single, concise, targeted *search query* (named output field `query`) tailored to efficiently retrieve authoritative documents or data to confirm or refute the claim.

---

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Analyze the Claim Thoroughly:**

   - Identify all *key entities* in the claim: persons, organizations, films, songs, sports teams, events, places, awards, championships, seasons, etc.
   - Extract the *main factual assertions*: dates, titles, roles, awards, competition results, geographic relationships, or any unique qualifiers.
   - Understand *relationships* between entities (e.g., who acted in which film, who won which championship, who is from which country).
   - Note any *temporal bounds*, such as specific years, seasons, or decades.
   - Include all relevant alternate names, aliases, nicknames, or epithets if applicable.

2. **Review and Incorporate Notes Insightfully:**

   - Extract domain-specific contextual details that clarify or refine the claim components.
   - Identify any contradictions, partial truths, or confirmations in the notes compared to the claim.
   - Use notes to detect corrections or nuanced details (such as correct titles, award nomination years, exact geographic size comparisons).
   - Note when elements mentioned in the claim appear false or unsupported, and plan for queries that explicitly check those disputed facts.
   - Leverage domain-specific subtleties present in the notes, for example: 
     - *Music*: chart names (e.g., "Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks"), original vs. cover artists, genre, award nominations.
     - *Film and TV*: actor filmographies, awards (Golden Globe, Academy Awards) by role, family/biographical ties between cast members, verified film release years.
     - *Sports* (particularly motorsports and baseball): participation years, season number to year conversions, nationality of competitors, championship winners.
     - *Geography*: administrative divisions, comparison of geographic sizes, hierarchical place relationships.
   - Use the notes to anticipate and rule out disproved facts by including relevant keywords or phrases in the query.

3. **Constructing the Search Query:**

   - The query should be phrased as a natural language question or a focused keyword combination that directly targets the core components needed for fact-checking.
   - Include all crucial named entities, dates, and unique identifiers anchoring the claim.
   - Avoid broad or vague terms; instead, prefer specificity to focus retrieval on authoritative and highly relevant results.
   - When useful, logically combine terms with AND / OR or pose precise fact-check questions:
     - e.g., “Who won [championship] in [year]?” 
     - “Did [person] receive [award] for [role]?”
     - “Which songs by [artist] reached number one and who originally recorded them?”
   - Incorporate all relevant aliases or alternate names to improve recall.

4. **Domain-Specific Considerations:**

   - **Music Domain:** Highlight chart position details (e.g., Billboard rankings), original recording artists versus cover artists, and genre information.
   - **Film & TV Domain:** Confirm casting specifics, award nominations and years, familial relations among cast or creators, and film adaptation accuracy.
   - **Sports Domain:** Validate participation years and season numbering, nationality of athletes, final championship winners, and dates of key events.
   - **Geography:** Accurately identify administrative-geographic hierarchies, and compare geographic sizes or locations if relevant.

5. **General Strategic Guidelines:**

   - Prioritize queries that can simultaneously verify multiple key claim elements to maximize retrieval efficiency.
   - Explicitly include disputed or corrected elements from the notes to verify or refute those aspects.
   - Formulate queries that guide retrieval systems toward multidocument evidence rather than open-ended explorations.
   - Keep queries concise but comprehensive enough to yield relevant, authoritative evidence.
   - Avoid extraneous language or explanation; output only the final `query` string.
