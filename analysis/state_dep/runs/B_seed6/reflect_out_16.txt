Task Overview:

You will receive two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a factual statement that needs fact-checking or verification. The `notes` are extracted pieces of information—summaries or key points—from various sources that relate to the claim, containing potential evidence either supporting, contradicting, or neutral to the claim. 

Your goal is to produce a single concise, precise *search query* (output field `query`) that will be used to retrieve authoritative documents or data that verify the claim’s truthfulness.

Detailed Task Instructions:

1. **Dissect the Claim:**
   - Identify **key named entities**: people, places, organizations, events, titles, awards, seasons, films, songs, and other unique identifiers.
   - Determine the **main factual assertions**: dates, roles, achievements, relationships, geography, participation years, titles won, etc.
   - Understand the **relationships** between entities (e.g., which person did what, when, where, and in what context).
   - Extract **temporal bounds** where relevant (specific years, seasons, decades).
   - Recognize and include **aliases, nicknames, or alternate names** if pertinent, to cover all possible references.

2. **Utilize the Notes as Contextual Refinement:**
   - Extract any clarifications, corrections, contradictions, or confirmations that refine or modify the initial claim.
   - Identify any **domain-specific knowledge** embedded in the notes that can sharpen specificity:
     - **Sports:** season equivalences (e.g., season number to calendar year), player/coach tenure, team locations or relocations, championship results, nationality of athletes.
     - **Geography:** administrative divisions, hierarchical relationships (e.g., town within shire, county, or region), existence or proximity of geographic features.
     - **Film and TV:** actor names, role details, familial or biographical relationships, award nominations or wins, filmographies.
     - **Music:** chart names (e.g., "Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks"), original vs cover artists, genre, chart performance.
   - Detect any contradictions or implicit negations in the notes (e.g., claim states a fact that is negated in notes) and include relevant terms to explicitly check them.
   - Use the notes to **narrow query focus** on missing or uncertain elements, especially when some claim parts are unsupported or contradicted.

3. **Constructing the Search Query:**
   - Compose a **natural language question** or **targeted keyword phrase** that directly addresses the core fact(s) needing verification.
     - For example, “Who won X championship in year Y?”, “Did person A receive award B?”, “Which songs by artist C reached number one and who originally recorded them?”
   - Include **all key entities, dates, and unique identifiers** to anchor the query contextually and avoid ambiguity.
   - Use **logical connectors (AND, OR)** or phrasing that enhances precision without adding unnecessary complexity.
   - Include **alternative names or aliases** when relevant to cover possible variations.
   - Avoid vague, broad, or multi-topic queries that would dilute retrieval relevance.

4. **Domain-Specific Nuances to Keep in Mind:**
   - **Music domain:** Focus precisely on chart names, chart positions, original versus cover versions, and genre to distinguish different songs or artists.
   - **Film/TV domain:** Verify key cast members and their award nominations or wins. Note any relevant family or biographical connections mentioned in the notes.
   - **Sports domain (Motorsports/Baseball particularly):** Confirm chronological relationships such as which seasons correspond to which years, verify player/manager participations in those seasons, check nationality, and championship wins.
   - **Geography:** Confirm administrative and jurisdictional hierarchies and physical proximities when claims relate to location facts.

5. **General Strategy and Best Practices:**
   - Use the notes to **exclude impossibilities or prior negations** by including skeptical or contradicting elements explicitly.
   - Prioritize generating **queries that can retrieve multiple relevant documents**, combining related entities and facts when possible.
   - Formulate queries as **factual verification questions** rather than broad or open-ended queries.
   - Queries should be **concise yet comprehensive**, covering all critical fact components needed for verification without extraneous words.
   - Always **double-check for domain-specific terminology or key phrases** to help retrieval target the correct authoritative literature.

6. **Output Format:**
   - Output only a single field:
     - `query`: a string representing the refined, targeted search query as described above.
   - Do not include any explanations, reasoning, or additional commentary in the output.

By carefully applying the above, your search queries will effectively guide document retrieval systems towards authoritative and relevant evidence for the claims at hand, across a range of subject domains such as music history, filmographies, sports seasons and results, and geography.
