Task Description:  
You will be given three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to analyze these inputs and produce two fields as output: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Details:  
1. **Inputs:**  
   - **claim:** A factual statement or set of statements that require verification or contextual analysis.  
   - **notes:** A list of information snippets extracted or derived from documents or other sources related to the claim. These notes may contain supportive, contradictory, or neutral information relevant to the claim. They might include references to people, films, games, awards, or other entities connected to the claim.  
   - **context:** A list of document summaries or excerpts that provide background information. These documents cover various domain-specific topics such as films, literature prizes (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), board games and designers, actors and filmographies, or other relevant entities. The context often contains nuanced domain knowledge like award histories, film adaptations, game designers’ portfolios, or global popularity of games.

2. **Outputs:**  
   - **new_notes:** A synthesized, concise subset or refined list of notes focused on directly supporting, contradicting, or clarifying the claim based on the input notes and context. The new_notes should:  
     - Extract key factual information relevant to the claim.  
     - Remove redundancies or irrelevant information seen in the original notes.  
     - Highlight supporting or disconfirming evidence from both the notes and context.  
     - Capture domain-specific knowledge such as award details, film associations, or comparative popularity.  
   - **titles:** A list of succinct, informative titles or topics representing the main knowledge areas or documents that informed the analysis. These titles should reflect:  
     - Important entities (e.g., person names such as “Nelly Sachs”, “Leo Colovini”, or “Choi Woo-shik”).  
     - Specific works or items (e.g., film titles like “Office (2015 South Korean film)”, games like “Cartagena (board game)”).  
     - Relevant thematic areas (e.g., “Nobel Prize in Literature”, “Popularity of Chess Worldwide”, “Screenwriters and Nobel Prizes”).  
     - Titles help identify the source or category of knowledge that grounded the reasoning process.

3. **Reasoning and Approach (implicit but should guide output):**  
   - Carefully cross-reference the claim against both notes and context to confirm or refute individual elements within the claim.  
   - Use domain-specific background knowledge included in the context, such as:  
     - Recognition of notable awards and their recipients (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature winners).  
     - Understanding film production details (screenwriter, source novel, directors).  
     - Knowledge about board game designers and their works, including relative popularity and cultural reach.  
     - Actor filmographies and possible roles in films and whether they directed or acted.  
   - Disambiguate similarly named or related topics (e.g., distinguishing “The Night Hunter” from “The Night of the Hunter”).  
   - Distill relevant evidence and disregard unrelated details.  
   - The new_notes should be factually accurate and grounded explicitly in the provided inputs.

4. **Additional instructions for quality:**  
   - Emphasize clarity and precision in new_notes.  
   - Ensure that titles provide a comprehensive snapshot of used documents or topics, aiding traceability.  
   - Address multi-part claims carefully, handling each segment based on evidence.  
   - Maintain domain-specific terminology where appropriate to preserve nuance.  

5. **Output format:**  
   Return exactly two fields:  
   - `new_notes`: A list of strings (concise factual notes relevant to the claim).  
   - `titles`: A list of strings (appropriate document or thematic titles summarizing key knowledge areas).

Summary:  
Analyze the given claim using the scattered but related information in notes and context, synthesize and refine the key facts into `new_notes` supporting or disputing the claim, and create an informative list of `titles` that capture the essential reference points or domains used in your analysis. This task requires integration of niche factual data (awards, films, games, actors) along with logical reasoning to produce a targeted and domain-aware output.
