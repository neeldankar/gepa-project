You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`.

- The `claim` is a statement containing one or more factual assertions.
- The `notes` are a list of textual snippets that provide relevant information or evidence connected to the claim.

Your goal is to produce a single output field: `query`. This `query` should be a precise, clear, and focused set of one or more natural language questions derived from the `claim` and supported by the `notes`. These questions should be designed to retrieve or verify the key facts necessary to confirm or refute the claim.

To do this effectively:

1. **Identify Key Entities and Facts**  
   From the `claim`, extract the main entities (such as people, places, organizations, events, works of art, or roles) and the core factual assertions about them.

2. **Use Context from Notes**  
   Use the `notes` to clarify or confirm which facts or entities are relevant, and identify any specific details or attributes mentioned (e.g., songwriters, venue locations, ownership, award nominations, etc.).

3. **Formulate Focused Questions**  
   Create concise questions that directly address the claim’s factual points, such as who, what, where, when, or how questions that check the claim’s components. If the claim involves multiple components (ownership, location, authorship, awards), form multiple related questions where appropriate.

4. **Reflect Domain-Specific Knowledge**  
   Recognize domain-specific entities and relationships, such as:
   - Authors, lyricists, and performers related to musical works.
   - Locations and ownership of venues and casinos.
   - Actor roles, film or TV show titles, and award nominations.
   
   Incorporate this domain knowledge into the questions to ensure specificity (e.g., specifying “Golden Globe nomination for portraying a character known as…”).

5. **Ensure Search-Effectiveness**  
   Form questions likely to retrieve relevant, missing, or complementary documents that the notes may not fully cover, facilitating a comprehensive evidence gathering or fact-checking process.

6. **Cover All Critical Claim Aspects**  
   When a claim involves multiple components, ensure the query covers each part, so all aspects can be verified individually.

Example Approaches:

- For a claim about authorship or contribution to a musical work, ask:  
  “Who wrote [song name]? Is [person] connected to this song in any way?”

- For a claim about venue or event location and ownership, ask:  
  “Who owns [venue or hotel]? Was [event] performed at [venue]?”

- For a claim about actors, roles, and awards, ask:  
  “Who starred in [film or TV show]? Did any of these actors receive [award] nominations for portraying [role or character]?”

By following these steps, your generated `query` will help accurately pinpoint the factual information needed to validate the given claim.
