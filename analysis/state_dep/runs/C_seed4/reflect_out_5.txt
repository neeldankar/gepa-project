Task Description:
You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` that can be used to retrieve supporting documents or evidence to verify the truthfulness of the `claim`. The `notes` contain relevant factual snippets extracted from various knowledge sources, which you should use to build a precise, targeted, and factually accurate `query`. The query should be designed to retrieve all key entities and details necessary to validate or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual or composite statement involving one or more key entities, events, or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of textual facts or informational snippets related to the claim, derived from various authoritative or encyclopedic sources.

Output:
- `query`: A clear, focused natural language question or search query aimed at retrieving documents or information necessary to verify all major components of the claim.

Detailed Task Instructions:
1. Parse the `claim` carefully to identify all entities, attributes, relationships, and claims embedded within.
2. Analyze the `notes` to identify:
   - Key named entities (people, organizations, creative works, roles).
   - Temporal information (dates, years).
   - Specific relationships (e.g., "directed by," "born in," "authored by," "acted in").
   - Confirmation or refutation of facts mentioned in the claim.
3. Determine exactly which aspects of the claim require verification or further supporting documentation.
4. Formulate a `query` that explicitly includes all critical entities and relationships. The query should:
   - Use precise named entities (full names, titles of works).
   - Specify relevant attributes (birth year, roles portrayed, affiliations).
   - Address each major component or implied fact in the claim.
   - Avoid ambiguity or mixing unrelated concepts (e.g., avoid conflating multiple TV shows or individuals without justification).
5. Your `query` should be comprehensive enough to retrieve all necessary gold documents pertinent to the claim, as reflected in the feedback provided.
6. When the claim references multiple entities or comparisons (e.g., "Person A enjoyed X but Person B did not"), ensure your query explicitly covers all relevant entities and contrasting points.
7. When the claim incorporates nuanced or complex relationships (e.g., a person’s involvement in multiple fields, or compound attributes like "actor and director"), reflect these appropriately in the query.
8. Your query may be phrased as a natural language question that explicitly states what is being verified or in a structured search formulation that can guide retrieval.
9. Ensure queries are factually consistent with the information in `notes` and avoid introducing unsupported claims or speculative elements.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Use the notes to disambiguate potentially confusing or conflated information in the claim.
- Identify and include all entities that the claim connects; often, multiple documents related to different entities are necessary for full verification.
- Craft queries that aim to retrieve documents underpinning each assertion or relationship in the claim.
- Where the claim involves negative assertions or contrasts, ensure the query addresses these distinctions clearly.
- Aim for queries that can retrieve all relevant documents needed to comprehensively verify (no omissions).
- Avoid queries that only partially capture the entity set or relationship scope, which causes missed documents.

By applying this approach, you will generate well-constructed queries that yield maximum coverage of supporting facts needed to verify complex factual claims accurately.
