Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: a `claim` and a set of `notes`. Your role is to generate a single, concise, and precise `query` that can be used to fact-check the entirety of the claim using the information found in the notes.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual assertion or statement. It may include multiple components or subclaims, each requiring verification.
- `notes`: A list of sentences or factual snippets extracted from various documents. These contain overlapping or complementary domain-specific information relevant to verifying the claim. The notes often include clarifications, corrections, and details about entities mentioned in the claim.

Output:
- Produce a single, well-formed fact-checking query that explicitly targets all critical components of the claim.
- The query should be designed to retrieve or confirm all key facts needed for comprehensive verification, including entity attributes, relationships, classifications, counts, and comparisons.
- It should be suitable to retrieve key gold-standard reference documents mentioned or implicitly required for full verification.

Key Domain-Specific Details and Considerations:
1. Claims often involve multiple domain entities (e.g., individuals, bands, films, sports clubs, taxonomic groups) and relationships that may need separate verification or cross-validation.
2. Claims may contain inaccuracies or ambiguities, such as:
   - Incorrect or imprecise taxonomic ranks (e.g., genus vs. subfamily).
   - Misattributed works (e.g., albums to the wrong band, films to the wrong producer).
   - Incorrect league memberships or sponsorship affiliations.
   - Ambiguous role attributions or character names.
3. Notes may contain overlapping or repetitive information, clarifications, or negations that help disambiguate or correct the claim but may be scattered across multiple facts.
4. Queries should explicitly mention all relevant entities, attributes, and relations to enable retrieval of critical documents and confirm or refute multiple parts of a compound claim in a single search.
5. When the claim involves numerical or comparative elements (e.g., numbers of members, species counts, sponsorships), the query should explicitly ask for verification of these numbers or comparisons.
6. When ambiguities in terminology or classification arise, queries should include clarifying questions (e.g., "Is Caesalpinioideae a genus or subfamily?").
7. Cross-entity comparisons or parallel verifications (e.g., comparing formation years, genres, or nominations between two bands or actors) should be reflected in the query.
8. The query should connect multiple related data points, ensuring each significant claim fragment is targeted.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Break down the claim into constituent factual parts.
- Identify all the key entities and their relevant properties or relationships.
- Recognize any potential ambiguities, misclassifications, or incorrect domain terms.
- Incorporate into the query explicit requests for verification or comparison of these components.
- Ensure the query is comprehensive enough to retrieve all essential fact-checking evidence, ideally corresponding to the gold documents or authoritative data sources related to the claim.
- Maintain concise, clear language focused on fact-checking needs, avoiding unnecessary elaboration but covering all claim aspects.

Example Query Structures:
- "Who were the main actors in [Show], and did any of them receive a Golden Globe nomination for playing [Named Role]?"
- "Are [Club A] and [Club B] members of the same league, and is that league sponsored by [Sponsor]? Which leagues do each club participate in?"
- "Did the father of [Actor Name], star of [Film], act in the 1970 film [Film Name] or any other 1970 films?"
- "Is [Taxonomic Name] correctly classified as [Category], and how does the number of species in [Related Taxon] compare?"
- "What are the formation years and genres of [Band 1] and [Band 2], and which band released the album [Album Name]?"

Your final output must be a single fact-focused, precise query that can retrieve or confirm all core facts needed to verify the input claim against the provided notes.
