You are given three fields as input:

- `claim`: a factual statement or set of related factual statements that assert certain facts.
- `notes`: a list of summary statements or extracted facts from preliminary evidence relevant to the claim.
- `context`: a list of background documents or excerpts from source material related to the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: a refined, concise, and factually accurate summary list of notes that directly support, clarify, or contest the `claim`. These should be selected or synthesized from the original `notes` and `context`, removing redundancy, ensuring clarity, and emphasizing relevance and accuracy regarding the `claim`'s factual components.

2. `titles`: a list of the most pertinent document titles or topic names from the `context` that serve as key sources underpinning the claim. This list should aim to cover all major factual elements of the claim and include all significant entities, locations, events, or concepts appearing in either the `notes` or `context` that are necessary to validate or understand the claim.

---

Detailed guidelines and background information to help you perform the task:

- The `claim` often combines multiple factual assertions which require you to verify and address each discrete part.

- The `notes` typically contain repetitive, overlapping, or partially accurate statements. Your job is to synthesize, consolidate, and clarify these into a non-redundant, accurate, and clearer set of supporting statements (`new_notes`) rooted firmly in the evidence.

- The `context` includes detailed source snippets and document descriptions, some of which directly confirm facts in the claim while others may be tangential or irrelevant. Use it to validate, correct, or enhance the notes and to identify authoritative titles relevant to the claim.

- The `titles` serve to identify key documents, topics, or entities that are critical for fully understanding and verifying the claim. This includes notable people, places, songs, albums, shows, organizations, or other proper nouns referenced in the claim or in the supporting evidence.

- Your approach should involve:
  * Careful cross-checking of fact statements in `notes` and `context` against the `claim`.
  * Eliminating contradictions or unsupported assertions.
  * Ensuring that each factual element in the claim is supported by at least one note in `new_notes`.
  * Selecting `titles` that cover all main entities and concepts relevant to the claim and its validation.
  * Avoiding irrelevant or loosely related documents/entities in `titles`.

- Domain-specific knowledge patterns, as seen in examples:
  * Verifying locations of cathedrals and associated artists (e.g., Guildford Cathedral, St Paul's Cathedral, Dennis Huntley).
  * Identifying songwriters, frontmen, nationalities, and musical works (e.g., The Vines, Craig Nicholls, Boy George, song "Get Free").
  * Understanding TV shows’ airing schedules, network programming blocks, episode titles, and musical album track placements (e.g., KidsClick programming, Wax Fang’s "La La Land", American Dad! episode titles).

- The goal is to produce a factually precise, succinct evidence summary and a comprehensive yet focused list of source titles that together form a solid evidentiary basis for evaluating the claim’s truthfulness.

---

Format of your output should be:

### new_notes
[A list of selected and/or synthesized notes]

### titles
[A list of selected document or entity titles]

---

This task checks your ability to synthesize multi-source evidence, resolve ambiguities, and identify key sources relevant to complex factual claims in domains such as cultural history, music, television programming, and biographical data.
