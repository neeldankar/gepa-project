Task Description:
You will be given three inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to analyze these inputs and produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A factual assertion or statement that may involve multiple connected entities, events, or assertions.
- `notes`: A list of factual snippets—some supporting, some refuting, and some providing background—related to the claim. These notes often clarify or challenge specific points within the claim.
- `context`: A set of detailed, encyclopedic-style documents or descriptions that provide relevant background information, often about people, organizations, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim or notes.

Outputs:
1. `new_notes`: A concise list of the most relevant, accurate, and factually supportive or corrective notes derived from the given `notes`, synthesized with the information in `context`. These should directly address the claim’s truth and its underlying factual components, removing redundancies and minimizing contradictions. This set of notes should help clarify the claim’s accuracy by focusing on the core factual elements discussed.

2. `titles`: A list of document or article titles (topics) that best represent the key entities or concepts necessary to fully understand or verify the claim. These titles correspond to authoritative sources or articles that cover the main subjects involved. Ideally, titles should be specific (e.g., including disambiguators like years, professions, or roles when needed) and directly pertinent to the claim’s key parts.

Detailed Guidelines and Domain Knowledge:
- Claims frequently involve nuanced historical, biographical, or organizational facts (e.g., musical collaborations, film release dates and their soundtracks, historical political events, or journalistic disputes).
- The claims often involve multiple interconnected factual assertions (e.g., the nationality of bands and labels, dates of film releases vs. composers’ activities, or the roles of individuals in complex historical episodes).
- Notes often contain factual corrections, confirmations, and contradictions; your role is to distill this into a coherent summary that directly supports or denies the claim.
- Context snippets serve as authoritative background that can resolve ambiguities or fill gaps that notes alone cannot clarify.
- Effective reasoning includes identifying false or unsupported parts of the claim using both notes and context, clarifying who did what and when, and highlighting the connections or disputes between people or organizations that underpin the claim.
- Titles should reflect the main entities relevant to the claim—people (with identifiers), organizations, creative works, or historical movements—and may sometimes need to be composite or explain relationships (e.g., adding descriptors such as “(band)”, or specifying roles like “David Shire (composer)”).
- When a claim involves a disputed assertion, note the parties and summarise their opposing positions in `new_notes`, capturing the resolution or dominant verified fact.
- Remove extraneous or repeated notes; focus on clarity, direct relevance, and factual accuracy.
- Maintain consistent factual terminology as seen in the input, such as exact names, dates, events, and roles.

Approach:
- Carefully parse the claim to identify its composite factual assertions.
- Cross-check these against the notes and context to verify truthfulness, accuracy, and completeness.
- Extract or synthesize a minimal set of notes that firmly establish the facts relevant to the claim.
- Select or infer the core titles necessary to support understanding or verification of the claim.
- Make sure your outputs allow a third party to grasp the essence of the claim, its veracity, and its key entities without referring back to the original inputs.

This task demands a mix of critical reading, fact verification, and summarization tailored to domain-specific knowledge in history, music, film, and political biography.
