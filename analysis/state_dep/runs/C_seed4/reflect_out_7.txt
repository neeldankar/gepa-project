Given the input fields:
- `claim` (a factual statement involving people, entities, works, events, or places),
- `notes` (a list of text snippets containing relevant factual information, corrections, or context related to the claim),

produce an output field:
- `query` (a natural language fact-checking query or set of clarifying questions)

The purpose of the `query` is to guide a knowledge retrieval system or fact verification pipeline to obtain evidence that verifies or refutes the elements of the claim. The `query` should target all critical factual components (entities, dates, relationships, memberships, roles, and attributes) mentioned in the claim, decomposed into clear, precise, and answerable questions.

---

Detailed instructions and nuances:

1. **Comprehensively Identify Key Fact-Checkable Elements**  
   Analyze the `claim` carefully to extract all core factual assertions, including:
   - Named entities (e.g., people, bands, movies, TV shows, songs, awards)
   - Relationships between entities (membership, authorship, starring roles, dates, geographical bases)
   - Dates, time frames, and premiere or release years
   - Specific roles or contributions (e.g., who wrote what, who performed on which track)
   - Negations and exclusivity claims (e.g., person A is not a member of group B)

2. **Use Notes to Validate and Refine the Query Focus**  
   The `notes` provide corroborating, correcting, or clarifying background about the claim. Use them to:
   - Identify which facts are disputed or require emphasis in the query  
   - Highlight entities or facts missing from the claim but necessary for verification  
   - Disambiguate similar names or multiple entities referenced by the claim

3. **Formulate the Query as Multiple Clear, Interrelated Questions**  
   The `query` should not be overly broad or vague but focused on the factual points most relevant to verifying the claim.  
   - Combine related questions to capture multiple aspects (e.g., relationship and date) without losing clarity  
   - Prioritize precise information retrieval (e.g., "Did Band X originate in County Y?" rather than just "Where is Band X from?")  
   - Include queries about disputed dates or membership where notes indicate discrepancies  
   - Address any implicit assumptions in the claim revealed by the notes

4. **Domain-Specific Considerations**  
   - For claims about **media and entertainment**, include queries about:
     * Cast, crew, and their roles (writer, actor, vocalist)  
     * Release dates, premiere dates, and time spans of series or albums  
     * Membership and lineups of bands or groups over time  
     * Awards or achievements mentioned  
   - For claims about **music**, include clear queries about:
     * Who sings or performs on specific songs  
     * Band memberships and formation places  
     * Album releases and track details  
   - For claims about **geographic and biographical data**, queries should confirm:
     * Locations linked to people or bands  
     * The correctness of dates such as premieres or founding years  

5. **Structure and Wording**  
   - Phrase queries naturally and explicitly for fact lookup  
   - Use question formats that can guide retrieval of authoritative documents or knowledge-base entries  
   - Where appropriate, frame queries to resolve contradictions found in the notes (e.g., premiere date discrepancies)  

---

Overall goal:  
Generate a targeted fact verification query that effectively covers all factual dimensions of the claim, integrating insights from the notes to ensure completeness and precision in retrieving evidence from multiple relevant sources.
