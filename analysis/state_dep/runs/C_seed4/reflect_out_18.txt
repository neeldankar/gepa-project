You are given three input fields:  
- `claim`: a statement or assertion that mentions one or more entities or facts. The claim may compare, relate, or combine information about those entities or facts.  
- `notes`: a list of textual evidences, facts, or supporting sentences extracted from various sources relevant to the claim. These notes contain verification details and supporting or clarifying information about the claim components.  
- `context`: a list of documents or longer text excerpts that provide background knowledge or detailed encyclopedia-style information related to the entities or topics in the claim.

Your task is to process these inputs and produce two outputs:  
1. `new_notes`: a concise, relevant, and factually supported subset of notes that directly verify or clarify the components of the claim. These should be distilled from the original `notes` and should enable validation of the claim’s key assertions, ideally avoiding redundancy or irrelevant information.  
2. `titles`: a list of document or source titles from the `context` that are most relevant to the claim and that correspond to the chosen `new_notes`. These titles serve as citations or references for the supporting facts included in `new_notes`.

Key instructions and domain knowledge to apply:  
- Identify the specific entities or persons mentioned or implied in the claim (e.g., named people, bands, locations, events) and connect them with the `notes` and `context`.  
- Extract from the `notes` only those facts that concretely support the claim's key parts, ensuring a clear logical link between claim elements and the facts chosen.  
- In cases where a claim makes compound or comparative assertions (e.g., "two people are sportsmen," or "band A and band B are both rock bands"), verify each component separately and include notes addressing each part.  
- Use the `context` documents to verify which document titles best correspond to the facts in `new_notes`. Titles should be those of documents clearly focused on the entities or topics central to the claim and notes.  
- When some information in the claim cannot be verified due to lack of evidence in `notes` or `context` (e.g., missing references or unknown details), this should be reflected in the reasoning, but the `new_notes` and `titles` should focus on what is actually supported.  
- Avoid adding missing or extraneous information not present in the inputs. Use the provided context and notes only.  
- In producing your outputs, ensure clarity and relevance: `new_notes` should be informative but concise, and `titles` should be precise and well-matched to the supporting notes.

By following this process, the assistant helps validate claims with precise evidence and trustworthy source titles, useful for fact-checking or summarization tasks involving complex multi-source data.
