Task Description:
You will receive two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` that can be used to fact-check the given claim based on the information in the notes.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that may include multiple components which require validation.
- `notes`: A list of sentences or facts extracted from multiple documents, containing relevant information related to the claim, often repeating or overlapping. These notes contain key domain-specific factual details needed to verify parts or all of the claim.

Output:
- Produce a `query` which is a concise, precise, and fact-checking question that targets the key points necessary to verify the claim accurately.
- The `query` should help retrieve or verify all critical entities, relationships, and attributes referenced in the claim.
- The query typically includes explicit comparisons, clarifications of terms (e.g., correcting taxonomy or genre), or confirmations of data (e.g., number of members, species counts, release years).
- The query should integrate multiple relevant pieces of information from the notes, especially when the claim includes compound facts or comparisons.

Key Points and Domain-Specific Details to Consider:
1. Claims may involve multiple entities or concepts such as bands, albums, taxonomy (e.g., genus vs. subfamily), or counts (number of members, species).
2. Claims might incorrectly assert facts that require correction or disambiguation (e.g., mislabeling taxonomic rank, misattributing an album to a band).
3. Notes may include overlapping or repetitive information, multiple entities related to the claim or the same entity represented differently (including clarifications or corrections).
4. Your query should explicitly reference the key entities or concepts (e.g., band names, album names, taxonomic names) and the relationships being verified (such as formation years, genre, member counts, species counts).
5. When estimating quantities or comparisons (e.g., "more members than," "more species than"), your query should explicitly ask to verify those comparisons.
6. Include clarifying questions when the claim involves ambiguous or misclassified terminology (e.g., "Is Caesalpinioideae a genus or a subfamily?").
7. The goal of the query is to retrieve key missing or partially retrieved gold documents mentioned for verification, ensuring a comprehensive check.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Decompose the claim into its individual factual components.
- Identify any potential misconceptions or terminology errors.
- Cross-reference related information in the notes.
- Formulate the query to explicitly target verifying these components together, including comparisons or categorical clarifications as needed.
- Ensure the query is suitable for retrieving the critical domain-specific documents or facts necessary to confirm or refute the entire claim.

Example Template for Queries:
- "What is the [property] of [entity1], and how does it compare to the [property] of [entity2], especially regarding [specific claim detail]?"
- "Is [entity] correctly classified as [category], and what are the relevant facts about [property1], [property2], etc.?"
- "Did [entity1] and [entity2] share [attribute] such as formation year or genre, and which entity released [album/song]?"

Your output should be a single, clear, and fact-check oriented query designed to retrieve or confirm all critical facts relevant to the claim.
