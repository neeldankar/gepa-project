Given two fields: 

- `claim`: a statement or set of statements asserting a factual relationship or classification involving entities (such as organizations, bands, military units, films, people, etc.),  
- `notes`: a list of supporting factual sentences or evidence extracted from various sources that relate to the claim,

your task is to produce a `query` field, where the query is a precise, well-formed natural language question or set of questions designed to retrieve relevant supporting documents or evidence to verify, refute, or investigate the claim.

---

Detailed task description and guidelines:

1. **Understand the claim fully:**  
   Identify the main entities involved (e.g., bands, people, military units, films, directors), the relationships asserted (e.g., collaboration, classification as a genre or unit type, formation dates), and any temporal or contextual details included.

2. **Leverage the notes to inform the query:**  
   The `notes` contain factual, domain-specific background information that frames the claim's context (e.g., genre classifications, dates, connections between individuals and entities, film details). Use these to refine and disambiguate the claim’s key points.

3. **Formulate a targeted query that mimics a search or information retrieval request:**  
   - The query should explicitly ask about the verification of the factual assertions made in the claim, including relationships and classifications.  
   - When multiple entities or aspects are involved, either combine them into a single, coherent query with multiple parts or ask sequential, logically connected questions.  
   - Include entity names, their relevant roles (e.g., “cast as a rock band”, “Jacob Gentry collaborated with”), and temporal or descriptive attributes (e.g., formation years, music genres, film titles/directors).  

4. **Domain-specific knowledge to include in the instructions for future context:**  
   - Bands may be classified by genres with possible nuance: e.g., a band might be primarily metal but also classified as rock due to sub-genres like progressive or psychedelic rock.  
   - Collaboration claims typically involve identifying the band or entity a person is associated with or collaborates with and confirming its genre or classification.  
   - Film-related claims often involve linking a military unit with historical formation dates and film depictions, requiring recognition of combined units (e.g., the 100th Infantry Battalion/442nd Regimental Combat Team) and director names to confirm timelines and subject matter.  
   - The query should be framed to facilitate retrieval of authoritative documents about these specific entities, their relationships, and attributes.

5. **Generalization of the task-solving strategy:**  
   - Analyze the claim to isolate the factual assertions to verify.  
   - Use the notes as corroborative evidence to identify which entities and attributes are key.  
   - Construct natural language queries structured as fact-checking requests that ask explicitly about the claim’s components, tailored to retrieve relevant documents covering those components.

Example template for the query:

- For a claim about a band and collaboration:

  “Are [Band A] and [Band B], associated with [Person], both considered rock bands? Did [Person] collaborate with [Band B]?”

- For a claim about a military unit and a film depiction:

  “Did the infantry regiment featured in the [Year] film [Film Title] directed by [Director] begin formation in [Year]? Was this unit the [Unit Name(s)]?”

This approach maximizes the likelihood of retrieving the exact documents that confirm or disprove the claim.
