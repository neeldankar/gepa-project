Task Description:  
You will be provided with three input fields:  
- `claim`: A statement or assertion that needs to be evaluated or substantiated.  
- `notes`: A list of extracted factual statements or observations related to the claim, drawn from various sources. These notes may contain direct facts, evidential support, refutations, or contextual information.  
- `context`: A list of source summaries, descriptions, or encyclopedic snippets that provide background knowledge related to the entities, events, or topics mentioned in the claim and notes.

Your goal is to synthesize and produce:  
1. `new_notes`: A concise and focused subset of the original `notes` that directly address or support the claim evaluation, distilling relevant factual points. This subset should integrate clear, verifiable evidence relating specifically to the claim, avoiding redundant or peripheral information. Where possible, emphasize clarifications that refute or confirm the claim, citing critical distinctions or key data points. The output should be in list form of clear, factual sentences.

2. `titles`: A prioritized list of relevant document or article titles that best represent key knowledge sources needed to support the evaluation of the claim. These titles should correspond with important entities, topics, or source documents referenced in the context or notes, and help a reader or system quickly identify authoritative sources for further detailed investigation. Titles should reflect major subjects (e.g., people, works, systems, events) directly connected to the claim.

Reasoning Strategy and Domain-Specific Details:  
- Evaluate the claim by comparing and contrasting extracted facts from `notes` and background in `context`.  
- Identify domain-specific entities such as board games, operas, voice actors, or characters mentioned, considering their historical or cultural context. For example:  
  * In board games: note designers (e.g., Leo Colovini), game popularity, rating systems (e.g., chess's Elo system), and cultural impact.  
  * In performing arts/opera: distinguish composers (Rameau vs Puccini/Auber/Massenet), performance dates, revivals, and adaptations to determine if works are by the same or different creators.  
  * In voice acting and films: verify original voice actors, language versions, dubbing details, and cross-references between actors and roles.  

- Use a fact-based approach to confirm or refute claims, focusing on non-overlapping attributes such as time periods, compositional credits, or voice acting credits.  
- Filter `notes` to those directly substantiating or negating the claim with minimal duplication. Prioritize notes that clarify factual contradictions or confirm critical details.  
- Select `titles` to cover key concepts, primary individuals, works, or systems integral to understanding the claim context (e.g., relevant operas, voice actor profiles, historical details of board games or rating systems).

Output Format:  
- Provide `new_notes` as a list of relevant, fact-checked sentences.  
- Provide `titles` as a list of document or article titles related to the core entities and topics involved.  
- Optionally, you may include a brief reasoning summary (not required unless otherwise instructed), showing how the evidence supports the claim.

This process ensures a factual, well-sourced, and concise presentation of information to evaluate the claim effectively and help downstream retrieval or verification tasks by highlighting essential documents.
