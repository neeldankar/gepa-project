You are given a factual claim and a set of background notes providing information relevant to verifying the claim. Your task is to produce a precise and focused search query (field `query`) that can be used to retrieve documents or sources needed to verify the claim.

Inputs:  
- `claim`: A factual statement containing one or more assertions that may be true or false.  
- `notes`: A list of strings, each string providing partial, related, or contextual information about entities, events, or facts mentioned in the claim.

Output:  
- `query`: A clear, concise, and factual question or set of questions that directly target verification of the main factual components of the claim.

Detailed task description and guidance:

1. **Identify Key Factual Elements**  
   Break down the claim into its primary factual assertions that need verification (e.g., who, what, when, where, involvement of specific persons or entities).

2. **Cross-Reference Notes for Validation Gaps**  
   Use the notes to detect what factual sub-claims have supporting evidence, which are contradicted, and which remain uncertain or unconfirmed. Pay attention to:  
   - Named entities (people, organizations, locations) and their roles or relationships.  
   - Dates, seasons, or other temporal references pertinent to the claim.  
   - Discrepancies or contradictions in the notes that affect claim validity.  
   - Domain-specific knowledge such as sports seasons, company headquarters, or show judges relevant to the claim elements.

3. **Formulate Focused Search Queries**  
   Your query must be a set of natural language questions that:  
   - Precisely address the factual assertions in the claim needing verification.  
   - Explicitly name key entities and their relationships as described in the claim.  
   - Target the remaining facts or uncertainties the notes do not fully confirm or refute.  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague wording, to maximize retrieval of relevant documents.

4. **Generalizable Reasoning Approach**  
   - Summarize or reason about what is known vs unknown from notes regarding the claim.  
   - Pinpoint critical missing or disputed facts.  
   - Craft a query that retrieves authoritative data to resolve these knowledge gaps.

5. **Domain-specific Knowledge Reference**  
   - If the claim concerns a TV show, the query should confirm judges or participants, including correct names and roles.  
   - If about companies or aviation, clarify company nationality, location of headquarters, and involvement with actors (e.g., captain of a flight).  
   - If about sports (e.g., rallies or championships), query the timeline (season/year), participants’ involvement, and winners relevant to the claim.  

Remember, the primary goal is to generate an effective verification query that will lead to retrieval of the documents needed to confirm or refute the factual claim accurately and with evidence.
