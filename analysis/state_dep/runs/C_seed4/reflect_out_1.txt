You are given two inputs: a `claim` (a factual statement or set of statements potentially containing named entities, events, or assertions) and a set of relevant `notes` (text passages or facts extracted from documents related to the claim). Your task is to produce a `query` — a concise set of well-formulated, fact-checking questions that can be posed to an external knowledge source or database to verify the factual accuracy of the claim.

Detailed instructions:

1. **Inputs:**
   - `claim`: A statement containing one or more factual assertions. It may involve named entities (people, places, events, works), relationships (such as sponsorships, authorship, awards), and temporal information.
   - `notes`: A list of text snippets or document facts that provide background information and evidence related to the claim.

2. **Output:**
   - `query`: A set of precise, fact-checking questions designed to verify key factual components of the claim. The query should:
     - Address all major factual points from the claim that can be corroborated or refuted by the notes.
     - Include disambiguation when necessary (for example, clarifying ambiguous names or titles).
     - Be phrased as clear questions to retrieve or verify evidences or supporting documents.
     - Cover nuanced or domain-specific entities and facts (e.g., Nobel Prize winners, event sponsorship by companies, album tracklists, film screenplay authorship).

3. **Guiding principles for query construction:**

   - **Identify Named Entities:** Extract names of people, films, albums, companies, events, awards, etc., mentioned in the claim and check their correct form or spelling as per notes.
   - **Disambiguate Potential Confusions:** If names or titles in the claim may have multiple versions or spellings (e.g., "The Night Hunter" vs. "The Night of the Hunter"), include clarifying questions.
   - **Verify Relationships and Attributes:** Many claims involve relationships such as "person A wrote screenplay for film B," "company X sponsors event Y," or "musician Z covered song S on album A." Formulate questions explicitly verifying these relationships.
   - **Temporal Consistency:** When relevant, include time-related questions to verify years or dates related to events, album releases, awards, or participations.
   - **Cross-Check Key Facts:** For claims involving multiple related facts (e.g., two people’s Nobel prize status), queries should check each fact separately to cover all assertion parts.
   - **Use Domain-specific Factual Concepts:** For example:
     - Nobel Prize in Literature winners and their award years
     - Details of college football bowl games, their sponsors, and participating teams
     - Music album types (studio album vs others), famous covers and release years
     - Film titles, screenplay authorship, and source novels

4. **Example Strategies:**
   - Start with clarifying the identity of key entities when name variants appear.
   - Include multiple sub-questions if the claim makes multiple assertions.
   - If the claim states two people have or have not both won an award, ask individually about each person’s award status.
   - When dealing with sports events, clarify the sponsoring company’s location and participation of the team/year.
   - For musical claims, ask about the specific album type, song inclusion, and artist’s career timeline.

5. **Formulate the query in natural, clear English, combining sub-questions logically (using "and" / "or") where appropriate but avoid excessive verbosity.**

In summary: The goal is to transform the `claim` and supporting `notes` into a factual verification query that queries external sources about the key entities, events, and relationships mentioned, thereby enabling precise fact-checking with a focus on domain-specific nuances like awards, sponsorships, authorship, and temporal details.
