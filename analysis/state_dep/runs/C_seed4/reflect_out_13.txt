Task Description:

You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to analyze these inputs to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A factual or semi-factual statement that you need to evaluate or discuss.
- `notes`: A list of extracted evidence statements or facts supporting, contradicting, or contextualizing the claim.
- `context`: A collection of textual documents or excerpts that provide background information and detailed content related to the claim or involved entities.

Outputs:
- `new_notes`: A curated, condensed list of the most relevant, evidence-based facts derived from the input `notes` and supported by `context`. This list should:
  - Summarize or highlight key evidence confirming or refuting parts of the claim.
  - Correct or clarify inaccuracies in the claim using details from `notes` and `context`.
  - Be concise but comprehensive enough to precisely capture the relationship between claim and evidence.
  - Avoid repetition and irrelevant details.
  - Be grounded strictly on the provided supporting material – do not assume or invent facts not present in `notes` or `context`.

- `titles`: A list of document or article titles (usually from the `context`) that are most pertinent to the claim evaluation and the evidence in `new_notes`. The titles should:
  - Reflect the main sources underpinning the evaluation of the claim.
  - Correspond to entities, topics, or works directly relevant to the key points in `new_notes`.
  - Include specialized topic titles or named entities in the domain (such as specific bands, albums, laws, operas, or songs) that appear in the context and notes.

Detailed Guidance:
1. Carefully compare the `claim` against facts and evidence in `notes` and `context`. Identify factual inaccuracies or unsupported assertions in the claim.
2. Highlight definitive facts, evidence, or consensus points from `notes` and the corroborating `context` documents.
3. Reason step-by-step and synthesize an explanation or summary clarifying the truth or nuances behind the claim.
4. Include in `new_notes` only key, relevant facts that support clarification or verification of the claim.
5. Select `titles` that represent the authoritative or primary information sources present in `context` and referenced in the notes that contribute to these conclusions.
6. Domain-specific knowledge often involves:
   - Music-related facts: details about albums, songs, artists, chart rankings, and record labels.
   - Historical or legal domain: information about acts, dates, and named entities.
   - Narrative or structural features of artistic works (e.g., acts in operas vs. films).
7. Strive for factual precision and keep the scope limited to evidence from the input `notes` and `context`.
8. When possible, address and explicitly mention conflicting facts found and clarify the claim with respect to these.
9. Use clear, formal language appropriate for knowledge verification and fact-checking tasks.

The overarching goal is to produce an evidence-based, factually accurate summary (`new_notes`) that directly addresses the claim and a targeted list of relevant source titles (`titles`) from the given context to support your summary.
