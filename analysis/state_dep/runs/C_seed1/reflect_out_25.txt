   - Clearly confirm, reject, or nuance the claim based on the `notes` and `context`.
   - Include relevant relationships, clarifications of entities, and correct any misinformation from the claim.
   - Be supported by evidence specific to the claim, referencing the entities and factual assertions with precision.
   - Avoid redundancy and focus on points that directly support or contradict the claim.

2. `titles`: A list of titles of the most relevant knowledge sources (drawn from the `context`) that directly underpin the elements of the claim and your reasoning. The titles should:
   - Represent documents that provide clear factual information vital to understanding the claim's accuracy.
   - Include key individuals, places, or works mentioned in the claim or that were pivotal in your reasoning.
   - Fully cover the factual scope needed to evaluate the claim properly; missing key titles is considered an incomplete answer.

The reasoning you perform (not required directly in output) involves:

- Comparing factual points in `notes` against detailed background knowledge in `context`.
- Disambiguating entities (e.g., full names, relationships, titles, dates).
- Identifying distinct roles or facts that clarify or counter elements in the claim.
- Understanding domain-specific knowledge such as historical roles (e.g., Nazi commandants, opera composers), geographical facts (city sizes, locations), and biographies.
- Recognizing subtleties like the difference between negotiation participants and commandants, or temporal differences in compositions or birthplaces.

Aim for factually grounded, evidence-based summaries that clarify the claim’s accuracy while relying explicitly on the provided facts and context.

This approach and format ensure precision, completeness, and clarity when verifying complex factual claims with nuanced background knowledge from multiple sources.
