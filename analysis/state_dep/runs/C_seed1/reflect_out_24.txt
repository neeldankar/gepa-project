Task Description:

You are given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a `query` field based on these inputs.

- The `claim` is a statement or assertion that needs verification or fact-checking.
- The `notes` field is a list of factual statements, evidences, or relevant background information pertaining to the claim.
- Your output (`query`) should be a set of natural language questions that, if answered, would directly help verify the truthfulness or accuracy of the claim.

Detailed Instruction:

1. Understand the Claim:
   - Carefully read and comprehend the claim to identify the core factual components that require verification.
   - Identify all key entities (people, places, organizations, works [films, songs, stations], dates, and relationships) and facts stated or implied in the claim that need to be fact-checked.

2. Analyze the Notes:
   - Review the notes to extract relevant facts, evidence, or counter-evidence about the claim’s entities or asserted facts.
   - Use the notes to understand commonly accepted or disputed facts, relevant relationships or timelines, and clarifications related to the claim.

3. Formulate the Query:
   - Your `query` must be constructed as one or more natural language questions, precise and specific enough to locate authoritative sources that could confirm or refute the claim.
   - Include questions that ask about:
     - The identity or attributes of key entities mentioned (e.g., "Who voiced character X?", "Is this person a member of group Y?", "What is the location of station Z?").
     - Relationships or roles (e.g., "Is person A associated with institution B?", "Did person A perform on song C?").
     - Relevant contextual or categorical facts that clarify ambiguous or disputed points (e.g., "Is location L part of jurisdiction J?", "What are the members of band B?", "What is the nationality of actor A?").
   - If the claim involves multiple interconnected facts (e.g., multiple people or entities), ensure the query covers all those components.
   - The query should reflect any contradictions or points of ambiguity in the claim or notes, so that documents resolving those conflicts can be retrieved.

4. Domain-Specific Considerations:
   - For claims about films and voice actors: queries should identify the film title, year, character names, the actors linked to those roles, and their nationalities if relevant.
   - For music-related claims: queries should identify band membership, song performance credits, and confirm or deny associations or collaborations.
   - For geographic or transport claims: queries should clarify ownership of stations, geographical boundaries, terminus locations, and administrative/jurisdictional divisions relevant to the claim.
   - Use consistent and accurate naming conventions for entities (e.g., "Radiohead" for the band, name of the station including acronym expansions).

5. Generalizable Strategy:
   - Begin by clarifying the primary factual claim components with questions.
   - Then, follow up with questions that resolve any discrepancies found in the notes.
   - Aim to retrieve documents that provide clear evidence around the main elements of the claim.
   - Avoid overly broad queries; maintain the focus on key entities and facts necessary for claim verification.

Outcome:

Your final output is a set of carefully constructed, fact-targeted question(s) in natural language (`query`) whose answers will enable assessment or verification of the original claim with the support of the notes.
