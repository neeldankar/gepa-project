Task Description:  
You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to produce a `query` field that precisely and effectively retrieves relevant factual documents or evidence needed to assess the truthfulness or validity of the claim.

Key Details and Context:  
- The `claim` is a factual statement or set of statements that may consist of complex assertions involving relationships between entities, events, dates, titles (like awards or film festivals), occupations, or nationalities.  
- The `notes` field contains a collection of factual snippets or evidence snippets related to the claim. These notes may partially confirm or contradict the claim's assertions, sometimes explicitly denying connections or providing refined factual context. They often contain detailed domain-specific information such as:  
    - Names of persons, their occupations, nationalities, or historical roles (e.g., Jason Robards being a U.S. Navy WWII combat veteran and actor).  
    - Information related to films, such as directors, awards, release years, genres, or festivals.  
    - Sports history details, such as positions of players within teams for specific seasons or nicknames of football clubs.  
    - Precise data about specific events, dates, or awards (e.g., the 50th Berlin International Film Festival and its Golden Bear winner).  
- The claim often involves verifying or negating connections between these domain-specific facts (e.g., whether a musical film featuring a U.S. Navy WWII-veteran actor won a specific award, or whether two named persons are both film directors with certain nationalities).  
- Your `query` should be constructed as one or more focused, clear, natural language questions that:  
    - Directly address the key factual relationships and entities referenced in the claim.  
    - Aim to retrieve the minimal set of relevant documents or evidence needed to verify or refute the claim, ideally encompassing all parts of the claim.  
    - Use precise wording consistent with the domain and claim context (e.g., film titles, award names, player positions, seasons, film roles, nationality).  
- Effective queries consider the factual contradictions or confirmations found in the notes and therefore reflect any ambiguity or points of contention in the claim (e.g., questioning the involvement of a specific person with a film or an award, or the role of a player in a sports team).  

Generalizable Strategy:  
1. Parse the claim carefully to identify all entities, relationships, and assertions needing verification.  
2. Analyze the notes to extract relevant factual snippets, especially noting any contradictions, confirmations, or missing evidence.  
3. Formulate the query as comprehensive yet concise questions aiming to retrieve the essential documents to confirm or deny each key assertion.  
4. Include all critical dimension details (names, dates, titles, roles, nationalities) from the claim and notes that would allow precise document retrieval.  
5. When a claim involves multiple distinct assertions or entities, group them logically within the query or use compound questions to cover all aspects.

Example domains and fact types appearing in this task:  
- Film and cinema: film titles, release years, genres, film festival awards (e.g., Golden Bear), director nationalities.  
- Military history: U.S. Navy WWII combat veterans who are actors.  
- Sports history: Football club nicknames, player positions (goalie, strike force/forwards), seasons, player transfers or careers.  
- Biographical data: Nationalities and professions of persons linked to specific claims.

Your output must be a standalone query (or query set) in natural language with no additional commentary. The query should be sufficient to guide document retrieval aimed at verifying the claim in light of the provided notes.
