Given inputs consisting of a `claim` (a factual statement or assertion) and `notes` (a list of relevant factual information or evidence related to the claim), produce an output field `query` that formulates a precise, focused search query or question. This query should be designed to retrieve specific documents or pieces of information that are directly relevant to verifying or investigating the key factual components of the claim.

Detailed task description and considerations:

1. **Input Structure:**
   - `claim`: A sentence or paragraph presenting one or more related facts or assertions that need verification or further information.
   - `notes`: A list of factual statements, evidence, or data points related to the claim, often extracted or summarized from knowledge bases or documents.

2. **Goal:**
   - Construct a clear, concise, and focused query or set of queries that target the essential factual elements of the claim, especially those that require verification or further evidence.
   - The query should be framed as a natural language question or search phrase that, when used to retrieve documents or data, will help confirm or refute the claim.
   - The query should aim to retrieve all critical components of the claim, ideally covering all relevant entities, temporal details, relationships, or events mentioned.

3. **Key Components to Identify from the Claim:**
   - Named entities: people (actors, musicians, etc.), locations (cities, provinces, countries), works (movies, TV shows), dates/years.
   - Relationships between entities (e.g., an actor's appearance in multiple productions).
   - Temporal ordering or dates (release years, timeline comparisons).
   - Geopolitical or hierarchical relationships (e.g., jurisdiction overlaps).
   - The claim’s logical structure that connects multiple facts (e.g., "A is under B, and both B and C are in the same country").

4. **Usage of Notes:**
   - Use the notes to identify specific factual evidence and key terms that are relevant to the claim.
   - Confirm which facts from the notes are essential for inclusion in the query to retrieve documents that substantiate or refute the claim.
   - Avoid introducing unsupported assumptions not grounded in the notes.

5. **General Strategy to Formulate the Query:**
   - Break down complex claims into their factual components.
   - Formulate a question or query that asks for the identity or attributes of the relevant entities and their relationships.
   - Include key entities and their relations explicitly.
   - Combine temporal or hierarchical details where relevant (e.g., release dates, jurisdictional relationships).
   - Avoid overly broad or general queries; focus on specificity and relevancy.
   - When appropriate, phrase the query as a confirmatory question tying together all key facts.

6. **Examples of Query Focus:**
   - For claims about actors and their roles: "Who is the actor who starred in [Work A] and also appeared in [Work B], and is known primarily for [Role]?"
   - For claims about timelines: "What year was [Movie or Event A] released, and what year was [Related Movie or Event B] released? Did [A] occur before [B]?"
   - For geopolitical claims: "Is [Place A] under the jurisdiction of [City B]? Are both [City B] and [City C] located in [Country]?"

By following these guidelines, the assistant will generate queries that are targeted, comprehensive, and optimized for document retrieval to verify the claim.
