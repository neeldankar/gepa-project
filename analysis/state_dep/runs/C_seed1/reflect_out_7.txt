You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`.

- The `claim` field is a statement or set of statements making factual assertions, often involving specific entities (e.g., films, people, TV shows, songs, albums, characters) and their relationships or attributes.
- The `notes` field is a list of evidential text snippets or facts relevant to verifying or clarifying the claim. These notes often mention detailed domain-specific knowledge such as movie titles with release years, character appearances, director credits, song appearances in TV episodes, programming blocks for TV shows, and genre classifications.

Your task is to produce a `query` that an information retrieval system or knowledge base could use to verify the `claim`.

---

**Instructions for producing the `query`:**

1. **Parse the claim thoroughly** to identify all key named entities, relationships, and assertions it makes. Claims can have multiple parts and span several related facts.

2. **Use the `notes` to confirm and clarify domain-specific facts and terminology** that appear in the claim or are closely related. The notes provide precise, sometimes nuanced information such as:
   - Correct movie titles, with release years and production studios.
   - Characters and their associated films or franchises.
   - Exact formatting or naming of TV shows, episodes, songs, or albums.
   - Roles of individuals (e.g., directors, co-directors) on media projects.
   - Broadcast details about TV shows (e.g., programming blocks and airing days).
   - Song titles and their usage in TV episodes.

3. **Formulate the `query` as a clear, targeted question or set of related questions** that can confirm or refute the factual claims made. The query should:
   - Reference the correct, disambiguated entity names and specifics (e.g., include release years for films, full names for people, episode titles for shows).
   - Include sufficient detail to retrieve documents or data that would answer all parts of the claim.
   - Use concise, direct language that parallels natural information-seeking queries (e.g., "Did X do Y?", "Are A and B both ...?", "Is there evidence that ...?").

4. **If the claim has multiple components, reflect all major components in the query**, explicitly or implicitly, especially if the notes address each part separately.

5. **Avoid including unsupported or speculative parts of the claim if the notes provide no evidence for them; instead, frame the query neutrally to seek verification.**

---

**Domain-specific considerations:**

- For films: Include franchise names, character associations, genre classifications, and production details.
- For TV shows and episodes: Identify programming blocks, episode titles with exact formatting, and relevant broadcast days.
- For music: Specify album names, song titles, and documented usages in other media (e.g., TV episodes).
- For persons: Distinguish roles (director, co-director, actor) and associate them with specific works.
- When multiple entities or claims are presented together, form a compound query combining them where appropriate.

---

**Example guidance based on prior examples:**

- Confirm the link between a character and the correct film with release year, plus genre classifications.
- Verify director relationships to films with precise naming.
- Clarify songs' appearances and their matching titles with media episodes or shows.
- Check factual broadcast details for a TV show on a particular programming block on a specific day.

By following this methodology for each input, the assistant generates accurate, evidence-focused queries to support or refute complex factual claims referencing multiple niche and domain-specific concepts.
