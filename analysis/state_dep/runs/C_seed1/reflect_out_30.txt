Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a `query` field that formulates one or more precise, fact-checking questions. These questions will be used to retrieve relevant documents or evidence to verify all aspects of the claim based on the information found in the `notes`.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A complex factual or compound statement that may contain multiple sub-claims or assertions involving named entities, dates, professions, and relationships.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets or facts that provide background information, evidence, or context related to the entities and assertions in the claim.

Output Requirements:
- Generate a concise, clear, and fact-focused `query` (a question or a set of questions) aimed at verifying the truthfulness of the claim.
- The `query` should explicitly focus on the key factual components of the claim that need further verification or deeper evidence retrieval.
- Queries should be designed to retrieve documents or evidence related to all critical entities mentioned in the claim, including people, films, publications, professions, dates, or relations.
- Queries should aim to resolve ambiguities or fill gaps of knowledge noted in the `notes`, such as uncertain dates, unclear relationships, or classification statuses.

Guidelines and Domain-Specific Details:
- Extract named entities (persons, films, magazines, terms, organizations) from the claim and cross-reference them with the `notes`.
- Identify temporal and relational conditions (e.g., sequence of film releases, authorship dates, profession overlaps) and formulate queries targeting those aspects.
- When claims are composed of multiple parts, each part requiring verification, questions should be structured to cover all parts thoroughly, preferably in a single, compound question if possible.
- Queries should target clear factual verification rather than vague or overly broad searches.
- For professions or classification claims, compare occupations or categories directly, formulating queries that test whether two individuals or entities share a characteristic.
- For temporal claims (events occurring before or after others), formulate queries that verify the timeline or existence of earlier instances.
- The ultimate goal of the queries is to support effective retrieval of evidence confirming or refuting the claim by precisely pinpointing what is missing or uncertain from the notes.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Analyze the claim’s assertions and identify the key factual elements for verification.
- Use the notes to detect what is known, what is uncertain, and what is needed to confirm the claim.
- Transform the verification needs into one or few precise, fact-oriented questions that cover all critical aspects of the claim.
- Ensure that the queries are framed to retrieve documents or data relevant to all major named entities and claim components.
- Avoid ambiguity and overly complex questions; keep them direct, specific, and comprehensive.

Example Application:
- If the claim involves whether a film was released before another, and notes provide release years, query whether there exists any film featuring a specific actor released prior to a known date.
- If the claim involves whether two publications are periodicals, query about the classification of both named publications.
- If the claim involves whether two people share a profession, query directly whether those individuals share the same profession.

By following these detailed instructions, the assistant will generate queries that enable efficient document retrieval and thorough fact-checking of the provided claims.
