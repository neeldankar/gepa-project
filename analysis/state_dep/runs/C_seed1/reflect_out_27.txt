Given the fields `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a set of fact-checking queries framed as questions that directly address the specific claims made, enabling effective retrieval of evidence to verify or refute the claim.

To accomplish this:

1. **Analyze the Claim:** Break down the claim into its core factual assertions, paying attention to:
   - Named entities (people, places, organizations, events)
   - Relationships between entities (e.g., who said what, who was where, ownership claims)
   - Specific numeric or temporal details (dates, years, sequences)
   - Implicit or explicit causal or descriptive claims (e.g., "X is the first Y," "X is located in Y")

2. **Review the Notes:** Identify which components of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unverified based on the provided notes. The notes contain fact-based statements, corrections, or clarifications relevant to the claim.

3. **Formulate Focused Verification Questions:** Create concise, precise, and fact-checking queries that:
   - Directly correspond to each distinct factual assertion in the claim
   - Aim to retrieve evidence for verifying or refuting those assertions
   - Address any conflated or mixed facts separately to avoid ambiguity
   - Explicitly ask for key details such as identities, affiliations, locations, actions, dates, or factual relationships when relevant
   
4. **Consider the Domain and Context:**
   - When claims mention named individuals, verify their relevant roles, titles, or attributes (e.g., nationality, profession, achievements).
   - For venues or organizations, verify correct naming, locations, and ownership or affiliation.
   - For events or productions, verify performer involvement, dates, or existence.
   - Pay attention to common confusions—like mixing up names, roles, or timelines—and separate these into distinct queries.

5. **Generalize to Help Retrieval:**
   - Frame queries in question form to explicitly request verification.
   - Use entities and relationships mentioned in the claim as anchors.
   - Separate multiple distinct claims into multiple related questions if needed to enable targeted evidence retrieval.
   
Example template based on patterns above:
- "Who [performed specific action]?"
- "Is [entity A] associated with [entity B]?"
- "Did [person/organization] [do/own/occur at] [event/place]?"
- "Is there evidence that [specific claim element] is true?"
- "What is the nationality/role/status of [person mentioned]?"
- "Is [film/show] related to [named person] or [entity]?"

By following this approach, you will generate queries that facilitate precise and effective fact-checking of complex, multi-part claims, accounting for domain-specific nuances such as sports figures, entertainment venues, filmographies, and factual corrections.
