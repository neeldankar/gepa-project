Given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to produce a `query` field that can be used to verify or fact-check the claim based on the provided notes and relevant historical or domain-specific knowledge. 

**Input format:**  
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving potentially complex or nuanced domain knowledge (e.g., historical events, sports records, biographical information).  
- `notes`: A list of extracted facts, evidence, or verifications relevant to the claim, often containing corrections, clarifications, and counter-evidence.

**Task objective:**  
- Generate a concise and focused `query` that can be used to validate the key factual points or contradictions within the claim.  
- The query should explicitly target the main entities, events, or facts involved in the claim, aiming to resolve ambiguity and verify truthfulness.

**Domain-specific considerations:**  
- Claims often reference specific persons (e.g., historical figures, athletes), specific events (e.g., sports seasons, historical milestones), and relationships between them (e.g., roles, achievements, timelines).  
- Notes may provide authoritative historical records, outcomes, or corrections (e.g., actual commandants, winner names, dates, records).  
- The query should incorporate these elements, focusing on established facts to confirm or refute the claim.

**Generalizable reasoning strategy:**  
- Identify key factual elements in the claim: who, what, when, where, and how they relate.  
- Contrast these elements with evidence or contradictions found in the notes.  
- Formulate a question that:  
   - Asks directly about the critical facts underlying the claim (e.g., identity, achievements, roles)  
   - Seeks verification of disputed or unclear points highlighted in the notes  
   - Uses precise historical or domain terms and names as they appear in notes  
   - Avoids ambiguity by referencing specific events, timeframes, or persons mentioned in inputs

**Query structure tips:**  
- Start with a clear question word (Who, Did, Was, When, Is) suitable for verification.  
- Mention key named entities to disambiguate claims (e.g., names of persons, teams, events).  
- Include time references or context if relevant to resolving the claim’s claim.  
- Where the claim bundles multiple assertions (often mixing facts), your query should address each key assertion distinctly if possible, or combine them to highlight their connection.

By following this approach, the produced `query` should enable precise, document-based fact-checking and help retrieve or confirm authoritative sources.
