You will be given three input fields for each task: `claim` (a textual assertion to verify or explain), `notes` (a list of evidence sentences and facts relevant to the claim), and `context` (a list of document excerpts or knowledge snippets related to the subject matter of the claim).

Your task is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. **new_notes**:  
   Extract and synthesize a concise, coherent set of factual statements drawn from the `notes` and supported by the `context` that directly support, clarify, or explain the `claim`.  
   - Remove any redundant or duplicated information within these notes.  
   - Ensure the notes specifically address key entities, relationships, or properties referenced in the claim.  
   - Prioritize facts that collectively validate (or refute) the claim or provide necessary elaboration.  
   - Avoid speculative or unrelated facts.

2. **titles**:  
   Identify and list relevant document titles or entity names that correspond to the key concepts, entities, or subjects referenced within the `claim` and the refined `new_notes`.  
   - Titles should be clearly representative of the source or subject matter the `new_notes` are drawn from.  
   - Include multiple titles if the claim involves multiple distinct entities or topics.  
   - Titles often correspond to Wikipedia page titles or canonical knowledge resources associated with the claim's key points.

**Domain-Specific and Task-Specific Guidelines:**

- Claims often relate to factual verification or explanation in diverse domains such as film/media industry personnel, botanical distribution and taxonomy, and board game popularity comparisons.  

- When the claim references people, places, or entities (e.g., film directors, species subspecies, game designers), ensure the new_notes confirm their roles, locations, or attributes as related to the claim.

- Claims involving comparative popularity or industry involvement require the new_notes to reflect recognized authority, reach, or membership within a domain (e.g., film directing as media industry work; chess’s worldwide popularity vs. niche board games).

- For geographic or biological claims (e.g., about national parks and plant distribution), distinguish between related but geographically or taxonomically separate entities (e.g., Teide National Park on Tenerife vs. Caldera de Taburiente National Park on La Palma) to accurately support/refute nuances in the claim.

- Avoid simple verbatim repetition of notes; instead, synthesize and consolidate multiple related evidence points.

- Pay attention to subtle distinctions in naming conventions, dates, or geographic locations that are critical to the accuracy of the claim.

- When selecting titles, verify that each corresponds to a principal knowledge source or entity directly relevant to the claim and the synthesized notes, not loosely connected topics.

**Generalizable Reasoning Strategy:**

- Identify the key entities or concepts in the claim.

- From the notes and context, select precise facts that confirm attributes, relationships, or timelines of these entities.

- Reconcile any conflicting information by referencing authoritative contextual snippets.

- Structure new_notes to answer "Who/What is involved? What is their role/attribute/status? Where/when is relevant? How do these facts correspond to the claim?"

- Titles should map unambiguously to the principal entities or topics substantiating the claim and supporting evidence.

This approach ensures that your output provides a focused, factually grounded summary of evidence aligned with the claim, coupled with clear pointers to relevant source materials for verification or further reference.
