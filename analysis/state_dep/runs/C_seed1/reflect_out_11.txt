Given the input fields `claim` (a factual statement or assertion) and `notes` (a list of relevant factual statements, corrections, or contextual information related to the claim), your task is to produce the output field `query`.

**Task Description:**

- Your goal is to formulate a concise, clear, and focused natural language query (or set of queries) aimed at retrieving authoritative documents or verifying the factual components of the claim.
- The generated `query` should target the critical factual elements or disputed points embedded within the `claim` that require verification or further evidence.
- Use the `notes` to understand nuances, identify any errors or corrections in the claim, and to pinpoint the exact entities, dates, roles, or relationships needing validation.
- The `query` should be designed to help retrieve documents that fully support or refute all key components of the claim, minimizing unneeded or tangential information retrieval.
- Ensure your query covers all distinct factual points or entities referenced in the claim to maximize recall of relevant evidence. 
- If there are multiple factual components, frame the query in a way that comprehensively addresses them, using conjunctions or multiple focused questions if necessary.
- Where names, dates, titles, or relationships are inaccurate or potentially conflated in the claim (e.g., incorrect premiere years, misspellings, or confusing multiple individuals’ roles), incorporate clarifying questions to separate these aspects clearly.
- Use terminology in the query that is as close as possible to canonical or widely used forms for easier information retrieval (e.g., correct actor names, correct titles of works, accurate years).
- The query should be phrased as fact-finding questions rather than statements or explanations.

**Domain-specific Notes and Strategies:**

- Claims often involve verifying film credits (writer, director), premiere or release years, actor roles and names, associations of people with works (e.g., co-writer vs. director distinctions), technological product origins, or factual timelines.
- Common factual elements include: people’s full correct names, dates of release/premiere, roles in productions, product origins by country, and clarification of possibly conflated identities or facts.
- The input `notes` contain evidence or corrections that help identify what needs to be validated.
- The final query must aim to retrieve key source documents that confirm authorship, involvement, dates, or product origins, to confirm or refute the claim.
- Queries should be precise enough to retrieve relevant major documents (e.g., authoritative pages on a film, actor, director, or product) but broad enough to include all vital evidence.

**Example schema:**  
- For a claim about a movie's writer and cast year:  
  *"Did [Person A] write and/or co-write a [year] movie starring [Person B]? What year did [TV show] premiere starring [Person B]?"*  
- For a claim mixing identities or roles:  
  *"Who directed [Documentary X]? Was the co-writer of [TV show Y] involved in this documentary?"*  
- For a tech/product origin claim:  
  *"What country was the platform for [game] developed in? What is the country of origin of [product Z]?"*  

By following these guidelines, you will generate precise, informative queries that facilitate retrieval of the critical documents necessary for claim verification.
