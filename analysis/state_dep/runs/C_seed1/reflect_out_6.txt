Task Description:
You are given three input fields: `claim` (a statement to verify or analyze), `notes` (a list of extracted or relevant factual statements related to the claim and context), and `context` (a set of documents or text snippets potentially containing evidence or information about the claim). Your goal is to produce two output fields, `new_notes` and `titles`.

- `new_notes` should be a concise, accurate, and consolidated summary of the relevant information that supports or refutes the claim, based on both the `notes` and the `context`. When multiple sources corroborate the same facts, merging them into clearer, less redundant statements is preferred. Remove contradictions or irrelevant information, clarify ambiguities (e.g., misnamed entities), and address the two parts of the claim separately if needed.

- `titles` should be a list of the document titles (taken from the `context`) that are most critical and sufficient for verifying or supporting the claim. Strive to select all key documents directly relevant to the claim, including those about people, organizations, events, or entities mentioned in the claim. Avoid including unrelated or marginally relevant documents. This aids in evidence retrieval and building a strong fact-checking rationale.

Guidance and Best Practices:
1. Read the `claim` carefully to identify all components or assertions it contains, especially when the claim contains multiple parts or complex relationships.
2. Use the `context` documents to understand the background, verify details, and resolve ambiguities or potential name mismatches in the claim or notes (e.g., "The Night Hunter" vs. "The Night of the Hunter").
3. Cross-reference the `notes` with the `context` to identify overlaps, supplement missing critical facts, and discard unsupported or irrelevant notes.
4. Summarize and rewrite the `new_notes` in precise, factual language to directly address the claim’s assertions.
5. For `titles`, pick the minimal and most relevant set of document titles that covers all key facts necessary to verify the claim fully, including titles about relevant people, organizations, events, or geographic entities.
6. When the claim involves biographical or historical facts (e.g., birthdates, leadership dates, awards), ensure inclusion of reliable sources confirming those facts.
7. When the claim involves geographic or organizational relationships (e.g., affiliation of political parties, location within countries), ensure to include titles clarifying hierarchical or spatial relations.
8. When applicable, note corrections or clarifications uncovered through context (e.g., correcting mistaken names), specify this clearly in the `new_notes`.
9. Avoid duplicating information; aim for clarity, sufficiency, completeness, and relevance in your outputs.

The assistant’s reasoning should be grounded in synthesizing information from both `notes` and `context` and reflected clearly in the `new_notes` and `titles` to enable verification or further retrieval.

In sum:
- Input: `claim` (string), `notes` (list of strings), `context` (list of document texts with titles)
- Output: 
  - `new_notes`: A focused, logically structured and precise list of notes that factually supports/refutes the claim.
  - `titles`: A concise list of document titles from `context` critical for claim verification.

This task is domain-agnostic, requiring fact checking, disambiguation, and evidence selection from heterogeneous domains like literature, politics, geography, and organizations. Use careful, critical evaluation and synthesis of given information.
