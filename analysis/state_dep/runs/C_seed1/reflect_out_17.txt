Task Description:  
You will be given two fields as input: `claim` (a statement asserting certain factual information) and `notes` (a list of informational snippets relevant to the claim). Your goal is to generate a field `query` that formulates an effective, targeted question or set of questions to verify or refute the claim. The query should be designed to retrieve key, authoritative documents and facts that directly address the factual elements of the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A factual or composite factual statement, often containing proper nouns, dates, relationships, or comparative assertions.  
- `notes`: A list of strings, each string providing relevant contextual facts or evidence, possibly including clarifications, contradictory details, or related background information extracted from different sources.

Requirements and Guidelines:  
1. **Purpose of the Query:** The query must explicitly seek factual data points necessary to verify or disprove the claim. It should aim at retrieving documents or knowledge entries most relevant to the key entities, attributes, or relationships mentioned in the claim.  
   
2. **Content Inclusion:**  
   - Include core entities mentioned in the claim (e.g., names of people, places, works of art, scientific taxa).  
   - Include key attributes or qualifiers involved in the claim (e.g., birth dates, nationalities, opus numbers, genus species counts, roles and associations).  
   - Frame the query so it hinges on verifying all essential qualifiers that affect the truthfulness of the claim.  
   - Include comparative elements explicitly if the claim involves comparison (e.g., “Which genus has more species?”).

3. **Factual and Domain-specific Nuances:**  
   - When the claim mentions works with specific catalog or opus numbers, include these details precisely (e.g., “Rhapsody, Op. 1”).  
   - When languages or translations are relevant (e.g., the name “Saule” in French), include the connection between the common name and the scientific or common English name.  
   - When the claim references roles in TV shows or commercial appearances, include relevant episode names, character roles, and actor identifiers (including birth years and nationality if mentioned).  
   - When negations or exclusivity are involved (e.g., “distinct from Johann Christoph Pepusch” or “not Greek”), ensure the query addresses these distinctions clearly.

4. **Query Format and Style:**
   - Use clear, natural language questions that could be answered by authoritative sources.  
   - The query can be compound or multipart if necessary to ensure all aspects of the claim are addressed (e.g., “Who composed X? What is their nationality and gender?”).  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague queries. Focus specifically on the facts needing verification as indicated by the claim and notes.

5. **General Strategy to Form Queries:**
   - Identify the main factual elements and their qualifiers from the claim.  
   - Use the notes to understand which facts need confirmation and what common confusions or contradictions exist.  
   - Construct queries targeting the most relevant authoritative information sources or knowledge bases that can resolve uncertainty in the claim.  
   - Include enough context in the query to avoid ambiguity (e.g., specify full names, opus numbers, or episode titles).

In summary, your output `query` should be an expertly crafted set of fact-seeking questions tailored to validate or invalidate the claim using the most pertinent factual elements, carefully contextualized and explicitly constructed to target the exact verification needs surfaced in the input claim and supporting notes.
