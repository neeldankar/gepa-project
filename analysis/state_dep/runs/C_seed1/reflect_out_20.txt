Task Description:

You will be given three inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim` is a factual statement or assertion that may include complex, compound, or subtly inaccurate information.
- `notes` is a list of distilled sentences summarizing evidence from various sources about the claim. These sentences often include contradictions, clarifications, confirmations, or refutations related to the claim.
- `context` is a list of excerpts from documents relevant to the claim, providing detailed background and source material to support verification or rejection of the claim.

Your Goals:

Produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes` — a concise, clear, and authoritative summary that:
   - Synthesizes and critically evaluates the input notes and context.
   - Corrects inaccuracies or contradictions in the claim.
   - Explicitly notes absence of evidence where appropriate.
   - Removes redundant or irrelevant information.
   - Incorporates relevant domain-specific factual information to clarify or refute the claim.
   - Uses a neutral, formal, and fact-checking tone suitable for knowledge verification.
   - Presents a coherent summary rather than a raw aggregation of facts.

2. `titles` — a prioritized list of document or topic titles that are:
   - Drawn from or inspired by the documents and topics in the context.
   - Inclusive of key source documents, notable persons, organizations, concepts, events, or entities critical to understanding and verifying the claim.
   - Balanced between broad thematic topics and specific source documents or entities.
   - Designed to aid transparency, further research, or evidence retrieval.

Domain-Specific Details and Strategies:

- Claims frequently pertain to specialized domains such as:
  - Entertainment: Film casting, voice acting distinctions (speaking vs singing voices), credit attributions, production roles, filmographies, sequel or related titles.
  - Historical or biographical subjects: leadership roles, timelines of political figures or events, rivalries among reporters or correspondents.
  - Sports: Foundations and histories of football/soccer clubs, league membership nuances, naming conventions, sponsorship distinctions, personnel career histories.
  
- Pay attention to nuanced factual details such as:
  - An actor vs. voice actor distinctions and their credited roles.
  - Political timelines and leadership terms with precise dates.
  - Naming iterations of sports clubs and specific league sponsorships.
  - Production credits overlap or absence thereof.
  - Differentiating a person's role (e.g., reporter vs president).
  
- When encountering contradictions in `notes`:
  - Evaluate evidence from the `context` and prioritize authoritative or corroborated information.
  - Explicitly point out inaccuracies within the claim or notes.
  - Avoid speculative assumptions; state when data is lacking or unverified.
  
- Redundancy must be eliminated by distilling key facts rather than repeating similar sentences.

- Address any implicit or explicit errors in the claim, such as:
  - Incorrect chronological assertions (e.g., show premiere dates).
  - Misattributions of roles or productions.
  - Incorrect or unsupported connections between individuals or productions.
  - Confusions about casting (e.g., actress portraying a male actor).
  
- Ensure `titles` facilitate knowledge enrichment by including:
  - Primary source documents or articles directly cited.
  - Biographical or filmography entries critical to the claim.
  - Key concepts, organizations, or historical events mentioned in the context.
  - Proper noun disambiguations and related entities to guard against ambiguity.

Generalizable Approach:

- Analyze the claim carefully to identify its component assertions.
- Cross-reference these with facts in `notes` and verify them against the `context`.
- Address contradictions by determining which note or context element is most reliable.
- Construct a synthesized summary that reflects a fair and fact-based verdict on the claim.
- Finally, select document or topic titles that best capture the evidence base, key entities, and thematic categories relevant to the claim.

Use this detailed, factually rigorous, and domain-aware methodology consistently to generate well-grounded summaries and useful titles that aid in claim verification, reference tracing, or knowledge enrichment.
