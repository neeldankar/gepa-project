Task Description:
You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a `query` field that asks a clear, focused, and fact-checking question whose answer can verify the truthfulness or accuracy of the given claim based on the information provided in the notes.

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Input Understanding:**
   - `claim`: A factual or assertive statement that often contains multiple pieces of information or entities.
   - `notes`: A list of factual sentences or excerpts relevant to parts of the claim. These may confirm, refute, or provide contextual information about the claim.

2. **Output - `query`:**
   - Generate one or more concise, natural language questions that directly test the key factual assertions within the claim.
   - Each question should be answerable by the note evidence provided.
   - If the claim is compound or makes multiple assertions, split it into distinct questions covering each fact.
   - The query should enable retrieval or verification of all the gold documents or knowledge sources relevant to the claim.

3. **Reasoning and Fact-Checking Context:**
   - Use the notes as your evidence base. Analyze which parts of the claim are supported or contradicted by the notes.
   - The questions in the query should be designed to retrieve critical entities, dates, events, or relationships mentioned in the claim.
   - If the notes indicate that certain facts in the claim are false or unsupported, the query should explicitly focus on clarifying those discrepancies.

4. **Domain-Specific Considerations:**
   - The claims often refer to entities like musicians, albums, sports teams, events, movies, and their associated facts (e.g., album release dates, actors voicing characters, venues hosting events).
   - Pay attention to precise relationships: e.g., which album included a certain song, who voiced a character in a specific movie, where a sports final was held, or where a team is based.
   - Distinguish between closely related but distinct entities (e.g., "The Jungle Book" original film versus sequels, or "Olympic Stadium" in Kyiv versus in England).
   - Include years, locations, and named entities (albums, team names, stadiums, films) in your query to enhance specificity.

5. **Query Style:**
   - Use clear, precise, and factual language.
   - Avoid ambiguous or overly broad questions.
   - Ask questions that are explicitly answerable from the notes.
   - Emphasize fact-checking; queries should help confirm or deny the claim's veracity.

6. **Example Strategy Demonstrated:**
   - Identify the core factual claim(s).
   - Find notes confirming or denying these facts.
   - Formulate direct questions addressing those facts, often including key entities, dates, and relationships.
   - When claims are false or partially false, questions should contrast the claimed facts with correct facts from notes.

Summary:
You transform the given claim and notes into effective search or verification queries to retrieve relevant documents or evidence that can support or refute the claim. The queries should be fact-focused, entity-centric, and clearly scoped to enable precise retrieval of gold standard knowledge supporting factual verification.
