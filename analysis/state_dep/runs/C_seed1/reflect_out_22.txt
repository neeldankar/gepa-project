Task Description:  
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a factual statement that may contain inaccuracies or ambiguities. The `notes` are detailed supporting or clarifying information consisting of multiple bullet points or sentences that evaluate or provide evidence about the claim. Your task is to produce a `query` field that formulates an effective, precise, and focused information retrieval query based on the claim and notes. This query should be designed to retrieve relevant authoritative documents or data that help verify or refute the claim comprehensively.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Considerations:  
1. **Understanding Taxonomic and Domain-Specific Accuracy:**  
   - Claims may involve specialized terminology, such as biological taxonomy (e.g., genus, subfamily, species counts) or proper names (people, places, organizations).  
   - Notes often clarify taxonomic ranks or hierarchical classifications (e.g., genus vs. subfamily). Your query should reflect these clarifications to avoid conceptual errors (e.g., do not treat a subfamily as a genus).  
   - When claims confuse ranks or categories (e.g., referring to a subfamily as a genus), the query should redirect to correct entities or relevant components within the broader category (e.g., focusing on a genus within a subfamily).  

2. **Fact-Checking and Entity Disambiguation:**  
   - Claims may conflate or incorrectly attribute information (e.g., nationality or headquarters of a company). Notes explicitly correct these errors.  
   - Your query should explicitly address the corrected facts and distinguish between entities involved (e.g., distinguishing a U.S. airline headquartered in Georgia from Swiss airlines headquartered in Switzerland).  
   - Ensure that the query includes all key entities needed to retrieve evidential documents regarding the claim (e.g., person names, company names, works like books or flights).

3. **Verification Focus:**  
   - The query should be constructed to confirm or disconfirm the specific factual elements in the claim by referencing supporting or counter evidence from the notes.  
   - Queries often include comparative or relational questions to check multiple aspects simultaneously (e.g., species counts comparison, whether a person enjoyed a sport, the operational details of a flight).  
   - When there is uncertainty or ambiguity in the claim, tailor the query to focus on clarifying or disambiguating key terms or entities.

Generalizable Strategy for Constructing Queries:  
- Analyze the claim for its key factual assertions or comparisons.  
- Cross-reference with notes to identify corrections, clarifications, or additional needed entities.  
- Reformulate the claim into a concise, explicit question or set of questions targeting the critical facts.  
- Include all relevant entities and concepts explicitly in the query, respecting correct domain taxonomy or classifications.  
- Ensure the query is phrased to elicit confirmatory evidence, addressing any noted inconsistencies or confusion.

Expected Output:  
A clearly stated, succinct question or set of questions that precisely capture the fact-checking or verification goal implied by the claim and supported or corrected by the notes, suitable for retrieving authoritative evidence.
