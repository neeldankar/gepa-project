Task Description:
You will be given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`. Your job is to produce the field `query`.

The `claim` is a natural language statement asserting one or more factual points about a subject.

The `notes` are a collection of textual evidence or contextual facts, possibly extracted from various documents, that can be used to verify or refute the claim. The notes may include names, dates, places, affiliations, and other relevant specific details tied to the claim.

Your objective is to generate a well-structured, precise, and informative `query` that:

1. Captures the key elements and entities of the claim.
2. Reflects the specific facts or assertions within the claim that need verification.
3. Integrates any nuanced or compound parts of the claim (e.g., multiple attributes or conditions related to the subject).
4. Is phrased as a clear and direct question or set of questions that could be addressed by gathering targeted factual evidence.
5. Supports retrieval of documents or information sources relevant to verifying all parts of the claim.

Additional guidelines and domain-specific knowledge to incorporate:

- Identify the primary entities mentioned in the claim, such as:
  - People (e.g., actors, musicians)
  - Works (films, performances, bands)
  - Locations (cities, counties, venues)

- Extract and connect key factual attributes associated with these entities, including:
  - Birthdates or birth months (e.g., verifying a birth month to confirm if a person matches a claim)
  - Roles or titles (e.g., "star," "professor," or roles in productions)
  - Membership or affiliation (e.g., band membership, association with places)
  - Events or venues where a work was performed or produced
  - Temporal information (e.g., years, formation dates)

- When a claim involves compound assertions (e.g., a person is both born in a particular month and held a certain profession, or starred in multiple works), the query should ask about all those components to ensure comprehensive verification.

- Use the notes to understand which entities and factual points are supported or missing, but do not merely rephrase the notes. Instead, derive a query that directly tests the truth of the claim, incorporating multiple parts of it coherently.

- Queries should be precise to retrieve evidence that helps confirm or deny the claim in full, minimizing ambiguity.

- Pay attention to factual and domain-specific terminology, such as film titles, band names, geographic locations (including appropriate hierarchical relationships, e.g., city within county), and personal attributes.

- If multiple related entities are involved (e.g., several bands or actors), specify them clearly in the query.

- Where appropriate, use natural language interrogation compatible with information retrieval or verification systems focusing on fact-checking.

Summary:  
From the `claim` and the associated `notes`, generate a concise, well-formulated question or set of questions (`query`) that fully captures the factual assertions to be tested, using domain-specific entities and attributes. The `query` should be sufficient to locate and retrieve all relevant supporting or refuting evidence.
