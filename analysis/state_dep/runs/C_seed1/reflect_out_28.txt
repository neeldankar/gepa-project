Task Description:

You are given a claim and accompanying notes (a list of factual statements or evidences relevant to the claim). Your goal is to generate a concise, focused **query** (or set of queries) that would guide the retrieval or further investigation of information to verify or refute the claim. The query should be phrased as explicit questions that target the specific factual components necessary to confirm each part of the claim.

Inputs and Outputs:
- Input fields:
  - `claim`: A complex factual statement that usually involves multiple entities and relationships.
  - `notes`: A list of sentences containing relevant background facts or evidences related to the claim, which may partially validate or refute it.

- Output field:
  - `query`: A few clear, precise, and well-formulated questions that, if answered, would directly address the truth of the claim.

Key Details and Domain Knowledge:

1. Claims often contain multiple embedded factual components. Your generated queries should break down the claim into these components and ask specific questions to verify each.

2. Queries should identify relevant named entities (people, films, bands, albums, roles, etc.) and their relationships (e.g., “Who composed the soundtrack of X?”, “Is Y the first film scored by this composer?”, “How many albums have these bands released?”, “Was actor A part of film B?”, “Who co-wrote series X and were they also directors?”).

3. Avoid ambiguous or overly broad queries; questions must be precise and unambiguous.

4. Use the notes to guide what questions are necessary. For example, if the notes contain information that a subject was a film director but do not link them to another role, you should ask explicitly if the co-writers were also directors.

5. When a claim compares quantitative facts (e.g., album counts), but evidence is incomplete or missing for one or more entities, your queries must explicitly ask for the missing pieces from both entities to verify the relative comparison.

6. For claims involving multiple works (films, albums, series), queries should seek confirmation about:
   - Identities and roles of relevant persons (composer, director, actor, writer, etc.)
   - Chronology or order of works (e.g., was this composer’s first film)
   - Connections between entities mentioned in the claim (e.g., did this composer score that film, was that person involved in X, etc.)

Generalizable Strategy:

- Decompose the claim into atomic factual questions that reflect its key assertions.
- Use the notes to understand what information is already known or unclear.
- Formulate questions to explicitly fill the knowledge gaps necessary for verification.
- When a claim links two or more entities or facts, always ask about each relevant entity and their attributes or relationships mentioned.
- Keep queries focused on the claim components rather than unrelated details.
- When appropriate, clarify potential ambiguities in the claim (e.g., titles vs people, multiple roles).

Example:

For a claim like:
“Veyyil was the first film scored by the man who composed the soundtrack for the film in which Ashrita Shetty had a part.”

The queries would be:
- Who composed the soundtrack of "Veyyil"?
- Was "Veyyil" the first film scored by that composer?
- Which other films did this composer score?
- Did Ashrita Shetty act in any of the films scored by this composer?

This style comprehensively tests each element of the claim.

Summary:

Given a complex claim and some partial evidence notes, produce a targeted set of disambiguating, factual queries that would either verify or refute the claim by seeking all relevant entities, roles, and relationships involved. The queries should be explicit, precise, and structured so they can guide a factual retrieval system or human verification process effectively.
