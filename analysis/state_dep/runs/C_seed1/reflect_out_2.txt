You will be provided with three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A statement or assertion that involves factual or refutable information.
- `notes`: A list of sentences summarizing evidence, contradictions, or reasoning related to the claim and drawn from the context or other known facts.
- `context`: A list of text passages or document excerpts relevant to the entities, people, places, organizations, or works mentioned in the claim.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: A concise, focused set of notes that clearly capture the strongest, most relevant factual points that support or refute the claim, drawn from the input `notes` and context. They should summarize key evidences, clarifications, or contradictions that address the claim’s validity and its components — including people’s membership in groups, factual relationships between entities, publication details, timelines, and any other domain-specific relationships. Redundant or weaker statements should be omitted.

2. `titles`: A set of document titles or entity names (typically corresponding to likely Wikipedia article titles or canonical references) that represent core knowledge sources related to the claim. These should be the most relevant titles that help verify or refute the claim in question. Titles should ideally correspond to notable entities (e.g., bands, albums, books, publications, people, organizations) explicitly or implicitly involved in the claim. Titles should be specific and disambiguated when possible (e.g., "All I Need (Radiohead song)" rather than just "All I Need," "APB (Scottish punk band)" rather than just "APB").

General Guidance and Domain-Specific Notes:

- The claims often involve factual relationships such as band memberships, collaborations, publication credits, and the nature or identity of entities (e.g., record labels’ nationality, musicians’ roles, magazine versus periodical classification).
- Look for key domain indicators: for music-related claims, focus on band membership, song authorship, record label origin, album or song titles. For publication claims, confirm whether entities are periodicals or magazines, the authorship and publication dates of works, and the relationship between publications.
- Validate temporal claims carefully (e.g., exact years or time spans of collaborations).
- Distinguish between entities with similar or overlapping names by using parenthetical disambiguation (e.g., band, song, magazine).
- The `new_notes` should focus on factual correctness and rejection or support of claim parts, avoiding repeated or overly detailed historical or background data unless critical to the claim.
- The `titles` should include all key entities clearly connected to the claim; for missing or unknown elements (e.g., an entity mentioned in the claim but missing from context), still include a plausible canonical title.
- The reasoning (formulated internally but not required in output) should involve: cross-checking claim details against evidence in notes and context, assessing contradictory or supporting points, establishing veracity or falsity of claim components, and determining relevant documents/entities for titles.

Deliver concise, accurate, and relevant outputs that facilitate claim verification and support evidence retrieval.
