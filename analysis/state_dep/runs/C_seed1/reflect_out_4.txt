- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion combining various entities, events, dates, or people.
- `notes`: A list of distinct fact statements relevant to the claim, drawn from knowledge sources, often clarifying timelines, identities, relationships, and corrections to misconceptions.
- `context`: A list of textual excerpts or summaries from retrieved documents providing detailed background information about entities, events, or concepts referenced in the claim and notes.

Your task is to analyze these inputs and produce two outputs: 

1. `new_notes`: A synthesized, concise, and accurate summary list of fact statements that:
   - Directly address the truthfulness, accuracy, or aspects of the claim, incorporating the most relevant facts found in the `notes` and `context`.
   - Highlight key relevant information, corrected misconceptions, or clarifications necessary to understand or verify the claim.
   - Avoid irrelevant or contradictory details, focusing only on information substantiated by the context or notes.

2. `titles`: A list of document or topic titles most pertinent to the claim, notes, and context that would be essential to verify or understand the claim. These should include:
   - Names of key entities (people, bands, films, books, historical figures).
   - Titles of works (albums, films, songs, plays).
   - Disambiguation or related titles where ambiguity exists.
   - Titles corresponding to factual evidence supporting or refuting the claim.

### Detailed task guidelines and domain-specific considerations:

- The claims often involve nuanced factual relationships such as:
  - Real names vs. stage names or character roles (e.g., Vilyam "Willie" Genrikhovich Fisher = Rudolf Abel).
  - Debut works or first occurrences (e.g., first film scored by a composer).
  - Formation dates and name changes of bands or entities.
  - Connections between people, their works, and historical or fictional characters.
  - Distinguishing between different works with similar or overlapping names (e.g., different films or albums).

- The `notes` may contain corrections, clarifications, or refutations of the claim. The `context` provides more detailed documentary evidence.

- Use a fact-checking and synthesis approach:
  - Cross-reference claims with notes and context to confirm or refute.
  - Identify and highlight contradictions or conflations in claims.
  - Summarize the main points about the claim’s accuracy using precise and domain-specific information.
  
- Ensure `new_notes` are factual and clear, as if explaining to a reader unfamiliar with the claim but acquainted with the domain.

- Ensure `titles` include all significant entities or works that are central to understanding or verifying the claim, particularly those that were cited in either notes or context.

### Generalizable strategy:
- Parse the claim into its key factual components (entities, their relationships, dates, events).
- Evaluate whether notes and context support or dispute these components.
- Extract the pivotal facts and remove redundancy from the notes while retaining all essential clarification or correction information.
- Identify all key relevant titles that are tied directly to the claim’s verification or understanding.
- Compose a clear, concise summary for `new_notes` and a comprehensive list of relevant `titles`.

By following this methodology, the outputs will effectively assist in verifying and contextualizing the claim with precise factual evidence and references.
