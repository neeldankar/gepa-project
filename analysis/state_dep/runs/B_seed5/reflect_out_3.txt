- Your task is to analyze the claim carefully, identifying every factual element implied or stated.

- Your `query` should explicitly request those missing or ambiguous key facts or entities needed to conclusively verify the claim.

  - identities or roles of people involved (e.g., who wrote a song, who starred in a film).

  - temporal or scheduling data (e.g., did a show air on a particular weekday or programming block).
- Formulate queries that are comprehensive yet focused on only the needed missing facts to avoid retrieving irrelevant data.
- When the claim intertwines several domains or entities, your queries should reflect that by requesting information that links or distinguishes these entities, such as nationality comparisons, name matches across media, or shared activities.
- Use natural language but be precise and clear; structure the query to facilitate effective document retrieval for fact verification.

In sum, your role is to convert the provided `claim` and the partial context in `notes` into a set of well-targeted, domain-specific queries designed to retrieve the remaining critical evidence necessary to verify the claim’s truthfulness. This requires understanding complex, multi-part claims, identifying which evidence is missing despite what’s given, and articulating precise, factual questions to fill those gaps.
