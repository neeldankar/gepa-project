Task Description:  
You will receive two inputs: a `claim`—a factual statement about specific entities, their attributes, or relationships—and a corresponding list of `notes`—contextual sentences containing factual information relevant to verifying the claim. Your goal is to generate a concise, precise, and focused natural language `query` that targets all critical components of the claim. This query should be suitable for fact verification by checking against the evidence provided in the notes.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A declarative sentence or set of statements describing facts about one or more entities (e.g., persons, films, events, albums, dogs), their attributes (such as dates, nationalities, specific roles), and their relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of text strings containing factual statements extracted from relevant documents. These notes may partially or fully corroborate or contradict the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A precise and clear interrogative sentence directly questioning the key factual statements and relationships posited in the claim. This question must comprehensively cover all the main entities and relations to enable targeted verification.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Knowledge:

1. **Identify Core Entities and Relations Within the Claim:**  
   Parse the claim to extract all essential entities (e.g., actor names, director names, song titles, dog breeds), their attributes (e.g., nationality, birthdates, specific album or movie titles, competition years), and relationships (e.g., starring roles, sequencing of films, breed recognition).  

2. **Incorporate All Crucial Components From the Claim:**  
   The generated query must include every significant factual element present in the claim, especially if multiple entities or relations are asserted together (e.g., an actor starring in two specific films, an album containing a certain cover song, a dog winning a specific award). This ensures the fact-checking question fully addresses the claim.  

3. **Leverage Facts and Contradictions in the Notes to Shape the Query:**  
   Use the contextual factual details from the notes to guide the construction of the query. When the notes expose contradictions, negations, or missing evidence, your query should explicitly address these issues to clarify the claim’s truthfulness (e.g., if the claim states a nationality that is disputed, ask about the nationality; if the claim asserts an event did not occur, form a yes/no question about that event’s occurrence).

4. **Handle Negations and Conflicting Data With Care:**  
   Where the claim includes negatives or contradictory information (e.g., "no person X competed in series Y," or "the dog breed is not recognized"), rephrase into a direct yes/no style question that clearly checks both aspects (existence/absence, recognition/non-recognition).  

5. **Use Clear, Concise, and Precise Language:**  
   Avoid ambiguity or unnecessary complexity. The query should be straightforward enough to facilitate natural language search, retrieval, or automated fact verification within documents.  

6. **Generalizable Strategy for Query Generation:**  
   - Extract and note all entities, events, dates, locations, and relationships from the claim.  
   - Cross-reference entities and facts from notes to assess which need to be targeted in the query.  
   - Formulate a direct interrogative that queries the key aspects, ensuring all are reflected in the question.  
   - Prioritize clarity and focus to maximize relevance and traceability during verification.

Relevant Domain-Specific Context to Consider (Based on Past Examples):  
- Actors and their roles in specific films, sequels, or series (including nationality details, release years, and film titles)  
- Directors and their works (including nationality and birthdates)  
- Musicians and albums (including years, song titles, and cover versions)  
- Racing drivers, racing series, competition years, and nationalities within motorsport contexts  
- Dog breeds, specific individual dogs, breed recognition status, and award history  

Example Reference Points:  
- Verifying whether a Spanish rally driver won a particular racing series’ season and whether another driver competed during specific years  
- Confirming if a Welsh actor starred in two particular films released in distinct years  
- Checking birth dates and nationalities of directors connected to specific films  
- Combining data on musicians, album releases, and song covers to verify musical claims  
- Distinguishing between recognized and unrecognized dog breeds or verifying awards for named dogs  

Summary Instruction:  
Given a `claim` and supporting `notes`, generate a single, precise question in natural language that explicitly asks whether all key aspects of the claim are true, based on the entities and relationships involved. This question should be structured to facilitate efficient and thorough fact verification by targeting all critical factual points embedded in the claim and informed by the notes.
