Task Description:

You will be given three inputs:

1. `claim` — a factual statement or set of related assertions that need to be verified or discussed. The claim may contain multiple subclaims or discrete factual points.

2. `notes` — a collection of extracted factual snippets related to the claim. These snippets may be partially overlapping, duplicated, or incomplete. They mention events, relationships, dates, biographical data, or compositions relevant to claim verification.

3. `context` — a set of longer, detailed documents or entries (such as encyclopedia or Wikipedia-style articles). These may provide authoritative and comprehensive background information, biographies, event summaries, or descriptions of works. Each contextual document has an associated title.

Your goal is to:

- Cross-reference the `notes` and `context` to accurately and clearly assess each part of the claim.
  
- Produce two outputs:

  1. `new_notes`: A distilled, concise synthesis of factual notes that directly support or refute individual claim components. These notes must be:
     - Factually accurate and verifiable using the input data.
     - Non-repetitive, merging or summarizing overlapping facts.
     - Focused on material that confirms or disconfirms the claim explicitly.
     - Explicitly state when no supporting evidence exists for a particular part of the claim.
     - Free of irrelevant or redundant details.

  2. `titles`: A list of the titles of the key context documents that form the “gold documents” or foundational references underpinning your factual validation. Your choice of titles must:
     - Reflect the authoritative sources that you relied upon to confirm or refute the claim points.
     - Include biographical entries or domain-specific documents when relevant (e.g., composer biographies, filmographies, sports seasons).
     - For claims involving events in a given year or domain (sports, music, film), include relevant event or season articles.
     - Indicate missing but expected documents if such authoritative context is absent.

Domain-Specific Considerations:

- Claims and facts may span multiple domains such as music history, sports, film, literature, academic careers, or biographical data. Recognize domain-specific entities like composer names, catalogue numbers (e.g., Op., Sz., BB numbers in music), sports teams, player names, film titles, roles, or novel adaptations.

- Biographical claims often require validation of personal details (nationality, gender, dates) and career achievements or associations.

- In music-related tasks, note catalogue numbers, opus numbers, and distinctions between composers with similar names or historical periods.

- In film and literature tasks, confirm actors’ roles, film adaptations, source novels, and character portrayals carefully.

- Sports claims may require verification of competitions, seasons, teams, players, or event outcomes.

Generalizable Strategy:

1. Parse the claim into discrete verified assertions or subclaims.

2. Identify evidentiary notes within `notes` that relate to each claim part, filtering redundancies.

3. Cross-check these notes with detailed facts in the `context` documents to confirm accuracy and consistency.

4. Synthesize the confirmed facts into concise but complete new notes, explicitly stating when evidence is lacking.

5. Identify and list the titles of the core context documents that provide the basis for the verified facts.

6. Explicitly note any contradictions or unresolved ambiguities in evidence.

7. Ensure that extracted facts are domain-appropriate, precise, and factually correct.

Instructions on Output:

- The `new_notes` should be a minimal set of fully factual, non-redundant supporting/refuting points derived from both notes and context, directly tied to claim components.

- The `titles` list must accurately reference supporting context documents. These serve as key evidentiary sources.

- If parts of the claim have no supporting facts in the inputs, the new notes should explicitly acknowledge this.

- Avoid including documents that do not contain relevant supportive information unless needed to clarify absence or contradictions.

- Your response should convey clear reasoning implicit in your synthesis, but the final output format is limited to the `new_notes` list and the `titles` list.

Note:

- If a claim references detailed historical figures or works (for example, classical music compositions with opus numbers, or films with specific source novels), verify these details carefully in context.

- Always differentiate among similarly named persons or works (e.g., Johann Christoph Pepusch vs. Béla Bartók, or various portrayals of Marilyn Monroe).

- Missing context documents that would be expected for a full verification should be reported in feedback or noted explicitly.

This task requires careful fact-checking, summarization, and domain knowledge to produce authoritative, unambiguous verifications supporting or refuting complex factual claims.
