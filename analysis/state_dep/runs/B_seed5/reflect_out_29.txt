Task Description:  
You are required to generate a concise, precise natural language query that effectively verifies the factual accuracy of a given claim by referring to a provided set of notes. The claim will be a declarative factual statement involving specific entities (e.g., people, films, albums, dog breeds, botanical taxa) and their relationships, attributes, or events. The notes consist of relevant factual sentences extracted from documents that may support, contradict, clarify, or partially inform the claim.

Your mission is to transform the claim into a targeted, direct question that encapsulates all key factual elements while reflecting any clarifications, contradictions, or disambiguations provided by the notes. This query should be optimized to enable efficient and precise fact-checking or document retrieval against the notes.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A single declarative factual assertion involving one or more named entities and their attributes or relationships (e.g., actor starring in particular films, screenplay authorship and source, award recipients, taxonomic classification and species counts).  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets containing relevant, factual information drawn from related documents, including clarifications of names, roles, awards, taxonomy, or factual corrections.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A concise, clear, natural language interrogative that precisely targets the factual core of the claim, incorporating all essential entities, attributes, relationships, and disambiguations.

Detailed Guidelines and Domain-Specific Knowledge:

1. Extract Core Entities and Relationships:  
   Identify all primary entities from the claim (e.g., actors, directors, film or album titles, musicians, dog breeds, taxa) along with key relationships and attributes (e.g., starring roles, dates, authorship, species counts). The query must explicitly name these entities and attributes.

2. Incorporate Every Claim Component:  
   Build a query that explicitly references each crucial factual component in the claim, including entity names, roles, dates, qualifiers (nationality, genre), and relationships. For example, if the claim implies a person’s dual roles or specific sequence of events, the query must explicitly ask about those combined facts.

3. Use Notes to Inform Query Phrasing and Clarifications:  
   Utilize the notes to correct or clarify ambiguous or potentially mistaken references in the claim:
   - If the claim includes imprecise or incorrect names or titles (e.g., film titles, taxonomic rank), adjust the query to reflect the correct nomenclature as revealed by the notes.
   - When the notes clarify contradictory or overlapping claims (e.g., award recipients, director nationality), include these clarifications to sharpen the question.
   - For temporal assertions (e.g., order of performances or releases), explicitly ask whether the sequence holds as claimed.

4. Handle Negations and Contradictions Explicitly:  
   Reformulate negative or contradictory claims into clear confirmatory questions (yes/no or verification style) that expose the truthfulness of all claim parts. If the claim confuses names, roles, or honors, the query should distinctly confirm or deny these attributions.

5. Maintain Clarity, Precision, and Conciseness:  
   Avoid verbosity, pronoun ambiguity, or vagueness. Use full names, exact titles, and precise terms for clarity, enabling swift and accurate fact-checking or retrieval.

6. Domain-Specific Considerations:

   - **Film and Theater:** Confirm exact film titles (disambiguate similarly named works), actor participation, directorial credits, screenplay authorship, and source material (e.g., novel basis). Incorporate nationality or genre details where relevant.  
   - **Literary Awards:** Confirm if named persons have won specific prizes (e.g., Nobel Prize), including instances of claiming joint or exclusive winners.  
   - **Music:** Confirm album releases, track covers, and artist contributions.  
   - **Dog Breeds and Individual Dogs:** Confirm breed identification, official recognition status, and notable individuals.  
   - **Botany and Taxonomy:** Use correct scientific classification levels (e.g., genus vs. subfamily), species counts, and formal names.

7. General Strategy for Query Construction:  
   - Parse the claim thoroughly for all named entities, dates, roles, relationships, and qualifications.  
   - Cross-check these elements with the notes to identify corrections, contradictions, or clarifications.  
   - Construct a clear, direct question that fully encompasses the combined factual scope, replacing ambiguous or incorrect terms with accurate ones informed by the notes.

Examples of Effective Queries:

- "Did Michael York, an English actor, star in both 'Hollywood Goes Classical' and 'Lethal Obsession'?"  
- "Was the screenplay of 'The Night of the Hunter' written by James Agee and based on a novel by Davis Grubb?"  
- "Did Nelly Sachs and James Agee both win the Nobel Prize in Literature?"  
- "Is Caesalpinioideae a genus of flowering plants, and does it contain more species than the genus Achimenes?"  
- "Was Rudolf Höss the longest-serving commandant of Auschwitz, and did Rainer Hoss’s grandfather negotiate with Zyklon B suppliers there?"

Summary:

Given a claim and a set of notes, produce a succinct, precise natural language question that thoroughly covers all critical factual elements from the claim—entities, attributes, roles, dates, and relationships—while incorporating corrections and clarifications from the notes. The final query should be clear, unambiguous, and optimal for precise fact-checking and document retrieval in diverse domains including film, literature, music, dog breeds, and taxonomy.
