Task Description: 

You will be provided with two fields: 
- `claim` — a statement or assertion to be verified or explored.
- `notes` — a list of factual snippets, pieces of context, or evidence relevant to the claim.

Your objective is to generate the field `query` — a concise, precise, and targeted question (or series of questions) that can be used to verify or fact-check the original claim based on the provided notes. The query should explicitly reference the key entities, events, dates, and relationships mentioned in the claim and relevant notes, ensuring it adequately covers all crucial aspects needed to confirm or refute the claim.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Claim Decomposition and Fact Linking**  
   - Identify all main entities (people, places, works, roles) and relationships in the claim.  
   - From the notes, identify factual assertions connected to these entities and relationships that support or contradict the claim.  
   - Consider any relevant temporal or hierarchical information, such as dates (e.g., "1940"), roles (e.g., "President of Cuba"), or relationships between people (e.g., "was at odds with").

2. **Capturing Nuances in Query Construction**  
   - Include both positive and negative evidence if necessary to fully cover the claim.  
   - The query should be framed in a way that it can distinguish between competing pieces of evidence, e.g., who claimed what, and who proved it wrong.  
   - When the claim implies absence or negation (e.g., "is not Otep Shamaya"), the query should seek confirmation of the actual responsible party or lyricist instead of only confirming a negative.

3. **Generalizable Query Strategy**  
   - The query should be comprehensive but as concise as possible to target fact-checking sources efficiently.  
   - Use full names, titles, and relevant qualifiers (e.g., "Fulgencio Batista publicly claimed...", "Herbert Matthews, who was at odds with Ruby Hart Phillips...").  
   - When multiple entities are involved, include the relationships among them explicitly to enable retrieval of all key evidences.  
   - When the claim is about an entity's role in two or more distinct contexts (e.g., actor roles in two movies), the query should join these contexts with an intersectional question ("Did any Welsh actor appear in both...?").

4. **Handling Insufficient Direct Evidence**  
   - If the notes do not directly name the responsible party or if exact information is missing, formulate the query to extract that information based on typical or authoritative sources (e.g., “Who is credited as the lyricist or writer...?”).

5. **Output Format**  
   - Provide ONLY the final `query` text as a single clear question or interrogative sentence. Do not include reasoning or explanation in the output.

Summary:

Given a `claim` and supportive/challenging `notes`, your task is to synthesize a precise verification question that fully encapsulates the factual elements required to confirm or falsify the claim. Your query must leverage domain knowledge such as political figures, historical events, film/TV casting, songwriting credits, and their inter-relations as indicated by the notes. The query should be clean, targetable, fact-checkable, and context-rich enough to guide efficient retrieval of all relevant evidence.
