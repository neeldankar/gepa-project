Task Overview:  
You will be given three inputs:  
1. **claim** — One or more factual assertions that need verification, often composed of multiple related subclaims.  
2. **notes** — A list of factual snippets or extracted pieces of information relevant to the claim. These may partially overlap, contain duplicates, and include implicit or explicit evidence about parts of the claim.  
3. **context** — A broader collection of longer reference documents, articles, or entries (e.g., Wikipedia-style articles) that provide detailed, authoritative information potentially supporting or refuting the claim components.

Your goal is to produce two outputs:  
- **new_notes**: A carefully distilled, clear, and concise set of factual notes synthesized from the input notes and context. The notes must:  
  * Directly support or refute each discrete part or assertion of the claim.  
  * Remove redundant, irrelevant, or tangential information.  
  * Focus on verifiable, domain-specific facts such as dates, names, event details, biographical data, film productions, opera acts, sports competitions, or tournament results.  
  * Explicitly acknowledge if evidence for any part of the claim is missing or contradictory.

- **titles**: A list of document or article titles from the input context that you used or imply as the authoritative supporting sources for verifying the claim. These should correspond to “gold documents” or key references underpinning the factual basis of the claim, reflecting the main sources validating or clarifying the claim’s elements.

Detailed Domain-Specific Considerations:  
- Claims often involve domain knowledge in areas like sports history, filmography, opera, music biographies, board games, or professional careers. You must recognize relevant entities accordingly (e.g., player names, movie titles, opera acts, designer names, production studios).  
- Dates, venues, nationalities, production companies, cast members, and other types of proper names or factual attributes frequently affect claim validation, so they require careful cross-checking.  
- Sometimes production or collaboration details differ subtly in the claim versus notes/context (e.g., a film’s production countries or the inspiration source). Note these discrepancies explicitly.  
- Some claimed relationships or collaborations (e.g., actors starring together in the same film) may not be supported by the evidence, which must be clearly stated.  
- Recognize specialized terminology relevant to the domain, such as “acts” in opera versus sections/scenes in film, or “German-style board games,” and how this affects fact comparison.

Recommended Generalizable Approach:  
1. Parse the claim into discrete factual assertions or subclaims to be individually verified or discussed.  
2. Analyze the notes to extract facts that directly relate to those assertions, identifying any overlaps or duplicates for synthesis.  
3. Cross-validate the extracted notes against relevant detailed context documents to confirm, refine, or refute factual claims.  
4. For each part of the claim, produce clear, factual notes that:  
   - Integrate evidence from both notes and context  
   - Remove redundancy and focus on the strongest, clearest facts  
   - Explicitly mention when no evidence was found or contradictions arise  
5. Compile a list of context document titles that form the core evidence base for these new notes, ensuring these documents are central to the claim’s verification.  
6. If some expected authoritative documents related to the claim remain unreferenced after your analysis, acknowledge their absence.

Presentation and Quality Expectations:  
- Your new_notes should be precise, factually accurate, and understandable without ambiguity.  
- Clearly distinguish which parts of the claim are supported, which are refuted, and which remain unverified due to lack of evidence.  
- titles must be exact document/article titles from the context input, corresponding to your underlying reasoning and evidence sources.  
- Be attentive to subtle factual distinctions (e.g., years, production companies, actor appearances, game designer names) that can affect claim accuracy.

This rigorous fact-checking and synthesis process ensures holistic, domain-aware verification of complex claims, capturing nuanced information and multiple interrelated factual components.
