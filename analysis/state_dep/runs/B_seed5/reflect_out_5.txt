You are given two input fields: `claim` (a factual statement or set of statements) and `notes` (a list of extracted knowledge snippets or context related to the claim, which may contain partial, missing, or conflicting information). Your task is to produce a `query` field that formulates precise, focused questions designed to guide information retrieval or fact verification efforts to verify the claim.

Specifically:

1. Analyze the `claim` carefully to identify all factual components that require verification. Claims often involve multiple entities, relationships, events, or attributes that must be checked.

2. Use the `notes` to understand what information is explicitly present or missing. The notes may include confirmations, contradictions, ambiguous data, or gaps for key facts related to the claim, such as involved people, places, dates, roles, or domain-specific attributes (e.g., film cast and crew, beverage flavors, burial sites, geographical or institutional affiliations).

3. The `query` you produce should:
   - Clearly reflect all the distinct fact-checking sub-questions derived from the claim’s components.
   - Explicitly request any entity identification needed (e.g., "Who starred alongside X?", "What is the main flavor of Y?", "Where is person Z buried?").
   - Incorporate the relevant scope or contextual conditions highlighted in the notes (e.g., geographic boundaries, organizational relationships, temporal constraints).
   - Be phrased as well-formed natural language questions, suitable for retrieving authoritative information to verify the claim.

4. When constructing the `query`, strive for completeness and clarity while avoiding redundancy. Each question should appear only once, but all claim components should be covered if verification demands multiple data points.

5. Domain-specific considerations observed in prior examples include:
   - Film and media domain: verifying actor co-stars, film appearances, and directors.
   - Beverage domain: the main flavor of specific soft drinks and associated advertising links.
   - Historical/geographical domain: burial locations, cemeteries, and their relationship to park or recreation areas.
   - The need to crosscheck multiple entities’ relationships within the claim and notes to resolve potential confusions or overlaps (e.g., similarly named places).
   - Identifying whether there is direct or indirect evidence supporting the claim or clarifying its missing links.

6. Your generated query should thus serve as an effective, granular fact-finding request that aligns with the complexities and subtleties surfaced in the `notes` for verifying multi-part claims.

Example structure (not to replicate exactly but as guidance):

- "What actors starred alongside [Actor] in [Film]? Among them, who appeared in [Other Film]? Who directed [Other Film]?"

By following these guidelines, you will produce queries that can effectively retrieve the necessary documents or data to confirm or refute the claim.
