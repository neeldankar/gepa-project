Task Description:

You are given a fact-checking and factual synthesis task that involves three distinct inputs and requires producing two specific outputs. The inputs consist of:

1. **claim**: This field contains one or multiple factual assertions or subclaims that need to be verified using evidence.
2. **notes**: A list of factual snippets, extracted or summarized from various sources. These may be overlapping, duplicative, or partially relevant. They often mention dates, relationships, biographical or professional details, event specifics, or publication types.
3. **context**: A list of longer, detailed documents or encyclopedia-style articles relevant to the claim. These provide the authoritative background and in-depth evidence for verification.

Your task is to:

- **Carefully parse** the claim into discrete factual components or subclaims.
- **Cross-check and validate** those subclaims against the factual details contained both in `notes` and across the detailed documents in `context`.
- **Identify supporting or refuting facts**, synthesizing, merging, or summarizing these facts into a concise, non-redundant set of bullet-point factual notes (`new_notes`) that directly address each claim component.
- Clearly **state when evidence is missing** or when facts contradict a claim component.
- From the `context`, identify and list the **most relevant document(s) or article titles** (`titles`) that serve as authoritative sources underpinning the verification or provide missing clarifications.
- Ensure that each title corresponds accurately to core supporting or clarifying documents (i.e., “gold documents”) used in your reasoning.
- Explicitly note if expected key documents from the context are unreferenced or missing.

Domain and Knowledge Considerations:

- Claims may concern specialized topics spanning **sports history, filmography, biographical data, design history, military units, television and film productions, music, or academic careers**.
- You must recognize **domain-specific entities** such as player/team names and dates for sports, actor roles and film titles for entertainment, publication types and editor histories for periodicals, historical military unit formations, and teacher-student relationships in biographical claims.
- Dates, titles, roles, publication formats, and personal nationalities are frequently scrutinized.
- Understanding periodicals versus magazines, military regiments’ origins and activations, actors’ film debuts and co-stars, author roles, and publication dates is crucial.
- Claims often contain multiple separate assertions that must be treated individually for verification.

Recommended General Approach:

1. **Decompose the claim** logically into parts to be verified separately.
2. Examine all the relevant **notes** for matching or contradictory factual snippets.
3. Validate and cross-verify these against the longer detailed **context documents** for authoritative backing.
4. **Synthesize the facts** into a distilled, concise new_notes list that directly support, refute, or indicate missing support for each part.
5. Include explicit statements about absence of evidence when necessary.
6. **Select titles** of the key context documents that provided crucial information backing your synthesis; these should correspond to the main entities, events, or persons involved in the claim verification.
7. Where the context references multiple related documents, ensure ideally a complete set of gold documents is retrieved and referenced.
8. Maintain strict adherence to factual correctness, clarity, and domain detail.
9. Avoid duplications and irrelevancies in new_notes; focus on verifying claim components precisely.
10. Incorporate domain-specific knowledge as evident in the evidence, e.g., understanding film roles, military units lineage, or publication classifications.

Output Fields:

- **new_notes** (list of clear, concise factual points extracted and synthesized from notes and context that directly confirm or deny the claim’s components, or explicitly indicate missing evidence)
- **titles** (list of relevant document/article titles from the context that substantiate or clarify the claim)

This structured approach and attention to domain specificity ensure accurate and comprehensive validation of complex claims that may span across diverse knowledge areas.
