Task Description:  
You are tasked with generating a concise, precise natural language query to verify the factual accuracy of a given claim using a provided set of notes. The claim will be a declarative statement related to specific entities (e.g., persons, films, albums, dog breeds, biological taxa) and their relationships or attributes. The notes are factual sentences drawn from relevant documents, potentially providing supporting, contradictory, or partial evidence about the claim.

Your goal is to create a query that directly targets the key factual elements of the claim, framed in a way that facilitates efficient and effective fact-checking or document retrieval against the notes.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A textual factual assertion involving one or more entities, their attributes, or relationships. For example, the claim might state that an actor starred in certain productions, that a screenplay was based on a novel, that a musical artist covered a specific song on an album, or that one botanical taxon contains more species than another.  
- `notes`: A list of text strings containing relevant factual information from related documents that can support or refute the claim, including clarifications about names, roles, taxonomy, awards, or factual corrections.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A concise, clear natural language question that forms a targeted and precise interrogative about the truthfulness of the claim, reflecting all crucial entities, attributes, and relationships in the claim.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Knowledge:  

1. **Focus on Core Entities and Relationships:**  
   Identify the primary entities mentioned in the claim (e.g., names of actors, film titles, musicians, albums, songs, dog breeds, botanical taxa) and the factual relationships or attributes asserted about them (e.g., starring roles, screenplay authorship and source material, Nobel Prize recipients, dog breed recognition, taxonomic rank and species counts). The query must explicitly mention these to enable precise fact-checking.

2. **Incorporate All Essential Claim Components:**  
   Ensure the query covers every key element in the claim — all entities, any dates, qualifiers (e.g., nationality, roles), and all relationships or properties asserted. For example, if the claim involves a person being both a commandant and a negotiator, the query should ask whether the same person held both roles.

3. **Leverage Evidence from Notes to Inform Query Phrasing:**  
   Use information from the notes to disambiguate or clarify references from the claim. For example:  
   - If the notes reveal a naming confusion (e.g., "The Night Hunter" vs. "The Night of the Hunter"), the query should correct this and explicitly question the known correct title and its screenplay authorship.  
   - If the notes state that a term in the claim is taxonomically incorrect (e.g., a "subfamily" being called a "genus"), the query should reflect the correct scientific understanding and ask whether the claim’s taxonomic assertions are accurate.  
   - When the claim mentions potential overlapping or mutually exclusive honors (e.g., winning a Nobel Prize), the query should clearly query the status of each individual.

4. **Explicitly Handle Negations, Contradictions, and Misattributions:**  
   If the claim is negative or involves a contradiction (such as "the writer and Nelly Sachs have not both won the Nobel Prize"), reformulate the query into a clear yes/no or confirmatory question that directly probes the truth of both parts. Likewise, if the claim confuses names or roles, the query should ask whether those attributions are correct or if they belong to different people.

5. **Maintain Clarity, Precision, and Conciseness:**  
   Avoid verbosity or ambiguity. The query must be straightforward, enabling quick document retrieval and accurate fact verification. Use proper names, titles, and terms rather than vague pronouns or ambiguous phrases.

6. **Generalizable Strategy for Query Construction:**  
   - Parse the claim thoroughly to extract all named entities, attributes, relationships, and modifiers (e.g., dates, nationalities, roles).  
   - Cross-reference these elements with the notes to identify any clarifications, corrections, or contradictions.  
   - Reformulate the claim as a direct question that encompasses the entire factual scope asserted, reflecting correct nomenclature and pointing out ambiguous or potentially incorrect components implicitly.

7. **Domain-Specific Fact Knowledge to Consider:**  
   - **Film and Theater:** Confirm actor identities, production titles (correct titles vs. mistaken ones), screenplay authorship, and source materials (e.g., novels).  
   - **Literary Awards:** Confirm Nobel Prize history for named individuals, especially in possible overlapping claims.  
   - **Music:** Confirm album releases, cover songs, and artists involved.  
   - **Dog Breeds and Individual Dogs:** Confirm breed recognition and identification of specific show dogs or breed characteristics.  
   - **Botany and Taxonomy:** Recognize correct taxonomic ranks (e.g., subfamily vs. genus), typical species counts, and correct scientific nomenclature.

Examples of Typical Claims and Queries:  
- Did Michael York, an English actor, star in both "Hollywood Goes Classical" and "Lethal Obsession"?  
- Was the screenplay of "The Night of the Hunter" written by James Agee and based on a novel by Davis Grubb?  
- Did Nelly Sachs and James Agee both win the Nobel Prize in Literature?  
- Is Caesalpinioideae a genus of flowering plants, and does it contain more species than the genus Achimenes?  
- Was Rudolf Höss the longest-serving commandant of Auschwitz, and did Rainer Hoss’s grandfather conduct negotiations with Zyklon B suppliers there?

By following these directives and embedding domain-specific context, the assistant will generate high-quality query questions that encapsulate the claim's facts effectively for fact verification against the notes, reduce confusion from ambiguous or incorrect claim elements, and support thorough document retrieval.

Summary Instruction:  
Given a `claim` and related `notes`, produce a natural language question that succinctly and precisely probes the correctness of every key factual element within the claim, incorporating all critical entities, their attributes, and relationships as clarified or informed by the notes, making the query optimal for document retrieval and factual verification tasks.
