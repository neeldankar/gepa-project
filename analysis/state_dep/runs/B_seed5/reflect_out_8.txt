You will be given three input fields: 

1. `claim` - a factual statement or assertion that needs to be evaluated and supported.  
2. `notes` - an array of supporting textual evidence snippets or fact statements relevant to the claim. These notes often include detailed, domain-specific factual information like plant species counts, historical event dates, biographical or career details of individuals, or clarifications of terminology (e.g., French names for species).   
3. `context` - a broader set of related knowledge excerpts or document summaries relevant to the entities, concepts, or events involved in the claim.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

- `new_notes`: a concise, coherent synthesis of the key supporting facts from the `notes` and `context` that directly validate or refute the claim, removing redundancies and focusing on the most relevant and precise pieces of information. Include domain-specific data such as taxonomy details, establishment dates, professional roles, or name origins as relevant to support the claim clearly and accurately.

- `titles`: a list of document or topic titles representing authoritative, relevant sources that underpin the claim and your supporting notes. These titles should correspond to significant entities, concepts, or subjects mentioned in the `claim`, `notes`, and `context` and help establish the factual basis for the claim’s verification.

### Important aspects and best practices:

- Understand the claim fully and identify the core factual elements to verify, such as species counts comparisons in biology topics, biographical career roles and timelines for individual persons, or the relative establishment dates of institutions.

- Use the `notes` as your primary evidence, as they contain distilled factual assertions. Use the `context` to supplement or clarify these facts, especially when `notes` are incomplete or ambiguous.

- When synthesizing `new_notes`, prioritize clarity, precision, and relevance. Avoid repetition by consolidating repeated data points into a single clear fact (e.g., if several notes state a genus has ~400 species, state this once succinctly).

- Extract and name relevant key entities as `titles` that reflect the main topics necessary to substantiate the claim (e.g., genus names, institution names, individuals' names, film titles), rather than overly generic or unrelated terms.

- When multiple documents or entities are referenced in the evidence, include these in `titles` if they contribute significantly to understanding or verifying the claim.

- The task requires both factual accuracy and effective organization of evidence, illustrating the assistant’s ability to verify and explain claims with appropriate sourcing.

- Domain-specific knowledge may include:  
  * Botanical taxonomy (species counts, genus names, common names in different languages),  
  * Film and media industry terminology (roles like director and co-director, film titles),  
  * Academic and institutional history (postdoctoral training, founding dates of universities),  
  * Proper understanding of naming conventions and synonyms relevant to the claim.

- The reasoning should be implicit in how notes are selected and organized; the assistant should reflect the logical connection between claim and verified facts in the notes and titles.

In summary: given a `claim`, a set of factual `notes`, and broader `context`, generate a distilled, factually supported note summary (`new_notes`) and a relevant source list (`titles`) that together clearly validate or refute the claim with domain-specific precision and clarity.
