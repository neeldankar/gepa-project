You will be given three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim` is a natural language statement or set of statements whose factual accuracy and relationships are to be evaluated.
- `notes` is a list of statements or observations derived from various sources that relate to the entities, facts, or events involved in the claim. These notes may include corrections, confirmations, or contradictions regarding the claim's content.
- `context` is a set of textual excerpts or document summaries relevant to the entities or topics mentioned in the claim. These often contain detailed factual information, biographical data, or domain-specific knowledge which can support or refute aspects of the claim.

Your overall task is to analyze the claim in light of the notes and context, then produce two outputs:

1. **`new_notes`**: a distilled and cleaned-up subset of the original notes that:
   - Correctly identify and clarify factual information about the entities involved in the claim.
   - Explicitly note any contradictions or inaccuracies found in the claim, especially with respect to personal details (e.g., biographical information, professional roles).
   - Highlight missing information from the context relevant to aspects of the claim that cannot be confirmed or denied.
   - Remove redundant or tangential statements, focusing on key factual insights that support or challenge the claim.
   - Are precise, factual, and cover core domain-specific facts relevant to verifying or contextualizing the claim.

2. **`titles`**: a list of the most relevant document or topic titles from the context that are essential for understanding or verifying the claim. These should:
   - Include all key entities or works referenced or involved in the claim.
   - Reference primary sources of factual information for the claim’s components (e.g., biographical articles for persons, official titles for works, event names).
   - Aim for completeness in capturing core documents necessary to confirm or deny claim elements.
   - Avoid overly general or unrelated documents.

**Domain-specific considerations and factual information to keep in mind:**

- Personal names, their exact professional roles, nationalities, or dates (e.g., Angelique Kerber is a German tennis player, not a Texas native).
- Distinctions between roles (e.g., director vs. co-writer) and accurate attribution of works or accomplishments.
- Confirmation or denial of participation in events or works (e.g., defending champion status in sports events, authorship of novels).
- Handling absence of information with careful wording: note when there is no evidence to confirm or deny specific interests or facts.
- Distinguishing between similarly named or related entities and their relationships, such as differentiating between documentary directors, TV show co-writers, and their works.
- Focus on exact titles, event names, and years to anchor evidence (e.g., “2005 China Open – Men's Doubles”, “The Hundred Secret Senses”, “Che strano chiamarsi Federico”).
- Avoid conflating unrelated entities even if they have similar names or thematic overlap.

**Generalizable strategy for performing the task:**

- Parse the given claim carefully, identifying its discrete factual assertions.
- Review notes for correctness, redundancy, and evidence strength; select concise, non-repetitive notes that provide the strongest factual support or refutation.
- Cross-reference notes with the context to ensure the selected notes are supported by appropriately relevant documents.
- Identify and list all distinct, relevant titles from the context that cover each core component or entity mentioned in the claim; prioritize those that directly confirm or deny claims or clarify entity identities.
- Be explicit where information is missing or insufficient to fully verify claim parts.
- Ensure final outputs are clear, factually precise, and focused on resolving or clarifying the truthfulness and context of the claim.

The goal is to produce a factual and well-sourced summary of evidence (`new_notes`) and a concise, comprehensive list of key source titles (`titles`) to enable informed verification or understanding of the claim.
