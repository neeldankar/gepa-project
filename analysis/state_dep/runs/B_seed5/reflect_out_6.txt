Task Description:

You are given two fields as input:
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that needs to be verified or evaluated.
- `notes`: a list of sentences or snippets containing relevant background information or contextual facts related to the claim.

Your goal is to produce a `query` field that encapsulates the precise information needed to assess or verify the `claim`. This query should be explicitly constructed to:
- Identify the key entities or concepts in the claim,
- Reference the important attributes, relationships, or facts connecting those entities,
- Focus on information gaps implied in the notes (e.g., birthdates, roles, geographic locations, historical facts),
- Be formulated clearly and succinctly, as if posing a question to an information retrieval system or knowledge base.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. The `claim` may involve comparisons of dates (e.g., birthdates), relationships between people and their roles or works (e.g., who composed the soundtrack of which film), or geographic and organizational associations (e.g., locations of companies and airports).
2. The `notes` serve as partial or full evidence and may confirm, partially confirm, or highlight missing pieces of information about the claim. They often repeat facts about specific entities (people, organizations, films, songs, locations).
3. Your `query` must target the missing factual elements which if answered will confirm or refute the claim.
4. The queries often require multiple elements to confirm the claim, such as:
   - Identification of a person’s role or association (e.g., who is the lead guitarist of a certain band),
   - Temporal data (e.g., birth dates or release dates),
   - Locations or organizational relationships (e.g., countries where a company is based and where an airport is located).
5. Do not simply restate the claim; instead, break it down into concrete fact-finding questions focused on:
   - Entities (people, places, organizations, creative works),
   - Attributes (birth dates, headquarters, roles, works composed),
   - Relationships (who is associated with what and when).
6. When multiple factual checks are required, combine them logically in one query if possible.
7. Reasoning should follow from the provided notes to highlight what information is missing and what is necessary to validate the claim.
8. The output `query` should be precise and suitable for retrieving the exact knowledge pieces needed to confirm or disprove the claim.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Parse the claim to extract the main entities and the nature of their relationships or comparisons.
- Review the notes to understand what is already known or unavailable.
- Identify missing or uncertain facts that prevent direct validation.
- Formulate a query (or a set of tightly coupled queries) to obtain these critical missing facts, ensuring all relevant entities and attributes are included.
- Aim for clarity, completeness, and minimalism in the query phrasing for optimal retrieval success.

Example:

If the claim states a person was born before another associated with a particular band and song, and the notes lack birthdates, the query should ask:
"Who is the lead guitarist of the band that recorded [song], and what is their birth date? Also, what is the birth date of [person]?"

This approach allows retrieving only the necessary documents to confirm the claim.
