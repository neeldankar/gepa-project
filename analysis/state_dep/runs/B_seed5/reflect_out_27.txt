Task Description:

You will be given three input fields:

- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that may be true, partially true, or false.
- `notes`: A list of textual evidence, extracted facts, or relevant statements that address the claim, often containing information that confirms, refutes, or clarifies it.
- `context`: Background information, encyclopedic knowledge, or document excerpts related to the claim and notes, providing additional relevant facts, details, and entities.

Your objective is to process these inputs and produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: A refined, factually accurate, and concise set of notes derived from the input `notes` and supported by the `context`. These should directly address the claim by confirming or refuting it, citing relevant facts. Notes that are redundant, irrelevant, or contradicted by more reliable information should be excluded or revised. Aim for clarity, precision, and factual accuracy—summarize or synthesize information when necessary to improve coherence and relevance.

2. `titles`: A list of document or entity titles (from the `context` or otherwise) that are most pertinent to understanding or verifying the claim. This set should cover key subjects or sources essential for substantiating the refined notes and the claim's status. Titles should be directly relevant, well-known, and sufficient to represent the foundational evidence behind the claim assessment.

Guidelines and Domain-Specific Considerations:

- The `claim` will often be about factual details in entertainment (films, actors, awards, bands), history (military veterans), or other niche domains.
- The `notes` may contain conflicting information; your job is to use the `context` and your reasoning to resolve conflicts, identify inaccuracies, or fill gaps.
- Use the contextual data to:
   - Verify or disprove key aspects of the claim (e.g., actor nationality, award winners, band genres, collaborations).
   - Identify the correct attribution of roles, titles, or facts (such as who voiced a character, who won an award, band membership).
- Your reasoning should consider nuanced domain information, such as:
   - Distinctions between singing and speaking roles in film voice actors;
   - Types and classifications of music genres (e.g., rock vs. alternative rock vs. metal);
   - Film festival awards and their history;
   - Biographical details relevant to verifying claims about individuals’ military service or professional roles.
- Be attentive to proper entity disambiguation (e.g., differentiating between people with similar names or bands with related titles).
- When producing `new_notes`, prefer evidence-based corrections, clarifications, or summarized truths over merely restating original notes verbatim.
- When generating `titles`, focus on key documents/entities that provide authoritative verification of the claim, prioritize primary sources or well-focused encyclopedic entries.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Scrutinize the `claim` against the `notes` for internal consistency and factual support.
- Cross-reference with the detailed `context` summaries to confirm or contradict any part of the claim or notes.
- Synthesize a concise assessment that encapsulates the refined understanding.
- Identify and list the minimal yet comprehensive set of titles most central to addressing the claim.

This approach ensures high-precision factual validation and clear, well-supported output that can be used for tasks in claim verification, fact checking, or evidence consolidation in specialized knowledge domains.
