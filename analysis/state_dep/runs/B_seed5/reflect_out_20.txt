You will be given three inputs:

- `claim`: a factual statement or set of statements, which may include multiple discrete assertions or subclaims, often involving domain-specific entities (e.g., sports teams, actors, historical figures, events).
- `notes`: a list of extracted factual snippets related to the claim. These notes often contain partially overlapping, duplicated, or fragmentary information drawn from various sources.
- `context`: a list of longer documents, articles, or entries (e.g., Wikipedia-style texts) relevant to the claim’s topic, providing detailed background, biographical data, event summaries, or organizational histories.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`:  
   - Create a distilled, synthesized, and concise set of factual notes directly extracted, merged, or summarized from the given `notes` and `context` that support or refute the individual components of the `claim`.  
   - Remove redundancies and irrelevant information.  
   - Focus on verifiable facts that specifically address each part of the claim (including dates, relationships, roles, affiliations).  
   - If any parts of the claim lack supporting evidence or contradict the data, explicitly note these gaps or contradictions.  
   - When information overlaps or is repeated in the input, integrate it into fewer, clearer statements to improve readability and clarity.

2. `titles`:  
   - Provide a list of relevant document or article titles from the `context` that you explicitly or implicitly used as authoritative sources underpinning your `new_notes`.  
   - These titles should correspond to the “gold documents” or key references that validate or clarify the claim’s components.  
   - The list should be accurate, reflect the main sources of corroborating or contradictory evidence, and include all critical sources needed to verify the claim (e.g., biographical entries, event timelines, league overviews).

---

Domain-specific considerations and examples:

- Claims may relate to sports leagues and teams (including their membership, league levels, sponsors, and locality), requiring precise recognition of club histories and sponsorship details at given dates or seasons.
- Claims about actors and films often require cross-checking actor filmographies, TV roles, and relationships between cast members of different projects.
- Historical or political claims may involve verifying timelines of public statements, political roles, and contrasting journalistic coverage or rival correspondents’ positions.
- Always recognize and differentiate entities with similar or confusingly related names (e.g., Ford Motors F.C. vs. Vauxhall Motors F.C.).
- Identify relationships between claim components (e.g., who contradicted whom, who served in which roles first, which league is at what level).
- Understand sports league structures (tiers, sponsors, divisions) and historical transitions for accurate verification.

---

Recommended generalizable approach:

- Parse the `claim` into distinct assertions or subclaims.
- Search the `notes` and `context` thoroughly for matching, supporting, or contradictory facts, prioritizing verifiable and authoritative data.
- Cross-validate facts found in `notes` with longer, richer information from the `context` documents.
- Synthesize overlapping or duplicate information into concise, coherent factual statements in `new_notes`.
- Explicitly document any missing evidence or contradictions.
- From the `context`, select the most relevant document titles that directly support or clarify the claim components for `titles`.
- Aim to maximize coverage of claim elements by including corresponding authoritative documents.
- Maintain precision and clarity, ensuring `new_notes` cleanly address each part of the claim with factual rigor.

---

This task requires careful fact-checking, reasoning about partial or ambiguous data, and domain knowledge of varied fields including sports history, entertainment (film and TV), political history, and more. Your outputs must be accurate, concise, and reflect only what is reliably supported or refuted by the input data.
