Task Description:  
You are given a `claim` — a factual statement asserting specific information about entities, their attributes, or relationships — and a set of `notes` — sentences extracted from relevant documents containing supporting or contradicting factual details related to the claim. Your goal is to transform these inputs into a precise, concise natural language `query` designed for fact-checking the claim against the provided notes. The query should focus on the critical factual points needed to verify the truthfulness of the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A text string stating specific facts about one or more entities, attributes, or relations. Entities may include people (e.g., actors, pilots, musicians), organizations (e.g., bands, companies, school districts), productions (e.g., films, albums), locations (e.g., counties, cities), or categories (e.g., dog breeds).  
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences drawn from documents, which may partially overlap, support, or contradict elements of the claim. The notes provide evidence for fact verification but may vary in granularity or focus.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A concise, focused natural language question that clearly references all the core claims for targeted verification. It must target the key entities and relationships in the claim in a single, well-structured interrogative form, facilitating efficient fact checking or document retrieval.

Detailed Guidance:

1. **Identify and Extract Core Entities and Relations:**  
   Parse the claim to identify the main entities (people, places, organizations, productions) and their asserted attributes or relationships (e.g., membership, location, starring roles, breed classification). Every important element and their interconnection must be represented in the query.

2. **Incorporate All Crucial Claim Components:**  
   The query must cover every significant part of the claim. For example, if multiple schools or bands are listed with specific relationships (membership, location), all must be referenced. If the claim asserts an entity is part of or excluded from a group, the query should explicitly reflect that inclusion/exclusion.

3. **Use Notes to Enhance Query Precision:**  
   Analyze the notes to understand nuances, clarifications, or contradictions relevant to the claim:
   - If notes clarify the exact composition of a collection (e.g., school district membership), incorporate the verified elements in your query and address any contradictory claims explicitly (e.g., presence or absence of a certain school).  
   - When multiple entities are involved (e.g., a band member’s multiple affiliations), the query should include the relevant entities and their connections to locations or attributes mentioned in the notes.
   - Where notes specify geographical hierarchies or clarifications (e.g., city within county), use them to refine geographic descriptors in the query.
   - If notes disprove part of the claim, reflect that tension by asking whether the claim is true or false regarding that disputed fact.

4. **Negations and Contradictory Facts:**  
   When the claim contains negations or contradictions (e.g., “not both,” “excluded school,” “a company headquartered in Georgia but Swiss”), the query should be reformulated to explicitly ask a yes/no question about these claims to verify their truthfulness.

5. **Maintain Clarity, Precision, and Brevity:**  
   The query should be clear and concise — avoid verbosity or ambiguity. Prefer direct questions that yield straightforward verification. Use precise names, places, dates, and modifiers from the claim and notes.

6. **Generalizable Strategy:**  
   - Parse claims to identify all significant facts and relationships.  
   - Cross-check the entities and relationships with notes to determine relevant verifiable facts.  
   - Formulate one tightly focused question encompassing all critical parts.  
   - Phrase queries in natural language that directly correspond to fact-checkable claims.

7. **Domain-Specific Knowledge and Context Examples:**  
   - Actors and productions: Query should verify whether a specific actor starred in given plays, films, or events.  
   - Musical artists and works: Query should incorporate artist names, album titles, song covers, and release information.  
   - School districts and their member schools: Query should check inclusion/exclusion of schools and geographic service areas.  
   - Band membership and geographic bases: Query should ask about band membership and locations like counties or cities.  
   - Company headquarters and national affiliations: Query should confirm company nationality and headquarters location.  
   - Dog breeds and lineage: Query should verify breed classification or official recognition.

By following these instructions, you will create queries that effectively and precisely address the truthfulness of the claim, using the notes as evidence, enabling reliable fact-checking or document retrieval.
