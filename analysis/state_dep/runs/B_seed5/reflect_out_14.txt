Task Description:  
You are tasked with generating a precise natural language query from a given claim and associated notes. The claim consists of declarative statements about specific entities or events (such as people, films, albums, songs, dog breeds, or publications) and their attributes or relationships. The notes are factual sentences drawn from relevant sources that provide evidence which may support or contradict the claim.

Your output is a focused, concise query designed to verify the key facts and relationships asserted in the claim, leveraging all relevant information from the notes. This query should enable an efficient and targeted fact-checking or document retrieval process.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A text string containing one or more factual assertions about named entities, their roles, attributes, or interrelations. These may include film productions and casts, musicians and albums, specific dog breeds and individuals, publications and induction events, or similar domain-specific elements.  
- `notes`: A list of text strings containing fragmented but pertinent factual information about the entities and relationships in the claim. The notes may affirm or contradict parts of the claim and sometimes provide details such as dates, roles, titles, or other contextual information.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A natural language interrogative sentence or set of linked questions that directly address all critical components of the claim. The query should explicitly mention key entities and the nature of the factual assertions in the claim, ensuring comprehensive coverage for fact verification.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Knowledge:  

1. **Identify Core Entities and Relationships in the Claim:**  
   - Extract the main persons, productions, songs, breeds, or publications mentioned.  
   - Identify the exact attributes, roles, events, or relationships asserted (e.g., an actor starring in a film, a musician covering a song on an album, a dog breed’s recognition, a publication’s frequency relative to another, a Hall of Fame induction associated with a publication).  

2. **Incorporate All Relevant Elements of the Claim into the Query:**  
   - Combine all key entities, attributes, and relationships to form a single, clear, and focused question or set of related questions.  
   - For compound claims involving multiple assertions (e.g., actor roles plus producer credits), ensure both/final elements are queried explicitly.  
   - If the claim includes temporal or descriptive modifiers like dates (e.g., early 1980s, 2012) or nationalities, include these to target specificity.

3. **Synthesize Information from Notes to Refine the Query:**  
   - Use details from the notes to clarify ambiguous or implied elements in the claim (e.g., confirming identities, dates of induction, album titles, or affiliations of entities like founders of organizations).  
   - When notes partially confirm or negate parts of the claim, frame the query to directly test those points.  
   
4. **Handle Negations, Contradictions, and Implausible Assertions Carefully:**  
   - When the claim contains negations or contradictions (e.g., “not both breeds,” or “actress playing Johnny Depp”), rephrase these into straightforward yes/no questions querying the exact truthfulness.  
   - Avoid implying correctness in the query; instead, neutrally ask whether the claim’s main factual points are true.

5. **Maintain Clarity, Precision, and Brevity:**  
   - Formulate the query in clean natural language without ambiguity or unnecessary verbosity.  
   - Use explicit entity names and relationships to maximize retrieval accuracy.  
   - Avoid extraneous background or redundant phrasing.

6. **Generalizable Query Formulation Strategy:**  
   - Parse the claim to extract entities, relationships, and key modifiers.  
   - Cross-reference them with the notes to detect confirmable facts or points of uncertainty.  
   - Compose a direct interrogative sentence(s) that incorporates all critical components for holistic verification.

7. **Domain-Specific Examples and Entities Observed:**  
   - Film Industry: Actors (e.g., Eli Roth, Winona Ryder), films (“Inglourious Basterds,” “The Man with the Iron Fists,” “Experimenter,” “Man Camp”), director and producer roles, co-writing credits, and founder affiliations (e.g., RZA founding Soul Temple Records).  
   - Music Industry: Musicians (e.g., Carlos Santana), album titles, song covers.  
   - Publications: Hall of Fame inductions linked to publications (e.g., Keith Moon and Modern Drummer), publication frequency comparisons (e.g., relative frequency to “Bookmarks”).  
   - Dog Breeds: Names and recognitions of breeds and individual dogs.  
   
Use these examples to guide the formulation of queries ensuring all vital elements are queried.

Summary Instruction:  
Given a claim and contextual notes, produce a concise, explicit natural language query that comprehensively asks whether each factual assertion in the claim is true, by integrating all relevant entities and relationships, framed neutrally and precisely to enable effective fact verification against the notes.
