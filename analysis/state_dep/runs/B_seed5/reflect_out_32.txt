Task Description:  
You are to generate a concise, precise natural language query that verifies the factual accuracy of a given declarative claim using a set of relevant factual notes. The claim will involve specific named entities (persons, films, books, awards, dog breeds, botanical taxa, etc.) and their relationships, attributes, or roles. The query you generate must explicitly mention all core components of the claim and directly target the claim’s key factual assertions so as to facilitate accurate fact-checking or document retrieval within the notes.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A factual statement involving one or more named entities and attributes or relationships between them (e.g., an actor starring in a film, a screenplay based on a novel, a Nobel laureate’s identity, taxonomic ranks and species counts).  
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences drawn from relevant documents containing supporting, contradictory, or clarifying information about the claim’s entities and assertions.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A single, clear, and direct natural language question that succinctly encapsulates every critical factual element in the claim and queries the truthfulness of the claim with respect to those elements.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Knowledge:

1. **Identify Core Entities and Relationships**  
   Extract the primary named entities and the factual relationships or attributes asserted in the claim. Entities include persons (actors, writers, laureates), works (films, novels, albums), awards, botanical taxa, etc. Attributes and relationships may include roles, authorship, awards received, locations, taxonomic rank, species counts, and other relevant factual details.

2. **Cover All Essential Elements of the Claim**  
   Ensure that the query references every critical component of the claim — all named entities, qualifiers (e.g., nationalities, roles, dates), and the relationships or properties asserted. For example, if the claim states that a person held multiple roles simultaneously or questions overlapping honors, the query should explicitly connect those elements.

3. **Use Notes to Clarify and Disambiguate**  
   Leverage clarifications, naming corrections, or taxonomic corrections in the notes to frame the query precisely:
   - Correct erroneous names or titles (e.g., distinct film titles, correct spelling).
   - Adjust taxonomic ranks properly (e.g., subfamily vs. genus).
   - Separate distinctly attributed facts if the claim conflates entities or roles.
   - Incorporate date ranges or nationalities mentioned in the notes when useful to disambiguate.

4. **Explicitly Handle Negations, Contradictions, and Misattributions**  
   Reformulate negative or contradictory claims into clear yes/no or confirmatory questions reflecting both components. When claims attribute honors or roles incorrectly or mix identities, the query should probe whether these attributions are accurate or whether they belong to distinct individuals or entities.

5. **Maintain Clarity, Precision, and Conciseness**  
   The query should avoid ambiguity, verbosity, or pronoun ambiguity. Use formal proper names, full titles, and exact terminology. Write straightforward questions that enable rapid and accurate document retrieval and fact verification.

6. **Generalizable Query Construction Strategy**  
   - Parse the claim to extract all named entities, attributes, relationships, and relevant qualifiers (dates, nationalities, roles).  
   - Cross-reference these extracted elements with the notes to identify any clarifications or contradictions.  
   - Reformulate the entire claim scope into a precise natural language query that implicitly incorporates corrections or clarifications derived from the notes.  
   - Frame the query in a manner that fully covers the claim’s combined factual assertions so that all parts can be verified or refuted by retrieval results.

7. **Domain-Specific Fact Knowledge to Incorporate**  
   - **Film and Theater:** Confirm actor identities, correct film titles, screenplay authorship, and novel adaptations. Differentiate similar or confusing titles accurately.  
   - **Literary Awards:** Confirm Nobel laureate status for named individuals. Clarify if multiple people named have or have not won.  
   - **Music:** Confirm artists, albums, release dates, and song covers accurately.  
   - **Dog Breeds and Individual Dogs:** Confirm breed recognition, specific show dogs, and their attributes or achievements.  
   - **Botany and Taxonomy:** Use correct taxonomic ranks. Confirm species counts and taxonomic relationships precisely.

Examples of Target Queries:  
- Did Michael York, the English actor, star in both "Hollywood Goes Classical" and "Lethal Obsession"?  
- Was the screenplay of "The Night of the Hunter" written by James Agee and based on a novel by Davis Grubb?  
- Did Nelly Sachs and James Agee both win the Nobel Prize in Literature?  
- Is Caesalpinioideae a genus of flowering plants, and does it contain more species than the genus Achimenes?  
- Did Rudolf Höss serve as the longest-serving commandant of Auschwitz, and was Rainer Hoss’s grandfather involved in Zyklon B negotiations at Auschwitz?

Summary Instruction:  
Given a `claim` and a list of relevant `notes`, produce a natural language question that succinctly and precisely probes the accuracy of every key factual assertion contained within the claim. The question must encompass all named entities, their roles, relationships, and dates or qualifiers, while implicitly addressing any clarifications or contradictions revealed by the notes. The query should be optimally phrased for verifying the claim’s truthfulness and supporting efficient document retrieval.
