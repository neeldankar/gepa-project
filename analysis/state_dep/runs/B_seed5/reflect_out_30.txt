Task Description:  
You are given two inputs: a `claim`—a declarative sentence or set of statements about specific entities, events, or relationships—and a list of `notes`—contextual sentences containing factual information relevant to the claim. Your goal is to generate a concise, precise natural language `query` that can be used to verify the truthfulness of the claim based on the evidence provided by the notes.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A factual assertion mentioning one or more entities (such as persons, works, places, dates, breeds, or attributes) along with relationships or attributes about them.  
- `notes`: A list of sentences presenting factual, sometimes overlapping or partially contradictory, information relevant to the entities and facts mentioned in the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A clear, focused interrogative sentence that directly tests the key factual assertions of the claim. The query should be designed to support automatic or manual fact-checking systems by targeting all core aspects of the claim with minimal ambiguity or excess detail.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Knowledge:  

1. **Identify Core Entities and Relations Explicitly:**  
   Extract all key entities (people, places, titles, works, breeds, dates) and the specific relationships or properties asserted about them in the claim. For example:  
   - Actor names linked to film/theatre productions;  
   - Song titles connected to artist discographies, chart positions, or cover artists;  
   - Universities or institutions linked to specific roles or classifications;  
   - Dog breeds linked to individual dogs or breed recognition status.

2. **Cover All Crucial Elements from the Claim in the Query:**  
   The query must incorporate every relevant component and asserted relationship in the claim. For example, if the claim involves an actor starring in two different productions, the query should ask about both productions and the actor’s involvement explicitly. If the claim involves a university’s status and a person’s role there, both points should be queried together.

3. **Leverage Details from the Notes to Frame the Query:**  
   Use the evidence provided to tailor your query to what facts can be verified. If notes emphasize specific relationships or contradict aspects of the claim, phrase the query so it distinguishes these points clearly and invites verification. For example, if notes specify an actor played a character in one film but not another, a combined yes/no question contrasting these works is ideal.

4. **Pay Special Attention to Negations and Contradictory Claims:**  
   When the claim includes negative or mutually exclusive assertions (e.g., that something did NOT happen, or that two breeds are not both recognized), form a direct yes/no question to verify these conditions precisely.

5. **Maintain Clarity, Precision, and Brevity:**  
   The query should be concise but comprehensive, avoiding unnecessary verbosity or ambiguity. Clear phrasing enables efficient fact verification and better retrieval of relevant evidence.

6. **Generalizable Strategy for Query Formulation:**  
   - Parse the claim thoroughly to identify entities, their attributes, dates, and relationships.  
   - Cross-reference these entities and relations with the notes to determine verifiable components and possible contradictions or supporting evidence.  
   - Convert the claim into a well-structured natural language question that addresses all key points, formatted for straightforward fact checking.  
   - When the claim combines multiple assertions, phrase the query to address all simultaneously, either by conjunction or a compound question, so the truthfulness of the full claim is verifiable.

7. **Examples of Common Domain-Specific Entities and Contexts:**  
   - **Persons & Roles:** Actors (e.g., Michael York, Mark Rylance), their starring roles in films or stage productions.  
   - **Productions & Media:** Films, theatre productions, albums, or songs (e.g., "Hollywood Goes Classical," "Bridge of Spies," "Double Bass," the album "Inner Secrets").  
   - **Music Industry Details:** Songs covered by other artists, chart peak positions, original recording artists with genre tags.  
   - **Academic Institutions & Roles:** Universities’ public or private status, persons’ roles such as chairpersons on boards of trustees.  
   - **Dog Breeds & Individuals:** Recognition status of breeds (e.g., Villanuco de Las Encartaciones), identification of individual dogs bearing specific breed names.

Summary Instruction:  
Given a `claim` and a set of relevant `notes`, produce a precise natural language `query` that incorporates all salient entities and relations from the claim. Ensure the query directly targets the exact facts or relationships whose verification will confirm or refute the claim’s accuracy. The query should be structured to enable clear, concise fact checking using the evidence in the notes and should handle any contradictions, negations, or multiple assertions carefully and explicitly.
