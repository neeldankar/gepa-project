Task Description:  
You are given two inputs: a `claim` (a declarative sentence or set of statements about specific entities or events) and a list of `notes` (contextual sentences containing factual information relevant to the claim). Your goal is to generate a concise, focused `query` that can be used to verify the accuracy of the claim based on the notes.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A text string stating something factual about one or more entities, their attributes, or relationships (e.g., persons, events, songs, albums, breeds, productions).  
- `notes`: A list of text strings containing factual information drawn from relevant documents which relate to the claim. The notes may provide partial or overlapping evidence supporting or contradicting the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query`: A natural language question that directly addresses the truthfulness of the claim by precisely querying the key factual points and entities involved.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Knowledge:  
1. **Focus on Core Entities and Relations:**  
   Identify the key entities or events in the claim (e.g., names of actors, films, albums, songs, dog breeds), and the relationships or attributes asserted about them (e.g., starring roles, song covers, breed recognition).  
   
2. **Incorporate All Relevant Elements from the Claim:**  
   The generated query must reflect all crucial components present in the claim and the relationships between them to enable targeted fact-checking. For example, if the claim refers to an actor starring in two productions, the query should ask if the same actor starred in both specific productions.

3. **Use Evidence from Notes to Formulate Queries:**  
   Synthesizing information from the notes is key. For example:  
   - When multiple notes confirm that Michael York is an English actor starring in certain productions, the query should ask about this identity and his participation explicitly.  
   - When the notes cover a specific album by an artist and a particular song cover, the query should combine these facts to verify the claim.

4. **Handle Negations and Contradictions Carefully:**  
   In cases where the claim asserts a negative or contradictory fact (e.g., "not both breeds of dog"), the query should be rephrased into a direct yes/no question about the truth of both conditions to verify the claim properly.

5. **Maintain Clarity and Precision:**  
   The query should be concise and clearly formulated to facilitate document retrieval or fact verification. Avoid unnecessary verbosity or ambiguity.

6. **Generalizable Strategy:**  
   - Parse the claim for entities, events, relationships, and modifiers (e.g., nationality, dates, locations).  
   - Cross-reference these with the notes content to identify verifiable components.  
   - Convert the claim into an interrogative form that directly targets the relevant factual assertions in a way a document retrieval or fact-checking system can handle efficiently.

7. **Examples of Domain-Specific Entities and Contexts Observed:**  
   - Actors and stage or film productions (e.g., English actor Michael York starring in "Hollywood Goes Classical" or "Lethal Obsession")  
   - Musicians, albums, and cover songs (e.g., Carlos Santana covering "Stormy" on the 1978 studio album "Inner Secrets")  
   - Dog breeds and individual dogs (e.g., Banana Joe V Tani Kazari as a toy Affenpinscher breed, Villanuco de Las Encartaciones as a recognized breed)  
   
Following these instructions will help you generate queries that cover key claim aspects, use the notes effectively, and support comprehensive fact verification.

Summary Instruction:  
Given the `claim` and `notes`, produce a precise natural language `query` that directly asks about the truthfulness of the claim by incorporating all critical entities and relations from the claim, structured to facilitate fact checking against the evidence in the notes.
