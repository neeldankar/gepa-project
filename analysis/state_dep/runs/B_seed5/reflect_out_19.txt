Task: Generate an effective and focused query to fact-check or verify a given claim using the provided notes.

Input format:  
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that typically combines historical dates, geographic locations, entities (such as plants, bands, sports teams, individuals), or relationships between them.  
- `notes`: A list of factual points or evidential statements relevant to the claim, which may include confirmations, contradictions, or gaps in information.

Output required:  
- `query`: A concise but comprehensive natural language question that enables retrieval of relevant information to verify the claim. The query should target the key entities and relationships needing validation, guided by the available evidence and any noted uncertainties or contradictions.

Detailed task description and domain-specific considerations:  
1. **Identify verification targets:**  
   - Pinpoint the factual elements in the claim that require confirmation, especially those contradicted or unconfirmed by the notes (e.g., specific dates, locations, relationships between entities).  
   - Use the notes to determine which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or missing evidence.

2. **Structure queries for effective document retrieval:**  
   - Formulate queries that directly ask about entities, their characteristics, or their relationships as implied or questioned by the claim.  
   - Where a claim links multiple elements (e.g., a plant subspecies and its habitat, a band and its album count, a sports team and a player's career timeline), the query should explicitly address the relationship or comparative fact in doubt.  
   - Include geographical, temporal, and entity-specific details from the claim that are relevant for precise retrieval.

3. **Incorporate negative or missing evidence indicated in notes:**  
   - When notes indicate lack of confirmation or contradictory facts (e.g., absence of evidence for a date, a mismatch in locations, uncertain album counts), queries should explicitly seek to clarify this missing or disputed information.

4. **Generalizable reasoning strategy:**  
   - Begin by summarizing the crucial factual elements relevant for verification within the claim and contrast these with the notes.  
   - Identify gaps or contradictions needing more evidence or retrieval.  
   - Frame a targeted question (query) that will retrieve documents addressing these exact points, thereby efficiently supporting or refuting the claim.

5. **Examples of domain-specific facts to consider:**  
   - Geographical distributions of subspecies within national parks in the Canary Islands.  
   - The discography counts of rock bands and their aliases.  
   - Founding dates and player/manager associations of North American baseball teams, including their operational timelines and affiliations.

6. **Query phrasing style:**  
   - Clear, unambiguous, and targeted for fact retrieval.  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague questions.  
   - When appropriate, use confirmatory format ("Is X true that...?") or exploratory format ("What is the...?", "Which team...?", "Who managed...?").

Summary:  
Given the `claim` and supporting or contradictory `notes`, produce a precise, fact-focused `query` that centers on verifying the claim’s key factual assertions, especially those uncertain or contradicted in the notes, to facilitate retrieval of the most relevant evidence supporting or refuting the claim.
