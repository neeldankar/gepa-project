Task Description:
You will be given three inputs:
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion involving entities (e.g., people, bands, places, albums, organizations).
- `notes`: a list of sentences extracted or generated from relevant documents, containing factual information related to the claim.
- `context`: a collection of detailed background documents, each with an informational title and text, providing supporting or related knowledge about entities or topics mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to produce two outputs:
1. `new_notes`: A filtered and distilled selection of notes that are most relevant and directly support or refute the factual components of the claim. The new_notes should:
   - Include specific, factual information that verifies or contradicts parts of the claim.
   - Avoid redundancy and overly broad or tangential notes.
   - Emphasize key pieces of information necessary to assess the truth or details of the claim.
   - Provide evidence about entities’ relationships, attributes (such as formation dates, membership counts, genres, locations), and other verifiable facts that pertain to the claim.
   - Capture nuanced distinctions where applicable (e.g., genre differences, membership counts, formation years).

2. `titles`: A list of entity or document titles covering the primary topics needed to evaluate or contextualize the claim. Titles should:
   - Correspond to individual entities or topics that are central to the claim.
   - Include relevant bands, albums, people, locations, or other named entities.
   - Help map the new_notes back to authoritative or known reference documents.
   - Be precise and relevant, avoiding unnecessary or unrelated titles.
   
Key Instructions and Strategies:
- Analyze the claim carefully to identify all entities, attributes, and relationships it contains (e.g., band names, albums, city and county names, individual names).
- Use the notes and context to verify factual components such as:
   - Locations (e.g., cities within counties),
   - Entity types and genres (e.g., alternative rock vs. country music),
   - Formation dates,
   - Membership counts,
   - Associations between entities (which band recorded which album or song),
   - Other specific facts supporting or refuting the claim.
- When notes contradict each other, prioritize those that are directly supported by the context documents.
- Summarize multiple notes into a concise, representative set that covers all essential facts relevant to the claim’s verification or elaboration.
- Select titles of entities, albums, songs, or places that clearly map to the information included in new_notes and that would be most useful for end-users seeking references.
- Aim to capture all essential documents to cover the claim's facts—retrieval of all relevant titles is critical.
- Avoid adding generic or overly general titles (e.g., "Scottish Alternative Rock") unless they serve as key clarifications for the claim.
- Make sure that new_notes and titles are consistent with each other.

Domain-Specific Knowledge Embedded:
- Understanding of entities such as music bands and their attributes (formation year, genre, membership count).
- Familiarity with album releases, song recordings, and their associated artists.
- Geographical knowledge regarding cities, counties, and institutions within US states.
- Awareness of naming conventions (e.g., shorthand or full names of bands, acronyms like UNLV).
- Discerning subtle factual differences like genre classification and counting members versus number of albums.
- Recognizing that some claims may be partially true and require nuanced explanation.

In summary, the task requires you to carefully verify the claim using the notes and context, produce a minimal but comprehensive set of notes that directly bear on the claim's truthfulness or factuality, and identify the key titles corresponding to authoritative documents that enabled that verification. The output should help someone understand the factual verification with clear references.
