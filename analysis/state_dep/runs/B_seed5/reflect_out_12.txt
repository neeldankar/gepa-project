Task Description:  
You are given two inputs:  
- a `claim`: a declarative statement asserting factual information about specific entities or events, such as people, bands, films, songs, albums, dog breeds, or productions, and  
- a list of `notes`: factual contextual sentences relevant to the claim, which may partially or fully support or contradict the claim.  

Your goal is to generate a concise, focused natural language `query` that directly tests the truthfulness of the claim by covering all key entities, attributes, and relationships from the claim, grounded in the provided notes. This query should enable efficient fact verification or document retrieval about the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A declarative factual sentence or set of statements involving entities and their relationships or attributes.  
- `notes`: A list of textual evidence sentences relevant to the entities and relations in the claim.  

Output Format:  
- `query`: A clear, precise natural language interrogative sentence that encapsulates all the essential components of the claim and the key relations or attributes asserted, structured to verify its accuracy against the notes.

Detailed Instructions, Domain-Specific Knowledge, and Strategy:  

1. **Identify Core Entities and Relations:**  
   Parse the claim to extract the main entities (e.g., actor names, film titles, band names, dog breeds, song titles), their modifiers (such as nationality, dates, roles), and relationships or attributes asserted (e.g., starring roles, musical collaborations, breed recognition, genre classification, professional commonalities).

2. **Include All Relevant Claim Components in the Query:**  
   The generated query must reflect every crucial aspect of the claim, both entities and their relationships, to allow comprehensive fact checking. For example:  
   - If the claim names an actor starring in multiple productions, ask explicitly if that actor starred in all those productions.  
   - If the claim involves a song covered by an artist on a particular album, incorporate all those details.  
   - If the claim asserts joint characteristics or commonalities (e.g., shared profession), the query should verify these for all relevant entities.  
   - If the claim involves negations or contradictions, rephrase the query into a yes/no question explicitly testing those conditions.

3. **Leverage the Notes to Formulate the Query:**  
   Use factual content from the notes to confirm which entities and relations can be verified. The query should focus on these and not introduce unsupported claims. For instance, when notes specify an album’s release year or an artist's nationality, include these in the query if relevant.

4. **Maintain Clarity and Brevity:**  
   The query should be concise, unambiguous, and straightforward, facilitating efficient retrieval or verification. Avoid verbose or overly complex phrasing.

5. **Generalizable Approach for Query Generation:**  
   - Parse the claim for entities, relationships, and modifiers.  
   - Cross-reference these with the contextual notes to identify verifiable facts.  
   - Reformulate the claim into a natural language question that explicitly tests the truthfulness of the claim’s key assertions.  
   - When multiple entities or relations appear, cover them all within a single, coherent query where possible.

6. **Domain-Specific Entities and Context Examples:**  
   The task covers a range of factual domains encountered in entertainment and biological contexts, such as:  
   - Actors and film/theater productions, including film franchises and roles (e.g., Michael York starring in "Hollywood Goes Classical" and "Lethal Obsession").  
   - Musicians, albums, and song covers (e.g., Carlos Santana covering "Stormy" on the 1978 album "Inner Secrets").  
   - Bands and recording labels, including national or regional descriptors (e.g., Scottish punk band APB working with Young American Recordings).  
   - Vocalists and songwriting collaborations (e.g., Matt Thiessen of Relient K co-writing "Rebuild" with Jon Foreman).  
   - Dog breeds and individual dogs, including breed recognition status (e.g., toy Affenpinscher breed, Villanuco de Las Encartaciones).

7. **Handling Negations, Durations, and Multiple Entities:**  
   Claims involving negations (e.g., "not both breeds") or specific numerical claims (e.g., "worked 3 years") require the query to frame these points explicitly as yes/no checks.  
   For claims involving multiple entities or multiple facets, the query should combine these effectively to verify all core assertions in one question when feasible.

Summary:  
Given a declarative factual claim and a set of supporting or contradicting notes, produce a natural language query that explicitly asks whether the claim is true by focusing on all core entities, attributes, and relationships in the claim, in a clear, concise manner, and using evidence from the notes as a guide for which facts to incorporate. This makes verifying or retrieving evidence for the claim straightforward and comprehensive.
