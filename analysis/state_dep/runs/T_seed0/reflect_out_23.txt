Task Description:  
You will be provided with three input fields:  
- `claim`: A factual or semi-factual statement that may contain one or more assertions about people, events, entities, or relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of textual observations, evidence snippets, or collected facts related to the claim and context, often drawn from external documents or knowledge sources. These represent pieces of information extracted or generated to analyze the claim.  
- `context`: A list of paragraphs or document summaries relevant to the entities or topics mentioned in the claim. These provide source-level information to ground your verification or elaboration of the claim.  

Your goal is to produce two output fields:  
1. `new_notes`: A concise, relevant, and factually accurate subset or rephrasing of the input notes that directly support, refute, or clarify the claim based on the provided context. The new notes should:  
   - Explicitly state the linkage (or lack thereof) between the entities and facts in the claim, referencing key details from context.  
   - Highlight any contradictions, confirmations, or inability to verify portions of the claim.  
   - Remove redundant or irrelevant notes and improve clarity and factual precision.  
   
2. `titles`: A list of concise, descriptive document or topic titles that correspond to the most relevant knowledge sources from the context used to assess the claim. These titles should:  
   - Represent key entities, events, or topics necessary to understand and verify the claim.  
   - Match or closely relate to actual document names or subjects from the context.  
   - Reflect comprehensive coverage of critical aspects of the claim’s factual basis without superfluous additions.

Key Considerations and Domain-Specific Instructions:  
- The `claim` may combine multiple sub-claims or mix correct facts with incorrect ones. Your response should break down these elements clearly in reasoning and new notes.  
- Verify named persons, roles, historical data, nicknames, university statuses, and detailed sports statistics by cross-referencing multiple context paragraphs where applicable.  
- Distinguish between similarly named entities (e.g., persons with similar names, similarly titled institutions or events) using the context provided to avoid misinformation.  
- Address ambiguous or indirectly connected claims by noting the absence of confirming evidence rather than assuming correctness or making unsupported assertions.  
- The `notes` often contain partially or wholly correct statements, speculation, or negations; synthesize these to produce well-reasoned, coherent conclusions.  
- When possible, explain why the claim is supported, unsupported, or unverifiable, showing an informed understanding of the underlying domain knowledge (e.g., film roles and actor histories, university public status, football club nicknames and player roles).  
- In forming `titles`, prefer administrative, biographical, or topic-specific document headings that best represent the claim components to enhance retrievability and clarity.  

Generalizable Strategy:  
- Carefully extract key entities and assertions from the claim.  
- Identify supporting or refuting evidence in the notes with direct linkage to the context.  
- Eliminate redundancies and remove irrelevant information from notes.  
- Explicitly state confirmation or refutation according to context, or clarify that the claim cannot be fully verified.  
- Select document titles that comprehensively cover these evaluated aspects for easy reference.

Format of output:  
- `new_notes` as a list of well-formed sentences summarizing or clarifying the factual support or contradiction to the claim.  
- `titles` as a list of concise but descriptive document or topic names extracted or derived from the context metadata.  
- Include a brief reasoning summary before producing new_notes and titles to clarify your logical process (if part of internal generation, reasoning may be implicit).  
  
By following this precise and methodical approach, you will generate factual, precise, and domain-informed outputs suitable for reliable claim verification and evidence presentation.
