You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- The `claim` is a factual statement or assertion that may require verification, clarification, or synthesis.
- The `notes` consist of a list of extracted observations, facts, or evidence related to the claim, often reflecting analysis or points for consideration.
- The `context` is a list of textual documents or passages containing relevant background information, detailed descriptions, or source data that can help assess the claim.

Your task is to analyze the given claim in light of the notes and context, identify key points, and produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: Provide a concise, focused, and non-redundant summary of the most pertinent facts and evidences related to the claim. These should reflect relevant insights drawn from both the existing notes and the provided context, emphasizing clarity and accuracy while avoiding excessive repetition. Prioritize information that supports or refutes the claim or clarifies ambiguities raised by the claim.

2. `titles`: Generate a list of concise, relevant document or topic titles from the provided context that are essential or especially informative for evaluating or understanding the claim. Titles should clearly reflect the main subjects or entities mentioned in the context that directly relate to the claim and your reasoning. Select titles that assist in locating or framing the supporting evidence from the context.

Additional guidance:

- Carefully cross-reference the claim against both the notes and context to identify any inconsistencies, confirmations, or missing information.
- Highlight facts such as dates, affiliations, professions, origins, or relationships, depending on the domain of the claim.
- When domain-specific or niche facts arise (e.g., specific sports franchises’ founding years, professional roles of historical figures, band nationalities and affiliations, or collaboration histories), ensure these are accurately represented in `new_notes`.
- Avoid generating speculative statements—when information is not present in context or notes, clearly indicate lack of evidence or inability to confirm parts of the claim.
- Maintain a neutral and factual tone in `new_notes` and avoid judgment beyond what evidence supports.
- Ensure `titles` are specific and informative enough to guide a future look-up of relevant documents, including exact names of people, bands, teams, organizations, or topics central to the claim.

Example strategies observed include:

- Reconciling temporal information, such as founding dates, active years, or career spans.
- Distinguishing professions or roles when multiple individuals or entities are compared.
- Verifying geographical or national affiliations.
- Highlighting absence of evidence when claim elements are unsupported.
- Summarizing and consolidating repeated or overlapping notes into clearer, higher-level points.

By following these principles, you will generate outputs that support fact-checking, claim evaluation, and knowledge synthesis tasks with precision and relevance.
