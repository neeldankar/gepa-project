You will be given three input fields:

- `claim`: A fact or assertion that may be true or false.
- `notes`: A list of extracted factual statements and observations relevant to the claim, often used to evaluate or verify it.
- `context`: A list of background documents or excerpts that provide additional information potentially related to the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

- `new_notes`: A concise, accurate, and factually relevant subset or synthesis of the input `notes` that directly supports the evaluation of the claim. This should clarify or summarize the key factual points necessary to determine the claim’s veracity or to understand its components. Avoid including redundant, unrelated, or speculative statements. The `new_notes` should represent a clear, evidence-based assessment aligned with the claim.

- `titles`: A list of titles of authoritative, factually relevant documents (usually from the `context`) that directly pertain to or support the key factual points in `new_notes`. These titles should help identify the core sources of information used to judge the claim. Include documents whose content was critically relevant or provided essential facts used to confirm or refute the claim.

### Additional Details and Domain-Specific Guidelines:

1. **Claim Evaluation Context:**  
   The claim often involves specific real-world entities (e.g., sports teams, locations, publications) or historical facts. The `notes` may contain supporting or contradictory evidence and sometimes multiple overlapping details about these entities. Your job is to distill this into a focused factual summary that addresses the claim comprehensively.

2. **Use of Notes and Context:**  
   - Use the `notes` to identify, verify, and correct details in the claim.  
   - Use the `context` to identify authoritative sources or documents from which the notes might have been derived or that provide background to verify the entities or facts mentioned.  
   - If the claim references a particular entity or fact (e.g., a stadium, a publication, a railway), prioritize including titles related to those key entities.  
   - When multiple relevant documents exist, select the most pertinent ones that directly support or dispute the claim.

3. **Reasoning Approach (implied):**  
   - Extract the key facts necessary to validate or refute the claim from `notes`.  
   - Identify factual inconsistencies or confirmations.  
   - Summarize these facts precisely in `new_notes`.  
   - Map these facts back to source documents in `context`, listing those whose titles correspond to key factual evidence.  
   - Do not include documents or notes unrelated to the core facts needed to evaluate the claim.

4. **Factual Accuracy and Specificity:**  
   The task requires strict adherence to factual correctness. Avoid speculation or assumptions beyond the provided notes and context. When a claim is refuted, explicitly note the evidence countering it. When a claim is supported, highlight the confirming evidence.  

5. **Formatting notes and titles:**  
   - `new_notes` should be a list of concise factual sentences derived from your synthesis of `notes`.  
   - `titles` should be a list of document titles as found in `context`, corresponding to the key factual sources.

### Summary of workflow:
- Analyze the `claim`.  
- Review `notes` to identify factual information relevant to the claim’s validation.  
- Construct `new_notes` that accurately and succinctly summarize this information.  
- Identify `titles` of key supporting contextual documents that pertain to these facts.  
- Ensure both outputs help in clearly and verifiably addressing the truth or falsehood of the claim.

This approach enables consistent, evidence-based fact checking and concise documentation of relevant sources.
