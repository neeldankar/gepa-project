You are given two input fields: 

- `claim`: A statement asserting some factual information, often involving entities, relationships, or attributes.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or evidential snippets relevant to verifying or investigating the claim.

Your task is to produce an output field called:

- `query`

The purpose of `query` is to formulate a clear, precise, and targeted inquiry or set of inquiries that would best help verify, refute, or understand the truthfulness or context of the claim using the information seen in `notes`. The query should be answerable by retrieving or confirming relevant knowledge, documents, or data.

Key points and domain-specific understanding for this task:

1. **Nature of Input and Output**  
   - The `claim` often involves specific entities (e.g., football clubs, composers, bands), their attributes (e.g., sponsorship, nationality, discography), or relationships (e.g., membership in a league, authorship, album release counts).  
   - The `notes` provide detailed contextual facts, sometimes contradicting the claim or clarifying entities and their relationships.  
   - The `query` should directly address the core factual components of the claim in a way that, if answered, confirms or refutes the claim reliably.

2. **Use of Domain Knowledge and Reasoning**  
   - Extract from the `notes` the relevant entities and their factual attributes or relationships that relate directly to the claim.  
   - Identify contradictions or confirmations in the notes with respect to the claim.  
   - Formulate queries that ask about these key facts:  
     - Entity membership and sponsorship details for sports leagues and clubs  
     - Composer identity, nationality, gender, and their works for musical compositions  
     - Discography or album release counts for musical groups  
   
3. **Query Specificity and Clarity**  
   - Queries should explicitly include every crucial entity involved in the claim.  
   - Include the relevant attributes or relationships that need verification — e.g., membership status, team sponsorship, composer authorship, nationality, or gender, album counts.  
   - Queries can be natural language questions or structured database-like queries when detailed data counts or comparisons apply, particularly when numeric verification (like album counts) is necessary.

4. **General Strategy for Generating Queries**  
   - Decompose the claim into factual sub-claims or components.  
   - For each sub-claim, generate a query that directly seeks the facts needed to verify that component using the knowledge hinted at in the notes.  
   - When entities or facts are missing from the notes or no concrete data exists, formulate queries that request the precise missing facts or data source verification.  
   - Ensure the query covers all relevant claims, e.g., inclusion of sponsors, league divisions, composer identities, or comparing numeric data like album counts.

5. **Avoid unnecessary or irrelevant details**  
   - The query should remain focused and unambiguous. Do not include unrelated facts or speculative questions.  
   - Keep phrasing concise but complete to maximize retrieval accuracy and relevance in follow-up evidence gathering.

6. **Examples**  
   - For verifying football club memberships and league sponsorships:  
     "Are [Club A] and [Club B] currently members of a football league sponsored by [Sponsor]? If not, which leagues are they currently part of, and who sponsors them?"  
   - For verifying composer identity and attributes:  
     "Who composed [Musical Work], and what are the composer's nationality and gender?"  
   - For verifying album release counts or numeric comparisons:  
     Use structured queries or explicit comparative questions such as:  
     "How many albums have [Band A] and [Band B] released respectively? Does [Band A] have twice as many albums as [Band B]?"

By following this approach, your generated `query` will precisely target the factual verification of the claim, leveraging the provided notes and enabling effective evidence retrieval and validation.
