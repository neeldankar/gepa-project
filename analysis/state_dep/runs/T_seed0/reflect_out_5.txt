You will be given two fields as input: `claim` (a factual or biographical assertion or statement) and `notes` (a list of evidentiary statements, facts, or pieces of supporting or contradicting information related to the claim). Your task is to produce a `query` field — a clear, focused, and specific search query or question intended to retrieve relevant authoritative documents or knowledge sources that could verify, refute, or clarify the claim.

Key points and domain-specific guidelines:

1. **Purpose of the Query**:  
   - The query must explicitly target the key factual components or relationships in the claim that need verification.  
   - Include key named entities (persons, events, organizations, films, dates, awards, roles, affiliations) and the precise context or relationships that connect them as presented in the claim and notes.  
   - Frame the query to check for alignment or discrepancy between the claim’s assertions and the evidence in the notes.

2. **Utilizing the Notes**:  
   - Use the notes as an information base to identify relevant entities and contextual clues that need to appear in the query.  
   - Incorporate details from the notes that clarify or contradict the claim to form focused queries that can retrieve missing or confirming evidence.  
   - If specific named entities appear multiple times across notes (like specific films, festivals, politicians, actors, organizations), ensure those are included in the query to aid precise document retrieval.

3. **Generalizable Query Construction Strategy**:  
   - Identify all primary elements of the claim (e.g., people, dates, events, roles) that need verification.  
   - Explicitly express the relationships or assertions connecting those elements (e.g., “actor X was a WWII Navy Combat veteran”, “politician Y led party Z on a particular date”, “film A won award B at event C”).  
   - Ask clear, factual questions or “Who/What/When/Did” style queries that precisely capture the verification need, avoiding ambiguity.  
   - When multiple entities or aspects are involved, include them conjunctively to filter for the exact fact pattern in retrieved sources (e.g., “Which musical films won the Golden Bear at the 50th Berlin International Film Festival, and is there any record of an actor being a WWII Navy veteran?”).

4. **Domain-specific knowledge considerations**:  
   - Film festivals (e.g., Berlin International Film Festival) awards are named (Golden Bear).  
   - Political party leadership, birthdates, and affiliations between youth wings and main parties are commonly verified facts.  
   - Actors’ roles and filmographies are crucial for resolving claims about who starred in which films or TV shows.  
   - Dates (like election dates, birthdates, award years) are critical to confirm timelines and identities.  
   - Titles of films, TV shows, and organizations should be used precisely to prevent conflating unrelated entities.

5. **Conciseness and Clarity**:  
   - Formulate queries that are concise enough for efficient search or retrieval but comprehensive enough to cover all claim verification angles.  
   - Avoid conversational or speculative phrasing; queries should be formulated as factual, information-seeking questions.

Summary: Given a claim and supporting/conflicting notes, produce a precise, targeted verification query that clearly outlines the key entities, dates, roles, and factual assertions from the inputs, enabling retrieval of relevant authoritative documents to support or refute the claim.
