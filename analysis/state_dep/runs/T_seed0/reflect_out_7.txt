Task Description:
You are given a `claim` — a factual statement or assertion — and a set of detailed `notes` containing verified information related to the claim. Your goal is to produce a focused and comprehensive `query` that can be used to verify the claim through retrieval or fact-checking against documented knowledge. 

Input Format:
- `claim`: A sentence or short paragraph stating a factual assertion or combination of assertions.
- `notes`: A list of bullet-point facts or sentences that provide context, clarifications, corrections, or relevant background information about entities, locations, dates, people, or events related to the claim.

Output:
- `query`: A clear, precise, and well-formed question or set of questions that collectively target the main factual points in the claim. The query should be designed to retrieve authoritative and relevant documents or evidence to confirm or refute the claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. Carefully analyze the `claim` and identify its key factual components — entities (people, bands, places, species), dates, relationships, locations, and specific assertions made.
2. Review the `notes` to:
   - Understand the factual context.
   - Identify contradictions or confirmations in relation to the claim.
   - Extract relevant domain-specific factual information (e.g., geographic distinctions between islands and parks, band membership and location, casting and biography of actors, native habitat of subspecies, administrative divisions).
3. Construct a query that:
   - Explicitly mentions the critical entities and facts necessary to verify the claim.
   - Reflects nuances or corrections found in the notes (e.g., distinguishing between different islands or species subspecies, specifying the county of a city in Norway, clarifying the identity and attributes of an actor).
   - Is focused on retrieving the minimal yet sufficient set of authoritative documents to verify the specific factual assertions.
4. When the claim involves multiple interconnected facts, compose a query that covers all relevant subclaims succinctly and logically, enabling targeted evidence retrieval.
5. The query should be formulated as natural language questions or question sets that a search or retrieval system can understand.
6. Use domain-specific knowledge as reflected in the notes to ensure precision, such as:
   - The taxonomy and geography of Echium wildpretii subspecies and corresponding Canary Islands national parks.
   - Norwegian administrative geography: bands based in Bergen and Bergen’s placement within Hordaland county.
   - Actor biographies, character roles, and associated commercial appearances, including clarifying nationality and birthdates related to specific TV series episodes.
7. Avoid extraneous content or ambiguity in the query; aim for compactness and clarity while preserving all necessary fact-checking targets.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Break down the claim into elemental facts.
- Cross-reference these facts with the notes to identify the true/false status or ambiguity.
- Build targeted natural language queries reflecting these core facts and their necessary context.
- Ensure queries can lead to retrieval of all potentially relevant evidence mentioned in the notes/feedback.

This approach facilitates precise evidence retrieval, supports rigorous fact verification, and improves downstream accuracy in assessing claims.
