Task Description:  
You will be given a factual claim—a specific statement or assertions involving entities (such as people, locations, species, organizations, events, or dates)—and a set of detailed notes containing verified, contextual, or contrasting information related to the claim. Your task is to generate a precise, well-formed natural language query or set of queries. These queries should be designed to efficiently retrieve authoritative and relevant evidence that can verify or refute the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A short sentence or paragraph asserting one or more factual points.  
- `notes`: A list of bullet points containing verified facts, clarifications, corrections, or relevant background information pertaining to the entities, dates, places, relationships, or events referenced in the claim.

Output:  
- `query`: One or more clear and concise natural language questions that explicitly target the key factual elements of the claim and incorporate important context or corrections from the notes. The query should be compact but comprehensive enough to support retrieval of all relevant authoritative documents or data needed to validate or refute the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Decompose the Claim:  
   - Identify all critical factual components: names of people, groups, bands, or species; places including cities, counties, parks, and islands; dates or date ranges; relationships between entities (e.g., band membership, actor roles); and specific assertions (such as formation dates, scoring events, or native habitats).  
   - When multiple facts or subclaims exist, consider each independently and how they connect logically.

2. Analyze the Notes Carefully:  
   - Use the notes to understand the verified background and domain-specific facts, e.g.:  
     - Taxonomy distinctions between species and subspecies (e.g., Echium wildpretii subspecies native to specific Canary Islands with unique corresponding national parks).  
     - Precise Norwegian administrative geography, e.g., that a band based in Bergen falls within Hordaland county.  
     - Film or actor specifics, such as actor birthdates, nationalities, character roles, and associated commercial appearances.  
   - Identify any contradictions, corrections, or clarifications in the notes relative to the claim, and reflect these nuances in the query to prevent ambiguity or conflation of facts.

3. Construct the Query:  
   - Explicitly mention all key entities and their relevant factual attributes needed to check the claim (e.g., "Nathaniel 'Nate' Jacks" vs a football team name, or who scored in a particular FIFA match).  
   - Incorporate corrections or finer domain distinctions from the notes (e.g., clarify if a regiment began in a year different from the claim, or separate similarly named films and the involvement of specific composers).  
   - Combine all related factual verification questions into a minimal set of precise, logically ordered natural language questions.  
   - Avoid vagueness or extraneous details; aim for specificity ensuring the query leads directly to retrieval of the minimal sufficient body of authoritative fact sources.  
   - Use question formats understandable by retrieval or fact-checking systems (e.g., "When was X established?", "Did Y score in match Z?").

4. Example Domain-Specific Knowledge and Nuances to Embed:  
   - Sports and players: Distinctions between indoor American football and international soccer; verifying player participation and scoring in specific historical matches.  
   - Military history and films: Correct regiment names (e.g., 100th Infantry Battalion/442nd Regimental Combat Team), accurate formation dates vs combat timelines, and confirming film representations and directors.  
   - Films and music scores: Accurate movie release years, composer assignments, distinguishing unrelated films with similar names, confirming sequel or related film statuses.  
   - Geography: Norwegian counties and cities; Canary Islands national parks and native species distributions.  
   - Actor biographies: Birthdates, nationalities, credited roles, and commercial appearances linked to specific TV series or characters.

5. Generalizable Strategy:  
   - Break down the claim into elemental fact assertions.  
   - Cross-reference each fact with the notes to identify confirmations, contradictions, or additional clarifying data.  
   - Formulate natural language questions that explicitly include all key entities, dates, relationships, and corrections necessary to verify or refute each component.  
   - Combine questions succinctly when they verify interconnected facts in the claim, ensuring logical flow and minimal redundancy.  
   - Ensure queries are suitable for targeted evidence retrieval and downstream rigorous fact-checking.

6. Avoid:  
   - Ambiguity or overly broad/general phrasing that might retrieve irrelevant or excessive information.  
   - Mixing unrelated domains or conflating entities that the notes specify as distinct.  
   - Inclusion of commentary or reasoning in the output—only output the final query in natural language question form.

By faithfully applying these instructions, you will create queries that enable efficient and precise retrieval of all relevant authoritative documents or evidence required to assess the truthfulness of the claim.
