You will be given two inputs: a `claim` (a statement or assertion about entities such as films, companies, bands, or locations) and `notes` (a list of factual excerpts or evidence related to the claim). Your task is to produce a corresponding `query` that will help verify or fact-check the claim.

The `query` should be a clear, concise, and focused question or set of questions that can be used to retrieve authoritative documents or sources confirming or refuting the claim.

Key points and domain-specific details to consider when crafting the `query`:

1. **Identify the core factual elements** of the claim that require verification. These often involve:
   - Classification or genre of films, music bands, or other media properties (e.g., Is this film classified as an American family comedy action adventure movie? Is a band considered a rock band?).
   - Geographic or corporate relationships (e.g., Are these companies/airports located in the same country?).
   - Association or collaboration relationships between people and groups (e.g., Did Jacob Gentry collaborate with a specific band?).

2. **Use entities explicitly mentioned** in the claim but, when relevant, include the most well-known or canonical names and qualifiers (e.g., "101 Dalmatians" when referring to the Disney animated film with Cruella de Vil).

3. **Formulate the query as a verification question** referencing the key entities and attributes central to the claim. Avoid yes/no-only queries; frame questions to enable retrieval of documents that provide definitive evidence about the relationships or classifications in question.

4. **If multiple entities or aspects are mentioned, compose the query to cover all points** necessary to fully verify the claim. For example, queries may ask about relationships between entities, or parallel classifications of two different entities.

5. **Leverage the provided notes to guide your query scope and phrasing,** ensuring it targets the facts needed to confirm or refute the claim, not superfluous information.

6. **When reasoning, the assistant should internally confirm:**
   - Which entities require population of attributes from external sources.
   - What categories or relationships must be investigated.
   - How to link the claim details into a coherent search query.

In summary, your output should be a single, well-formed, targeted query (or multiple queries if logically necessary) that can be used to retrieve relevant authoritative documents to verify the truthfulness of the given claim based on the underlying factual evidence.

Example formulation pattern:  
- For genre or classification claims: "Are [Entity A] and [Entity B] classified as [genre/type]?"  
- For location claims: "Are [Entity A] and [Entity B] located in the same country?"  
- For association claims: "Is [Person] associated with [Entity] and are both considered [category]?"

Do not restate the claim; transform it into an information-seeking query tailored for document retrieval and fact verification.
