You will be given three input fields: 

1. `claim` — a statement that asserts some factual information.  
2. `notes` — a list of remarks or observations related to the claim, often highlighting missing evidence, contradictions, or confirmations.  
3. `context` — a list of relevant text passages or documents containing facts, biographical details, filmographies, award histories, production credits, etc., that may confirm or refute the claim.

Your task is to analyze these inputs to produce two output fields: 

- `new_notes`: a concise, coherent distillation of the key points from the provided `notes` and `context`. This should summarize verified facts, highlight gaps, and capture contradictions or confirmations relevant to the claim. Avoid verbosity by focusing on core evidence that supports or refutes the claim or identifies unresolved aspects.  

- `titles`: a list of concise, descriptive titles or headings reflecting the main topics, entities, and relationships discussed or needed to understand the claim’s validity. Titles should include names of people (actors, directors, producers), works (films, TV shows), awards/nominations, roles, or production details directly relevant to the claim or noted evidence.

### Key Detailed Instructions and Domain Knowledge:

- The task often involves verification of claims related to entertainment industry facts such as:  
  - Actors’ roles and critical acclaim (including specific characters and films)  
  - Awards and nominations (e.g., Golden Globe nominations/wins, Academy Awards) and their link to actors or productions  
  - Directorship and production credits (e.g., co-directors of animated films)  
  - Associations between individuals and projects (e.g., who produced or starred in which film/series)  
  - Verification or correction of mistaken claims about roles or individuals

- When evaluating the claim, synthesize insights from both the `notes` (which typically reflect prior research or preliminary assessments) and the `context` (which contains the source factual information). The `new_notes` should integrate these perspectives logically.

- Explicitly identify:  
  - Confirmations: facts from `context` that support parts or the whole claim  
  - Contradictions: clear factual errors or mistaken associations in the claim (e.g., wrong actor or role)  
  - Missing information: when context lacks information to confirm/refute a claim or to link entities

- Avoid repeating all notes verbatim; instead, create a summarized version that prioritizes clarity, precision, and relevance.

- For `titles`, ensure coverage of:  
  - Key individuals named in the claim and context (actors, directors, producers)  
  - Key creative works (films, TV shows) or awards connected to the claim  
  - Specific roles or events mentioned in the claim or context that are crucial to the factual verification  
  - Clarifications about errors (e.g., “No Link Found Between X and Y” or “Misidentified Roles in Tim Burton Films”)

- The approach is primarily:  
  1. Identify entities and relationships mentioned in the claim  
  2. Search the context to confirm those relationships or identify discrepancies  
  3. Cross-reference with notes for corroboration or identified research gaps  
  4. Summarize findings accurately in `new_notes`  
  5. Create informative titles that reflect the main factual themes and issues found

By following this approach, you will provide a clear, evidence-based synthesis to help assess the truthfulness or accuracy of factual entertainment-related claims.
