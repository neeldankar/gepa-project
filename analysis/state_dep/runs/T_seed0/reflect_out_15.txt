Task Description:  
You will be given a factual claim, which is a concise statement or set of assertions involving specific entities such as people (with roles or attributes), places (cities, counties, islands, parks), species (with taxonomy or native habitats), organizations, historical events, or dates/time periods. Alongside the claim, you receive a set of bullet-point notes containing verified, authoritative, or clarifying information relevant to the entities, their relationships, or the factual assertions in the claim.

Your goal is to generate one or more compact, precise, and well-formed natural language queries. These queries should be designed to efficiently retrieve authoritative and relevant evidence that can fully verify or refute every factual component of the claim, carefully incorporating any nuanced details, corrections, or clarifications from the notes.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A short sentence or paragraph asserting one or multiple factual points about entities, dates, events, relationships, or locations.  
- `notes`: A list of bullet points presenting verified facts, distinctions, corrections, or relevant background information on the entities or factual details present in the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query`: One or more natural language questions that explicitly and comprehensively target the critical factual elements of the claim, enriched with pertinent context or corrections from the notes. The queries should be concise yet sufficient to retrieve all minimal authoritative sources needed for full fact-checking.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Nuances:

1. Decompose the Claim Precisely:  
   - Identify every discrete factual component within the claim, including but not limited to:  
     - Specific people (including specifying their roles, birthdates, nationalities, or credited works where applicable),  
     - Groups, bands, or species (including precise taxonomy, subspecies, or native habitats),  
     - Geographical entities (detailing exact city, county, island, park, or administrative divisions as relevant),  
     - Dates or timelines (such as formation dates, lifespan periods, event dates),  
     - Relationships between entities (e.g., band membership, director-film association, who scored in a sports match).  
   - Handle multiple facts or subclaims independently but ensure logical coherence and connection where they relate.

2. Leverage Notes for Clarification and Corrections:  
   - Use the notes to identify taxonomy details (e.g., distinguishing species from subspecies with native ranges), administrative geography (e.g., Norwegian county-city relationships), film industry specifics (correct film titles, release years, director/composer identity, actor biographies, character roles), and sports contexts (distinguishing competitions, player nationalities, scoring events).  
   - Detect and encode any clarifications or corrections in the notes that modify or refine the claim, including avoiding conflation of distinct entities or events.  
   - Incorporate temporal overlaps or historical timelines explicitly as needed.

3. Formulating the Queries:  
   - Explicitly state every critical named entity and their defining attributes needed to verify each claim segment (for example, full names, band affiliations, film roles, exact dates).  
   - Break compound claims into multiple logically ordered questions when necessary but combine questions to avoid redundancy when verifying related facts.  
   - Use question formulations that target the minimum sufficient authoritative evidence, such as "Did X write Y?", "When was Z established?", "Was A alive at the same time as B?", or "Is species S native to location L?"  
   - Reflect any necessary domain-specific precision from the notes in queries to prevent ambiguity (e.g., distinguishing between indoor American football and soccer, or between a species and its subspecies native to different islands).  

4. Avoidance Criteria:  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries that could retrieve irrelevant information.  
   - Avoid mixing unrelated or conflated entities where notes specify distinctions.  
   - Avoid adding reasoning, explanations, commentary, or extraneous details in the output: only output the final natural language query or queries.

5. Generalizable Strategy:  
   - Perform thorough claim decomposition to elemental factual assertions.  
   - Cross-check each assertion with notes for confirmations, contradictions, or clarifications.  
   - Translate each assertion (with necessary context) into direct questions aimed at retrieving authoritative data.  
   - Combine or order questions logically to cover all verification needs efficiently without redundancy.  
   - Produce clear, minimal, and domain-informed natural language queries suitable for downstream evidence retrieval or fact-checking systems.

Examples of Domain-Specific Knowledge and Nuances to Embed:  
- Sports: Differentiate between types of football (e.g., indoor American football vs. international soccer), specific players' participation and scoring in historic matches (with exact teams and dates).  
- Film and Entertainment: Accurate film names and release years, director/composer credits, differentiation between similarly named films, sequel relationships, actor birthdates and nationalities, credited roles, and commercials linked to characters or series.  
- Military/History: Correct regiment names and timelines, formation vs combat dates, and accurate film representation details.  
- Geography: Norwegian county-city relationships, island-specific native species, and associated national parks.  
- Botany/Taxonomy: Distinctions between species and subspecies, precisely defined native habitats and geographic distributions.

By following this detailed framework and applying domain knowledge embedded in the notes, your queries will accurately and efficiently target the exact authoritative evidence needed to verify or refute each factual claim.
