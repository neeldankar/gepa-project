Given the input fields `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a precise and focused `query` that targets the key pieces of information needed to verify or investigate the `claim`. The goal of the `query` is to retrieve the most relevant documents or data sources that will confirm, refute, or clarify the components of the claim.

To do this, follow these detailed steps:

1. **Understand the claim's components:**  
   Break down the claim into discrete facts, individuals, entities, or relationships that need verification. Claims often involve multiple parts, such as identifying persons, events, dates, titles, roles, or attributes (e.g., flavor, participation, identity, titles).

2. **Use the notes as evidence and context:**  
   The `notes` field contains various relevant and sometimes ambiguous pieces of information extracted from prior data retrieval that partially relate to the claim. Identify which facts in the claim are supported, contradicted, or remain unverified based on these notes.

3. **Spot knowledge gaps or ambiguities:**  
   Recognize which elements in the claim lack sufficient evidence or where multiple interpretations or ambiguities exist (e.g., unclear identity of a person, unknown relationship, unverified attribute).

4. **Formulate a focused, clear, and answerable natural language query:**  
   - The query should specifically ask for the missing or unclear parts that need to be resolved to fully verify the claim.  
   - Include the entities or concepts from the claim that are ambiguous or need cross-referencing.  
   - If the claim references multiple entities (e.g., two people, or two drinks), the query may ask about their relationship or identification together.  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague queries; instead, tailor the query narrowly to address the key verification gap.

5. **Incorporate domain-specific terminology when relevant:**  
   For example, in sports claims, clarify team names, positions, seasons, or roles precisely; in literature or cultural claims, specify exact titles, awards, nationality, or professions with precision.

6. **Confirm consistency and cross-link entities:**  
   When the claim connects two entities (e.g., that two named individuals are the same person or share a role), the query should explicitly ask about that connection or identity to resolve confusion.  

7. **Prioritize queries that can retrieve missing gold-standard documents** relevant to claims (e.g., a specific song article, team season record, or writer’s biography).

By following these principles, you produce contextual, informative queries that help retrieve documents essential for thorough claim verification in specialized domains such as biographical data, sports records, cultural figures, and product details.

**Summary of the task:**  
- Input: `claim` (a factual or composite statement), and `notes` (related evidentiary excerpts).  
- Output: `query` (a focused question designed to retrieve the most relevant documents to verify the ambiguous or unsupported parts of the claim).  

The query should effectively bridge the gap between existing extracted information (`notes`) and the unverified or ambiguous portions of the `claim`, maximizing retrieval of pertinent documents and reducing ambiguity.
