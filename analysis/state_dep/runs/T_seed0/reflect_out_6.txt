You are given three input fields: `claim` (a statement to be verified or analyzed), `notes` (a list of sentences extracted as preliminary relevant factual information), and `context` (a list of documents or excerpts related to the entities or events mentioned in the claim). Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Detailed task description:

1. **Purpose**: The primary goal is to assess and clarify the factual accuracy and completeness of the given `claim` by analyzing the information in `notes` and `context`. This involves verifying key factual elements, addressing ambiguities or inaccuracies, and synthesizing corroborative or corrective information.

2. **Inputs**:
   - `claim`: A declarative sentence possibly containing assertions about people, events, dates, roles, or relationships.
   - `notes`: A set of extracted sentences, often repetitive or partially overlapping, containing factual data relevant to the claim, drawn from various sources.
   - `context`: A set of detailed document excerpts or knowledge units about entities, events, or related background information referred to in the claim and notes.

3. **Outputs**:
   - `new_notes`: A carefully curated and concise list of distilled factual statements that accurately reflect the verified or corrected information from `notes` and `context`. Redundancies should be removed, contradictions clarified, and misinformation corrected where possible. If the claim contains unverifiable elements or missing data, explicitly note that the information is unavailable or unknown.
   - `titles`: A list of document or entity titles corresponding to the key `context` entries that provide the most relevant information supporting, explaining, or clarifying the claim. These titles effectively represent the primary sources or articles utilized for factual verification.

4. **Approach and reasoning**:
   - Identify the main factual assertions within the `claim` to focus verification on.
   - Cross-check facts in the `claim` against both `notes` and `context` to confirm accuracy or identify discrepancies.
   - Use `context` to resolve ambiguities or missing data when `notes` are incomplete or inconsistent.
   - Update and refine `new_notes` to create a coherent, accurate summary of facts relevant to the claim, including corrections or highlighting unverifiable parts.
   - Select all relevant source titles from `context` that underpin these verified facts, ensuring comprehensive coverage of critical points in the claim.
   - Avoid including unrelated or irrelevant documents in `titles` (e.g., context entries not germane to the claim).
   - If certain entities are central to the claim (like named persons, events, organizations), their related documents should generally be included in `titles`.
   - When comparing or relating entities (e.g., age, roles), explicitly mention limitations of available data if any key data (e.g., birthdate) is missing.

5. **Domain specificity and niche details to consider**:
   - Historical and biographical assertions often involve familial relationships, titles, roles, and actions (e.g., commandants of camps, negotiation roles in WWII history).
   - Sports-based claims involve tournament outcomes, player relationships, and career milestones, requiring temporal and factual precision (e.g., match results, ages at career start).
   - Music and entertainment domain facts include artist roles, formation of bands, and birthdates of key individuals.
   - Precision in identifying who performed which actions (e.g., negotiations) and correcting misattributed actions is critical.
   - Clearly distinguish between who did what, e.g., whether a person held a specific role or performed a specific act, vs. related persons with potentially confusingly similar names.
   - Note when repeated facts in `notes` are reconfirmed in `context` or contradicted.
   - Maintain neutrality and avoid speculative conclusions where data is insufficient.

6. **Example strategies**:
   - Summarize and distill repetitive notes into a concise set of true, verified statements.
   - Explicitly mention when claim components cannot be verified due to missing data.
   - Use contextual knowledge to correct factual inaccuracies in claims (e.g., who performed negotiations).
   - Always include the key entity titles relevant for understanding and verifying the claim in `titles`.

In summary, your output should reliably reconstruct a factual baseline from available data, accurately clarifying or contesting claim elements, and pointing to the key relevant knowledge sources by title.
