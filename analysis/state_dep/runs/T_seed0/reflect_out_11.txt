```
Given two inputs:
- `claim`: a factual assertion or statement that may combine multiple pieces of information or entities.
- `notes`: a list of relevant background facts, clarifications, or evidence that support or refute aspects of the claim.

Your task is to produce a `query`—a clear, precise, and targeted information retrieval question or set of questions—that, when answered by retrieving or verifying authoritative sources, will effectively confirm or refute the claim.

Key guidelines to generate the `query`:

1. **Analyze the claim carefully**:
   - Identify key entities (people, places, events, objects), relationships, temporal or spatial contexts, and factual assertions embedded in the claim.
   - Detect any mixes or confusions (e.g., nationality, gender, timeframes, event types) mentioned.

2. **Use the notes as supporting factual evidence to refine your understanding of the claim**:
   - Extract domain-specific or niche knowledge (e.g., the nationality and gender of a person, the geographic scope of a park or cemetery, technical origins of a platform or device).
   - Recognize when the claim combines facts incorrectly or conflates unrelated information.
   - Identify what facts have insufficient evidence or are missing from the notes.

3. **Formulating the `query`**:
   - The query should directly focus on the critical elements necessary to verify the claim, especially those brought up by the notes.
   - The query should seek clarification on ambiguous or unsupported claim components.
   - Where multiple aspects are relevant, the query can be a compound or multipart question.
   - Include relevant entities or concepts that are essential to retrieving gold documents or authoritative sources critical to validating the claim.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; be specific enough to retrieve precise evidence related to the claim.

4. **Domain-specific factual knowledge exemplified**:
   - Sports domain: player nationality, gender category, event types (e.g., women's singles vs men's doubles), and historical champions.
   - Geography and landmarks: understanding distinctions between similar locations (e.g., Golden Gate National Cemetery vs Golden Gate National Recreation Area), park boundaries, and cemetery locations.
   - Technology history: origin countries of hardware platforms, manufacturing companies, and software development targets.

5. **Generalizable strategy for verification**:
   - Break down the claim’s assertions into verifiable facts.
   - Cross-reference these with the notes to identify contradictions, gaps, or needed clarifications.
   - Generate a targeted query that is likely to retrieve documents containing key verifiable facts (e.g., specific people’s nationality, burial locations, production origins).

By following this approach, your `query` output will facilitate effective and focused document retrieval that can confirm or dispute the claim with high precision.

Format your output as:
```
### query
<your formulated query here>
