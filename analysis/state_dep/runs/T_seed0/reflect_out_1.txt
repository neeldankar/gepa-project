Task Description:

You are given two input fields: `claim` (a factual claim that may contain multiple sub-claims or components) and `notes` (a list of factual statements relevant to the claim or its verification). Your goal is to produce a `query` field containing one or more precise, fact-checking questions that can be used to retrieve or verify necessary evidence to confirm or refute the claim.

Key Details and Requirements:

1. **Input Format:**

   - `claim`: A natural language sentence or sentences asserting facts that need verification. Claims sometimes combine multiple atomic factual assertions, which should be identified.
   - `notes`: A list of contextual, factual observations or facts drawn from knowledge or documents potentially relevant to parts of the claim.

2. **Task Objective:**

   - Read the claim carefully and break it down into its constitutive factual components if needed.
   - Use the notes to identify which pieces of information are relevant and what evidence would be needed to verify each component of the claim.
   - Generate one or more targeted, explicit questions (queries) that seek to verify the truthfulness of each factual component in the claim.

3. **Query Construction Guidelines:**

   - Queries should be relevant, succinct, and fact-focused.
   - If the claim has multiple factual parts, create a separate query or a sequence of queries covering all these parts.
   - Queries should aim to retrieve documents or evidence to validate or falsify the statements in the claim. For example, asking about relationships, identities, dates, counts, or named entities mentioned in the claim.
   - Use precise entity names and terms from the claim and notes to ensure queries can target the correct documents (e.g., full names of people, exact film titles, scientific names).
   - Clarify ambiguous references in the claim through explicit questions (e.g., if "the writer" is mentioned but unknown, formulate a query that attempts to identify "the writer," or ask about that writer's relevant achievements).
   - Use natural language questions that could be answered by a knowledge base or document retrieval system.

4. **Reasoning Approach:**

   - Decompose complex claims into atomic fact-checking targets.
   - Cross-reference the notes to find missing information or gaps in knowledge.
   - Formulate queries aimed at resolving those gaps.
   - Queries should be designed such that the retrieved documents or data directly support or contradict the claim’s components.

5. **Domain-Specific Nuances Seen in Examples:**

   - Claims often relate to cultural or historical facts, such as film credits, literary prizes, biological taxonomy, or translations.
   - The terminology of the domain (e.g., "Nobel Prize in Literature," film names with years, genus/species counts, familial relationships in film casts) should be preserved and emphasized in queries.
   - Where multiple people or entities are involved, queries should seek to clarify their interrelations (e.g., who is the father of an actor? which films did a person star in?).
   - When exact identity of an entity is unknown from the claim or context, queries should reflect that uncertainty by explicitly asking for identification.

6. **Goal of Queries:**

   - To retrieve documents that cover:
     - Identities of people or entities mentioned,
     - Confirming factual details such as filmography, dates, award winners,
     - Verifying relationships and counts (number of species, family relations),
     - Confirming translations or alternative names of entities.

By following these instructions, the assistant can generate queries that are actionable, clear, and targeted toward retrieving gold-standard documents needed to verify the claims.

Summary:

Given a multi-part factual claim and supporting notes, produce clear, fact-oriented verification questions that if answered, will confirm or refute each part of the claim. Use fine-grained decomposition, clarify ambiguous references, and incorporate domain-specific details like entity names, dates, counts, and relationships in queries.
