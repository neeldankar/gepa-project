Task Overview:  
You will be given a factual claim containing one or more specific assertions about entities such as people, groups, bands, locations, species, organizations, events, or dates, and a corresponding set of carefully curated notes with verified facts, clarifications, corrections, or domain-specific background information relevant to those entities or assertions. Your goal is to produce a precise, well-structured natural language query or sets of queries designed to retrieve authoritative evidence that can confirm or refute the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A concise statement or paragraph that asserts one or multiple factual points involving named entities, dates, places, roles, and relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of bullet points furnishing validated facts, corrections, taxonomy distinctions, or clarifying context that relate to the claim's entities and assertions.

Output:  
- `query`: One or more natural language questions that explicitly address each critical factual component from the claim and incorporate all pertinent nuances from the notes. The queries must be clear, compact yet comprehensive, and targeted for efficient retrieval of minimal but sufficient authoritative evidence to verify or disprove the claim.

Detailed Instructions for Query Construction:

1. Decompose the Claim:  
   - Identify and separately consider all elemental factual assertions including:  
     - Names of individuals, groups, bands, artists, or species.  
     - Geographic locations including cities, counties, islands, and parks.  
     - Dates or date ranges.  
     - Relationships such as membership, roles, directorships, coaching, and scoring in sports events.  
     - Specific claims like formation or establishment dates, voice acting roles, medal achievements, and species habitat.  
   - For multi-part claims, isolate each fact or subclaim before integration.

2. Leverage Notes for Domain-Specific Insights and Corrections:  
   - Use detailed notes to resolve ambiguities and correct misinformation in the claim.  
   - Examples of domain-specific technicalities include:  
     * Taxonomy: Distinguish species vs. subspecies native to particular regions or protected parks (e.g., Echium wildpretii subspecies endemic to different Canary Islands and linked to their unique national parks).  
     * Geography: Norwegian administrative divisions (e.g., Bergen city within Hordaland county).  
     * Filmography and Acting: Correct attribution of voice roles across different actors/actresses by nationality and role type (speaking or singing), identification of directors and screenwriters, and distinctions between unrelated yet similarly named films or series.  
     * Sports History: Differentiate between indoor American football and international soccer, verify which players scored in specific historical matches.  
     * Military History: Clarify regiment names, formation versus combat dates, and film representations of military units.  
     * Athletic Achievements: Confirm chronological medal wins and ethnic or national identifiers.  

3. Constructing Effective Queries:  
   - Include all relevant entities, attributes, dates, and relationships explicitly in each question, avoiding vagueness or conflation.  
   - Address potential contradictions or boundary conditions revealed by the notes (e.g., distinguishing a trainer from the person who called another player the greatest).  
   - Where possible, combine related queries into a minimal set arranged logically (e.g., confirming identity and role, then verifying related associations or events).  
   - Use question forms designed for fact checking or retrieval systems, such as:  
     - "Who named [Person] ‘the greatest football player of all time’?"  
     - "Did [Person] coach the [Team] during [Time period]?"  
     - "Who provided the voice for [Character] in the [Year] animated film [Film title]?"  
     - "What is the nationality of [Actor] known for [Role]?"  
     - "Was [Species/Subspecies] native to [Island], and does it grow in [Named National Park]?"  
     - "Did [Military Unit] form in [Year], and did [Film] depict it accurately?"  

4. Avoid Pitfalls:  
   - Do not mix unrelated domains or conflate entities explicitly separated in the notes.  
   - Refrain from vague or overly broad queries that invite irrelevant or excessive retrieval.  
   - Exclude commentary or reasoning; output only natural language question(s) suitable for evidence retrieval.

5. Generalizable Strategy Recap:  
   - Break claims into elemental facts.  
   - Cross-reference each with notes to capture correct names, dates, relationships, status, and corrections.  
   - Explicitly encode these in precise, logically connected questions.  
   - Combine queries efficiently without redundancy, maintaining specificity and clarity.  
   - Focus queries on minimal authoritative evidence needed for rigorous fact verification.

6. Examples of Applied Domain-Specific Nuances:  
   - Film/TV: Correct attribution of directorial or writing credits, distinguishing unrelated productions with similar names, confirming actors’ nationalities and character roles, including voice roles differentiated by speaking vs singing voice and adult vs young characters.  
   - Sports: Differentiating player identities, teams coached, scoring events, and ethnic/national heritage linked to achievements.  
   - Geography: Mapping cities to counties, linking species or subspecies to specific islands and national parks.  
   - Military History: Regiment naming and formation timelines vs combat periods, as well as cinematic portrayals.

By adhering to these instructions, you will produce focused, comprehensive queries that facilitate pinpoint retrieval of the authoritative facts necessary to assess claim accuracy accurately and efficiently.
