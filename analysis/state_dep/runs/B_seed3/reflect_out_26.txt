Given two fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a concise, precise, and focused `query` designed to verify or investigate the truthfulness of the `claim` based on information (or lack thereof) in the provided `notes`.

---

### Input Fields

- **claim**: A factual assertion (or set of assertions) about named entities such as people, works (films, books, bands), places, events, dates, or relationships between these entities. Claims often imply multiple connected facts that may need separate confirmation or disambiguation.

- **notes**: A list of factual statements or contextual snippets extracted from various sources (biographies, discographies, geographic data, etc.). These notes may confirm, contradict, or leave uncertain specific details of the claim; some entities or facts may be absent or only partially addressed.

---

### Output Field

- **query**: A single, clear, and entity-rich question explicitly constructed to:

  - Identify and address all ambiguous, missing, contradictory, or unsupported elements within the claim as found with reference to the notes.
  
  - Explicitly incorporate all key entities (persons, works, organizations, locations) mentioned or implied by the claim, particularly those absent or unclear in the notes.
  
  - Seek targeted, discrete factual information critical for verifying or refuting the claim in its entirety.
  
  - Guide a fact-checking or retrieval system toward comprehensive evidence gathering for the claim.

---

### Detailed Task Description and Domain-Specific Considerations

1. **Entity Extraction and Inclusion**

   - Identify all relevant named entities appearing in the claim and notes. These typically include:
     - **Persons:** full names, aliases, roles (e.g., musicians, actors, teachers).
     - **Works:** films, albums, books, bands, art pieces.
     - **Locations:** towns, counties, natural landmarks, administrative jurisdictions.
     - **Organizations:** councils, record labels, publications, halls of fame.

   - Ensure that the query explicitly references each relevant entity to maximize the retrieval of all pertinent documents.

2. **Fact Types to Verify**

   Your query should target core fact categories essential for verification:
   
   - **Biographical Data:** birth/death dates, nationality, education, known mentors or teachers.
   
   - **Professional/Artistic Data:** roles or functions (director, actor, band member), affiliations with organizations and works, timeline and membership in bands or projects.
   
   - **Geographic Relationships:** locations, administrative jurisdictions, proximity to landmarks, base of operations for bands or works.
   
   - **Existence and Linkage:** confirming whether entities, relationships, or connections claimed are documented or valid.
   
3. **Addressing Missing, Ambiguous, or Contradictory Information**

   - If the claim references entities or facts not found or contradicted in the notes, the query must target verification or clarification of these omissions or contradictions.
   
   - If the claim references connections or facts about entities not mentioned at all in the notes (e.g., a teacher, a publication name), the query must explicitly seek their identity, existence, and relationship to other entities in the claim.
   
   - Avoid broad, vague queries; focus tightly on discrete, pivotal verification points needed for confirming the whole claim.
   
4. **Formulating Effective Queries**

   - Queries must be direct, specific questions formulated clearly to request factual information.
   
   - When multiple related entities or works are involved, list them explicitly in the query to guide retrieval of all relevant evidence.
   
   - The query should cover all components of the claim that are not already supported by the notes, enabling comprehensive validation.
   
   - Avoid generic or thematically open-ended questions.

5. **Generalizable Strategy**

   1. **Parse the claim carefully:**
      - Break down complex claims into discrete factual assertions.
      - Identify all entities and relationships referenced or implied.
      
   2. **Cross-reference with notes:**
      - Highlight which assertions are confirmed, contradicted, unclear, or absent.
      - Note missing entities or date details relevant to the claim.
      
   3. **Query construction:**
      - Formulate a precise, entity-rich factual question addressing only the unsupported or ambiguous points.
      - Include explicit references to all key people, works, organizations, and places.
      - Ensure the question aims to retrieve exact data necessary to confirm or refute the entire claim.

---

### Examples of Effective Queries

- Given a claim involving mentorship and education:
  > "Who was Ariel Ramírez's science teacher, and is there evidence that this teacher also taught Marta Lambertini?"

- For verifying geographic and membership claims involving multiple bands:
  > "Are the bands God Seed, Sahg, Ov Hell, and Gorgoroth all based in Bergen or the Norwegian county of Hordaland, and what is King ov Hell’s membership and association with each?"

- For clarifying administrative jurisdictions and geographic proximity:
  > "Which town is administered by the Eurobodalla Shire council and is located near Higgin's Creek and Deua National Park?"

### Summary

Your final output `query` should be:

- A clear and concise question.

- Rich in explicit references to all key entities involved.

- Targeted at obtaining specific factual information missing or ambiguous in the notes.

- Constructed to enable retrieval systems or fact-checkers to conclusively verify or refute the entire claim.

---

By following these comprehensive instructions, the assistant will maximize the completeness, precision, and effectiveness of queries generated to verify complex factual claims using provided contextual notes.
