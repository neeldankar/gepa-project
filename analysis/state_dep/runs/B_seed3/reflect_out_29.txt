Task Description:

You are given two inputs:  
- `claim`: A statement containing one or more factual assertions related to people (e.g., actors, directors, producers, singers), works (films, operas, albums, songs, TV shows), dates, events, or relationships between these entities.  
- `notes`: A collection of factual snippets extracted from documents that provide partial, supporting, or contradictory information about the entities or assertions in the claim. These notes contain biographical details, professional roles, filmography/bookography details, relationships between works, dates, and contextual clarifications.

Your task is to generate a precise, focused `query`—a clear, information-seeking question—that will help verify or refute the claim by identifying and targeting specific uncertain, ambiguous, or unsupported factual elements from the claim based on the information (or lack thereof) in the notes.

---

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Entity Identification:**  
   - Identify all key named entities mentioned in the claim and the notes. These can include: people (actors, producers, judges, authors), artistic works (movies, operas, albums, songs, TV shows), and related organizations or events.  
   - Pay special attention if entities appear contradictory or are not confirmed in the notes (e.g., a judge who may or may not be on a panel, a producer credited on one work but not on another).

2. **Factual Components to Verify:**  
   The core factual components frequently involve:  
   - **Biographical information:** dates of birth, nationality, professions.  
   - **Professional involvement or roles:** Whether a person acted in, directed, wrote, produced, judged, or was otherwise involved in a named work/event.  
   - **Existence and official details of works:** Film release years, countries involved, number of acts or structure (in operas vs. films).  
   - **Relationships between works and people:** e.g., whether a film was inspired by a particular opera, or whether a producer worked on two films.  
   - **Correctness of claimed associations:** For example, if a show’s judges include a certain person or if a singer joined others on a panel.

3. **Cross-Referencing Claim and Notes:**  
   - Use the notes to confirm if any parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or missing information.  
   - Identify ambiguities or contradictions in the claim relative to the notes (e.g., a date mismatch, nationality mistake, unconfirmed professional role).  
   - When notes provide partial or conflicting information, your query should seek the missing or clarifying facts.

4. **Formulating the Query:**  
   Your output must be a **concise, targeted question or set of questions** that:  
   - Explicitly references the relevant entities (persons, works, organizations) central to verifying the claim.  
   - Directly addresses the ambiguous, disputed, or missing factual points necessary for verification.  
   - Requests specific domain facts such as roles, involvement, birthdates, inspirations, or existence details.  
   - Avoids broad, vague, or overly general questions—focus on exactly what facts are crucial for establishing the claim’s truthfulness.  
   - Is often a compound query targeting multiple relevant points if required (e.g., "Who stars in X? Did producer Y also produce Z?").

5. **Domain-Specific Knowledge Nuances:**  
   - Understand that certain facts about works (such as the structure of an opera vs. a film) are domain-specific: e.g., operas have acts, films generally do not. Queries about counts of acts should reflect this knowledge.  
   - Judge panels, TV shows, and album or film credits can be complex and may require verifying precise membership or roles.  
   - Recognize discrepancies in nationality, production countries, or inspiration sources as important to verify.  
   - People may hold multiple roles (producer and singer) which should be explicitly asked about if unclear.  
   - Verify correct title names (including disambiguation between similar or related works).

6. **Generalizable Strategy to Apply:**  
   - Thoroughly parse the claim to extract all factual assertions and entities.  
   - Cross-check these elements against the notes to find supporting, contradicted, or missing facts.  
   - Focus on identified knowledge gaps or conflicting information as the core of your query.  
   - Craft questions that explicitly and clearly address these gaps, linking entities and roles as needed for precise retrieval.  
   - For claims involving multiple entities or complex relations, formulate compound queries that will retrieve all relevant gold documents.  
   - Ensure that the query can enable accurate retrieval of documents or knowledge sources that will help conclusively verify or refute the claim.
