Given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a concise, precise, and focused `query` that can be used to verify, clarify, or investigate the truthfulness of the `claim` based on the information present (or absent) in the `notes`.

### Input Fields
- `claim`: A statement asserting one or more concrete facts involving entities such as people, works (e.g., films, albums, books), events, dates, places, organizations, or relationships among them.
- `notes`: A list of factual snippets extracted from various documents providing potentially relevant biographical data, filmographies, discographies, release dates, venue information, professional roles (director, actor, member of a group), ownership details, and contextual clarifications related to the entities or events mentioned in the claim.

### Output Field
- `query`: A sharply focused, information-seeking question or query statement that:
  1. Directly targets ambiguous, missing, or contradictory information critical to confirming or refuting the claim.
  2. Explicitly references all relevant entities mentioned in the claim and notes (people, works, organizations, venues, events).
  3. Emphasizes domain-specific factual verifications, such as:
     - Biographical data (birth dates, nationalities, membership status).
     - Professional roles or involvement (acting, directing, leadership, residency).
     - Existence, titles, and associations of creative works or events.
     - Ownership and location specifics of venues or organizations.
     - Temporal relationships among events, releases, or memberships.
  4. Seeks clarity on any disputed or unsupported factual details presented in the claim or lacking in the notes.
  5. Enables retrieval of all relevant authoritative documents required for verification by focusing on unique identifiers (e.g., proper names, titles, dates).
  6. Avoids overly broad or vague questions — be as specific and narrowly focused as possible to distinguish relevant facts critical for claim evaluation.

### Key Domain-Specific Considerations and Strategies
- **Entity Identification:** Extract all key entities from the claim (e.g., people such as Jay Park, Harley Knoles; works such as "Winning America," "Take Off" DVD; organizations like Caesars Entertainment Corporation; venues like The AXIS auditorium).
- **Cross-Referencing Claims and Notes:** Compare the claim’s asserted facts with the notes’ contained information. Identify what is confirmed, contradicted, or missing.
- **Event and Temporal Clarification:** When claims reference temporal sequences (e.g., whether Jay Park left a group before a DVD release), formulate queries that explicitly ask about the timeline or membership status at relevant times.
- **Role Verification:** If the claim involves professional involvement (director, co-director, performer), the query should seek explicit confirmation of these roles tied to specific people and works.
- **Work and Venue Validation:** For claims involving a work (movie, album, residency) or venue, ask about their existence, official titles, and affiliations with named persons or organizations.
- **Ownership and Location Details:** When venues or properties are mentioned, queries must verify ownership and geographical location to resolve ambiguity.
- **Co-reference Handling:** When multiple persons or works are referenced together, include all of them explicitly in the query to ensure comprehensive retrieval.
- **Missing Information Focus:** If information is missing or uncertain in notes (such as unknown co-director names), direct the query toward filling that gap.

### Generalizable Approach to Formulating the Query
1. Parse the claim to identify all core factual assertions and relevant entities.
2. Examine the notes to determine supporting facts, contradictions, and gaps.
3. Distill the claim’s verification needs — what facts must be confirmed or disproven?
4. Formulate a clear, concise question that targets those verification points, specifying the key entities and relationships.
5. Ensure the query is structured to retrieve documents pertaining to the entities and facts central to the claim’s truthfulness.
6. Keep the query focused — avoid broad or multi-part questions that dilute the precision needed for fact-checking.

### Examples of Query Focus
- For membership timeline claims:  
  *"Did [Person A] leave [Group/Organization] before the release of [Work/Event title] in [Year]? What was [Person A]'s status in [Group] at the time of [Work/Event] release?"*

- For venue ownership and location claims:  
  *"Is [Venue name] located within [Resort/Casino name] in [City, State]? Who owns [Resort/Casino name]?"*

- For directors and creative roles:  
  *"Who directed [Film title]? Is [Person B] credited as a director or co-director of [Film title]? Is [Person C] known to be a film director?"*

By adhering to these instructions, you will produce queries that enable precise verification or refutation of claims through retrieval of the relevant factual documents, and improve overall fact-checking reliability and completeness in complex, multi-entity scenarios.
