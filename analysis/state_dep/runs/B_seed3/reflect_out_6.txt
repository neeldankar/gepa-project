Task: Given a factual claim and related factual notes, generate a concise, precise, and focused information-seeking query that can directly verify or refute the claim by retrieving relevant domain-specific documents or knowledge.

Input:
- claim: A statement containing one or more factual assertions about people, works (e.g., movies, books, operas, songs), dates, events, or relationships between these entities (e.g., authorship, involvement).
- notes: A list of factual information derived from multiple sources, providing biographical details, professional roles, authorship/credit information, dates, work titles, and other contextual clarifications related to the entities in the claim.

Output:
- query: A well-formed, targeted question or request for information that explicitly requests verification or details of the ambiguous, missing, or critical facts needed to confirm or deny the claim. This query should be precise enough to retrieve all relevant gold-standard documents or knowledge for evidence.

Detailed task requirements and domain-specific considerations:

1. Entity and Fact Identification:
   - Identify and extract all key entities from the claim and notes:
     - People (e.g., authors, actors, composers, athletes)
     - Works (e.g., operas, songs, movies, books)
     - Locations, institutions (e.g., colleges, counties)
     - Dates or temporal references (e.g., year of composition, revival, birth)
   - Understand the factual assertions made about these entities, such as:
     - Biographical data: birthdates, nationality, roles (e.g., lyricist, vocalist)
     - Authorship and creative contributions (who wrote or composed what)
     - Participation or involvement in specific works or events (e.g., an actor starring in a film, a player attending a college)
     - Existence or non-existence of certain entities or works

2. Handling Ambiguities and Contradictions:
   - Identify missing information or contradictions between the claim and notes.
   - When entities are referred to ambiguously or multiple works are mentioned (e.g., different versions of an opera), explicitly include them all in the query.
   - When the claim implies misattribution or absence of credit (e.g., denying Otep Shamaya wrote a song), direct the query to confirm or deny specific attributions clearly.
   - When a claim mentions relationships between entities (e.g., two operas composed by different people), form queries that clarify the composers/authors of each work distinctly.
   - If the claim’s validity hinges on location or institutional affiliation (e.g., whether a college is in a particular county), verify the presence and association precisely.

3. Query Formulation:
   - Queries must be:
     - Concise and focused only on unverified or pivotal pieces of information.
     - Explicitly mention the relevant entities, works, and attributes to guide retrieval.
     - Structured as direct questions suitable for knowledge bases or document queries, e.g., 
       “Who composed [work]?”, “Did [person] play baseball at [college]?”, “Are [work 1] and [work 2] composed by the same person?” 
   - Avoid vague, overly broad, or off-topic queries.
   - Include all relevant persons/works/entities if multiple are impacted by the claim or relevant for disambiguation.
   - Incorporate temporal and contextual clues (e.g., year, revival date, known aliases) when helpful to restrict the query scope.

4. Generalizable Strategy:
   - Parse the claim into factual components involving named entities and asserted relations.
   - Cross-reference these with the notes to isolate what is confirmed, what is contradicted, or what information is missing.
   - Formulate the query to directly seek verification on:
     - The existence or attributes of key entities (persons, works, locations).
     - Attribution and roles of individuals related to works or events.
     - Location or affiliation of institutions or entities mentioned.
     - Temporal or sequential relationships relevant to the claim.
   - Ensure the query can surface all relevant documents tied to each key entity or fact needed for reliable fact-checking.

By strictly following these instructions, the assistant will generate highly effective, targeted queries that maximize retrieval of relevant evidence to verify or refute claims accurately, addressing the various complex domains (e.g., music, literature, sports, geography) involved.
