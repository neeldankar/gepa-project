Task Description:
You are given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving one or more entities (e.g., people, places, organizations, bands, movies, buildings).
- `notes`: A list of extracted fact-like sentences relevant to the claim, often containing details or evidences extracted from knowledge sources.
- `context`: A list of document titles with brief descriptions or snippets, providing background knowledge and contextual information about entities mentioned in the claim or notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. `new_notes`:
   - Generate a concise, relevant subset of the original `notes`.
   - These should be factually accurate, evidence-based sentences that support, clarify, or invalidate parts of the `claim`.
   - Avoid redundancy and remove irrelevant or unsupported information.
   - The notes should collectively provide a clear, focused base of evidence related to the claim.
   - If there is contradictory evidence or lack of support, include notes that explicitly state these facts.

2. `titles`:
   - Generate a list of key document or entity titles relevant to the claim and the selected new_notes.
   - Titles should be accurate and specific names of entities, works, or locations (e.g., person names, band names, film titles, cathedral names).
   - The titles should correspond to trustworthy sources or knowledge base entries that would validate or provide background for the claim.
   - Cover all main entities related to the claim; ensure no essential entity is omitted.

Reasoning and Strategy:
- Carefully analyze the claim’s components (entities, relationships, dates, locations).
- Cross-validate these components against the `notes` and `context`.
  - Check for exact matches or close correspondence between claims and facts available.
  - Identify if any fact is unsupported by either notes or context (include a note stating no evidence).
- Distinguish entities properly, especially when different entities share similar or ambiguous names.
- Focus on factual correctness and relevance rather than exhaustive listing.
- Prioritize notes and titles that clearly support, explain, or refute the claim.
- When multiple related notes are present, select the most representative and direct ones.
- When feedback highlights missing documents or entities in `titles`, ensure coverage of all critical entities in future outputs.

Domain-Specific Knowledge and Details:
- Entities typically include people, movies, bands, cathedrals, record labels, and other named entities.
- Pay attention to fine details such as:
  - Exact years or time frames (e.g., a movie from 2007, a 3-year collaboration).
  - Specific roles or relationships (e.g., actor starred in a movie, sculptor contributed to a cathedral).
  - Geographic or national distinctions (e.g., Scottish vs British, American vs British labels).
  - Correct formatting of known entity titles and disambiguation (e.g., "Disturbia (film)", "APB (band)").
- Use context snippets to confirm entities’ key facts (e.g., birthplace, national origin, tenure).
- If a claim references a collaborative relationship or event (duration, participation), verify explicitly whether such a period or collaboration is documented.

Output Format:
- Provide `new_notes` as a list of strings (concise, relevant facts).
- Provide `titles` as a list of strings (clear entity or document titles).
- Both outputs should facilitate the evaluation of the claim's truthfulness with verifiable evidence.

Summary:
Your ultimate goal is to synthesize a focused set of evidential notes and identify key supporting documents/entities to accurately support or refute the claim, using the provided notes and context. Your selection should be precise, complete (with respect to claim entities), and avoid irrelevant or redundant information.
