Your task is to generate a precise, concise, and focused factual query designed to verify or investigate the truthfulness of a given claim, based on relevant notes provided. Both the claim and notes contain factual assertions about entities such as people, works (films, books, bands, etc.), places, events, dates, and relationships between these entities.

---

### Input Format

- **claim**: One or more factual assertions involving entities, potentially with multiple linked factual components (e.g., who led a sports team in a season, awards won by individuals, participation timelines in films or events).
- **notes**: A list of factual snippets or data points extracted from various documents that relate to the claim’s entities or assertions. These notes often include detailed biographical data, professional roles, affiliations, geographic or organizational relationships, timelines, and clarifications.

---

### Objective

Produce a **single, well-formed query** that:

- Explicitly targets and addresses all ambiguous, missing, contradictory, or unsupported elements in the claim.
- References all key entities (persons, works, organizations, locations) mentioned or implied by the claim and supported or contradicted by the notes.
- Is specific enough to retrieve or prompt verification of discrete facts critical for confirming or refuting the claim.
- Enables guided fact-checking or document retrieval focused on resolving the truthfulness of the claim as a whole, not just parts of it.

---

### Detailed Domain-Specific Guidance

1. **Entity Recognition and Inclusion**  
   Identify and explicitly include all relevant entities from both claim and notes:
   - Persons (full names, aliases)
   - Works (films, books, albums, bands)
   - Locations (cities, counties, landmarks)
   - Organizations (sports teams, record labels, councils)
   
   This ensures that the query is entity-rich and will help target all relevant documents and facts.

2. **Core Fact Categories to Verify**  
   Structuring your query around key factual categories often needed for validation:
   - **Biographical details**: birth/death, nationalities, mentors, educational background.
   - **Professional or artistic involvement**: roles held, affiliations, membership in organizations or works, career timelines.
   - **Geographic associations**: administrative districts, physical locations relative to landmarks.
   - **Temporal sequences**: dates or order of participation or events (e.g., whether A occurred before or after B).
   - **Existence and identity of entities or relationships**: confirming if claimed connections or roles exist or are documented.

3. **Handling Ambiguity, Contradiction, and Missing Information**  
   - If the notes contradict or do not support parts of the claim, explicitly query those disputed or absent facts.
   - For entities referred to in the claim but absent or unclear in the notes, ask targeted questions about their identity or connection.
   - Focus your query narrowly on the minimal set of facts that determine the truth of the entire claim, avoiding broad or vague questions.

4. **Query Construction Principles**  
   - Phrase the query as one or more clear, direct, fact-seeking questions.
   - Reference all implicated entities in the query to guide retrieval comprehensively.
   - If the claim contains multiple related factual components, keep the query focused on resolving all the critical gaps needed for validation.
   - Avoid general thematic questions and instead target specific verifiable facts.

5. **Examples of Effective Queries**  
   Use entity lists and precise fact categories to enable accurate document retrieval, such as:
   - "Is Kevin Reece Wilson the same person as Kevin Wilson who is currently Ohio State’s offensive coordinator, and what roles did each hold during the 2013 Big Ten Conference season?"
   - "Did any actors who appeared in the 2017 film Wonder Woman perform in Anomalisa after the Fargo season 3 premiere, and specifically, did David Thewlis’s voice role in Anomalisa occur after this premiere?"
   - "Is there a film titled 'The Night Hunter' whose screenplay was based on a novel, or is it an alternate title for 'The Night of the Hunter'? Who wrote that screenplay and did this writer or the novel’s author win the Nobel Prize in Literature alongside Nelly Sachs?"

---

### Generalizable Strategy for Constructing the Query

1. **Parse the Claim Thoroughly**  
   Identify all factual assertions and involved entities; segment complex claims into subcomponents if needed.

2. **Cross-Reference Notes**  
   Determine which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or omitted by the notes. Identify which entities or facts require verification.

3. **Formulate a Targeted Query**  
   Address all missing or contradictory elements directly by asking clear, entity- and fact-focused questions that can retrieve or confirm critical data.

---

### Summary

Your output must be a single, focused, entity-rich question (or tightly connected questions) that clearly target the unresolved factual issues in the claim based on the notes. This will facilitate precise retrieval of missing or disputed information and support accurate fact-checking or validation of the claim as a whole.
