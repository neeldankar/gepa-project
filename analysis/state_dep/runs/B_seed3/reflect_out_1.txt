Task Description:
You are given two fields as input: `claim` (a factual or argumentative statement) and `notes` (a list of extracted factual statements, clarifications, or evidence related to the claim). Your task is to generate a field `query` — a natural language information retrieval query designed to retrieve documents or sources relevant to evaluating the truthfulness or accuracy of the claim.

Key Details and Constraints:
- The `claim` may include taxonomic or domain-specific terminology, multiple intertwined assertions, or common misconceptions.
- The `notes` contain factual evidence, corrections, or clarifications that address, confirm, or refute the claim, often including:
  - precise domain-specific facts (e.g., taxonomic ranks in biology, album titles and release years in music, sports history facts)
  - clarifications addressing conflated roles, entities, or timelines
  - comparative quantitative details (e.g., species counts, release dates, medal awards)
- The query must be:
  - Focused precisely on key factual components needed to check the claim.
  - Explicit in distinguishing entities or concepts that are often confused or conflated (e.g., a subfamily versus genus, an individual versus a band, coach versus athlete).
  - Comprehensive enough to retrieve all relevant documents that contain crucial information to verify parts of the claim.
- The purpose of the query is to enable document retrieval systems to find authoritative sources that either support or contradict the claim.
- It may be necessary to reformulate or re-interpret the claim to correct errors or imprecise terminology while retaining the core factual points under scrutiny.
- The query should combine, when appropriate, multiple key questions or components that reflect:
  - Identity or classification of entities mentioned.
  - Quantitative or comparative data points critical to the claim.
  - Timeline or historical context relevant to the claim.
  - Attribution of statements or titles (e.g., who named whom, who performed what).
  
Generalizable Strategy:
1. Analyze the claim to identify core factual assertions and potential ambiguities or inaccuracies.
2. Use the notes to extract evidence and domain-specific facts that clarify or contradict aspects of the claim.
3. Identify the essential entities, concepts, relationships, and attributes necessary for verification.
4. Compose a clear, explicit, and focused natural language query incorporating these elements.
5. Where appropriate, correct or clarify claims by explicitly noting corrected categories or relationships in the query (e.g., “genera within subfamily X” instead of “genus X”).
6. Combine related factual checks into a single query to enable retrieval of all supporting or refuting documents.

Example:
If the claim incorrectly refers to a subfamily as a genus, and notes clarify the taxonomic rank and species counts, the query should target the taxa at their correct rank and ask for comparative species counts to validate the claim.

Output:
A single clear natural language `query` string aimed at retrieving documents that verify or refute the claim based on the provided notes.
