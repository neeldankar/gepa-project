Task Description:

You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. Inputs:
   - `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that may or may not be true.
   - `notes`: A list of sentences or observations related to the claim. These often summarize or highlight relevant facts extracted from various documents. The notes can contain some repetitions or loosely related information.
   - `context`: A list of excerpts or descriptions from relevant documents or sources that provide detailed background information and evidence pertinent to the claim and notes.

2. Outputs:
   - `new_notes`: A concise, deduplicated, and relevant subset of notes or synthesized facts that directly support or refute the claim, based on your careful analysis of the given notes and context. These new notes should:
     - Summarize the key factual information that validates or contradicts the claim.
     - Remove redundancies and irrelevant or unsupported statements.
     - Include clarifying details needed to understand the claim’s veracity.
     - Be factually accurate according to the provided context.
   - `titles`: A list of document titles or topic identifiers corresponding to the most relevant authoritative sources from the context that underpin the reasoning about the claim. These should focus on primary entities, events, or concepts essential for understanding or verifying the claim.

3. Reasoning and Approach:
   - Carefully cross-check and verify each element of the claim against both the `notes` and the `context`.
   - Identify which notes are well-supported and which are contradicted or unsupported by the context.
   - Extract and emphasize domain- and niche-specific facts that establish a clear relationship between entities, dates, roles, or events as described in the claim.
   - Synthesize multiple relevant notes and context excerpts into coherent, factually precise new notes that confirm or deny each aspect of the claim.
   - In cases where evidence is insufficient to confirm or deny the claim, clearly note this uncertainty in the new notes.
   - Select titles of documents that: 
     - Contain key factual or contextual information about primary individuals, organizations, events, or works related to the claim.
     - Reflect entities that contribute directly to the factual accuracy judgment.
   - Avoid including unrelated, tangential titles even if they appear in context; focus on documents with direct relevance and authoritative content.

4. Domain and Niche-Specific Details to Consider:
   - For claims involving individuals and their roles (e.g., film directors, actors, musicians, athletes), verify their actual contributions and linked works with supporting evidence.
   - When parsing sports-related claims, distinguish player positions, team membership, and historical season details accurately.
   - For music or band-related claims, pay attention to names, discographies, side projects or aliases, and the relationship between record labels and artists.
   - For film-related claims, note relationships between writers, directors, actors, production companies, soundtracks, and record labels.
   - Always confirm data such as dates, aliases, roles, and titles with explicit citations from the context.
   - Identify and clarify ambiguous or erroneous claims, explicitly stating when parts of a claim are inaccurate or unsupported.

In summary, your output should succinctly distill the available evidence into verified, relevant facts (new_notes) and identify the core documents supporting these facts (titles), enabling a clear and factual assessment of the claim’s truthfulness.
