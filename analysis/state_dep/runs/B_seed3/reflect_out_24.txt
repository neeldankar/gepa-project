Given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a clear, concise, and highly focused `query` that targets verifying or investigating the factual accuracy of the `claim` using the information in the `notes`.

---

### Input Fields

- **claim**: A factual statement or set of assertions about entities such as persons, creative works (films, books, bands), places, events, or dates. The claim may involve relationships or attributes, such as roles, affiliations, geographic jurisdictions, or co-authorship.
- **notes**: A list of factual snippets extracted from various documents. These notes contain biographical data, geographic details, roles, affiliations, and other contextual facts relevant to the entities mentioned or implied in the claim.

---

### Expected Output

- **query**: A specific, entity-rich, and direct question designed to:
  - Explicitly address any ambiguous, missing, unsupported, or contradictory facts found when cross-checking the claim against the notes.
  - Target the discrete factual components necessary to confirm or refute the claim.
  - Reference the key entities (people, works, locations, organizations) mentioned or implied in the claim and notes.
  - Guide retrieval or fact-checking processes by focusing precisely on the core verification points, avoiding broad or vague questions.

---

### Detailed Task Requirements and Domain-Specific Considerations

1. **Entity Identification & Coverage**  
   Identify and reference in the query every relevant entity involved in the claim or notes, including:  
   - **Persons**: full names, aliases, or professional roles (e.g., Matt Thiessen, Michael York).  
   - **Works**: titles of songs, films, books, performances, bands (e.g., "Rebuild," "Hollywood Goes Classical").  
   - **Locations**: administrative units, provinces, countries (e.g., Fàn County, Henan Province, China).  
   - **Organizations**: bands, councils, production companies, etc.

2. **Core Fact Categories to Verify**
   Ensure the query covers the crucial fact types necessary to validate the claim, such as:  
   - **Biographical data**: birthplaces, nationalities, mentors, dates.  
   - **Professional roles and affiliations**: membership in bands, roles in productions (actor, vocalist, co-writer), participation in events or tours.  
   - **Geographic relationships**: jurisdictional authority, administrative boundaries, relative location.  
   - **Existence and linkage**: confirm the existence of claimed connections, collaborations, or memberships.

3. **Addressing Ambiguity, Missing or Contradictory Information**  
   - If the claim asserts facts not clearly supported or contradicted by the notes, formulate queries that explicitly request verification of those specific elements.  
   - If some entities or connections in the claim are missing altogether from the notes, ask specifically for their identity, existence, or relationship.  
   - Avoid generic or overly broad questions; focus strictly on the minimal set of missing or ambiguous facts critical to validate the entire claim.

4. **Query Construction Principles**  
   - Queries should be direct, well-formed questions suitable for fact retrieval.  
   - Explicitly mention all relevant entities and works within the query to maximize the recall of documents about these entities.  
   - The query should enable retrieving all core missing or uncertain facts needed to assess the claim entirely, not just parts already confirmed.  
   - Avoid vague phrasing or thematic questions that do not precisely target factual gaps.

---

### Recommended Generalizable Strategy

1. **Parse the Claim**  
   Break down the claim into its factual assertions and identify all involved entities and relationships.

2. **Cross-Reference Against Notes**  
   Categorize facts from the claim as supported, contradicted, or missing in the notes. Identify ambiguous or unverified elements.

3. **Formulate the Query**  
   Construct a clear, focused question targeting these ambiguous, missing, or contradictory facts. Include all relevant entities and works explicitly to cover all aspects of verification.

---

### Examples for Illustration

- When claim involves educational connections and mentorship:  
  > "What is the identity of Ariel Ramírez's science teacher, and is there documentation that this teacher also taught Marta Lambertini?"

- When verifying band membership and geographic base:  
  > "Are the bands God Seed, Sahg, Ov Hell, and Gorgoroth all based in Bergen or the Norwegian county of Hordaland, and what is King ov Hell’s membership status with each?"

- When clarifying administrative jurisdictions and geographic proximity:  
  > "Which town is administered by the Eurobodalla Shire council and is located near Higgin's Creek and Deua National Park?"

---

### Key Reminders

- Always explicitly name the entities, organizations, and works involved in the claim within your query.  
- The query must be targeted to retrieve missing or unclear facts crucial to assess the truthfulness of the claim.  
- Avoid queries that only confirm already well-supported facts or that are too broad/general to guide precise retrieval.  
- The final query should enable retrieval of all relevant documents needed to fully verify or refute the claim.

---

By following these instructions, your queries will be optimally designed to drive precise fact checking and verification from the provided notes and related knowledge sources.
