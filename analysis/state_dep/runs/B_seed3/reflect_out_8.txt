Task: Generate a precise, focused information-seeking query to verify or investigate the truthfulness of a given factual claim based on provided supporting or related notes.

Inputs:
- claim (string): A statement containing one or more factual assertions about people, works (movies, books, events), dates, or entities.
- notes (list of strings): Extracted factual snippets related to the claim, usually containing biographical details, filmographies, authorship, dates, event descriptions, or clarifications.

Output:
- query (string): A concise, targeted question or query explicitly designed to retrieve or verify crucial domain facts that confirm or refute the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understand the Claim:
   - Parse the claim carefully to identify all factual assertions and their key entities:
     - Individuals (names, roles, birthdates, nationalities)
     - Works (movies, books, events, their release/publication dates)
     - Roles and relationships (director, actor, author, competitor)
     - Temporal or sequential facts (e.g., release order, event dates)
     - Claims about existence or attributes of entities or works.

2. Analyze Notes to Cross-Reference Claim Elements:
   - Identify which claim components are confirmed, contradicted, or missing based on the notes.
   - Pinpoint ambiguous, unsupported, or missing pieces of information critical to verifying the claim.
   - Note any relevant key entities mentioned in the notes that are absent or incomplete in the claim.

3. Formulating the Query:
   - The query must be a precise and focused question or request that:
     - Explicitly targets the unverified or ambiguous information necessary to validate or falsify the claim.
     - Mentions all relevant key entities (people, works, organizations) referenced in the claim or notes. When multiple entities are involved, include all to ensure targeted retrieval.
     - Requests specific domain facts such as:
       * Biographical data (birth dates, nationality)
       * Professional involvement (e.g., who directed, acted, wrote)
       * Existence or association with specific works (e.g., directors of a film, authors of a book)
       * Chronology or sequence of events or releases
       * Confirmation or clarification of contradictory claims or contested facts.
     - Is formulated as a clear, direct question (e.g., "Did X begin competing at age five?" or "Who scored the soundtrack for Y in 1985?").
   - Avoid vague, broad, or overly general queries.
   - Ensure the query references all key entities or documents critical to verifying the claim to facilitate maximal retrieval of relevant facts and gold-standard documents.

4. Domain-Specific Considerations:
   - When a claim includes an event involving competition or sports results, specify competitors, event, and timing.
   - When dealing with historical or political claims, include dates, roles (e.g., president), and relationships between individuals.
   - When film or music-related, include movie titles, related works (sequels, part sequels), composers, and precise release years.
   - Address explicitly if a person’s role or activity is in question, such as whether they performed an action or held a position.
   - When multiple documents or entities are involved in the claim, make sure the query covers all to cover all gold documents.

5. Generalizable Strategy Recap:
   - Extract all factual components out of the claim.
   - Check notes against those components for confirmed, contradictory, or missing facts.
   - Identify the minimal set of facts that can validate or refute the claim.
   - Generate a concise question seeking that specific information and include relevant key entities and works mentioned in both claim and notes.
   - The query should be tailored to enable retrieval of all critical supporting documents and evidence.

Example Patterns for Queries:
- "Did [Person X] begin [activity] at age [Y]?"
- "Who first served as [role/job] in [year], claimed [fact], and was opposed by [person(s)]?"
- "What is the release year of [Work A], and which sequel related to it did [Person B] score in [year]?"
- "Which player did [Person C] defeat in [event and year]?"
- "Did [Person X] compose the score for [Work title] and its sequel(s)?"

By following these detailed instructions, the generated query will be precise, focused, and optimally constructed to guide accurate fact verification or retrieval of all relevant supporting documents.
