Given two fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a concise, precise, and focused `query` that facilitates verifying or investigating the truthfulness of the `claim` based on the factual information and context provided in `notes`.

---

### Input Fields

- **claim**: A factual statement or set of assertions involving entities such as persons, works (films, books, bands, albums), places, events, dates, or relationships among these entities.
- **notes**: A list of discrete factual details or context snippets drawn from multiple documents. These may include biographical facts, professional involvements, geographic or administrative data, organizational memberships, affiliations, dates (formation, release, birth, death), or clarifying statements that relate to or overlap with the claim.

---

### Output Field

- **query**: A focused, entity-rich, and well-targeted question or information request that explicitly targets any ambiguous, missing, contradictory, or unsupported claims. The query is designed to retrieve or fact-check critical discrete facts necessary to confirm or refute the claim.

---

### Detailed Task Description and Domain-Specific Considerations

1. **Entity Extraction and Reference**  
   - Identify and explicitly reference all key entities involved in the claim and notes:
     - **Persons:** full names or known aliases.
     - **Works:** movies, albums, books, bands, or any creative works mentioned.
     - **Places:** towns, cities, counties, natural landmarks, or administrative jurisdictions.
     - **Organizations:** companies, councils, labels, sports teams, or event organizers.
   - Ensure the query includes all relevant and related entities to maximize retrieval coverage and focus.

2. **Core Fact Types to Verify or Clarify**  
   The query should seek to verify or clarify facts critical to the claim’s truthfulness, including but not limited to:
   - **Biographical Data:** identities, birth/death dates, nationalities, mentors, education.
   - **Professional and Artistic Roles:** involvement in, formation of, or membership in bands, films, books, or organizations; roles such as actor, director, member, author.
   - **Geographic and Administrative Data:** locations, proximities, and administrative jurisdictions relevant to entities or events.
   - **Date and Event Linkages:** dates of formation, event timing, release years, sponsorship periods.
   - **Existence and Attribution:** confirmation that the entities or connections in the claim exist and are factually attributed as stated.

3. **Addressing Missing, Ambiguous, or Contradictory Information**  
   - Explicitly highlight any entities, facts, or associations claimed but absent or contradicted in the notes.
   - If key entities in the claim do not appear in the notes, the query should request identification, existence, or evidence of their involvement.
   - Avoid broad or generic questions; focus tightly on the specific elements that determine the claim’s factual accuracy.

4. **Query Construction Principles**  
   - Phrase queries as clear, direct, and entity-rich questions or requests for information.
   - When multiple entities or works are involved, list them explicitly to ensure retrieval from all relevant documents.
   - Queries should enable comprehensive fact-checking by targeting all necessary missing or contradictory facts to assess the entire claim.
   - Avoid vague, thematic, or overly broad questions that do not clearly focus on the precise verification required.

---

### Generalizable Strategy (Step-by-Step)

1. **Parse the Claim**  
   - Identify all factual assertions and all involved entities.
   - Separate complex claims into discrete verification points if applicable (e.g., formation dates of multiple bands, identity and roles of persons).

2. **Cross-Reference the Notes**  
   - Determine which facts are confirmed, which are contradicted, and which are missing.
   - Note which entities or fact categories appear in the notes and which do not.

3. **Formulate the Query**  
   - Explicitly target the ambiguous, unsupported, or contradictory facts identified.
   - Include all pertinent entities and works mentioned in the claim and notes relevant to these facts.
   - Frame as a precise question that requests verification, identification, or clarification of the contested or unclear facts.

---

### Examples of Query Targets

- Clarifying ambiguous biographical or mentorship relationships:  
  > "Who was Ariel Ramírez's science teacher, and is there evidence this teacher also taught Marta Lambertini?"

- Verifying affiliation, membership, or geographic base of bands:  
  > "Are the bands God Seed, Sahg, Ov Hell, and Gorgoroth all based in Bergen or Hordaland, and what is King ov Hell's membership status with each?"

- Confirming administrative jurisdiction and geography:  
  > "Which town is administered by the Eurobodalla Shire council and is located near Higgin's Creek and Deua National Park?"

- Verifying formation years and linkage between works and entities:  
  > "What is the formation year of the band Franz Ferdinand, who released the album 'You Could Have It So Much Better,' and is there any connection between this album and Sunrise Avenue?"

- Identifying actors and verifying multiple roles or portrayals:  
  > "Who starred in the thriller '400 Boys,' and did this actor portray Marilyn Monroe in a film adaptation of the novel 'Northern Lights'?"

- Linking events, sponsorship, and participants:  
  > "Was the 2016 Liberty Bowl, sponsored by Memphis-based AutoZone and part of the 2015 college football postseason, the bowl game to which the 2015 Kansas State Wildcats football team was invited?"

---

### Summary

Your output query must be a clear, direct, and specific question referencing key entities and facts to enable precise fact-checking or retrieval of missing information. It should explicitly address any factual gaps, contradictions, or ambiguities found by comparing the claim against the notes. This ensures comprehensive, efficient verification of the claim’s truthfulness.
