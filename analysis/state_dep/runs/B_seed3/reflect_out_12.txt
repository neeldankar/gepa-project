You are tasked with generating a highly focused, concise, and precise `query` to verify or investigate the truthfulness of a factual `claim` based on provided contextual `notes`. The inputs you receive are two fields:

- **claim**: One or more factual assertions involving entities such as people, artistic or cultural works (films, bands, books), places, events, dates, or relationships among these.
- **notes**: A collection of factual snippets extracted from diverse documents, providing biographical details, geographic information, career histories, affiliations, awards, and clarifications relevant to the claim’s entities and facts.

Your goal is to formulate a direct, clear, and entity-rich `query` that explicitly targets any ambiguous, missing, contradictory, or unsupported elements in the claim relative to the notes. The query must be tailored to enable retrieval or fact-checking systems to uncover all necessary facts needed to confirm or refute the entire claim.

---

### Detailed Task Description

1. **Entity Identification and Explicit Coverage**
   - Extract and explicitly reference all relevant key entities from the claim and notes, including:
     - Persons (full names, aliases)
     - Works (films, albums, bands, books)
     - Locations (counties, towns, natural landmarks)
     - Organizations (councils, labels, festivals)
   - Make sure the query mentions these entities explicitly to maximize document retrieval for all pertinent information.

2. **Focus on Core Fact Types to Verify**
   The query should target fact categories critical to the claim's truthfulness:
   - **Biographical Data:** birth/death dates, nationalities, education, teachers/mentors, military service.
   - **Professional/Artistic Involvement:** roles/positions (director, actor, band member), group affiliations, formation or base locations of bands or artistic works.
   - **Geographical Relationships:** administrative jurisdictions, locality relative to landmarks or other places.
   - **Awards/Recognition:** types and numbers of awards won by individuals or works.
   - **Existence and Linkages:** confirmation that claimed entities exist, and connections (e.g., membership, participation) between entities.

3. **Addressing Ambiguities, Missing, or Contradictory Information**
   - Identify incomplete, ambiguous, or conflicting assertions in the claim vis-à-vis notes.
   - If an entity or fact in the claim is absent in notes, explicitly ask for verification or identification.
   - When elements in the claim conflict with notes, your query must explicitly address that contradiction to clarify or disprove the claim.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; instead, concentrate tightly on the essential disputed or missing elements that determine claim validity.

4. **Formulating Effective Queries**
   - Construct the query as a clear, explicit question or set of questions focused on retrieving discrete, verifiable facts.
   - When multiple entities or works are involved, enumerate them within the query to target all relevant documents.
   - Ensure the query covers all factual gaps needed to fully evaluate the claim.
   - Do not rely on thematic or generic questions; keep queries precise and domain-specific.

---

### Recommended Approach

1. **Parse the claim carefully:**
   - Identify all factual assertions and involved entities.
   - If the claim is complex, consider breaking it into key components (e.g., award counts, military service, film classification).

2. **Cross-reference with the notes:**
   - Confirm which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or missing.
   - Identify entities mentioned in the claim but absent from notes, and flag these for explicit query inclusion.

3. **Craft the query:**
   - Explicitly reference all crucial entities (persons, works, locations, organizations).
   - Directly address and seek verification for ambiguous, contradictory, or missing facts.
   - Phrase the query as a factual, information-seeking question designed to guide retrieval or fact-checking effectively.

---

### Domain-Specific Considerations Noted from Examples

- When claims involve **musical films and awards**, verify both the classification of the film (is it truly a musical?), the festival and year (e.g., "50th Berlin International Film Festival"), and the biographical background of associated actors (e.g., military service).
- When comparing **award counts between persons**, identify all relevant "awards" given in notes and explicitly ask for verifiable award data on all parties, especially if one party is ambiguous or absent in notes.
- When claims talk about **band memberships and side projects**, clearly associate the band members and side projects mentioned, confirm membership and roles, and locate origins or base locations.
- For **geographic claims**, be precise about administrative jurisdictions and relationships to landmarks and other places.
- Always disambiguate entities with similar or overlapping names (e.g., “American Frank Sinatra” vs. “German Frank Sinatra”) and clarify their identities and award histories.

---

### Summary

Your output should be a direct, entity-rich, precise question (or set of questions) that targets any factually critical gaps, contradictions, or ambiguities in the claim supported or contradicted by the notes. This question will guide retrieval of all relevant factual data necessary to assess the claim’s truthfulness comprehensively and accurately.
