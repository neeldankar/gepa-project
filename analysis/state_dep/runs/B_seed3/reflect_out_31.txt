You will be given three input fields:

- `claim`: a natural language statement that asserts a factual relationship or event involving people, films, plants, or other entities.
- `notes`: a list of factual sentences extracted from various sources that are relevant to the claim. These notes may contain overlapping or redundant information and may differ in detail or specificity.
- `context`: background paragraphs or document excerpts providing detailed information about entities mentioned in the claim or notes. These are typically descriptions from encyclopedic or knowledge-base style sources.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

- `new_notes`: a concise subset of the `notes` list that most directly support, clarify, or refute the `claim`. Select notes that are specifically relevant and contain key facts used to assess the claim's truthfulness or details. Avoid unrelated or marginally relevant notes.
- `titles`: a list of document or entity titles (ideally from the `context`) corresponding to the main sources or key topics needed to address the claim accurately. Titles should identify the key people, works (films, plants, etc.), or concepts central to verifying or explaining the claim.

Detailed instructions to guide your approach:

1. **Understand the claim fully:** Identify all entities mentioned (people, films, plants, genera, etc.) and what factual or temporal relationships are asserted between them.

2. **Evaluate notes critically:** From the `notes`, extract those that contain direct evidence relevant to validating or negating the claim. This includes biographical data (dates of birth and death), direct statements about film debuts or directorship, taxonomic or species counts, and factual corrections or clarifications conflicting with the claim.

3. **Cross-check with the context:** Use the `context` to disambiguate or confirm facts from the notes, such as nationality of a director, species counts in a genus, or the scope of an actor’s debut film. Context is often authoritative and should guide note selection.

4. **Synthesize evidence:** Summarize the minimal yet sufficient subset of notes that allow a reviewer to understand the basis for accepting or rejecting the claim as stated.

5. **Select informative titles:** From the context texts, choose titles of key documents or entities that cover the main claim topics. Titles should help a human reviewer quickly locate full authoritative information sources related to the claim.

6. **Domain-specific factual awareness:** 
   - Recognize that some claims involve nuanced distinctions such as the nationality of a film director or whether a “debut” refers to feature film debut or first film appearance.
   - Botanical claims may involve genus names, species counts, and relationships between common French names and scientific taxa.
   - Temporal claims about co-existence of individuals require accurate birth and death years.
   - Film claims require identification of director, co-writers, main actors, and their involvement timelines.

7. **General strategy:** Approach this as a fact verification or claim validation task by:
   - Extracting relevant known facts and evidence from notes and context.
   - Determining if the claim is supported, contradicted, or partially true.
   - Presenting the minimal but sufficient evidence as new_notes.
   - Listing key related entities or works as titles for further review.

This structured approach will ensure that your outputs focus on relevant factual evidence and informative references to support transparent and accurate claim assessments.
