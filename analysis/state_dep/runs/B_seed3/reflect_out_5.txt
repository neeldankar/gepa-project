```markdown
Given two fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a concise, precise, and focused `query` to verify or investigate the truthfulness of the `claim` based on the information contained in the `notes`.

---

### Input Fields

- **claim**: A factual statement or set of factual assertions about entities such as people, works (movies, books, bands, etc.), places, events, dates, or their interrelations.
- **notes**: A list of factual details or contextual snippets extracted from various documents. These often include biographical data, geographic information, career involvement, dates, affiliations, and clarifications that relate to or overlap with the claim.

---

### Output Field

- **query**: A targeted question or information request formulated to:
  - Explicitly address any ambiguous, missing, contradictory, or unsupported elements in the claim.
  - Focus on retrieving discrete domain facts critical for confirming or refuting the claim.
  - Reference the key entities (people, works, organizations, places) mentioned or implied by the claim and supported or contradicted by the notes.
  - Enable guided retrieval or fact-checking from relevant documents or knowledge bases associated with these entities.

---

### Task Details and Domain-Specific Considerations

1. **Entity Identification and Coverage**
   - Extract all relevant entities from both the claim and the notes, including:
     - Persons (by full name or alias)
     - Works (films, books, albums, bands)
     - Locations (counties, towns, natural landmarks)
     - Organizations (councils, record labels)
   - Ensure the query explicitly references these entities to maximize retrieval of all relevant supporting documents.

2. **Fact Categories to Address**
   The query should seek verification or details on core fact types regarding the claim's entities:
   - **Biographical Data:** birth and death dates, nationality, education, teachers or mentors.
   - **Professional/Artistic Involvement:** roles (director, actor, member, author), affiliations with works or organizations, formation or base location of bands/works.
   - **Geographic Relationships:** administrative jurisdictions, locations relative to landmarks or other places.
   - **Existence and Linkage:** whether the entities or connections claimed exist or are documented.
   
3. **Handling Missing or Contradictory Information**
   - If the claim asserts a connection or fact absent or contradicted in the notes, the query must explicitly address verification of that missing or ambiguous element.
   - If some entities referenced in the claim (e.g., a teacher, a location) do not appear in the notes, the query should ask for their identity, existence, or connection regarding the claim.
   - Avoid queries that are overly broad or generic; instead, focus tightly on the specific missing or uncertain elements that determine the truthfulness of the claim.

4. **Guiding Principles for Query Construction**
   - Formulate queries as clear, direct questions. They should be easily interpretable as requests for pertinent, factual information.
   - When multiple entities and/or works are involved, explicitly list them within the query to help target all relevant documents.
   - Queries should enable retrieval of all missing or disputed facts necessary to assess the entire claim, not just parts already confirmed.
   - Avoid vague phrasing or general thematic questions.
   
5. **Examples of Effective Queries**
   - When a claim involves education and mentorship relations:
     > "What is the identity of Ariel Ramírez's science teacher, and is there documentation that this teacher also taught Marta Lambertini?"
   - When verifying geographic and membership claims about bands:
     > "Are the bands God Seed, Sahg, Ov Hell, and Gorgoroth all based in Bergen or the Norwegian county of Hordaland, and what is King ov Hell’s membership and association with each?"
   - When clarifying administrative jurisdictions and geographic proximity:
     > "Which town is administered by the Eurobodalla Shire council and is located near Higgin's Creek and Deua National Park?"

---

### Generalizable Strategy

1. **Parse the Claim:**
   - Identify factual assertions and involved entities.
   - Break complex claims into subcomponents if practical (e.g., date of death and teacher connection).

2. **Cross-Reference the Notes:**
   - Confirm what is supported, contradicted, or missing.
   - Note which entities or facts appear or are absent.

3. **Formulate the Query:**
   - Target the ambiguous, missing, or contradictory facts in the claim.
   - Explicitly include all key entities and works tied to these facts.
   - Phrase as a clear, focused question requiring factual data.

---

### Summary

Your output query should be a direct, specific, and entity-rich question that addresses the core factual gaps or verification points for the claim, informed by the notes. This ensures retrieval or fact-checking uncovers all pertinent documents and facilitates accurate validation or refutation of the claim.
```
