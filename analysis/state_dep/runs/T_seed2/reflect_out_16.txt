Task Description:

You will be provided with three inputs concerning a factual claim:

- `claim`: A specific factual assertion involving one or more named entities (people, organizations, events, works, places), often including details such as dates, roles, nationalities, associations, or relationships.
- `notes`: A collection of extracted fact snippets from various documents or resources. These snippets may contain partial overlap, redundancy, or peripheral information, but collectively may provide evidence supporting or contradicting the claim.
- `context`: A set of documents or document extracts containing more extensive background, authoritative details, or structured knowledge regarding the entities and topics mentioned in the claim.

Your overall goal is to analyze the claim using the evidence from `notes` and `context`, then produce two outputs: a refined subset of evidence (`new_notes`) and a concise, targeted set of source document titles (`titles`) that collectively verify or contextualize the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Reasoning and Analysis of the Claim:
   - Identify the key entities, dates, roles, relationships, or events embedded in the claim.
   - Cross-reference these components against the information in the `notes` and the broader `context`.
   - Determine whether the claim is fully supported, contradicted, or cannot be conclusively verified based on the available evidence.
   - Pay special attention to nuanced details such as:
     - Political domain: political parties, leadership elections, politician affiliations, and career timelines.
     - Biographical details: birth dates, career milestones, identity attributes (gender, nationality).
     - Geographical relationships: hierarchical place relations (e.g., cemetery located inside a park inside a larger area).
     - Film/media: correct matching of actors, roles, and media release dates or sequences.
     - Timing/sequence of events: earliest accurate dates of release or occurrence.
   - Distinguish between direct contradictions, weak or missing evidence, and peripheral facts.

2. Generation of `new_notes` (Refined Evidence):
   - Select a minimal yet high-value subset of fact snippets from the original `notes`.
   - These should be snippets that either directly support, refute, or provide essential context for the claim.
   - Consolidate duplicates and remove near-duplicates to avoid redundancy.
   - Include important precise factual elements like dates, specific names, affiliations, or event details if they pertain clearly to the claim.
   - Exclude information that is irrelevant or only tangentially related to the claim.
   - Ensure clarity and factual precision; avoid assuming facts not supported by the `notes` or `context`.

3. Generation of `titles` (Document Sources):
   - Identify the titles or identifiers of the documents within the `context` which correspond best to the chosen `new_notes`.
   - The chosen documents should be those providing authoritative or direct verification or background relevant to the claim’s key elements.
   - Prioritize documents that mention the major entities or events central to the claim.
   - Aim to retrieve all gold-standard or key documents known to be relevant to the claim (e.g., specific biographies, official event pages, named works or productions).
   - Avoid including irrelevant or loosely related documents in the titles list.

Additional Domain-Specific and Best-Practice Details:

- Political Claims:
  - Reference documents related to politicians, political parties, leadership contests, and verified affiliations.
  - Use consistent career timeline data to verify leadership or role claims.

- Biographical Verification:
  - Reinforce identity claims with date of birth, nationality, gender, and career events.
  - Verify timelines coherently.

- Geographical and Location Claims:
  - Capture precise hierarchical relationships, e.g., cemetery inside a park inside a national recreation area.
  - Focus on specificity to reflect accurate geo-political or locational structures.

- Film and Media Claims:
  - Associate actor names with correct character roles and specify movie or media titles with verified release years.
  - Ensure event or release sequence correctness where timing or order is involved.

- Event and Temporal Claims:
  - Confirm event timing or sequence claims with earliest accurate and authoritative dates.
  - Avoid assuming chronology if not supported by evidence.

- Evidence Selection:
  - Carefully prune duplicates or near-duplicates.
  - Avoid extraneous or general knowledge that does not support the claim directly.
  - Produce succinct but information-rich evidence notes.

- Document Title Selection:
  - Extract exact or near-exact matches to core claim entities or events.
  - Do not guess missing documents; if relevant gold documents are missing, strive to include them if known from the context.
  - Titles should be clear and correctly identify the source of the evidence.

Summary:

Your output must concisely and precisely verify or contextualize the claim by delivering:

- A carefully curated, minimal set of `new_notes` that provides factual support or contradiction for the claim.
- A focused list of `titles` referencing the authoritative and relevant documents that underpin these notes.

Maintain accuracy, domain awareness, and factual precision throughout reasoning and output generation.
