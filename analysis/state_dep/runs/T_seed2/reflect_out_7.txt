Task Description:
Given a `claim` (a factual assertion typically involving entities, events, roles, dates, and relationships) and a set of supporting `notes` (retrieved documents or evidence snippets related to the claim), generate a precise and comprehensive `query` or set of queries that can be used to verify the claim by retrieving additional relevant documents or evidence.

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Understand the Claim Structure:**
   - Identify all key entities (people, institutions, places, organizations, dates, events) mentioned in the claim.
   - Extract each factual assertion or element embedded within the claim that requires verification. For example:
     - Roles or titles (e.g., "first chairman of the board of trustees")
     - Temporal facts (e.g., "founded in 1969", "played from 1973-87", "managed the Tampa Bay Devil Rays season")
     - Relationships between entities (e.g., "that university and the University of Idaho are public universities", "Craig Heyer played baseball at that college")
   - Where multiple assertions appear, ensure the queries comprehensively address all parts; don’t omit any claim segments.

2. **Leverage the Notes to Ground Queries:**
   - Use the notes to confirm which entities and aspects have already been confirmed or documented and which remain uncertain or lack direct evidence.
   - Identify gaps where the claim cannot be fully verified by the provided notes.
   - Consider referencing known facts or common knowledge evident in the notes (e.g., that University of Idaho is a public university) to either confirm or seek additional verification.
   - Where notes reveal ambiguous or conflicting evidence, design queries to clarify these points.

3. **Formulate Clear, Specific, Targeted Queries:**
   - Convert each key factual claim into one or more direct, detailed questions designed to retrieve documents specifically addressing those facts.
   - Queries should be phrased to maximize precision for document retrieval and to cover all components of the claim.
   - Examples of query types:
     - Identifying the precise entity referenced (e.g., "Which university was Reverend Lyttleton Morgan first chairman of the board of trustees for?")
     - Verifying institutional attributes (e.g., "Is Morgan State University a public university?")
     - Confirming temporal facts (e.g., "Which Canadian MLB team was founded in 1969?")
     - Connecting sequence of events or careers (e.g., "Who played for the Montreal Expos between 1973 and 1987 and later managed the Tampa Bay Devil Rays?")
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; instead, tailor them to isolate the claim’s facts.

4. **Domain-Specific Knowledge Considerations:**
   - Recognize common domain entities and terminologies relevant to the claim:
     - Universities and their public/private status, trust board roles
     - Geographic and administrative divisions (e.g., Clark County, Nevada; Las Vegas)
     - Sports teams, player careers, founding dates, managerial roles (e.g., MLB teams like Montreal Expos, Tampa Bay Devil Rays)
   - Incorporate knowledge of possible multiple entities that share names or locations and clarify these in queries.
   - Include relevant date ranges or temporal qualifiers to avoid generic queries.

5. **Output Format:**
   - Provide a concise set of one or more natural language queries as the field `query`.
   - Queries should be clear, as if asking a search engine or database to retrieve precise, relevant documents for claim verification.

6. **Generalizable Strategy:**
   - Decompose complex claims into constituent factual questions.
   - Cross-reference notes to confirm what is known and identify which facts need additional retrieval.
   - Construct queries that explicitly address unresolved or key verification points.
   - Ensure that the queries collectively cover the full claim, supporting comprehensive evidence retrieval.

Summary:
Produce precise, fact-targeted queries derived from analyzing the claim and notes, focusing on verifying each individual assertion—most often about identities, roles, temporal information, relationships, and institutional attributes—especially in contexts involving educational institutions, geographic entities, and sports teams/players. The queries should be optimized for document retrieval systems to fetch missing evidence or confirm stated facts.
