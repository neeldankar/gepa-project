Task Description:
You will be provided with three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Input fields:
- `claim`: a factual or semi-factual assertion that may involve specific entities, events, or attributes.
- `notes`: a list of sentences or short paragraphs derived from various sources that are relevant to the claim. These often include direct facts or refutations related to the claim.
- `context`: a list of full-text documents or sections that contain detailed background information, supporting facts, biographies, discographies, filmographies, or other domain-specific knowledge related to the claim.

Output fields:
- `new_notes`: a distilled and concise subset of the original `notes` that accurately reflects the verifiable facts relevant to evaluating the `claim`. If some notes are incorrect or irrelevant to the claim, exclude them. Summarize the key factual points that support or rebut the claim.
- `titles`: a list of titles of the most relevant documents from the `context` that directly pertain to the entities, works, or concepts mentioned in the `claim` and `new_notes`. These should be the primary sources that substantiate the key facts used in your reasoning.

Detailed guidance:
1. Understand the claim fully: identify entities (people, films, albums, songs), relationships, roles, dates, and any ambiguous or potentially false assertions.

2. Analyze `notes` by filtering out incorrect, irrelevant, or unsupported statements. Choose those that best confirm, clarify, or dispute the claim, ensuring accuracy and relevance.

3. Review the `context` list for documents directly related to the claim’s components, such as specific films, albums, artists, actors, directors, or other relevant topics. Select the minimal but comprehensive set of documents that underpin the chosen `new_notes`.

4. Your reasoning should be implicit in how you select notes and titles: choose supporting facts that resolve potential contradictions in the claim, clarify misattributions, and verify correctness with domain-specific knowledge.

5. When the claim involves biographical or discographical details (e.g., artist career timelines, album tracklists), use documents like discographies, filmographies, biographies, and detailed album/film summaries to validate assertions.

6. When claims involve relationship or role attributions (e.g., who starred in or directed a film, who covered a song, who produced a work), use detailed descriptions of cast, crew, production, and release history within context documents. 

7. Identify and exclude frequent errors in claims such as confusing actors with characters they portray, mixing up directors and actors, or misattributing production credits.

8. Respond with `new_notes` as a refined list that succinctly covers the key factual points necessary to evaluate the claim.

9. Respond with `titles` as concise, canonical document names that directly correspond to the key entities or works critical to understanding and verifying the claim.

Example domains include music albums and their tracks/artists, films and their cast/directors/producers, and notable events related to these.

The overall strategy:
- Thoroughly verify the claim facts by cross-checking notes and context.
- Filter notes to keep only accurate, relevant facts.
- Identify and provide the authoritative sources from the context.
- Present outputs clearly and succinctly reflecting precise support or rebuttal of the claim.

This approach ensures the verification process is fact-based, transparent, and leverages domain-specific knowledge embedded in the context documents to confirm or disprove the claim.
