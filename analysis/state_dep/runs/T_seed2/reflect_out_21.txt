Task Description:
Given two input fields—`claim` and `notes`—generate an output field `query` that formulates precise, targeted information retrieval queries or clarifying questions to verify or refute the claim based on the evidential context provided in `notes`. The queries you produce must guide subsequent searches or database queries to fetch the key facts needed for thorough claim validation.

Input Format:
- `claim`: a natural language statement involving one or more entities, attributes, relationships, or comparisons.
- `notes`: a list of natural language evidential or contextual statements related to the claim, providing partial facts, background, or highlighting ambiguities and information gaps.

Output Requirement:
- `query`: A set of structured, precise queries or questions targeting the retrieval of critical data to either confirm or deny the `claim`.

Key Goals:
1. Entity Disambiguation and Identification
   - Clearly identify each entity mentioned or implied in the claim. Consider aliases, nationality, or notable identifiers.
   - Where entities in the claim may be obscure or questionable, explicitly query their existence or well-known attributes.
   
2. Extraction of Critical Attributes
   - Determine which attributes are necessary to validate the claim, e.g., dates (birth, release, event), affiliations (membership in bands, universities, companies), locations (city, county, country), roles (positions held, participation in teams), or works associated (EPs, movies).
   
3. Retrieval of Relationship and Context Data
   - Form queries to confirm relationships or connections stated or implied in the claim, such as co-location (e.g., colleges within counties), membership (e.g., band membership), participation (e.g., sports teams), burial sites of individuals, or involvement in works (e.g., directors linked to films).
   
4. Addressing Ambiguities and Uncertainties
   - Use notes to identify uncertainties, contradictory information, or missing data.
   - Formulate follow-up queries that seek clarifications on ambiguous entities, disputed facts, or unknown information explicitly recognized in notes.
   
5. Explicitly Query for Missing or Key Evidence
   - If `notes` reveal missing links or unresolved issues crucial to validating the claim, generate queries to gather this missing data.

6. Query Expression Style
   - Use clear, unambiguous natural language questions if the evidence retrieval system supports them.
   - Alternatively, formulate structured queries or semi-structured requests that can be mapped onto knowledge bases, knowledge graphs, or structured databases (e.g., SPARQL-like queries).

Domain-Specific Guidance Derived From Examples:
- When comparing individuals by birth or career dates, specify queries that retrieve these attributes to settle chronological claims.
- When verifying locations, clarify whether entities (such as colleges, companies, cemeteries) reside within certain geographical boundaries (counties, cities, parks).
- When claims involve membership or affiliation (e.g., band memberships linked to musical works), query sought-after roles, group names, and nationalities.
- When entities or attributes are obscure or not well-attested (such as a lesser-known director or artist), generate queries explicitly validating their identity, existence, and the connection to claimed works.
- Recognize and include alternative names or historical changes (e.g., name changes of institutions) to cover background contexts.
- If notes contain multiple overlapping or repeated facts, synthesize these into complementary queries that cover each relevant angle efficiently.

Generalizable Strategy:
1. Parse the claim to identify all entities, relationships, and attributes needing verification.
2. Analyze notes to extract known facts, identify gaps, contradictions, or ambiguities.
3. Define precise queries to obtain confirming data for each fact or relationship involved in the claim.
4. Use a structured approach: for each entity—who/what/when/where—seek key verifying facts; for relationships—ask about connections, co-locations, or common attributes.
5. Incorporate explicit verification steps targeting flagged or unknown aspects, ensuring queries cover any missing or ambiguous claim elements noted.
6. Prioritize queries that will likely retrieve the gold standard evidential documents or authoritative data enabling final claim validation.

By following this comprehensive framework, queries produced will effectively target essential evidential retrieval, ensuring thorough, precise, and actionable fact verification for complex claims grounded in multifaceted real-world knowledge domains.
