You will be given three fields as input:

- `claim`: A statement or assertion that may be true or false.
- `notes`: A list of textual evidences or facts extracted from various sources, some of which may be redundant or partially overlapping, relevant to evaluating the claim.
- `context`: Additional background information, descriptions, biographies, or related information that may or may not be directly relevant but can provide broader context or support.

Your task is to analyze the inputs and produce two fields:

1. `new_notes` — a concise, deduplicated, and accurate selection of key pieces of information from the provided `notes` and relevant observations made while reasoning. These should focus on directly supporting or refuting the claim or clarifying uncertainties related to the claim. Avoid repeating redundant statements. Emphasize facts that establish relationships of dates, events, people, or entities critical to evaluating the claim.

2. `titles` — a list of relevant document or entity titles extracted or inferred from the `notes` and `context`. These titles should represent key topics, persons, works (such as operas, films, or albums), or specific biographies most pertinent to understanding or verifying the claim. Include all major related titles that were referenced as sources or have critical information relevant to the claim.

Additional guidelines and domain-specific considerations:

- Claims often concern biographical or historical facts including birthdates, professions, relationships between persons or works (e.g., composers, films, musicians), or temporal orderings (such as who is older, who created what).

- When resolving claims about relative ages or birthdates, if exact dates for one entity are missing, explicitly note the uncertainty in `new_notes`.

- Names of individuals and creative works often have multiple associated titles (e.g., different versions of an opera by different composers). Be sure to reflect all distinct applicable entities (e.g., "Manon Lescaut (Puccini)", "Manon Lescaut (Auber)", "Manon (Massenet)") in `titles`.

- The `context` may contain peripheral or tangential information that can clarify or complement the `notes`. Use it to fill gaps where possible, but do not introduce unsupported claims.

- Avoid conflating different people or works with similar names — maintain precise disambiguation in both notes and titles.

- When a claim combines multiple features (for example, birth month and profession), assess each separately and state clearly which are supported or contradicted by the evidence.

- The overall reasoning approach is to confirm or refute the claim based on factual evidence directly supported by the notes and context, and to surface uncertainties transparently when information is incomplete.

- Where available, indicate relationships between entities (e.g., "Gabe Saporta was born on X and is the former Midtown bassist and lead vocalist who created Cobra Starship", associating person and role/fact in the notes).

- When producing the `titles` list, ensure you include all relevant biographical entries, works (films, operas, songs), and any other notable entities referenced in reasoning, prioritizing those that are key to verifying the claim.

- The formatting of the output fields should be clear, structured lists for `new_notes` and `titles`.

In sum, your role is to serve as a fact synthesis system that, given an assertion and scattered supporting information, distills pertinent, accurate notes and enumerates key source titles that establish the truth or falsehood of the claim, identifying gaps or limitations in evidence as needed.
