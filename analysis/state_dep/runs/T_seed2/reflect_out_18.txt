Task Description:
Given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, your goal is to produce a concise and focused `query` that can be used to retrieve relevant information or documents to verify or refute the claim. The `claim` is typically a factual statement or assertion that may involve multiple entities or comparisons. The `notes` contain extracted facts, background details, or evidences related to the claim. Your output must be a clear, unambiguous query formulated in natural language or a database query format (e.g., SQL), that targets key entities, relationships, or attributes necessary to confirm the truthfulness of the claim.

Key Points and Domain-Specific Considerations:
1. The claims often involve:
   - Native habitat or geographic origin of species or objects.
   - Biographical facts about individuals (e.g., nationality, profession).
   - Relationships between entities (e.g., who directed a particular film, who wrote a song).
   - Comparative metrics (e.g., country area sizes).
   - Attribution of creative works (e.g., songwriters, lyricists, final films).

2. The `notes` typically contain multiple statements, some repeating the same facts, but also pointing out possible contradictions or gaps. Use them to identify:
   - The relevant named entities or subjects involved.
   - The key factual elements necessary to verify the claim.
   - Any missing entity or detail that is central to confirming the claim.

3. Your queries should aim to retrieve the minimal but sufficient set of authoritative documents or facts that directly address the core of the claim. For example:
   - When verifying geographic origin, include all species or locations referenced.
   - When verifying creative attribution, list the credited authors, lyricists, or directors explicitly mentioned.
   - When verifying comparative claims, include data points (e.g., country size metrics) and actor associations.

4. Where useful and feasible, formulate precise structured queries (e.g., SQL) to retrieve integrated facts across entities and their attributes, ensuring the queries:
   - Specify the entities clearly.
   - Include relevant join conditions between entities and attribute data (e.g., director-to-film-to-country).
   - Include necessary filtering conditions (e.g., film is final, country area comparison).

5. The reasoning output (not requested here but useful for your process) should synthesize the claim and notes to identify gaps or ambiguities and highlight which specific factual checks are required.

6. Use canonical entity names or titles where possible to maximize retrieval accuracy (for example, use full names of persons, complete film titles, or scientific species names).

Summary of Effective Strategy:
- Parse the claim to identify entities and relationships involved.
- Leverage the notes to extract relevant key facts and entities; identify missing but crucial entities.
- Construct a query that explicitly requests information connecting all relevant entities, attributes, and relationships.
- Frame the query to retrieve focused evidence that can conclusively verify or falsify the claim.
- When multiple entities are compared or involved, include them all explicitly in the query.
- When possible, use structured queries to join related data across entities and attributes, specifying exact filters.

This approach ensures efficient retrieval of authoritative evidence supporting or refuting the claim.
