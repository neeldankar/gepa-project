Task Description:

Given two inputs:
- `claim`: a natural language statement that may involve one or more named entities (persons, plants, animals, organizations, events, etc.), and assert relationships, comparisons, or classifications involving those entities.
- `notes`: a list of statements or evidential/contextual facts providing analysis, clarifications, or background related to the claim, including any contradictions or gaps in information relevant to validating or refuting the claim.

Produce an output field `query` that contains a set of carefully constructed, precise, and targeted information retrieval queries or questions. The `query` is intended to drive the retrieval of authoritative factual evidence or documents needed to verify or falsify the claim.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Details:

1. **Identify and Disambiguate Entities:**
   - Extract all named entities referenced in the claim, including persons, organizations, species, places, events, or informal references (nicknames, colloquial names).
   - Where entities are ambiguous, unknown, or possibly conflated (e.g., a personal name vs. a team nickname), the query must explicitly request clarification on entity identity and nature.

2. **Extract and Query Relevant Attributes:**
   - For persons: birthdates, roles, professions, affiliations (teams, companies), participation in events.
   - For species or biological taxa: genus/species names, common names (including foreign language terms like French names), total number of species, geographic distribution.
   - For organizations or groups: official and colloquial names, location of headquarters, membership or role of specific individuals, association with events or outputs (e.g., album contributions).
   - For events or competitions: participants, results, goal scorers, roster lists, dates.

3. **Focus on Key Relationships in the Claim:**
   - If the claim involves comparative counts (e.g., number of species between two genera), queries should retrieve authoritative taxonomic or statistical data from botanical or biological knowledge bases.
   - For claims involving membership or roles (e.g., band members linked to a song), queries should target recognized role assignments.
   - For claims about geography (e.g., company and airport co-location), queries should request geographic or jurisdictional data.

4. **Address Ambiguity, Missing Data, and Contradictions Highlighted in Notes:**
   - Identify gaps or unresolved points flagged in the `notes`.
   - Formulate queries to explicitly request information to resolve these gaps.
   - For example, if it is unclear if an entity name refers to a breed or an individual, query that distinction directly.
   - If sources disagree or no data exist for certain aspects (e.g., a person's connection to an event or team is unverified), the queries must attempt to confirm or deny with authoritative references.

5. **Use Domain-Specific Knowledge:**
   - Use knowledge of taxonomic naming conventions, organization of sports teams, breed classifications, and event rosters.
   - Recognize common contextual clues that differentiate entities (e.g., "Saule" in French corresponds to genus Salix).
   - For sports-related claims, distinguish between American football and soccer, confirm player identities and roles in specific matches.

6. **Query Format:**
   - Formulate queries as clear, concise natural language questions or as structured queries that fit knowledge base retrieval systems (e.g., SPARQL-like).
   - When appropriate, break down complex claims into multiple focused questions targeting one fact per query.
   - Queries should ask for authoritative, fact-based data sources: official breed registries, botanical databases, sports federations, or event records.

7. **Aim of Queries:**
   - Queries must enable retrieval of key documents or data points (e.g., authoritative biographies, official team rosters, botanical databases) necessary to assess the claim’s truthfulness.
   - The queries should prioritize precision and relevance to maximally reduce ambiguity and target critical verification points.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Analyze the claim to detect all entities and factual assertions.
- Cross-reference the entities and assertions with the `notes` to detect discrepancies, missing evidence, or ambiguities.
- For each critical fact necessary to prove or disprove the claim, generate targeted questions or queries addressing:
  - Verification of entity existence and identity.
  - Confirmation of key attributes or statistics.
  - Validation of relationships or roles asserted.
- Incorporate explicit queries to fill knowledge gaps flagged by the `notes`.
- Formulate queries suitable for querying structured knowledge bases or unstructured document retrieval.
- Use a series of focused, fact-seeking questions rather than overly broad or vague queries.

This approach ensures that when the output queries are executed, they will return relevant factual records needed for comprehensive verification or refutation of the original claim.
