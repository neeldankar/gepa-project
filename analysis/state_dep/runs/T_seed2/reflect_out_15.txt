Task: Given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, your goal is to produce a precise, focused natural language `query` that can be used to verify or fact-check the `claim` against the evidence provided in `notes`.

Input format:
- `claim`: A factual or composite statement whose truthfulness or correctness needs to be checked.
- `notes`: A list of factual, domain-specific, and verifiable statements or document excerpts relevant to various aspects of the claim.

Output format:
- `query`: A clear, concise, and targeted question or set of questions formulated in natural language that directly correspond to the key factual components of the claim and facts found in the notes. The query should be crafted so that retrieving or verifying its answer from a knowledge base or document corpus will resolve the claim’s truth.

Detailed instructions and domain knowledge:

1. **Purpose of the query:**  
   The query must be framed to support fact verification by pinpointing the exact information needed to prove or disprove the claim. It should directly reflect the claim’s elements and be answerable by the notes or by documents closely related to the notes.

2. **Incorporate key facts and entities from notes:**  
   - Identify the main subjects, entities, and relationships mentioned in the claim.  
   - Use factual corroborations or contradictions from the notes to clarify ambiguous or composite elements in the claim.  
   - Include relevant domain-specific identifiers (e.g., geographic jurisdictions, official titles, lifespan dates, administrative hierarchy, school district compositions, creators and roles in media productions) that appear in the notes.  
   - If the claim involves hierarchical relationships (e.g., jurisdictions, administrative divisions, lifespan overlaps), ensure the query touches on those explicit relationships.

3. **Generalizable reasoning strategy:**  
   - Summarize the relevant information from the notes that supports or refutes parts of the claim (implicitly in your mind) to inform what to ask.  
   - Frame the query as a question that distinctly targets the verifiable piece of information needed to settle the claim’s validity — typically by asking whether certain facts or relationships hold, or what entities are involved in a particular classification or category.  
   - When multiple possible entities or facts exist in the notes regarding the claim, incorporate them into the query to allow disambiguation or comprehensive fact verification.

4. **Query construction style:**  
   - Prefer explicit mention of key named entities and their attributes (e.g., names, dates, roles, locations) as found in the notes.  
   - Formulate the query in a natural, human-readable question format suitable for retrieval or fact-checking modules.  
   - Avoid ambiguity or overly broad queries; focus on the exact fact(s) the claim’s truth hinges upon.

5. **Examples and domain specifics referenced:**  
   - Administrative Geography: When the claim concerns jurisdictional relationships (e.g., "X County falls under Y City in Z Province, both X and another city are in the same country"), the query should confirm those jurisdictional links and the shared country.  
   - Lifespan Overlaps and Creators: When temporal co-existence between persons (e.g., actors, creators of a movie) is claimed, the query should target overlapping lifespans or involvement in the work.  
   - Institutional Composition: When a claim involves specific entities within a composite group (e.g., schools in a school district), ensure the query asks to confirm membership and relevant service areas, clarifying any contradictory components in the claim.

Summary:  
Your output `query` must be a clear, precise natural language question that encapsulates the core factual verification needed from the `claim` using information implied or explicitly given in the `notes`. It should facilitate retrieving or checking authoritative documents to support or refute the claim efficiently and accurately.
