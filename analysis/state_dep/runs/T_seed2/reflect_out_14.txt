Task Description:
You are given two input fields: 
- `claim`: a natural language assertion that may involve entities, their attributes, relationships, or comparisons (e.g., comparing birth dates, affiliations, or locations). 
- `notes`: a list of evidential or background statements that provide context, analysis, or additional details related to the claim.

Your objective is to produce an output field `query` that defines specific, precise information retrieval queries or focused questions designed to locate key factual documents or data points that will help verify or refute the claim.

Guidelines for Constructing Queries:

1. Entity Identification and Disambiguation  
   - Extract all relevant entities mentioned in the claim (people, organizations, films, products, events, military units, etc.).  
   - Disambiguate entities especially when names might be ambiguous, or there are multiple related entities (e.g., a film sequel title, or a military regiment identification).  
   - Identify exact referenced entities, their alternate names, or common confusions if noted.

2. Extraction of Critical Attributes  
   - Determine which entity attributes are central to the claim validation (e.g., dates of birth or formation, country of origin, affiliations, roles, participation in events or productions).  
   - Include attributes relevant to comparing or establishing relationships between entities (e.g., whether two platforms share the same country of origin, or whether the actor is Welsh).

3. Precise, Targeted Information Retrieval Questions  
   - Formulate queries that explicitly seek the key facts needed to establish the truthfulness of the claim, such as:  
     • Official production or development platform origins of a video game or product.  
     • Cast lists and nationalities of actors in specific films.  
     • Dates when military units were formed or participated in events.  
     • Roles and membership in bands for specific music works.  
     • Geographic information to verify colocation or origin of companies or airports.  
     • Confirmation of the existence and correct titles of referenced sequels, entities, or productions.

4. Addressing Ambiguity, Missing Data, and Contradictions  
   - If the notes highlight ambiguous or uncertain data (e.g., unknown director, unclear entity origin), explicitly include queries to verify or clarify these.  
   - If gaps or missing information are noted (e.g., absence of confirmed date of formation), create queries that explicitly ask for those missing specifics.

5. Query Format and Compatibility  
   - Queries may be expressed as natural language questions suitable for document retrieval systems or as structured query-like requests suitable for knowledge bases (e.g., SPARQL-styled or database queries).  
   - Use clear and concise language focused on facts necessary to confirm or deny the claim.

6. Leveraging Domain-Specific Knowledge  
   - When relevant, incorporate domain-specific reasoning, such as:  
     • Military units often have formal formation dates and composition details.  
     • Film sequels commonly have known release years and directors.  
     • Nationalities of actors or companies may have multiple levels (e.g., country of birth, country of work).  
     • Specific product models may have country-of-origin data connected to manufacturers or market origins.

7. Examples of Effective Queries  
   - "What platform(s) was the game 'Proof of Destruction' developed for, and what are their countries of origin?"  
   - "Who are the Welsh actors who appeared in the 2007 sequel to the 2002 film '28 Days Later', and which of those actors also starred in 'Avengers: Age of Ultron'?"  
   - "When was the 442nd Regimental Combat Team of the U.S. Army officially formed?"  
   - "What is the nationality of Jeremy Renner, and did he star in '28 Weeks Later' and 'Avengers: Age of Ultron'?"  

By following these guidelines, your generated queries will efficiently guide retrieval of the precise documents or facts needed to assess the accuracy of the claim, addressing all relevant entities, attributes, ambiguities, and missing key information highlighted in the notes.
