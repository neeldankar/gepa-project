Task Description:
Given a claim (a statement involving one or more entities and their relationships or attributes) and a list of notes (pieces of evidence or background information extracted from relevant documents), produce a concise and focused query that can be used to verify the claim by retrieving additional evidence. 

Key Details and Domain-Specific Requirements:
1. Input Fields:
   - claim: A declarative statement that makes a factual assertion involving multiple entities, attributes, or complex relations.
   - notes: A list of textual evidence snippets or facts extracted from potentially relevant sources related to the claim.

2. Output:
   - query: A single, clear, and well-formed natural language question that:
     * Targets verification of the key factual components in the claim.
     * Incorporates relevant named entities and concepts directly related to the claim's content.
     * Reflects any ambiguous or uncertain elements in the claim that require further disambiguation or fact-checking.
     * References multiple key aspects of the claim when necessary, combining related sub-claims into a single coherent query to minimize the number of retrievals.
     * Guides retrieval toward gold documents or authoritative knowledge pieces that substantiate or refute parts of the claim.

3. Reasoning Approach (implied from examples):
   - Carefully analyze the claim to isolate all factual assertions, including:
     * Named entities (people, places, organizations, films, events).
     * Temporal or contextual qualifiers (dates, festival editions, residency shows).
     * Relationships (ownership, location, participation, awards won).
   - Compare these against the notes to determine which parts are verified, contradictory, or unknown given the provided evidence.
   - Identify missing or ambiguous information necessary for verifying the claim, especially where notes reference partial knowledge or omit key facts.
   - Formulate a query that explicitly requests these missing verification points or relationships to guide further document retrieval.
   - When multiple sub-claims exist, combine them in the query if they are tightly related (e.g., "Who won X at event Y and was also Z?"), otherwise separate them to cover distinct missing pieces.
   - The goal is to maximize retrieval of gold documents relevant to all critical facets of the claim to support precise verification.

4. Examples from provided data demonstrate:
   - Inclusion of proper names and precise entity descriptors (e.g., "The AXIS auditorium," "2005 China Open Men's Doubles," "Golden Bear at the 50th Berlin International Film Festival").
   - Highlighting inconsistent or missing information separately in the query (e.g., verifying Angelique Kerber’s nationality alongside the defending champion’s identity).
   - Avoid asking for general information; focus queries narrowly on the claim's exact unknown or challenged aspects.
   - Queries often are multi-part to cover compound claims.

Summary:
Produce a focused question that enables fact-checking of the claim by explicitly targeting the exact entities, relationships, or facts that remain uncertain or unverified after reviewing the notes. The query should be detailed enough to prompt retrieval of all missing gold-standard documents pertinent to the claim’s validation, guiding downstream evidence gathering effectively.
