Task Description:
You will be provided with a claim stated in natural language and a set of notes containing evidential or contextual statements related to that claim. Your goal is to generate a precise and targeted information retrieval query (or set of queries) designed to guide the retrieval of factual documents or data points that verify or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A natural language statement that may reference entities, their attributes, relationships, or comparisons.
- `notes`: A list of statements that analyze, support, contradict, or add context to the claim. These can include details about entities, ambiguous or missing information, or domain-specific nuances.

Output Format:
- `query`: A structured or natural language information retrieval query (or set of queries) focused specifically on the facts necessary to assess the claim’s truthfulness.

Key Objectives and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Entity Identification and Disambiguation**  
   - Extract all named entities from the claim (persons, organizations, works, events, locations).  
   - Disambiguate entities that may be ambiguous or potentially conflated (e.g., distinguishing directors from co-directors, differentiating people with similar names).  
   - Queries should verify the correct identity and roles of these entities.

2. **Attribute and Relationship Extraction**  
   - Target key factual attributes relevant to the claim, such as dates (birth, publication, tenure), titles/roles (director, president, author), affiliations (companies, bands), or geographical locations (cities, countries).  
   - When the claim involves comparisons (e.g., birth dates, roles), construct queries that facilitate precise attribute retrieval for each entity.

3. **Contextual Nuances and Contradictions**  
   - Incorporate contradictions, ambiguities, or contested points from the `notes`.  
   - If the notes identify conflicting accounts or uncertain information (e.g., disputed birthdates, unknown directors), explicitly formulate queries that address these gaps or contradictions.

4. **Explicitly Address Missing Information**  
   - The notes may highlight missing or unknown aspects critical to claim verification.  
   - Include direct queries requesting the absent data (e.g., “Is there a documented co-director for film X?” “What is the birth date of Y?”).

5. **Query Formulation Guidelines**  
   - Queries may be natural language questions or precise, structured queries suitable for knowledge bases, document retrieval systems, or structured databases (e.g., biographical SPARQL queries).  
   - When multiple facts must be cross-checked (such as linking roles across different works), ask structured multi-part queries focusing on entities’ relationships and roles.  
   - Examples of query focus:  
     - Who directed/co-directed film X and what were their specific roles?  
     - What are the birth dates of person A and person B?  
     - Is magazine Y classified as a periodical?  
     - Who first served as president of country Z in year X?  
     - What is the historical context or biographical relationship between entities M and N?

6. **Domain-Specific Knowledge to Embed**  
   Ensure queries consider the following domain-specific facts revealed by prior examples:  
   - Film direction credit structures: distinguishing between director and co-director credits.  
   - Historical and political timelines: differentiating between claims about political figures’ roles and their first terms or key events.  
   - Classification of publications: understanding that magazines are a subset of periodicals, so queries should clarify the taxonomy of the publication types in the claim.  
   - Existence verification: querying whether an entity in the claim (e.g., a director or historical figure) is attested in authoritative records.  
   - Conflicting journalistic accounts: querying identities of individuals involved, their relationships, and contradictory claims.

7. **Ensuring Completeness and Precision**  
   - The queries must comprehensively cover all factual elements needed to resolve the claim, not just a subset.  
   - They should prioritize precision to avoid overly broad or vague queries that retrieve irrelevant data.  
   - When the claim or notes imply multiple lines of inquiry (e.g., multiple persons or events), produce coordinated queries reflecting those multiple facets.

Summary:
Your role is to infer exactly what facts must be retrieved from the claim and notes, producing perfectly targeted queries that guide document or database retrieval systems toward definitive, authoritative evidence. The queries should be unambiguous, domain-aware, and precisely scoped to verify or refute the claim by capturing all relevant entities, attributes, relationships, and missing or contradictory information indicated by the notes.
