You are given two inputs: `claim` (a factual statement or assertion) and `notes` (a list of extracted facts or evidences related to the claim). Your task is to produce an informative, precise `query` (or set of queries) that can be used to search for additional relevant documents or verify the claim based on the information from the notes.

Detailed task description:
- The claim is a factual statement that may combine multiple components/entities/dates/details.
- The notes provide fragmented, partial, or supporting evidence relevant to the claim; they may confirm, deny, or provide contextual information.
- Your goal is to analyze the claim in light of the provided notes and derive one or more focused, precise questions (queries) that, if answered by external resources, would resolve the truth or falsehood of the claim or help gather missing information.
- The queries should explicitly reference key entities, relationships, and temporal details from the claim necessary to confirm or refute it.
- When the notes explicitly provide partial answers but do not cover all aspects of the claim, the query should focus on missing or doubtful elements that remain unresolved.
- Queries should neither be too broad nor too narrow; aim to capture all critical aspects of the claim that are relevant for fact checking and cross-document retrieval.
- Where the claim involves comparisons (e.g., counts, timelines, relationships between two entities), the query should explicitly request information about both sides of the comparison.
- Include entities, attributes, and temporal markers mentioned in the claim and confirmed by the notes for clarity, especially when corroborating or denying time-sensitive or relationship-based facts.
- If the claim involves uncommon or domain-specific terms (e.g., sports positions, historical timelines, discography counts), ensure these are incorporated in the query for precise retrieval.
- Your query should facilitate retrieving all gold documents necessary to fully evaluate the claim, as shown by examples where one or more gold documents remained unretrieved due to incomplete queries.

Generalizable strategy:
1. Carefully parse the claim to identify the main factual assertions.
2. Review the notes to confirm which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unaddressed.
3. Identify gaps where the notes do not provide sufficient information to fully verify the claim.
4. Formulate one or two focused questions that align with those gaps or that explicitly connect the entities and facts involved.
5. Use clear, explicit phrasing to include entity names, dates, roles, and relationships to ensure precise retrieval.
6. Avoid vague or overly generic queries that might omit critical details or result in incomplete evidence collection.

Example (simplified):  
Claim: "Person A completed training at Institution X before Institution Y was founded."  
Notes: "Person A completed training at Institution X. Institution X was founded in year M. Institution Y was founded in year N."  
Query: "Did Person A complete training at Institution X, and was Institution X founded before Institution Y?"

Your output is the `query` text only, following these principles.
