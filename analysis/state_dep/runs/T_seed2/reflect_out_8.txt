You will be given three fields as input:

- `claim`: a statement or assertion possibly involving people, events, places, or factual details  
- `notes`: a list of text snippets or factual points extracted from evidence or documents relevant to the claim, often including clarifications, corrections, or refutations of parts of the claim  
- `context`: a list of background information or document excerpts potentially supporting or refuting the claim or providing factual details about entities mentioned in the claim

Your task is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: a concise list of relevant, distilled factual points derived from the `notes` and `context` that best address the truthfulness and factual substance of the given `claim`. This should include:
   - Verification or refutation of critical details in the claim  
   - Clarifications about key entities (people, places, events) mentioned  
   - Statements that directly support or contradict the claim based on the provided information  
   - Avoid redundancy — only include distinct, useful facts that contribute to evaluating the claim

2. `titles`: a short list of titles or labels of relevant supporting documents or key topics, derived mainly from the `context`, that illuminate the major components needed to understand or verify the claim. For example, these might be document names, event names, or key subjects/entities referenced in the context.

- The context usually contains formal or structured descriptions of entities like stadiums, sports events, persons, historical roles, or organizational facts, often with dates and relationships specified.

  - Filtering out repeated or near-duplicate information appearing across the note set  
- In selecting `titles`, focus on those documents or key topics that best overlap with the claim’s main entities or events; these often directly correspond to the gold or primary documents that could verify or refute the claim.  
- The reasoning behind the outputs should leverage cross-referencing among claim, notes, and context, resolving confusions or inaccuracies highlighted in the notes.  
- You do not need to provide reasoning explicitly in the final output, but your generation of `new_notes` and `titles` must reflect sound reasoning integrating all inputs.

The overall goal is to produce a distilled, accurate, and informative summary and topical index that reflect a well-supported assessment of the claim’s validity, emphasizing domain-specific factual correctness and relevance.
