Task Description:
You will be given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, and your goal is to generate a new field called `query`, consisting of one or more precise, concise, and well-formulated fact-checking questions.

Definitions:
- `claim`: A complex factual statement making one or more assertions about people, films, awards, professions, artistic works (such as operas, board games, films), roles (director, actor, voice actor), dates (e.g., a specific year), or other domain-specific entities.
- `notes`: A collection of relevant factual information extracted from various authoritative texts or knowledge sources. These may contain confirmations, contradictions, clarifications, temporal or authorship details, or partial information related to the `claim`.

Goal:
Your goal is to break down the `claim` into a set of atomic, domain-tailored verification sub-questions in the `query` field that enable effective retrieval of missing or supporting evidence.

Requirements and Key Elements:
1. **Identify core fact(s) for verification:** Distill the essential factual elements or assertions in the claim that need fact-checking. This includes entities (people, films, games), their attributes (nationality, professions, roles), relationships (who directed or starred in a film, who composed an opera), dates, and awards.

2. **Decompose complex or compound claims:** When a claim contains multiple distinct assertions or nested facts (e.g., multiple individuals involved in different roles, several works referenced), generate separate queries targeting each atomic fact for independent verification.

3. **Incorporate contradictions and corrections from notes:** If the notes contain information that contradicts, questions, or clarifies parts of the claim (such as mistaken nationality, disputed co-direction, different composers), explicitly design queries to address those ambiguities or contradictions.  
   - Example: If the claim states a person is French but notes say they are American, ask "What is the nationality of [Person]?"  
   - If there are disputed roles: ask "Did [Person A] direct [Film]?" and "Did [Person B] have any directing role in [Film]?"

4. **Address entity disambiguation and multiple versions:** For works with multiple versions or composers (e.g., operas titled "Manon Lescaut" by different people), queries should clarify which version is being referenced and verify composer identity or performer involvement accordingly.

5. **Use exact domain-specific terminology and entity names:** Use the same names, titles, and terminology as presented or alluded to in the inputs. For example, use "Mezzo soprano Manon Lescaut" or the exact film title "The Cutting Edge" as given. Avoid vague or generalized terminology that might retrieve irrelevant documents.

6. **Ensure queries are designed to retrieve relevant authoritative documents or data:** Make questions specific enough to retrieve key pieces of evidence such as cast lists, production credits, designer portfolios, composition history, nationality verification, or award records.

7. **Explicitly formulate questions to resolve any ambiguity highlighted by the notes:** When notes reveal missing data or potential confusion, generate questions that would retrieve missing documents or authoritative clarifications.

8. **Avoid vague or overly broad questions:** Instead, focus on atomic fact verification questions such as "Did [Actor] voice [Character] in [Film]?" rather than "Who are the voice actors in the film?"

9. **If multiple distinct facts are asserted, produce multiple queries:** Break down the claim so that each question targets one fact or relationship clearly.

10. **Incorporate temporal specificity where present:** If the claim or notes mention years or historical events (e.g., 1755 revival), include these in your queries to narrow down relevant data.

Generalizable approach:
- Read the claim carefully and list out all factual assertions made (persons, titles, roles, dates).
- Review notes to identify supporting or contradicting information, and point out gaps or ambiguities.
- For each assertion, produce a tightly scoped, precise fact-checking question.
- Formulate queries that reflect contradictions or clarifications from notes explicitly.
- Ensure the query set collectively covers all core aspects of the claim for robust fact verification.

Examples of domain-specific factual focus:
- People’s professions, nationality, awards.
- Film credits: actors, voice actors, directors.
- Opera productions: performers, composers, versions, and dates.
- Board games: designers, popularity, global reach.
- Historical productions: performance years, revivals, composer timelines.

Final Output:
The new `query` field must be a well-crafted, clear set of atomic questions with domain-specific precision, suitable for retrieving authoritative documents or records to confirm or refute the entire claim, accounting for all nuances surfaced in the notes.
