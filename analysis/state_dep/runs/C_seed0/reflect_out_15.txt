Task Description:
You will receive two input fields: `claim` and `notes`, and your output must be a new field called `query`.

- The `claim` is a statement asserting one or more factual elements involving people, films, awards, professions, events, or other domain-specific entities.
- The `notes` contain a set of factual information extracted from various sources that either confirm, contradict, clarify, supplement, or are otherwise relevant to the claim.

Your goal is to generate one or more precise, clear, and well-structured interrogative queries in the `query` field that dissect the claim into atomic, verifiable factual sub-questions. These queries should be designed to retrieve authoritative documents or information that can confirm or refute each aspect of the claim.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Details:

1. **Atomic Fact Decomposition:**
   - Break down complex or compound claims into the smallest verifiable factual assertions.
   - If multiple entities or multiple temporal or relational facts are involved, create separate focused queries for each.
     - Example: For a claim about two people’s overlapping lifespans connected to a film, ask for the birth/death years of each person separately, details about the film’s creators, and then query about lifespan overlap.

2. **Identification of Core Facts:**
   - Identify all core facts embedded in the claim that require evidence, such as entity identities, roles (actor, director, lead, composer), timelines (birth/death years, release years, departure dates), events (debuts, awards), and relationships/interactions (who starred with whom, who created or contributed to which work).
   - Consider all proper nouns, titles, and domain-specific terminology exactly as presented in the input.

3. **Incorporating Notes-Based Contradictions and Confirmations:**
   - Use information in the `notes` to:
     - Recognize contradictions or corrections (e.g., disputed origins, nationality, role attribution)
     - Highlight ambiguous or conflicting facts (e.g., different director names, disputed appearance in a film)
     - Reflect confirmations that help narrow or detail queries.
   - Frame queries that explicitly cover contradicted or disputed details to gather clarifying evidence.

4. **Temporal and Sequential Reasoning:**
   - When the claim involves timing (e.g., debut before another event, leaving a group before a release), include queries verifying the dates of relevant events and entities’ involvement.
   - Example: "When did Jay Park leave the group 2PM?", "When was the DVD 'Take Off' released?", "Did Jay Park leave before this release?"

5. **Entity Role Verification:**
   - Queries should specify the role attributed to people (e.g., "Was Megan Fox the star of 'Crimes of Fashion'?", "Was Carmine Coppola a composer for 'The Godfather Part II'?").
   - Where roles are contested or unclear, ask separate queries to verify each role individually.

6. **Clarity and Precision:**
   - Avoid vague or broad questions; focus on specific documents or facts that directly address each aspect of the claim.
   - Use the exact titles, names, and domain terminology presented or implied.
   - For claims involving multiple parts, produce separate queries for each to enable independent verification.

7. **Domain-Specific Terminology and Entities:**
   - Use domain-specific terms like "film debut," "blockbuster action film," "composer," "director," "DVD release," "music group," "leader of group," "Nobel Prize laureate," etc.
   - Always include entity names, film titles, group names, or other specific terms exactly as in the claim or notes.

8. **Example Strategy Patterns (Generalizable):**
   - Identify core entities (persons, groups, films) → verify their roles and relations with the claim → verify timelines and event sequences → verify any disputed facts using notes → generate multiple precise, narrow queries accordingly.
   - When verifying co-occurrences (overlapping lifespans, co-starring roles), create separate queries for start and end dates and membership/participation in works or groups.
   - When dealing with nationality or identity contradictions, explicitly query “Who is the [nationality] star of X?” and “Is [person] of [nationality]?”

9. **Output Format:**
   - Produce a numbered or bullet-pointed list of clear, natural-language questions.
   - Ensure queries are fully understandable standalone and are optimized to retrieve relevant factual evidence.

In summary, your output `query` must be a carefully crafted set of domain-aware, atomic fact-checking questions grounded in the claim’s assertions, thoroughly addressing all points and contradictions surfaced in the notes. This will maximize the effectiveness of document retrieval and allow for accurate verification of the claim.
