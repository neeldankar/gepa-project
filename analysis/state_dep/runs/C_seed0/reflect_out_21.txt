Task Description:

You will be provided with two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to produce a new field named `query`.

- The `claim` is a factual statement or set of assertions involving people, films, awards, professions, episodes, musical works, TV shows, and other domain-specific entities or events.
- The `notes` consist of extracted relevant information that may confirm, contradict, clarify, or provide corrections or additional context about the claim. These notes often originate from multiple authoritative sources or documents, such as film festival records, actor biographies, episode guides, discographies, award histories, and broadcast schedules.

Your task is to formulate one or more precise, concise, and well-structured questions (queries) in the `query` field that break down the claim into atomic factual verifications. These queries are intended to:

1. Identify the exact factual components of the claim that require evidence or disambiguation.
2. Address each critical part or entity in the claim, especially when multiple entities or events are involved.
3. Explicitly incorporate and reflect any contradictions, corrections, ambiguities, or missing information surfaced in the `notes`.
4. Use domain-specific, precise terminology relevant to the claim (e.g., names of actors, film titles, episode titles, award names, premiere dates, programming blocks).
5. Be crafted to optimally retrieve documents or authoritative data needed to confirm or refute each aspect of the claim.

Key Guidelines and Best Practices:

- Decompose complex or compound claims involving multiple roles, events, or titles into independent verification questions.
- When temporal relationships are critical (e.g., release dates, premiere dates, award years, order of events), create queries to establish the timeline explicitly.
- When the claim involves potentially disputed attributions — such as actor participation, episode titles, or award recipients — include queries that clearly specify the role or participation in question (e.g., “Did [actor] star in [film]?”, “Was [film] awarded [prize] at [event]?”).
- Identify absence or negation in the notes and reflect them by queries that ask, for example, whether a certain event occurred at all or whether a specific association exists, to test those negative claims.
- When the claim links across different media types or works (e.g., music albums to TV episodes), queries should explicitly connect those entities and their overlap or lack thereof.
- Reflect any entities or facts mentioned multiple times or with varying degrees of confirmation separately in queries to allow independent verification.
- Avoid vague or overly broad queries; focus on information that directly confirms or falsifies elements of the original claim.
- If multiple sub-claims or entities exist, produce a list of distinct queries to cover each precisely, rather than combining unrelated parts into a general question.
- Use the exact names, titles, episode names, festival names, award titles, and other terms as they appear or as clarified in the notes, maintaining proper spelling and capitalization.

Domain-Specific Knowledge and Terminology Considerations:

- Films and TV shows: Titles, release or airing years, genres (e.g., musical, action adventure), networks or programming blocks (e.g., Showtime, KidsClick).
- Actors and performers: Full names, known roles, participation in specific films or episodes, and biographical facts such as military service (e.g., WWII U.S. Navy combat veteran).
- Awards and festivals: Specific awards (e.g., Golden Bear), precise edition or year (e.g., 50th Berlin International Film Festival), awarded films, and award criteria.
- TV episodes: Episode titles, sequence numbers, season or episode premiere dates, and whether songs or other media appeared in specific episodes.
- Music albums and songs: Album names, song titles, associations to other media (e.g., songs featured in a TV episode).
- Temporal ordering: Release dates, premiere dates, award dates, and timelines critical to the claim’s validity.
- Negative evidence or absence of association (e.g., no episode with a certain title, no record of airing on a specific network).

Examples of Effective Query Formation:

- For cross-role or cross-work checks: “Did [actor] appear in both [film A] and [film B]?”
- For award verification: “Which film won the Golden Bear at the 50th Berlin International Film Festival?”
- For episodic content and music overlap: “Which songs from [album] were featured in which episodes of [TV show]?”
- For timeline verification: “Did [actor] perform in [work X] before or after [work Y] premiered?”
- For negative or no-found evidence verification: “Is there any record of a [show or episode] titled [name] airing on [network or block]?”

Your output must be a set of targeted, evidence-seeking questions designed to retrieve or confirm relevant authoritative sources to verify every essential factual element of the `claim` in light of the `notes`. This approach maximizes retrieval of necessary documents and supports thorough, systematic claim validation.

Summary:

- Input: `claim` (complex factual assertions) and `notes` (confirmations, contradictions, clarifications).
- Output: `query` — one or more focused, precise, domain-specific questions breaking down the claim’s factual assertions.
- Goal: Facilitate discovery of documents expressly addressing the truthfulness or falsehood of each factual component of the claim using the evidence and contradictions surfaced in the notes.
