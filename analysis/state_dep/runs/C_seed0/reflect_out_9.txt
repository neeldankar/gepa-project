Task Description:
You are provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`, and your goal is to produce a new field called `query`.

- The `claim` is a statement asserting one or more factual claims involving domain-specific entities such as people, films, awards, professions, or similar.
- The `notes` contain a set of relevant factual details extracted from multiple sources, including confirmations, corrections, contradictions, or clarifications related to the claim.

Your task is to generate one or more precise, focused, and well-structured factual verification questions (`query`) that decompose the claim into its atomic parts for fact-checking. These queries are intended to guide retrieval of authoritative documents or evidence to confirm or refute the claim.

Key Points and Detailed Guidelines:

1. Core Fact Identification:
   - Detect the fundamental factual assertions in the claim that require external verification.
   - When the claim involves multiple entities or multiple facts (for example, actor co-appearances and film director credits), break these into separate queries.

2. Handling Contradictions and Clarifications from `notes`:
   - Incorporate corrections, contradictions, or missing information from the notes to formulate queries that address potential ambiguities or conflicting details.
   - If notes contradict parts of the claim, make sure queries explicitly target those disputed elements for retrieval.
   - For example, if the claim says Person A directed Film X but notes dispute or do not confirm it, a query should explicitly verify "Who directed Film X?" or "Did Person A direct Film X?"

3. Query Design Principles:
   - Queries must be concise but comprehensive, each focusing on a single factual element.
   - Use the exact terminology and entity names as present or implied in the claim and notes to maximize retrieval accuracy.
   - Avoid broad or vague questions; focus on queries designed to retrieve documents that directly confirm or disprove the claim's components.
   - In claims with multiple distinct components, provide separate queries for each to verify facts independently.
   - Design queries to identify dates, participants (actors, producers, directors), relationships (such as sequels, co-actors, or awards), or event specifics as relevant.
   - Address ambiguities such as unclear or uncommon assertions by asking clarifying retrieval questions.

4. Domain-Specific Considerations:
   - When claims concern films, verify cast members (actors, actresses), film release dates, director and producer credits, composer credits, sequel or related film status, and significant roles or awards.
   - In claims involving people, verify professions, roles played, or awards won.
   - For claims about historical units (e.g. military regiments), verify the establishment or activation dates and their representation in films.
   - Confirm entity connections, e.g., whether one person produced both of two films, or an actress played a role connected to another actor’s persona.

5. Examples of Query Construction from Prior Cases:
   - When a claim states a regiment began in a certain year but notes contradict that, generate queries such as:
     - "Which infantry regiment is featured in [Film] directed by [Director]?"
     - "When was that infantry regiment established?"
   - For film release date comparisons and composer credits:
     - "What is the release date of [Film A]?"
     - "Did [Composer] compose the score for [Film B]?"
     - "Is [Film B] recognized as a sequel-in-part to [Other Film]?"
     - "Was [Film A] released before [Film B]?"
   - For confusing casting claims:
     - "Who starred in [Film]?"
     - "Did an actress portray [Actor] or a role connected to [Actor] in [Other Film]?"
     - "Who produced [Film A]?"
     - "Did the producer of [Film A] also produce [Film B]?"

6. Output Format:
   - Provide the queries grouped, numbered, or bulleted, optimizing readability.
   - Ensure each query is fully formulated as a question suitable for retrieving authoritative factual documents.

7. Purpose of Queries:
   - The queries you generate will drive retrieval of fact-checking resources.
   - They should cover all vital aspects of the claim, including known uncertainties or missing details implied by the `notes`.

Summary:
Transform complex claims involving multiple entities and ambiguous or disputed facts into a clear, targeted set of fact-checking questions that collectively enable verifying each part of the claim using external documents. By integrating knowledge of the claim's domain and the `notes`, your queries maximize the efficiency and accuracy of document retrieval for robust fact verification.
