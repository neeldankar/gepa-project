Task Description:

You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce an output field called `query`, which contains one or more precise, atomic, and well-formed factual verification questions derived from the claim and informed by the notes.

Definitions and Context:
- The `claim` is a sentence or passage that asserts one or more factual statements involving people, films, awards, professions, publications, institutions, or other domain-related entities.
- The `notes` comprise relevant information extracted from various documents, including confirmations, contradictions, missing details, or clarifications pertaining to the claim’s assertions.
- Your output `query` should aim to generate questions that will retrieve documents or evidence enabling fact-checking of the claim's specific components.

Guidelines and Considerations:

1. Decomposition of Complex Claims:
   - Break down claims that contain multiple assertions or nested relations (e.g., multiple persons, roles, works, or events) into separate verification questions.
   - Each question should focus on a single, atomic fact to retrieve targeted evidence.

2. Coverage of Critical Claim Elements:
   - Identify and isolate every factual assertion in the claim that requires external evidence for verification.
   - Ensure all entities mentioned (e.g., persons by full name, publications, films, institutions) and their substantive roles/attributes (e.g., director, chairman, public university, award recipient) appear explicitly in the queries.

3. Reflect Contradictions, Ambiguities, and Confirmations from Notes:
   - If notes present contradictions or multiple versions of a fact (e.g., disputed director’s identity, unclear publication frequency), formulate queries that explicitly address these alternative possibilities to disambiguate.
   - Where notes confirm facts partially or identify entities only by title or nickname, your queries should clarify such ambiguities.
   - Incorporate missing but necessary questions surfaced by feedback, ensuring that all parts of the claim have corresponding queries.

4. Domain-Specific Terminology and Precision:
   - Use domain-relevant terminology consistent with the claim and notes, such as specific movie titles, award names, official institutional titles, and professional roles.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions; focus questions so they can retrieve definitive, targeted documents (e.g., "Who was the co-director of [film name]?" rather than "Who directed that film?").

5. Multi-Part Claims and Independent Verification:
   - For claims containing multiple independent assertions, generate separate queries for each independent fact.
   - Examples include distinguishing queries for each named person’s profession or award status, each institution’s classification, or each publication’s frequency.

6. Handling Implicit or Missing Details:
   - If claims use pronouns or vague references (e.g., "this publication," "its Hall of Fame"), and notes do not clarify, include queries that explicitly ask to identify or confirm what these refer to.
   - Indicate questions about timing or ranges (e.g., "Was X inducted in the early 1980s?") when such temporal precision is part of the claim.

7. Strategy for Maximizing Document Retrieval and Fact Verification:
   - Frame queries to capture every fact or nuance that would appear in authoritative sources or documents relevant to the claim.
   - Prioritize generating queries that complete missing evidence as indicated by feedback (e.g., adding queries to retrieve documents on omitted entities).
   - Formulate queries to disambiguate overlapping or potentially confusing entities mentioned in the notes and claim.
 
Summary of Expected Output:

Your `query` field must consist of multiple clear, focused, and distinct fact-checking questions addressing:

- Who, what, when, and where details for persons, films, publications, or institutions mentioned.
- Verification of specific roles, titles, or statuses referenced in the claim.
- Clarifications to resolve any ambiguous or disputed information evident from the notes.
- Checks on comparative or attribute-based claims (e.g., publication frequency).
- Temporal qualification when the claim involves dates or time periods.

Each question should be answerable by retrieving relevant documents, making the fact-checking process efficient and thorough, and should employ exact entity names and domain vocabulary as used or implied in the input.
