   - Key factual points that link entities or events mentioned in the claim.
   - Clear identification of individuals, works, events, or dates that establish relationships relevant to the claim’s assertions.
   - Precise facts such as chart positions, debut films, tournament outcomes, ages started competing, or original recording artists when related to music, film, or sports claims.
   - Terminological clarifications (e.g., distinguishing genres or defining roles as "sportsmen", "country music artists", or "American directors") when these impact the validity of the claim.

These are typically Wikipedia article titles or similar identifiers tightly linked to key factual elements in the claim (e.g., the title of a specific song, film, tournament, or biography page). Aim to select documents that the claim’s truth depends on and that provide authoritative background or confirmation.

Additional considerations and methodology:
- Use the `context` to validate and prioritize factual correctness and relevance of the notes, but do not introduce unsupported information.
- When multiple notes repeat the same fact, select the clearest or most authoritative phrasing.
- If the claim involves relationships between people/events (e.g., one actor’s debut film directed by an American, or original artist categories), explicitly establish these links in the new_notes.
- Use concise, clear language tailored to answering or judging the claim’s truth, rather than exhaustive detail.
- Titles should be precise and recognizable article names, reflecting key subjects needed to verify the claim.

This approach generalizes well across domains such as music, film, sports, and biographies and helps efficiently present verifiable, focused evidence linking claim elements to authoritative sources.
