You will be given three inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim` is a statement that may be true or false, related to specific factual knowledge.
- `notes` is a collection of relevant pieces of information extracted or inferred during research or reasoning, often summarizing or verifying aspects of the claim.
- `context` is a list of reference documents or text snippets that contain detailed factual data related to the entities, events, or topics mentioned in the claim.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: a distilled, concise set of notes that:
   - Summarizes the key facts from `notes` and `context` that directly support the analysis of the claim.
   - Removes redundancy and syntheses evidence clearly, demonstrating reasoning on whether the claim is supported or contradicted by the data.
   - Includes important details such as names, dates, relationships, and domain-specific facts relevant to validating or invalidating the claim.
   - Reflects any clarifications or external knowledge (if mentioned in notes) that aid in verifying the claim.
   
2. `titles`: a list of document or entity titles extracted from the provided `context` that are most relevant for verifying the claim and supporting the reasoning. This list should:
   - Include key entities such as people, events, films, seasons, or institutions named in the claim and found in the context.
   - Cover documents crucial for verifying the claim’s factual components.
   - Avoid including irrelevant documents and aim to retrieve all gold-standard relevant documents if applicable.
   
Additional domain-specific instructions drawn from the examples:

- Pay close attention to specific roles, such as film directors, composers, or sports competitors, including their nationalities and career timelines.
- When the claim involves numerical or ordinal data (e.g., "28th season"), carefully cross-reference the season number with dates and entities involved.
- Recognize distinctions within domains (e.g., different racing series like WRC, NASCAR, Formula Renault), film industry roles (director vs. screenwriter), and music composition roles.
- Incorporate external clarifications provided in notes when context is insufficient (e.g., knowing Todd Field directed "Little Children" even if context does not say so).
- Provide reasoning that logically connects the claim with specific facts from `context` and `notes`, and reflect the presence or absence of evidence.
- Ensure that `new_notes` and `titles` reflect the core factual basis required for claim verification, summarization and retrieval accuracy, respectively.

Generalizable strategy to follow:

- Read and understand the claim precisely.
- Analyze `notes` for relevant facts and highlight confirmations, contradictions, or gaps.
- Examine `context` documents to identify entities and facts that directly bear on the claim.
- Cross-reference names, dates, roles, and facts between claim, notes, and context.
- Synthesize `new_notes` that focus on claims supported or refuted by the data.
- Select `titles` for documents central to the claim’s verification, ensuring completeness and relevance.
