Task Description:
You will be provided with three fields as inputs: `claim` (a statement or assertion to verify or elaborate on), `notes` (a list of multiple evidential snippets or pieces of information relevant to the claim), and `context` (a list of longer or more detailed documents and descriptions, potentially containing relevant background knowledge). Your task is to analyze these inputs and produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understanding the Claim:
   - Carefully read and comprehend the claim, which may involve specific facts, dates, relationships, or events related to people, places, media, products, or other domain-specific entities.
   - Claims often involve implicit or explicit connections (e.g., sponsorship, participation in an event, temporal relations, naming conventions).

2. Analyzing Notes:
   - The `notes` field contains relevant statements extracted from various sources.
   - Identify which notes directly support or refute parts of the claim.
   - Summarize the essential factual evidence from the notes to produce a concise and focused set of `new_notes`.
   - Remove redundant or repetitive notes; keep only distinct and relevant information aligned with the claim.
   - Highlight critical domain-specific information such as dates, names, sponsorships, event participation, episode titles, production years, and relationships between entities.

3. Leveraging Context:
   - Use the `context` field’s longer documents to verify, enrich, or clarify facts referenced in the notes or claim.
   - Identify authoritative sources and titles of relevant documents (e.g., event names, album names, persons, film titles) that substantiate or contradict the claim.
   - Ensure domain-specific knowledge is captured in reasoning, such as the naming conventions of sports bowls, details about album songs and TV episodes, release years of documentaries, historical facts about individuals, or sponsorship details.

4. Producing `new_notes`:
   - Consolidate supporting evidence from the notes and context into clear, factual statements.
   - Include explicit references to key domain-specific facts (e.g., “The 2016 Liberty Bowl, played on January 2, 2016, featured the 2015 Kansas State Wildcats.”).
   - Avoid speculative language; assert only what is supported by the evidence.
   - Ensure that `new_notes` collectively support, clarify, or refute the claim in a logically coherent way.

5. Producing `titles`:
   - Identify and list the titles of the most relevant documents or topics from the `context` that directly relate to the claim or the critical entities within it.
   - Titles typically correspond to proper nouns or well-known entities such as event names, documentary or film titles, albums, episodes, people, companies, or sports teams.
   - Include all necessary titles that help verify or contextualize the claim as supported by the evidence.
   - Ensure you retrieve all gold or key documents based on domain knowledge (e.g., for a claim involving a football bowl sponsored by a Memphis company, titles should include the bowl event, the sponsor’s name, and the sports team involved).

6. Reasoning Strategy (Implicit Best Practices):
   - Correlate dates, participants, and sponsorship information across notes and context.
   - Disambiguate entities with similar names by using temporal or other contextual clues (e.g., distinguishing between two 2016 Liberty Bowl games by their play dates).
   - Be precise with episode or song titles – identify exact matches or explicitly state mismatches, as exemplified by correcting an erroneous episode name.
   - For historical claims, verify that production or event years distinctly differ where relevant.
   - Avoid recommending or listing unrelated items even if they appear in context; be selective based on relevance to the claim.

7. Style and Format:
   - `new_notes` should be a list of clear, verifiable, and factual statements derived from the inputs.
   - `titles` should be a list of concise, exact document or entity names.
   - Avoid repetition or redundancy.
   - Use domain-specific terminology with accuracy (e.g., "FBS football season," “bowl games,” “episode titles,” “documentary release years,” "sponsorship").

Summary:
You are synthesizing multiple input sources to create a distilled, factual, and concise summary (`new_notes`) and an accurate list of relevant document titles (`titles`) that verify or explain the claim. Success in this task depends on your ability to understand domain-specific content, cross-reference details, and clearly separate confirmed facts from unsupported assertions.
