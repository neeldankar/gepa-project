Task Description:
You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`, and your goal is to generate a `query` field consisting of well-formulated fact-checking questions.

- The `claim` is a complex factual statement, typically involving multiple entities such as people, films, TV series, bands, professions, awards, or other domain-specific items.
- The `notes` provide factual information, corrections, contradictions, confirmations, or clarifications extracted from various documents or knowledge sources related to the claim. These notes might include confirmations, disputes, or missing information needed to fully verify the claim.

Your task: Decompose the claim into precise, atomic, and domain-specific factual verification questions that collectively target each factual assertion or sub-assertion within the claim. These questions form the `query` used for retrieving external evidence to confirm or refute the claim.

Key Guidelines and Domain-Specific Details:

1. **Decomposition and Atomicity**  
   - Break down complex or compound claims into individual factual questions.  
   - Each question should verify a single aspect, e.g., a person's involvement in a film, a premiere date, a role played, the location a band is based in, or the authorship of a book.

2. **Cover All Assertion Components**  
   - Identify all entities, relationships, dates, roles, and attributes mentioned in the claim.  
   - Ensure each portion (e.g., names, dates, roles, locations) becomes a separate, targeted question.  
   - For multi-part claims, produce multiple queries to verify each assertion independently.

3. **Use Exact and Domain-Specific Terminology**  
   - Use the exact names, titles, and terms as presented both in the claim and notes (e.g., "Battlestar Galactica," "Gaius Baltar," "King ov Hell," "Norman Alfred William Lindsay").  
   - Include domain-relevant terminology such as "premiere date," "cast member," "author," "band member," "location," "profession," or "award."

4. **Incorporate Contradictions and Corrections from Notes**  
   - When notes show contradictions or corrections (e.g., disputed premiere dates, confusing participation years, or ambiguous person name variants), explicitly generate questions to clarify these points.  
   - Include questions that disentangle overlapping facts, such as associations of a person with multiple entities at different times (e.g., distinguishing James Callis's roles in multiple TV series).  
   - Address ambiguities such as name variants or lack of identification by querying identity equivalences or authorship.

5. **Ambiguity and Negative Evidence Handling**  
   - If the notes indicate lack of information or contradict certain aspects of the claim, formulate queries to independently verify that absence or deny the particular fact.  
   - For example, if no evidence confirms a person’s interest or participation, ask questions that target that missing information to explicitly verify it.

6. **Query Scope and Precision**  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions; focus on questions that retrieve documents directly related to the verification of the claim’s specific facts.  
   - Design queries assuming they will be used to fetch documents related to the named entities, roles, dates, or events involved.

7. **Multiple Entities and Relations**  
   - When multiple persons or entities are involved, ask separate questions about each entity’s role independently and their interrelations if relevant.  
   - For instance, for a claim mentioning two bands and a musician's membership, generate queries on which bands the musician belonged to, the location of those bands, and the geographic location context.

8. **Examples of Query Types**  
   - “When did [TV series name] featuring [actor name] originally premiere?”  
   - “Did [actor name] play the character [role] in [show/film name]?”  
   - “In what year did [actor name] join the cast of [TV show]?”  
   - “Who is the author of [book title]?”  
   - “Did [author name] enjoy or participate in boxing?”  
   - “Which bands was [musician name] a member of?”  
   - “Where are the bands [band names] based?”  
   - “Is [person A] the same as [person B]?”  

9. **General Strategy**  
   - Parse the claim carefully to extract all factual propositions.  
   - Cross-reference these propositions with notes for contradictions or confirmations.  
   - For each proposition, craft precise queries to seek definitive factual evidence.  
   - Repeat this across all facets of the claim to ensure holistic verification.

10. **Output Format**  
    - Provide the `query` as one or more clearly separate fact-checking questions.  
    - Use natural, precise language suitable for retrieving authoritative documents or entries.  
    - Avoid redundancy but ensure completeness by covering every factual aspect of the claim aided by the notes.

Summary:
Your output `query` must be a carefully constructed set of domain-specific, atomic fact-checking questions that address every claim fact and the nuances surfaced by the notes. This will optimize the retrieval of targeted evidence, enabling accurate independent verification of the claim’s truthfulness or falsehood.
