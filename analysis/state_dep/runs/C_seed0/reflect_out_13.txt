Task Description:  
You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to output a new field called `query` that contains one or more concise, precise, and well-formed fact-checking questions designed to retrieve authoritative documents or evidence for verifying the claim.

Definitions:  
- `claim`: A factual statement usually involving domain-specific entities such as people, musical artists, bands, films, awards, sports teams, professions, or other related topics. The claim may include multiple facts or nested assertions involving dates, roles, collaborations, or affiliations.  
- `notes`: A set of extracted or observed facts, clarifications, contradictions, confirmations, or contextual information relevant to the claim. These notes often include corrections about timelines, disputed roles, contradictory origins, missing referenced albums, teams, or entities, and sometimes incomplete or partial data.

Goal:  
Generate one or more focused fact-checking queries that:  
1. Identify and decompose the atomic factual assertions in the claim that need evidence verification. This includes multiple entities, roles, dates, origins, collaborations, or titles.  
2. Reflect any contradictions, corrections, or ambiguities surfaced in the `notes`, guiding the queries to explicitly address ambiguous or disputed details.  
3. Cover all significant aspects of the claim independently and collectively, ensuring no critical fact is omitted.  
4. Use exact domain-specific terminology, proper names, titles, and entity references as presented or alluded to in the provided inputs.  
5. Construct questions that are narrow enough to retrieve precise supporting or refuting documents, avoid vague or overly broad questions.  
6. When claims involve multiple factual components (e.g., roles for different entities, multiple time spans, dual affiliations), create separate queries per component to allow independent verification.  
7. Incorporate timeline verification or origin confirmation as needed, especially when timelines or entity origins are disputed or unclear.  
8. When notes highlight missing information, aim queries to retrieve those missing authoritative sources.  

Key Domain-Specific Considerations:  
- Music domain: Verify album titles, song inclusions, cover versions, recording dates, artist fame timelines, and associated record labels. Distinguish between bands vs. record labels. Confirm years of releases if timelines are disputed.  
- Sports domain: Verify founding dates of teams (including MLB and Canadian teams), player career spans with specific teams, managerial tenures of named persons, and chronology of franchises.  
- Film and entertainment: Verify actor appearances, co-direction or collaboration credits, director identity (especially when disputed).  
- Geographic/origin: Confirm country of origin for bands, companies, labels, or teams especially when contradictory evidence arises.  
- Ensure queries are capable of retrieving missing or unconfirmed facts noted in the input context (e.g., missing album mentions, disputed collaborations).  

Generalizable Strategy:  
- Begin by carefully parsing the claim to identify each distinct factual assertion.  
- Analyze the notes for contradictions, confirmations, timeline conflicts, or missing data.  
- For each atomic fact or entity involved, formulate a targeted question that directly addresses its verification.  
- Address contradictions by querying both sides explicitly (e.g., “Did [Director A] co-direct film X?” and “Did [Director B] co-direct film X?”) to disambiguate roles.  
- Include timeline or date-specific queries if the claim’s dates are questioned.  
- Separate questions about different entities or facts rather than grouping multiple facts into one broad question.  
- Use exact names and domain terms as given, e.g., exact band names, album titles, managerial roles, or geographic references.  
- Aim to cover the full scope of the claim, even if some parts are already confirmed, to facilitate thorough verification.  

Output Format:  
- Provide queries as one or more numbered or bullet-point questions.  
- Each question should be clear, concise, and answerable with factual documents or authoritative knowledge sources.  

Example (not exhaustive):  
- If the claim mentions a baseball team’s founding date and player tenure and managerial history, generate separate queries for each:  
  1) When was [team name] founded?  
  2) Did [person] play for [team] during [years]?  
  3) Did [person] manage [team] during [years]?  

- If the claim involves a music artist and an album with a specific cover song and fame timeline, generate queries:  
  1) Which studio album by [artist] includes a cover of “[song title]”?  
  2) Did [artist] record “[song title]” on [album]?  
  3) When did [artist] first gain fame?  

- For claims mixing facts about collaborations, origins, and labels in music or bands:  
  1) Which Scottish punk band worked with [label] for [period]?  
  2) Are [labels or bands] based in [countries]?  
  3) Did the band work simultaneously with [labels]?  

By following these instructions, your queries will precisely target the critical evidentiary facts needed to verify or refute the claims, will accommodate and resolve contradictions arising from notes, and support retrieval of all necessary authoritative documents to perform a comprehensive fact-check.
