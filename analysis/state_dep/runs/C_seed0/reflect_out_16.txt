Task Description:   
You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. You must generate a new field called `query` containing one or more precise factual questions designed to verify all key factual assertions made in the claim by retrieving authoritative evidence or documents from the notes or external sources.

Input Fields:  
- `claim`: A statement making one or more factual assertions involving domain-specific entities such as people, films, awards, professions, or other related entities in fields like entertainment, sports, literature, or history. Claims often combine multiple facts (e.g., roles, co-authorship, professions, achievements, nationality).  
- `notes`: A collection of extracted factual information, clarifications, confirmations, contradictions, and corrections related to the claim, commonly derived from known documents or knowledge bases. These notes may affirm or dispute parts of the claim or leave gaps needing further verification.

Output Field:  
- `query`: One or more concise, well-formulated, and domain-specific factual questions that precisely decompose the claim into atomic sub-questions for fact checking. The queries must directly target key claim facts and any contradictions or ambiguities surfaced in the notes, so as to retrieve exact documentary evidence confirming or refuting each claim component.

Key Instructional Details and Guidance:  

1. **Identify and Decompose Core Assertions**  
   - Break down the claim into all atomic factual assertions needing verification (e.g., identities, roles, collaborations, awards, nationalities).  
   - If multiple entities or sub-claims exist, produce separate questions for each fact.  
   - Ensure questions are factually specific and unambiguous. Avoid vague or broad queries.

2. **Leverage Notes to Guide Query Focus**  
   - Use confirmations, contradictions, ambiguities, or gaps in the notes to pinpoint which parts of the claim require explicit verification.  
   - If notes show conflicting information (e.g., differing director names or disputed professions), create queries that explicitly target these conflicts to clarify the truth.  
   - Address distinct mentions of entities with positive vs negative assertions separately, formulating queries that disambiguate roles or relationships.

3. **Domain-Specific Precision**  
   - Use exact entity names, film titles, person names, roles, professions, awards, or domain-specific terminology as presented or implied by the input data.  
   - Questions should be framed in natural, formal language appropriate for domain fact retrieval (e.g., “Who is the lead vocalist of Relient K?”, “Did Jim Thorpe coach the Oorang Indians?”, “Is Jeremy Renner American or Welsh?”).

4. **Capture Multiple Fact Dimensions**  
   - The claim may involve intertwined facts about people, works, dates, collaborations, or achievements.  
   - Generate queries separately for each factual dimension (e.g., who co-wrote a song, who directed a film, what nationality an actor holds, who coached a sports team).  
   - When a claim involves multiple people’s professions or statuses, produce independent queries for each person’s fact.

5. **Clarify Ambiguities and Roles**  
   - When a role or title is ambiguous or disputed in notes (e.g., “trainer” vs “athlete”), formulate questions targeting the accurate identity and role of the person involved.  
   - Ask direct questions about identities, attributions, and relationships stemming from claim ambiguities or contradictions surfaced in the notes.

6. **Enable Document Retrieval for Verification**  
   - Create queries specifically designed to retrieve documents or knowledge base entries that will directly confirm or refute each claim component.  
   - Avoid overly general or vague formulations that might return irrelevant information.

Example Strategies (based on analyzed assistant outputs and feedback):  
- Queries aimed at confirming who held particular roles (e.g., lead vocalist, director, coach).  
- Queries targeted at confirming authorship or co-authorship of creative works (songs, films).  
- Queries that ask about the profession or nationality of named individuals.  
- Queries disentangling overlapping or nested roles in claims involving multiple people or entities.  
- Queries clarifying the existence or absence of specific facts when notes indicate contradictions or missing evidence.

Summary:  
Your output `query` must be a well-crafted, fact-focused set of questions that:  
- Covers all factual claims and entities present in the input `claim`.  
- Explicitly addresses any contradictions or gaps surfaced by the `notes`.  
- Uses domain-specific language and exact names/titles.  
- Is decomposed into multiple precise fact-checking questions if needed.  
- Is designed to guide the retrieval of precise evidence for claim validation.  
- Avoids vagueness and mixes questions only when clearly related.

This approach maximizes the effectiveness of document retrieval and enables comprehensive, reliable fact verification for complex multi-entity claims.
