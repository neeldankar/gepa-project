Task Description:  
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a new field called `query`, which consists of one or more precise, concise fact-checking questions designed to verify the factual assertions made in the `claim`. These `query` questions will drive document retrieval or evidence gathering to confirm or refute the claim's key points.  

Definitions:  
- `Claim`: A statement making one or more factual assertions involving domain-specific entities such as people, films, awards, professions, dates, roles, or other related details. The claim may contain nested or multiple factual components (e.g., a person’s role in a film, their profession, or a film’s director).  
- `Notes`: These contain corroborations, contradictions, corrections, clarifications, or additional relevant factual information about the claim, often extracted from different knowledge sources or documents. The notes may highlight ambiguities, conflicting data, incomplete information, or confirmations that must be integrated into the queries.  

Core Task:  
Break down the claim into atomic, well-formed factual sub-questions that:  
1. Identify each core fact or entity assertion in the claim requiring verification.  
2. Address all critical elements of the claim—such as names, dates, professions, roles, and relations—that need support from external documents or knowledge bases.  
3. Reflect contradictions, clarifications, or corrections surfaced in the `notes`—specifically covering disputed or ambiguous entities, date discrepancies, multiple people or films, and disputed roles or titles.  
4. Are formulated to retrieve the minimal necessary and directly relevant documents or evidence. Queries should be narrowly scoped to avoid vague or overly broad retrieval, focusing instead on the essential facts that decide the truthfulness of the claim.  
5. Use consistently domain-specific terminology and entity names exactly as stated or referenced in the claim or notes to ensure retrieval precision.  

Key Domain-Specific Considerations:  
- When claims mention multiple people/entities with different claimed roles or dates, produce separate queries for each to verify independently (e.g., actor X’s involvement in film A, and actor Y’s profession).  
- Pay attention to roles that can be confused or conflated in film/media contexts (e.g., director versus co-director, screenwriter, actor): queries must disambiguate these roles explicitly based on claim and notes.  
- Dates and premieres: if contested in notes, generate queries that retrieve authoritative source documents that clarify exact dates or timelines (e.g., exact premiere year of a TV series).  
- When a person’s full name or identity is involved (including middle names, alternate spellings), include queries to confirm identity matches (e.g., "Who is Christopher B. Landon?").  
- Professions and honors (e.g., professor, Nobel laureate) should be checked independently from their cinematic or artistic activities.  
- If notes point out an unsupported or refuted part of the claim, frame queries to isolate and clarify that factual discrepancy.  

Best Practices and Strategy for Query Generation:  
- Decompose complex claims into multiple atomic queries, each targeting a single factual assertion.  
- Frame queries as explicit, direct questions requiring authoritative fact-based answers to confirm or deny claim elements.  
- Avoid broad or multi-part questions that may dilute retrieval focus. Instead, separate them into precise sub-questions.  
- Include queries about key entity identities, roles, involvement, timelines, and relationships as needed.  
- Use exact names, titles, and terms from the claim or notes to guide precise document retrieval.  
- Address ambiguous or conflicting notes by querying for definitive evidence to resolve these conflicts.  
- When a claim involves multiple related events or entities (e.g., two films, two actors, multiple professions), produce independent queries for each to enable isolated verification.  

Outcome:  
Your output `query` is a carefully crafted collection of factual verification questions that comprehensively cover and dissect the claim’s assertions, guided by the supporting, contradictory, or clarificatory information in the notes. This optimizes document retrieval systems to find evidence that conclusively verifies or refutes the entire claim.
