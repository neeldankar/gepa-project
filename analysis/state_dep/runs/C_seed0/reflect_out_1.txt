Task Description:
You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single output field: `query`.

1. **Input Fields**:  
   - **claim**: A complex factual statement or set of statements that often involve multiple entities, events, or relationships. Claims may contain conjunctions or multiple propositions.  
   - **notes**: A list of factual snippets or document excerpts that relate to the entities and claims in the `claim` field. These snippets come from diverse sources and contain domain-specific, historical, scientific, or cultural facts. The notes enable verifying or refuting parts of the claim.

2. **Output Field**:  
   - **query**: A well-formed, precise, and comprehensive question or set of questions that, when answered with relevant information from external sources or knowledge bases, would allow verification or refutation of the original claim. This query should explicitly cover all components of the claim, often requiring a multi-part or compound question.

3. **Detailed Requirements and Domain-Specific Knowledge**:  
   - Claims often reference literary or cinematic works, biological taxonomy, prize laureates (e.g., Nobel Prize), familial relationships of public figures (actors, directors), and factual specifics like species counts or awards won.  
   - Common domains include: 
     - Literature (e.g., screenplay authorship, Nobel Prize winners),
     - Film (e.g., actors, relation of actors to films and years, familial ties between actors),
     - Botany (e.g., genus species counts, common and scientific names),
     - Awards and prizes (specific to domain, e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature).
   - The query must reflect the nuanced information needed to check the claim, for example: 
     - Identifying individuals linked to works or events by roles or relationships,
     - Verifying dates and factual relationships (such as family members and their filmographies),
     - Comparing quantitative facts (e.g., number of species in plant genera).

4. **Generalizable Strategy**:  
   - Parse the claim into its constituent factual assertions.  
   - Determine the key entities and relationships involved (e.g., people, works, events, names, awards).  
   - Cross-reference these with the given notes to understand what evidence pertains to which part of the claim.  
   - Formulate one or more precise natural language questions that together cover every aspect of the claim, enabling a seeker to retrieve or verify all necessary supporting or contradicting evidence.  
   - Ensure clarity and specificity; avoid ambiguity by explicitly naming entities and clarifying relationships and time frames.

5. **Additional Guidance**:  
   - When multiple claims or subclaims are intertwined, split the query logically but keep it connected to facilitate comprehensive verification.  
   - Use proper names, dates, titles, and domain-specific terminology as found in the notes and claim to ensure retrieval systems correctly identify relevant documents.  
   - The query should be answerable via factual information and not rely on subjective interpretation.

Summary:
Given `claim` and `notes`, generate a clear, focused, and comprehensive `query` (or queries) that enables thorough fact-checking of the claim through factual information retrieval, explicitly covering all persons, events, dates, relationships, and quantitative data involved.
