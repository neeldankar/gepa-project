You will be given three input fields: 
- `claim`: a factual or inferential statement involving people, entities, events, or concepts.
- `notes`: a list of supporting or related facts extracted from various documents, which may contain some redundancy.
- `context`: a list of document summaries or excerpts from relevant Wikipedia (or similar) articles that provide background and detailed information pertinent to the claim and notes.

Your task is to analyze the inputs and produce two output fields: 
- `new_notes`: a concise, non-redundant, and relevant selection of notes that directly support or are essential to verify or clarify the claim. These should be distilled from the original `notes` list, avoiding repetitive or tangential information.
- `titles`: a list of document or article titles corresponding to the key entities, events, or concepts needed to substantiate or understand the claim as supported by the selected notes. The titles should come predominantly from the `context` field or otherwise relevant sources referenced in the notes, ensuring coverage of the main subjects relevant to the claim.

Detailed considerations and methodology for the task:
1. **Understanding the Claim and Entity Relationships**  
   Carefully parse and comprehend the claim, identifying all entities, their relationships, events, or temporal aspects mentioned.

2. **Mapping Notes to the Claim**  
   Extract from the provided notes the facts that directly support the claim’s components (e.g., confirming an entity’s profession, origin date, collaborations). Avoid including redundant or only tangential notes.

3. **Understanding Domain-Specific Details**  
   Incorporate domain-specific knowledge exposed in the notes and context that might affect interpretation, such as:
   - Music genres and their hierarchical relationships (e.g., indie rock as a subgenre of alternative rock under the broader rock music category).
   - Military unit structures and histories, especially relevant dates of unit formation, merger, and combat actions.
   - Academic and professional factual alignments, such as identifying professions tied to individuals (e.g., paleontologists vs. writers).
   - Specific historical, biographical, or categorical details needed to confirm or nuance the claim.

4. **Selecting Titles**  
   Identify the minimal set of article titles that cover:
   - The main entities or collaborations referenced in the claim.
   - The events or concepts relevant to understanding or verifying the claim.
   - Any contextual background necessary to situate or interpret the facts (e.g., the film name for military units, or general genre pages in music).

5. **Avoiding Redundancy and Gaps**  
   The new notes should not repeat the same fact multiple times; instead, consolidate similar facts into a clear statement set. The titles list should cover all major subjects implied or required by the claim and notes.

6. **Reasoning Transparency**  
   While you do not need to output reasoning, ensure that the notes and titles logically support the claim, and the titles correspond to key documents that can be checked for further verification.

This task is akin to preparing an evidence synthesis and document retrieval summary relevant to a specific factual claim, focusing on completeness, relevance, and precision of supporting material.
