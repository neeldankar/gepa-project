Task Description:
You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a new field called `query`.

- The `claim` is a statement that makes one or more factual assertions involving people, films, awards, professions, or other related domain-specific entities.
- The `notes` contain a collection of relevant factual information, corrections, contradictions, confirmations, or clarifications related to the claim, often extracted from various documents or knowledge sources.
  
Your goal is to generate one or more concise, precise, and well-formulated questions in the `query` field that break down the claim into atomic factual verification sub-questions. These queries should:
1. Identify the core fact(s) being asserted in the claim that need verification.
2. Reflect all critical parts of the claim that require evidence from external sources or the knowledge base.
3. Address any ambiguity, contradictory details, or multiple entities involved in the claim as surfaced by the `notes`.
4. Be designed to retrieve the necessary documents or pieces of evidence that can confirm or refute the claim.
5. Use domain-specific terminology and relevant entity names exactly as presented or alluded to in the input data.

Guidelines and Key Points:
- Focus on decomposing complex claims involving multiple entities or nested relations (e.g., who starred with whom, who directed which film).
- Incorporate correction or contradiction information from `notes` to guide the construction of queries that explicitly cover those aspects (e.g., when a director’s name is disputed, ask about the director’s identity).
- Ensure your queries cover all parts of the claim, especially when multiple factual assertions are made (e.g., listing all relevant persons involved, cross-referencing their roles).
- Use precise and natural language questions that are answerable by retrieving factual documents or authoritative sources.
- Consider queries that will retrieve missing documents not yet obtained if feedback is provided.
- When entities are mentioned both positively and negatively in the notes (confirmed or denied roles), reflect these distinctions in the queries to disambiguate.
- Avoid broad vague questions; focus on retrieval of documents or facts that directly address the truthfulness or falsehood of the claim's components.
- If a claim involves multiple parts (e.g., two different people’s statuses or achievements), produce queries that can verify each part independently.

Example of reasoning behind queries:
- A claim about an actor appearing in two films and one film’s director is challenged by notes showing different directors and no evidence the actor appeared in the second film -> queries should ask precisely "Who starred in film X alongside [actor]?" and "Who directed film Y?" and "Did [actor] appear in film Y?"
- A claim about co-direction between two films referencing specific directors, where notes provide details confirming or denying involvement, should produce queries about "Did [director] co-direct film A?" and "Did [director] co-direct film B?" to clarify overlaps.
- A claim involving multiple people’s professions or awards (e.g., an Austrian poet-diplomat Nobel laureate and a noted writer) requires queries checking "Are there any Austrian poet-diplomats who won Nobel Prize in Literature?" and "Is [person] a writer?" separately to verify each fact.

In summary, your output `query` must be a well-crafted set of fact-checking questions grounded in the claim’s assertions and the contradictions, confirmations, or omissions presented in the notes. This will maximize document retrieval effectiveness and enable thorough claim verification.
