Task Description:  
You will receive two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a new field called `query`, which consists of one or more carefully formulated fact-checking questions. These questions serve to decompose the factual assertions in the claim into atomic sub-questions that target verification from authoritative external sources, documents, or knowledge bases.

Definitions:  
- **Claim**: A statement containing one or more factual assertions about people, films, awards, professions, or other domain-specific entities and their relationships.  
- **Notes**: A set of extracted facts, corrections, contradictions, confirmations, or clarifications relevant to the claim, derived from various sources or knowledge. They may confirm, refute, or leave gaps about the claim’s components.

Your Goal:  
Produce a precise set of queries that:  
1. Identify the exact factual elements the claim asserts, breaking down complex, nested, or compound claims into simple verifiable assertions.  
2. Incorporate all critical parts of the claim — including all entities involved, their roles, relationships, or characteristics — to enable comprehensive document retrieval and verification.  
3. Explicitly cover ambiguities, contradictions, or multiple entity references that appear in the `notes`, ensuring that conflicting information is factored into query formulation.  
4. Use the exact domain-specific terminology and entity names from the claim and notes to maintain precision and relevance.  
5. Separate the claim into multiple queries if it contains multiple independent factual components (e.g., a person’s birthdate and a film director’s identity).  
6. Avoid vague or overly broad questions; every query should be focused on retrieving documents or data that directly address a specific factual assertion.  
7. When notes indicate missing evidence or unresolved contradictions, include queries aimed at locating documents or facts that fill those gaps.

Key Domain-Specific Details and Strategies:  

- **Domain Entities and Relations**: Your queries should explicitly address named people (e.g., actors, politicians), films (including titles and release years), awards (e.g., Golden Bear), professions, dates (e.g., birthdates, leadership succession dates), character names, and organizational affiliations or relationships (e.g., party affiliation of political youth groups).  
- **Nested and Compound Claims**: Many claims include multiple factual assertions bundled together (e.g., a political leader’s birthdate plus the exact date and party leadership succession plus organizational affiliations). Your queries should break these into focused questions to check each fact independently.  
- **Use of Dates and Numbers**: When the claim includes specific dates or numeric values (e.g., percentages of votes), queries should explicitly mention them to verify accuracy.  
- **Handling Contradictions in Notes**: When notes provide conflicting information (e.g., differing directors attributed to a film), formulate queries to explicitly clarify these contradictions.  
- **Precise Naming and Definitions**: Use official names of awards, political parties, film titles (including alternate titles or years), and characters exactly as presented, to avoid ambiguity in retrieval.  
- **Separating Confirmed Vs. Unconfirmed Roles**: When notes confirm one aspect but deny or lack evidence for another (e.g., an actor confirmed in one film but unconfirmed in another), create queries that explicitly check each role or appearance independently.  
- **Extraction and Verification Goals**: Each query should aim to find source documents, authoritative records, or verifiable databases that can confirm or deny the factual element clearly.  

Example Query Formulations:  
- “Who won the Golden Bear Award at the 50th Berlin International Film Festival?”  
- “Is the film ‘One Hundred and One Dalmatians’ (1961) classified as an American family comedy action adventure movie?”  
- “Did Jagmeet Singh, born on January 2, 1979, become leader of the federal New Democratic Party on October 1st, and is this party affiliated with the Ontario New Democratic Youth?”  
- “Which actors in the 50th Berlin International Film Festival’s winner served as United States Navy Combat veterans during World War II?”  
- “Is the character Cruella de Vil present as the main antagonist in Disney’s animated ‘One Hundred and One Dalmatians’?”

Output Format:  
Provide the queries as a clear list of well-formed, domain-precise questions. Numbering queries is optional but recommended for clarity.  

Summary:  
Your output `query` field will be a highly focused, systematically deconstructed set of verification questions that correspond directly to all meaningful factual claims and contradictions surfaced in the input. This will enable efficient retrieval of grounding evidence and robust fact-checking.

By following these guidelines, you will generate high-quality fact-check queries that precisely target all critical facets of the claim, fully leveraging the information and contradictions present in the notes.
