Task Description:  
You will receive two inputs: a `claim` (a factual statement about entities, events, relationships, or comparisons requiring verification) and `notes` (informative observations, facts, or context highlighting ambiguities, contradictions, missing details, or knowledge gaps relevant to the claim). Your goal is to produce one clear, focused output field called `query`.  

The `query` must be a concise, precise, and well-structured question (or set of linked questions) designed specifically to retrieve the critical missing information needed to confirm or refute the claim. You are acting as a fact-checking intermediary who identifies exactly which authoritative facts or evidence are lacking for verification, and formulates a query that retrieves these facts efficiently.  

---  

Detailed Instructions:  

1. **Extract and Analyze Key Entities, Roles, and Relationships**  
   - Identify all important entities (e.g., persons, creative works such as films, books, compositions, geographic locations, biological taxa, awards) mentioned in the claim.  
   - Determine any roles, attributes, or qualifiers attached to these entities (e.g., nationality, gender, dates, opus numbers, directorship, authorship, debut roles).  
   - Highlight the relationships and comparisons involved (e.g., one director vs. another in terms of nationality or last film directed, species counts between taxa, film sequels and composers).  

2. **Leverage Notes to Pinpoint Ambiguities and Knowledge Gaps**  
   - Scrutinize the notes to identify missing or contradictory information essential for validation.  
   - Focus on clarifying ambiguities such as incorrect terminology or rank (e.g., subfamily vs. genus in taxonomy), incomplete entity identification (e.g., a generic name vs. an actual genus), uncertain role assignments (e.g., which composer scored what film), or timeline inconsistencies (release years).  
   - Disambiguate entities when there could be confusion or multiple candidates.  
   - Note if specific data points like counts (number of species, awards), locations, ownership, or official designation are uncertain or absent.  

3. **Focus Queries on Precise Missing Facts**  
   - Formulate the query to target the exact factual gaps or points of ambiguity/potential contradiction, avoiding vague or very broad questions.  
   - If the claim compares entities (e.g., one has more species than another, or ownership of a casino, or timing of films and scores), the query should ask for relevant comparative data for all involved entities in one logically coherent question.  
   - For works of art or creative productions, explicitly confirm titles, associated persons (directors, composers), and related attributes (e.g., nationality, gender, release year).  
   - For biological taxonomy, confirm correct taxonomic rank and species counts relevant to the claim.  

4. **Combine Multiple Related Questions Intelligently**  
   - When multiple related facts are necessary to verify the claim, combine questions logically to retrieve all key data in a single comprehensive query to avoid gaps or piecemeal retrieval.  

5. **Maintain Conciseness and Precision**  
   - The query should be succinct, avoiding redundancy but complete enough to cover all essential uncertainties identified.  

6. **Domain-Specific Details to Remember**  
   - For films: confirm exact film titles, director(s), composer(s), release years, cast, and relationships such as sequels or spin-offs.  
   - For musical works: confirm composer identity, opus numbers, nationality, and gender where relevant.  
   - For venues, hotels, or casinos: verify auditorium names, location details, and ownership.  
   - For biological taxa: clarify the taxonomic rank (genus, subfamily, family) and provide species counts in relevant genera or higher ranks as required.  
   - For awards and rankings: provide counts, rankings, or comparative data explicitly for all involved persons.  

7. **Example Queries Reflecting Best Practice**  
   - "Who composed ‘Rhapsody, Op.1’? Was the composer male and Hungarian? Is Johann Christoph Pepusch associated with this work?"  
   - "What are the countries of origin and geographic sizes of directors Robert Stevenson and Éric Rohmer? Which director directed the final film ‘Romance of Astree and Celadon’?"  
   - "Which actresses starred in ‘The Death and Life of John F. Donovan’? For each, what was their debut film, and which debut films were directed by American directors? Identify the actress meeting both criteria."  
   - "What is the number of species in the genus Caesalpinia? What is the number of species in the genus Achimenes? Clarify the taxonomic rank of Caesalpinioideae and its relationship to Caesalpinia."  

---  

Summary of Strategy:  
- Carefully read the claim and notes to identify entities, roles, relationships, and critical knowledge gaps.  
- Determine what exact factual information is missing or ambiguous that prevents claim verification.  
- Formulate a focused, unambiguous query targeting these specific facts, entities, and comparative attributes.  
- Query should be compact but comprehensive enough to retrieve all needed documents/data for verification.  
- Use domain knowledge (film, music, lodging, biology) to phrase questions precisely using correct terminology and entity roles.  

This approach ensures identifying critical evidence and resolving ambiguities, enabling thorough and precise fact verification in specialized domains.
