You will be given three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A factual or comparative statement involving entities such as people, events, organizations, or items, often requiring verification of dates, relationships, sponsorships, membership counts, or other specific attributes.
- `notes`: A list of textual facts, snippets, or assertions relevant to the claim, often overlapping or repeating, drawn from various sources.
- `context`: Extended background information or documents related to the claim, typically composed of encyclopedic-style or detailed descriptions, including historical dates, biographies, event details, and factual data about involved entities.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: A reduced, distilled list of notes that are factual, relevant, and directly support or refute the `claim`. The selection should clarify key points necessary for assessing the claim’s truthfulness or factual basis. Avoid redundant or unrelated information; synthesize commonly repeated facts into concise notes where possible.

2. `titles`: A set of document or article titles from the `context` that are most relevant to the claim. These should cover all core entities or concepts mentioned in the claim or necessary to understand or verify it. Titles should reflect precise focus (e.g., an individual’s biography, a specific event, a song, or an organization) to help disambiguate the claim and ground the relevant facts.

**Detailed task understanding and domain specifics:**

- Claims often concern dates (birthdates, event occurrences), identities (directors, band members), sponsorship relations (companies sponsoring events), scope (membership count comparisons), or existence/identity confirmation (whether a person/entity with a claimed attribute exists).
- Verification may depend on temporal context (e.g., the year a sports team played in a bowl game) or nationality/provenance (e.g., director nationality).
- Frequently, claims involve entities with similar or overlapping names, so precise disambiguation is critical.
- Notes may contain contradictory or insufficient information; the assistant must highlight uncertainty transparently if definitive verification is not possible.
- The output reasoning should condense evidence logically and factually but is not part of your output here; however, the `new_notes` should reflect sound reasoning by inclusion of evidence supporting or refuting the claim.
- Titles should collectively cover all relevant facets of the claim, even if some titles are only tangentially related but necessary to fully understand or verify the claim.

**Generalizable strategy:**

- First, identify key factual elements in the claim (entities, dates, attributes).
- Filter through `notes` to select concise, relevant facts that directly address those elements.
- Identify `context` documents whose titles correspond to those key elements for precise, disambiguated grounding.
- Avoid including irrelevant titles even if they appear in `context`.
- Maintain clarity about the certainty level—exclude notes that do not contribute clear evidence or indicate ambiguity when needed.

This approach ensures a focused evidence set and precise contextual grounding for fact-checking or validation tasks involving complex real-world factual claims.
