Task Description:  
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to generate one focused output field called `query`. The `query` should be a precisely formulated question (or logically combined questions) designed to elicit exactly the information missing, ambiguous, or contradictory as revealed by the `notes`—information critical to verifying or refuting the `claim`. 

---

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Identify Core Entities, Attributes, and Relationships from the Claim**  
   - Extract every key entity mentioned (people, bands, books, songs, films, awards, places, etc.).  
   - Identify roles, attributes, or classifications associated with each entity as described or implied (e.g., composer nationality/gender, director’s country of origin, artists’ debut films, song chart positions, genre classification).  
   - Note relationships or comparisons between entities (collaborations, original vs. cover recordings, ranking comparisons, debut status, etc.).

2. **Use Notes to Pinpoint Ambiguities, Missing Facts, or Conflicts**  
   - The `notes` contain critical knowledge gaps, contradictions, or incomplete information essential for claim validation.  
   - Carefully analyze these notes to determine what precise fact(s) the claim depends on but remain unclear or unverified.  
   - Look for missing confirmations of genre, nationality, chart performance, role attribution, collaboration linkage, or identity disambiguation.  
   - Identify whether the notes imply multiple entities/entities with similar names needing disambiguation.

3. **Formulate a Focused, Domain-Specific Query That Resolves Gaps**  
   - Explicitly request the missing facts needed for claim verification (e.g., names of collaborators, original artists, chart positions, origins of directors, nationality/gender of composers).  
   - If the claim involves comparative or relational assertions, ensure the query seeks the relevant attributes for *all* entities involved in the comparison.  
   - Be sure to include specific titles, dates, chart names, or other identifiers as found in claims or notes to target relevant knowledge. This precision is key in music, film, literature, and biographical data domains.

4. **Important Domain-Specific and Contextual Considerations**  
   - **Music domain**: Confirm chart performance (e.g., Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks chart positions), original recording artists vs. cover artists, genre classification of bands/artists, and songwriter information.  
   - **Film domain**: Confirm actors’ debut films, director nationality and filmography (including final films directed), co-director recognition, and cross-national productions.  
   - **Literature domain**: Distinguish authors and notable works accurately, check for personal interests or biographical details relevant to the claim (e.g., hobby like boxing).  
   - Always clarify identity when similar names could be confused (e.g., multiple people named Frank Sinatra).
   - For claims about preferences/attitudes (e.g., enjoying boxing), explicitly query documented evidence or absence thereof.

5. **Construct the Query to be Concise yet Complete**  
   - Avoid vague phrasing or overly broad questions; target precisely the knowledge gaps or contradictions uncovered.  
   - When multiple linked facts are needed to resolve the claim fully, combine questions logically with connectors such as "and," "if so," or "for each identified entity."  
   - Prioritize clarity and domain-specific precision in wording to maximize retrieval of authoritative evidence.

6. **Generalizable Strategy Recap**  
   - From the `claim` and `notes`, find the exact missing or uncertain fact(s) preventing verification.  
   - Frame the query selectively to request *only* those key facts about *all* relevant entities and relationships in the claim, respecting contextual domain specifics.  
   - Disambiguate entities when multiple possibilities exist by referencing contextual attributes (nationality, titles, dates, roles).  
   - For comparative claims, explicitly query all entities being compared on the attributes involved.  
   - Ensure that the query will call for retrieval of relevant documents, covering complete evidence necessary for comprehensive claim assessment.

---

Examples to illustrate:  

- **Music claim with ambiguity on composer and nationality**:  
  *Who composed "Rhapsody, Op. 1"? Was the composer male and Hungarian? Is Johann Christoph Pepusch associated with this work?*

- **Film claim comparing directors’ origins and final directed film**:  
  *What are the countries of origin and geographic sizes of directors Robert Stevenson and Éric Rohmer? Which director directed the final film "Romance of Astree and Celadon"?*

- **Actress debut and casting claim**:  
  *Which actresses starred in "The Death and Life of John F. Donovan"? For each, what was their debut film, and which debut films were directed by American directors? Identify the actress fitting both criteria.*

- **Music chart performance and original artist claim**:  
  *Did any song recorded by Kathy Mattea reach #1 on the Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks chart? If so, which song(s)? For each, who was the original recording artist, and was that artist a country music singer?*

- **Biographical interest claim**:  
  *What evidence exists about Norman Alfred Norman Lindsay’s interest or enjoyment of boxing? Additionally, what is known about Amy Tan’s (author of The Hundred Secret Senses) attitude or interest in boxing?*

---

By adhering to these instructions, you will craft highly targeted queries that efficiently seek authoritative data to address key gaps or uncertainties, enabling accurate verification or refutation of complex fact-based claims across music, film, literature, and biographical domains.
