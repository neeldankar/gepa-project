Task Description:  
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single output field called `query`.  

- The `claim` is a factual statement that may involve multiple entities, events, or comparisons.  
- The `notes` are observations and extracted information relevant to the claim. These notes often highlight missing data, ambiguities, or context needed to verify or assess the claim. They may also reference specific entities, works (e.g., films, books, bands), or people relevant to the claim.  

Your primary job is to generate a clear, focused, and precise `query` that will retrieve the necessary evidence or sources to verify the claim. The query should:  

1. Identify the key entities, roles, or relationships in the claim that require further information to confirm or refute.  
2. Address ambiguities noted in the `notes` — for example, unclear identities, missing award counts, missing cast information, or unclear film adaptation connections.  
3. Include comparative elements if the claim involves comparisons (e.g., number of awards won by two persons).  
4. Target the retrieval of documents or information that will help establish the truthfulness of the claim by filling knowledge gaps noted in the `notes`.  
5. Avoid redundant or overly broad questions; focus on the missing critical pieces of information needed for verification.  

Niche or domain-specific information to consider:  
- When the claim involves titles (films, books, bands), ensure these are explicitly mentioned in the query if relevant.  
- When multiple persons could fit a role (e.g., "German Frank Sinatra" could refer to multiple people), the query should seek disambiguation.  
- When claims involve connecting roles (e.g., an actor who starred in one film also portraying a character in another), the query should retrieve actor names, roles, and film adaptation connections.  
- When verifying director roles or similar, queries should explicitly ask for a person's involvement and their professional recognition.  
- Data such as award counts or comparisons require explicit delineation in the query to retrieve statistics or lists.  

Generalizable Strategy:  
- Use the `notes` to identify knowledge gaps or ambiguities critical to assessing the claim.  
- Construct queries that directly request the missing key information, including names, roles, relationships, or data points needed for verification.  
- Incorporate both entities and relationships or attributes mentioned in the claim to produce effective retrieval queries.  
- Keep the query concise but sufficiently descriptive to retrieve documents that can fully support or challenge the claim.  
- When multiple key entities appear, the query should connect them logically in the question (e.g., by comparison or by their joint involvement).  

Example approach:  
If the claim compares award wins between two ambiguous figures, the query should identify the figures clearly and seek award counts for each.  
If the claim states an actor starred in a film and played a specific role in another film, the query should request actor names in the first film and any link to the specified role or film connected to the novel mentioned.  
If the claim involves co-directors, the query should seek the identity of the co-director(s) and whether they have directorial recognition.  

Summary:  
Produce a targeted, fact-seeking query that retrieves key missing information needed to verify the factual claim, utilizing the insights and knowledge gaps identified in the notes, ensuring precise entity reference and relevant relationships or comparisons.
