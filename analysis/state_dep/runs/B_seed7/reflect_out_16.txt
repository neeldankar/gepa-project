Task Description:

You will be provided with three inputs related to a factual claim about people, places, institutions, films, songs, or other entities within entertainment, academic, or geographic domains:

1. `claim`: A factual statement involving one or more subclaims or assertions, often covering topics such as:
   - The country of origin or developer of technology, software, or hardware.
   - Film or documentary production details (year, collaborators, directors).
   - Artistic contributions to notable buildings or monuments.
   - Locations of institutions, landmarks, or cathedrals.
   - Attribution of credits (roles, creators, actors, composers).
   - Taxonomic, botanical, or geographic distinctions (e.g., endemic species, island-specific flora).

2. `notes`: An assorted list of extracted sentences or statements relevant to the claim, possibly including:
   - Overlapping or redundant information with minor variations.
   - Some inaccuracies, outdated facts, or incomplete assertions.
   - Varying detail levels or inconsistent references.
   
3. `context`: A set of encyclopedic-style paragraphs or authoritative snippets providing verified background information on the claim’s entities or topics. These serve as your primary evidence base and are expected to:
   - Contain precise names, dates, roles, locations, and technical or biographical details.
   - Resolve ambiguities or contradicting information found in notes.
   - Reflect domain-specific knowledge required for accurate fact checking.

Your Task:

Produce two outputs:

- `new_notes`: A clean, concise, and strictly accurate subset of notes that directly and explicitly:
  - Support or clarify the original claim’s factual subclaims.
  - Integrate and reconcile information from both the `notes` and the submitted authoritative `context`.
  - Correct any factual errors or inaccuracies present in the claim or notes, explicitly noting which subclaims are corrected.
  - Remove redundancies and irrelevant, unsubstantiated, or speculative content.
  - Emphasize unique, verified facts relevant to the core assertions.
  - Provide enough precision to stand alone as validated statements suitable for fact-checking and reference.
  - Indicate any lack of evidence when a claim or subclaim cannot be verified due to missing contextual support.

- `titles`: A comprehensive but precise list of exact titles or names of documents or entities from the provided context that:
  - Contain essential information substantiating or clarifying the facts in the new_notes.
  - Reflect “gold standard” authoritative references expected for thorough claim validation.
  - Use exact naming conventions as given in the `context`; avoid paraphrasing or partial titles.
  - Cover all validated elements of the claim, without including unrelated or unsubstantiated context entries.

Guidelines and Domain-Specific Nuances:

- Break down complex claims into individual factual subclaims for separate verification.
- Use the context to verify, correct, or refine each subclaim; trust the authoritative context over notes where conflicts exist.
- Confirm precise names and spellings for people, films, institutions, places, and species.
- Verify accurate dates such as release years, founding years, and periods of activity or tenure.
- Attribute proper roles, credits, or participation accurately (e.g., specific collaborators, director vs. cinematographer).
- Distinguish geographic details carefully, including country, city, region, and unique occurrences (such as species confined to specific islands).
- Avoid assuming or implying connections not explicitly supported by the evidence.
- When multiple context documents conflict, prioritize those with direct factual statements supporting the subclaim.
- Address domain-specific details, e.g.:
  - Film and entertainment: production year, director, actors, collaborators, documentarian vs. subject.
  - Academic institutions: public vs. private status, renaming history, location.
  - Botanical or geographic: species subspecies distributions, island endemism.
  - Art and architecture: exact attribution for sculptures or works, location of buildings.
- If the claim involves negative constructions (e.g., “not from the same country” or “not both made in the same year”), carefully verify both parts and clarify any verified information or absence of evidence for the second part.
- Notes may repeat or paraphrase similar facts; select or synthesize one clear, factual statement to avoid redundancy.
- Explicitly flag unverified or unsupported subclaims, noting the lack of evidence rather than speculating.

Output Format:

- Both `new_notes` and `titles` must be arrays (lists) of strings.
- Each entry in `new_notes` should be a standalone, factual, precise statement reflecting the validated claim or subclaim.
- Each entry in `titles` should match exactly a document or entity name from the provided `context`.
- Avoid including information beyond what is supported by the provided context and notes.
- Keep new_notes concise and free of superfluous detail while maintaining factual completeness relevant to the claim.

Summary of Effective Strategy:

1. Decompose the claim into discrete, verifiable subclaims.
2. Cross-check each subclaim with all notes and the authoritative context.
3. Use context to resolve conflicts and confirm details.
4. Correct mistakes or outdated facts explicitly; indicate if any subclaim is unsubstantiated.
5. Merge overlapping or redundant notes into precise, single statements.
6. Compile a list of all relevant context titles that together cover the evidence base.
7. Deliver a factual, clear, and concise fact sheet (`new_notes`) with matching authoritative references (`titles`).

By following these detailed instructions, you will produce an accurate, clarified, and verifiable factual summary that precisely supports or refines the original claim using authoritative, domain-specific evidence.
