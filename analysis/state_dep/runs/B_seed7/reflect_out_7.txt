Task:  
You will receive two textual inputs: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a single precise output string called `query`. This `query` should be an effective, targeted fact-finding search phrase or question designed to retrieve reliable and relevant evidence that can verify or refute the given claim based on the information and gaps highlighted in the notes.

Input Definitions:  
- `claim`: A factual or comparative statement that may mention several entities, events, persons, roles, works (e.g., films, games, teams), or relationships that require validation. Claims often involve verifying identities, roles, award counts, relationships, or popularity rankings.  
- `notes`: Extracted relevant observations, background facts, or knowledge gaps related to the claim. Notes often point out missing or ambiguous information critical for verification (such as unclear identity of an individual or team, missing award data, unclear connections between persons and works, or ambiguous nomenclature).

Output:  
- `query`: A clear, concise, and focused question or search phrase designed to retrieve authoritative information that fills the critical knowledge gaps necessary to verify or falsify the claim.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Considerations:  
1. Identify Key Entities and Roles:  
   - Extract all main entities (persons, teams, titles, events) and their roles or relationships involved in the claim.  
   - For film claims, include full film titles, director names, cast, character roles, and connections to source works (e.g., novels).  
   - For sports claims, identify the correct sport, players, teams (including colloquial or nicknames), and match or tournament details.  
   - For games or cultural items, specify the designer, title of the game, and popularity metrics or ratings.  

2. Address Ambiguities or Missing Data Highlighted in Notes:  
   - If the claim refers to ambiguous identities (e.g., nicknames, common names, titles), the query should explicitly ask for clarifications or confirm identities.  
   - When award counts or achievements are relevant but missing or unclear, query explicitly for those statistics or comparisons between figures.  
   - When multiple potential interpretations exist (e.g., multiple directors or co-directors, multiple games by a designer), specify all relevant possibilities or clarify which entity is referenced.  

3. Include Comparative or Relational Aspects:  
   - If the claim is a comparison (e.g., popularity, awards), the query should seek comparative data for all compared entities.  
   - When the claim involves relationships (e.g., a director also directed another film, or a player took part in a specific match for a particular team), include those connections explicitly in the query.  

4. Target Retrieval of Specific Authoritative Evidence:  
   - The query should be tailored to find documents or data sources that confirm critical facts or authoritative listings (e.g., official film credits, player rosters, award databases, popularity ratings).  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; focus on missing key facts and relationships needed for verification as highlighted by the notes.

5. Maintain Conciseness While Ensuring Completeness:  
   - Formulate queries succinctly but ensure all important entities and relationships are included to achieve sufficient precision for retrieval.  

Generalizable Strategy:  
- Analyze the claim to find the core fact or facts to verify.  
- Use the notes to identify what information is missing, unclear, or ambiguous and needs to be retrieved.
