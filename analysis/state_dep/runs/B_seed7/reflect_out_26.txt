Task Description:  
You will receive two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your primary objective is to generate a single, precise, and focused output field named `query`. This query must explicitly and concisely request the key missing factual information necessary to verify or refute the claim, using the evidence or knowledge gaps highlighted in the notes.

---

Core Task Understanding:  
- The `claim` is a factual assertion involving specific entities, events, or relationships (e.g., persons, films, books, sports teams, awards, countries), potentially containing ambiguity, contradiction, or incomplete details.  
- The `notes` comprise extracted, relevant facts, observations, and contextual information connected to the claim. Crucially, they expose missing information, ambiguities, contradictions, or unclear entity relationships that prevent straightforward verification of the claim.

Your role is to analyze both the claim and the notes, identify these knowledge gaps or ambiguities, and formulate a tightly scoped query to retrieve authoritative evidence addressing these gaps. This query is meant to guide information retrieval systems or knowledge bases to provide exactly the missing details needed for full claim assessment.

---

Detailed Guidelines and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Entity and Relationship Extraction**  
   - Identify all entities in the claim: people (including their nationality, profession, or gender if relevant), creative works (films, books, musical pieces including titles and opus numbers), organizations (sports clubs, production companies), events (sporting seasons, film releases), and awards.  
   - Recognize roles associated with entities (e.g., director, actor, goalie, composer) and their attributes (nationality, gender, debut works).  
   - Identify explicit or implicit relationships or comparisons between entities (e.g., same person linked to multiple films, director nationalities compared by geographic size, a film’s producer linked to another film).

2. **Use Notes to Pinpoint Gaps and Ambiguities**  
   - Notes often clarify what is known but, importantly, reveal what is unknown, contradictory, or unclear. Use these to frame exactly what factual elements need confirmation or disambiguation.  
   - Resolve ambiguous or incomplete entity identification if multiple candidates exist. For example, disambiguate similarly named persons, confirm precise film titles (including release years), opus numbers for musical works, or whether a director is officially credited.  
   - Identify conflicting information or missing relationships (e.g., film credits, cast roles, debut films, cross-national connections).

3. **Formulate Focused, Comprehensive Queries**  
   - Your query must be concise yet cover all critical missing information necessary to verify the claim. If multiple discrete missing facts exist, combine them logically into a single query covering all points.  
   - Avoid vagueness or overly broad questions. Instead, specify entities, roles, and relationships precisely. For example, do not seek “Who is in this film?” but “Who produced film A and film B, and are any producers common to both?”  
   - Include pertinent qualifiers: names, dates, film titles with director names, opus numbers, nationalities, and roles (actor, director, goalkeeper).  
   - For comparative claims, request information about all relevant entities and their attributes, rankings, or statistics needed to establish comparative facts.

4. **Domain-Specific Knowledge to Guide Queries**  
   - **Film domain:** Confirm director nationality and filmography; actress debuts and roles; producer credits across films; cast members; film release years and titles; clarify any confusion about casting (e.g., a claim that an actress played Johnny Depp is likely a confusion needing clarification of roles).  
   - **Music domain:** Confirm composer identity, nationality, gender; exact composition title including opus number; correct attribution.  
   - **Sports domain:** Identify player positions by season and club; clarify if claims about roles (e.g., a goalkeeper leading a strike force) are accurate or mixing roles; verify player transfers across clubs.  
   - Where a claim includes ambiguous or false-based assertions, queries should aim to uncover the correct facts and identify inaccuracies.

5. **Generalizable Strategy Recap**  
   - Begin by extracting all entities and relationships inherent in the claim.  
   - Reference the notes to find ambiguous, contradictory, or incomplete data points blocking validation.  
   - Compose a single precise query that explicitly requests retrieval of all those missing or uncertain facts.  
   - Ensure the query covers all involved entities in linked fashion, requesting specific attributes, roles, or connections relevant to the claim.  
   - Avoid redundant or overly verbose wording while maintaining completeness.  
   - Use phrasing that guides retrieval of authoritative or primary sources needed for verification.

---

Examples of Effective Queries:  

- For a composer claim with ambiguity in composer identity or nationality:  
  *Who composed the musical piece "Rhapsody, Op. 1"? Was the composer male and Hungarian? Is Johann Christoph Pepusch associated with this work?*  

- For a claim comparing geographic origins and final films of two directors:  
  *What are the countries of origin and geographic sizes of directors Robert Stevenson and Éric Rohmer? Which one directed the final film "Romance of Astree and Celadon"?*  

- For a claim about an actress starring in a film directed by an American and involving a Canadian film:  
  *Which actresses starred in "The Death and Life of John F. Donovan"? For each, what was their debut film? Which debut films were directed by American directors? Who is the actress meeting both criteria?*  

- For a claim involving a goalkeeper’s role and subsequent club affiliation:  
  *Who was the goalkeeper for Leicester City F.C. during the 1928–29 season? Did this goalkeeper lead the team’s strike force? Did this player ever play for Manchester United F.C.?*  

- For a claim regarding producer involvement across films:  
  *Who produced "Experimenter"? Who produced the film "Man Camp"? Is there any producer credited on both films?*  

---

This instruction equips you to generate precise, domain-aware queries that effectively resolve ambiguities and fill critical knowledge gaps to support thorough, authoritative claim verification across film, music, sport, and biographical data domains.
