Task Description:  
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single output field called `query`.  

- The `claim` is a factual statement, possibly involving multiple entities, events, roles, or comparisons that needs to be verified.  
- The `notes` include observations, extracted facts, or relevant contextual details related to the claim. They highlight what is known, what is ambiguous, or what information is missing to verify the claim. Key information in `notes` often clarifies entities, time frames, roles, counts, and connections.  

Your task is to generate a clear, precise, and focused `query` that will retrieve supporting or refuting evidence for the claim by filling critical knowledge gaps highlighted in `notes`.  

Key Requirements for Query Construction:  

1. **Identify Key Entities and Relationships**  
   - Extract the main entities referenced in the claim (people, organizations, places, titles, roles).  
   - Identify the principal relationships, roles, or events connecting these entities.  
   - If the claim involves a role (e.g., coach, vocalist, director), specify the exact role and the relevant entity.  
   - When the claim involves comparison (e.g., award counts or shared professions), ensure the query targets each entity in that comparison clearly and comparatively.

2. **Address Ambiguities and Missing Information in the Notes**  
   - Use the notes to pinpoint missing or ambiguous details such as unclear identities, ambiguous role assignments, missing award counts, temporal uncertainties (e.g., current position versus past), or unclear connections (e.g., who directed a film adaptation).  
   - Explicitly include these clarifications as questions in the query to retrieve concrete, disambiguating data.  

3. **Incorporate Niche or Domain-Specific Context Appropriately**  
   - For claims involving creative works (films, books, music, organizations), explicitly mention titles, locations, or works relevant for retrieval.  
   - For sports-related claims, specify seasons, teams, leadership roles, or divisions if applicable.  
   - For claims involving identities with possible multiple matches (e.g., "Kevin Wilson" might need distinguishing middle names or affiliated teams), include disambiguation criteria.  
   - For roles such as director, coordinator, vocalist, musician: clarify the person's exact role and associated entity or work.  
   - For geographic claims, specify precise locations and political or administrative designations (e.g., city, state, country).

4. **Focus on Critical Missing Pieces Only—Avoid Redundancy**  
   - Do not redundantly ask questions about information clearly available in the notes.  
   - Queries should be brief but comprehensive to retrieve data needed to either confirm or refute the claim fully.  
   - Avoid overly broad queries; target specifics required to settle ambiguities or missing facts.

5. **Structure Queries to Facilitate Retrieval of Relevant Documents**  
   - Queries should naturally include the connection between entities or facts in the claim (e.g., "Who coached X team in year Y?", "Are person A and person B both musicians?", "Are locations X and Y in the same country?").  
   - When appropriate, request comparative data explicitly (such as award counts for multiple figures).  
   - Use full proper names or fully qualified references to avoid confusion, relying on notes for disambiguation details.

General Strategy to Apply:  
- Review the notes carefully to identify what critical information is missing or ambiguous that prevents verifying the claim fully.  
- Construct the query around obtaining that missing information by logically linking entities and roles as necessary.  
- When multiple entities appear, connect them logically in the query (e.g., via comparison, joint involvement).  
- Produce final queries that are fact-seeking, focused, and structured to retrieve documents that can definitively prove or disprove the claim.

Examples of Domain-Specific Details to Include in Queries:  
- Specific football season and conference names, coaching roles, and team affiliations for sports claims.  
- Identification of singer, songwriter, and band names for music-related claims.  
- Full corporate names and locations including cities and states for company or geography claims.  
- Film titles, director names, or actor roles for claims about movies or adaptations.  
- Awards or counts explicitly identified when comparing achievements.

Output:  
A single factual, precise natural language question or set of related questions, designed to retrieve the minimal but sufficient information to verify the claim by targeting the knowledge gaps noted.
