Task Description:  
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to produce a single output field called `query`.  

- The `claim` is a factual statement involving multiple entities, events, or comparisons that needs to be verified or assessed.  
- The `notes` are observations, facts, or extracted information relevant to the claim. These notes often identify missing critical information, ambiguities, or contextual gaps needed to fully confirm or refute the claim. They may also reference specific people, works (films, books, bands), places, awards, roles, or events pertinent to the claim.  

Your goal is to generate a clear, precise, and focused `query` that, when used to search, will retrieve the essential evidence or documentation required to verify the accuracy of the claim by addressing the knowledge gaps or ambiguities highlighted in the `notes`.  

Detailed Instructions for Query Construction:  

1. **Identify and include all key entities and concepts from the claim that are necessary for verification.**  
   - Entities may include persons, works (films, operas, books), places, organizations, events, awards, or specific terms relevant to the claim.  
   - When multiple entities are involved, include their names and relevant descriptors (e.g., film titles, director names, festival editions).  

2. **Address all ambiguities or missing information noted in the `notes`.**  
   - If identities of persons or works are unclear, include queries that clarify or disambiguate these entities.  
   - If award counts or details about roles or involvement are missing, explicitly request these data points.  
   - If geographic or relational details are uncertain, include precise geographic or relational questions to confirm proximity or association.  
   - If structural or stylistic differences cause difficulty in comparison (e.g., comparing opera acts to film segments), include clarifying questions about these structures or ask for comparable units.  

3. **When the claim involves comparisons (e.g., award counts, number of acts, roles between entities), ensure the query explicitly requests comparative data for all relevant parties or works.**  
   - Queries should not just seek data for one entity if the claim is comparative; they must cover all compared entities equally.  

4. **Incorporate domain-specific factual considerations:**  
   - When dealing with films or operas, include exact titles, production years, director names, and related works mentioned in the claim or notes.  
   - When multiple persons might fit a role (e.g., “German Frank Sinatra”), include disambiguation questions to identify exact individuals.  
   - When awards are involved, specify the exact award name, edition/year of the event, and the specific film/person/role involved.  
   - When verifying involvement of individuals in films or productions (actor roles or director responsibilities), seek confirmation of names, roles, and relevant credentials or recognitions.  
   - When geographic claims are involved, include precise place names and their relationships (e.g., proximity to other geographic features or administrative jurisdictions).  

5. **Focus on producing a concise yet sufficiently descriptive query:**  
   - Avoid broad or redundant questions that do not hone in on the critical missing information.  
   - The query should be narrowly targeted to retrieve documents or data that will fill gaps or clarify ambiguities identified in the notes, enabling complete verification of the claim.  
   - Connect entities logically to reflect the relationships in the claim, especially in comparisons or when linking roles/events.  

6. **Generalizable strategy for building the query:**  
   - Start by pinpointing from the notes exactly what information is missing or ambiguous to validate the claim.  
   - Formulate direct questions that seek this missing information, integrating all relevant named entities and their relationships.  
   - When multiple key aspects appear (e.g., multiple films, persons, awards, places), construct queries that query them jointly or comparatively wherever appropriate.  
   - Confirm structural details when comparisons are involved, such as the number of acts in an opera versus segments in a film to enable meaningful comparison.  

Examples of domain-specific foci:  
- For award-related claims: explicitly ask for award recipients, award-winning films, or counts of awards for the people or works involved.  
- For film and opera claims: request act or segment counts, directorial credits, actor roles, relationships to source works (e.g., novel adaptations).  
- For geographic claims: ask which towns administered by a particular council are near certain geographical features or parks.  
- For identity-related ambiguities: seek to establish the exact person or work referenced by potentially ambiguous labels.  

Output format:  
Return a single, focused query string designed to search for and retrieve exactly the key documents or data filling the knowledge gaps necessary to assess the factual accuracy of the claim.

---

By following these instructions, you will generate effective, domain-aware, and precisely targeted queries that comprehensively address knowledge gaps, making it possible to verify complex claims involving multiple interwoven entities, roles, and relationships.
