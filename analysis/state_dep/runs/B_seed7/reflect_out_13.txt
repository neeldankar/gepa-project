Task Description:  
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single output called `query` that asks a clear, precise, and focused question designed to retrieve critical missing information needed to verify or refute the claim.

- The `claim` is a factual or composite statement involving entities such as people, creative works, places, or comparative attributes that requires validation or additional evidence. These often involve roles, relationships, or comparisons (e.g., identifying judges on a TV show, jurisdictions of administrative divisions, or biographical details of individuals).  
- The `notes` provide extracted relevant facts, observations, or context from various sources but often reveal key knowledge gaps, ambiguities, contradictions, or missing details essential to confirm or refute the claim. The notes typically point to what information is uncertain or incomplete, such as exact identities, roles, dates, titles, nationalities, or relational contexts.

---

Detailed Guidelines for Query Construction:

1. **Extract and Specify Key Entities and Their Roles or Attributes**  
   - Identify all critical entities from the claim, including persons (e.g., judges, authors, diplomats), creative works (films, songs, books, TV shows), geographical units (counties, cities, provinces, countries), and awards or honors (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature).  
   - Clarify their relevant attributes or roles mentioned or implied (e.g., judge on a particular show, producer/singer of an album, nationality, debut films, diplomatic status).

2. **Use Notes to Pinpoint Knowledge Gaps and Clarify Ambiguities**  
   - Focus the query on exactly what is unknown, ambiguous, or contradictory in the notes. For example, uncertain judge identities, unclear producer or singer roles related to works cited, missing information on administrative jurisdiction, or the precise identity of an “Austrian poet-diplomat Nobel laureate.”  
   - When multiple possible entities fit partial descriptions, the query should explicitly disambiguate by names, roles, or titles.  
   - Include reference to different but related entities when the claim involves relationships or comparisons (e.g., two cities in the same country, or comparing directors’ origins).

3. **Address Relational or Comparative Elements Explicitly**  
   - For claims with comparisons between entities or overlapping attributes (e.g., two directors from different countries, both judged shows, or writers vs. poet-diplomats), structure the query to request evidence covering all relevant parties and their comparative features.  
   - Ask for authoritative confirmatory details such as official judge panels, producer and performer credits, political jurisdictions, or awarded honors.

4. **Domain-Specific Detail Handling**  
   - Film and TV: Query the cast lists, directors, producers, show panels, and their official roles. Include exact titles and release dates if relevant.  
   - Music: Confirm composers, performers, production credits, and associated works with precise titles.  
   - Geography and Politics: Verify administrative jurisdiction chains, province and country memberships, and city status.  
   - Awards and Biography: Confirm identities, award recipients, and roles such as poet-diplomat status, writer professions, and nationality.

5. **Formulate a Concise, Complete, and Focused Query**  
   - Your query should aim to retrieve all missing or uncertain information critical for verifying the claim using as few questions as possible but without losing detail.  
   - Avoid vague or broad queries; instead, target specific questions that will clarify identities, roles, and relationships.  
   - When multiple critical facts are needed, combine related questions logically to ensure retrieval of comprehensive evidence.

---

Generalizable Strategy Summary:  
- Parse the claim to identify all entities, their roles, and relationships.  
- Analyze notes to identify gaps, contradictions, or lack of evidence tied to those entities or roles.  
- Build a query that explicitly requests:  
  - Names, professions, and roles (e.g., judges on "Music Icons," producers and singers of "Aaina").  
  - Geographical jurisdiction and country membership for places involved.  
  - Identities and credentials of individuals mentioned or implied (e.g., Austrian poet-diplomat Nobel laureate).  
  - Comparative facts when relevant (e.g., locations of cities, recognition or awards received).  
- Ensure the query is minimal yet sufficient to retrieve all documents needed to confirm or refute the claim fully.

---

Example Query Adaptations:  
- For a claim naming judges and music producers:  
  *Who were the judges on the TV show "Music Icons"? Did Meesha Shafi, Strings, Ali Azmat, and Junaid Khan serve as judges? Who produced and sang the album or song titled "Aaina," and did these individuals join the judging panel on "Music Icons"?*  

- For a geographic jurisdiction claim:  
  *Under which city does Fàn County fall within Henan Province? Are this city and Pu'er City located in the same country? If so, which country?*  

- For a claim about an individual with notable attributes:  
  *Who is the Austrian poet who was also a diplomat and won the Nobel Prize in Literature? Is this person recognized primarily as a writer? Also, was Alan Sillitoe a writer?*  

---

These instructions ensure that the assistant produces explicit, domain-specific queries that efficiently target precisely the missing information needed for verifying claims, especially in specialized domains such as film and TV casting and production credits, geography and political jurisdictions, and literary or biographical facts.
