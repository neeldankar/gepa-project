Task Description:  
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a single, clear, and focused output field named `query`.

- The `claim` is a factual statement involving one or more entities, events, relationships, or comparisons that require verification or further evidence.  
- The `notes` consist of extracted observations, facts, or contextual information relevant to the claim. These notes often reveal critical knowledge gaps, ambiguities, or missing details necessary to validate or assess the claim effectively. Notes may also highlight contradictions, incomplete entity identification, or ambiguity in relationships or roles.

Your main task is to formulate a precise, targeted query that will retrieve exactly the key missing information needed to confirm or refute the claim. This query will serve as a focused request designed to gather authoritative evidence or documents that fill the informed knowledge gaps or clarify ambiguities noted.

---

Detailed Guidelines:  

1. **Identify Key Entities, Roles, and Relationships**  
   - Extract all critical entities from the claim (persons, films, books, countries, awards, etc.).  
   - Recognize roles or attributes tied to these entities (e.g., composer nationality and gender, director origin and filmography, actor debut films and roles).  
   - Pay attention to relationships or connections between entities mentioned in the claim (e.g., comparison between two directors’ countries by size, a person’s involvement in two distinct films, co-directors, or authorship).

2. **Incorporate Ambiguities and Knowledge Gaps from Notes**  
   - Notes often highlight missing, contradictory, or uncertain information. Use these to shape and focus the query on exactly what is unknown or unclear (e.g., confirming the true composer of a piece, the nationality of a director, or the debut film and its director for an actress).  
   - Disambiguate entities when necessary, especially if multiple candidates exist (e.g., clarifying which “German Frank Sinatra” is referenced).  
   - Address details such as exact titles, opus or numbering conventions, and proper identification of creative works.

3. **Handle Comparative or Relational Claims Precisely**  
   - For claims involving comparisons (award counts, country sizes, career overlaps), explicitly query all involved entities on the attribute compared.  
   - Request statistics, rankings, or ordered lists as needed to establish relative standings.

4. **Be Explicit About Domain-Specific Entities and Context**  
   - When claims mention creative works (films, books, musical compositions), explicitly reference these titles in the query to target relevant documents.  
   - Confirm directors’ involvement and their recognition status (e.g., confirming if a co-director is officially recognized as a director).  
   - For actors, verify their roles in multiple films separately and whether those films relate to the claim (including the directors and geographic origins involved).  
   - For composers and musical works, confirm opus numbers, nationalities, and male/female identifications.

5. **Formulate a Concise but Complete Query**  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague questions; focus on retrieving the specific missing facts or evidence critical for verification.  
   - If multiple documents are needed to fully assess the claim, combine questions logically to retrieve all relevant information in one query.

6. **Generalizable Strategy Recap**:  
   - Use notes to pinpoint exactly what information is missing or uncertain needed to verify the claim.  
   - Construct the query to explicitly request these missing facts (names, countries, dates, roles, counts, titles).  
   - Incorporate both the entities and their relevant relationships or comparisons mentioned in the claim.  
   - Avoid redundant phrasing; keep queries precise but sufficiently descriptive.  
   - When multiple key entities appear, ensure the query asks about all in a connected logical way to retrieve comprehensive evidence (e.g., award counts for both persons, cross-casting details, geographic comparisons).

---

Examples:  
- If the claim involves a composer of a musical piece titled "Rhapsody, Op. 1," and notes indicate ambiguity about who composed it and their nationality, the query should ask:  
  *Who composed "Rhapsody, Op. 1"? Was the composer male and Hungarian? Is Johann Christoph Pepusch associated with this work?*  

- For a claim comparing directors’ geographic origins and final films directed, the query might be:  
  *What are the countries of origin and geographic sizes of directors Robert Stevenson and Éric Rohmer? Which director directed the final film "Romance of Astree and Celadon"?*  

- For a claim about an actress who debuted in a film by an American director and also starred in a Canadian film:  
  *Which actresses starred in "The Death and Life of John F. Donovan"? For each, what was their debut film, and which debut films were directed by American directors? Identify the actress fitting both criteria.*  

---

By following these instructions, you will generate queries that efficiently retrieve critical evidence, resolving ambiguities and filling knowledge gaps essential for thorough claim verification in specialized knowledge domains such as film, music, and biographical data.
