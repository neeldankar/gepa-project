Task Description:  
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a single, clear, and focused output field named `query`. The `query` should be a precise, targeted question or set of interconnected questions designed to retrieve the critical missing facts or authoritative evidence needed to verify or refute the `claim` fully.

---

Detailed Understanding and Domain-Specific Context:  

1. **Nature of the Claim and Notes**  
- The `claim` is a factual statement involving entities such as persons, creative works (films, books, musical pieces), events, awards, and their interrelationships or comparisons.  
- The `notes` consist of extracted contextual data, observations, or known facts relevant to the claim but often reveal knowledge gaps, ambiguous or contradictory details, or missing identification needed for full verification.  
- Often, `notes` reference specific domain knowledge:  
  - Film domain may include screenplays, directors, actors, co-directors, nationalities, film titles and release details, and notable award recognition.  
  - Music domain may involve composer names, opus numbers, nationality and gender attributes, and composition titles.  
  - Sports domain often hinges on identity, roles (player, coach, defender), participation in specific events, or achievement details (titles, medals).  
  - Literary domain involves author identity, award winnings (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), and relationships between source materials and adaptations.

2. **Key Entities, Roles, and Relationships**  
- Extract all named entities and their roles explicitly (e.g., "Jim Thorpe" as trainer/coach, "Eddie Mahan" as player, "Nelly Sachs" as Nobel laureate, film titles like "The Night Hunter").  
- Identify relationships or interactions between entities: who coached whom, which creative works were adapted from others, which persons won certain awards, actors who debuted in which films, the nationality or country of origin of involved entities, comparisons of geographic or award-related data, and other relational details.  

3. **Incorporating Ambiguities and Knowledge Gaps from Notes**  
- Use the `notes` to detect critical missing data or conflicting information essential for validation:  
  - Is it unclear who composed a particular piece or wrote a screenplay?  
  - Are the nationalities or birthplaces of persons ambiguous?  
  - Are there conflicting attributions or lack of proof regarding awards or medal wins?  
  - Are specific relationships (like a coach’s team or a screenplay’s source) not confirmed?  
  - Are details of film directing (co-directors vs. main directors) or participation of actors uncertain?  
- Where multiple possible candidates or entities could be involved, ensure your query disambiguates by requesting specific identifiers, titles, or descriptions.

4. **Formulating Comparative or Relational Queries**  
- When claims involve comparisons (e.g., who won more awards, which country is larger, who coached which tribes), explicitly include all entities and attributes under comparison in the query.  
- Ask for rankings, counts, or ordered listings as necessary.  
- When multiple attributes or entities intertwine, structure queries so they logically link to capture the full scope of evidence needed simultaneously.

5. **Domain-Specific Factual Context to Reference in Queries**  
- For Films: Include titles, screenplay authorship, directors (including co-directors), debuts, casts, and nationality of involved persons or films.  
- For Literature: Confirm prize laureates (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), authorship, and source materials for adaptations.  
- For Music: Confirm correct composers (with full names), opus numbers, gender, nationality, and association to specific compositions.  
- For Sports: Include player roles, coach identities, team names, medal record holders, competition names and dates, and nationality or home countries.

6. **Query Construction Guidelines**  
- Your query should be concise yet complete to retrieve all missing, ambiguous, or disputed information necessary to verify the entire claim.  
- Avoid overly broad or vague queries that might retrieve irrelevant data or fail to pinpoint the knowledge gap.  
- If multiple documents or pieces of information are needed to confirm the claim, combine logically related questions into a single query to maximize retrieval efficiency.  
- Use precise terms and include all key entities, attributes, and relationships from the claim.  
- Order query elements logically, grouping related questions where possible.

---

Generalizable Strategy Summary:  
- Parse the claim to identify all critical entities, roles, and relationships.  
- Examine the notes to locate gaps, contradictions, or insufficient evidence.  
- Formulate a focused query that explicitly requests only the missing or ambiguous facts—such as specific award histories, birthplaces, authorship, or directorial credits—that are essential for confirming or rejecting the claim.  
- Include all relevant entities when the claim involves comparisons or dual attributions, ensuring comprehensive evidence retrieval.  
- Maintain clear, direct phrasing avoiding redundancy while encompassing all elements needed for verification.

---

Example Application:  
- Claim: Composer of "Rhapsody, Op. 1" and their gender/nationality ambiguous.  
- Query: *Who composed "Rhapsody, Op. 1"? Was that composer male and Hungarian? Is Johann Christoph Pepusch associated with this work?*  

- Claim: Actors starred in two named films, debut films directed by Americans, nationality distinctions.  
- Query: *Which actresses starred in "The Death and Life of John F. Donovan," and for each, what was their debut film? Which of these debut films were directed by American directors?*  

- Claim: A sportsperson's birth origin and defending champion identity unclear.  
- Query: *What is Angelique Kerber's nationality and birthplace? Who was the defending champion of the 2005 China Open – Men's Doubles, and what is that player's nationality? Did both compete professionally in tennis?*  

By adhering strictly to these instructions, your generated queries will be tightly focused on the critical evidence needed to verify claims in specialized domains such as film, music, literature, and sports, and will maximize the retrieval of authoritative supporting data.
