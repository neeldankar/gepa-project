Task Description:

You will be given three input fields:
1. `claim`: A single factual statement that may contain multiple individual subclaims or assertions regarding people, works (films, songs, characters), dates, roles, or breeds (in cases of animals). These subclaims may be partially accurate, inaccurate, or incomplete.
2. `notes`: A list of extracted sentences or statements related to the claim. These notes may contain redundancies, minor variations in phrasing, factual inaccuracies, or partially outdated/corrected information. They vary in detail and reliability.
3. `context`: A list of encyclopedic-style paragraphs or document snippets. These contain authoritative background information about the entities, topics, or subjects referenced in the claim and notes. This context is often more comprehensive, structured, and factually reliable.

Your goal is to carefully evaluate the claim by verifying, refining, or correcting each of its subclaims through a rigorous fact-checking process using both the `notes` and the richer `context`. Then produce two output fields: 

- `new_notes`: A curated, concise, and factual set of notes that:
  - Directly support or clarify the core assertions in the claim.
  - Integrate information from both input `notes` and the authoritative `context`.
  - Eliminate redundant or contradictory statements.
  - Explicitly correct any inaccuracies or errors found in the claim or input notes.
  - Present verified, distinct facts to form a coherent understanding aligned with factual evidence.
  - Use precise and domain-appropriate terminology and correct name spellings, dates, and roles.

- `titles`: A list of relevant document or entity titles from the `context` that:
  - Substantiate the refined `new_notes`.
  - Cover all critical topics or entities mentioned in the claim and selected notes.
  - Are specific, correctly formatted (matching titles or entities as presented in the `context`).
  - Reflect gold-standard references, representing authoritative sources for claim validation.

General Methodology and Considerations:

- Decompose the `claim` into discrete factual subclaims (e.g., identifying a person’s nationality, film roles, character names, song credits, dates, or dog breed distinctions).
- Use the `context` as the primary authoritative source for verifying or correcting factual information.
- Treat the `notes` as supplementary, often noisy evidence that may include outdated, duplicated, or erroneous data.
- Cross-reference all subclaims against both `notes` and `context`, prioritizing context for resolving conflicts or confirming facts.
- If the claim contains inaccuracies (e.g., wrong nationality, incorrect attribution of roles, mistakes in dates, or confusion between individuals and breeds), explicitly identify these and provide corrected facts.
- Maintain domain specificity: 
   - For people, ensure accurate career details, notable works, roles, birth dates, and awards.
   - For films, characters, or songs, confirm correct titles, years, roles, voice actors, and production details.
   - For dog-related claims, distinguish between individual dogs and recognized breeds; check geographic origin and breed characteristics.
- Avoid adding unsupported assumptions or irrelevant information.
- The `new_notes` must be stand-alone factual statements, concise yet comprehensive for claim validation.
- The `titles` list should comprehensively reflect all key evidence sources relevant to the validated facts.

Formatting:

- Provide your final output as two fields, `new_notes` and `titles`, each formatted as a list (array) of strings.
- Each string must be a clear, concise factual statement or an exact title from the context.
- Avoid redundant or verbose phrasing.
- Ensure all content is factually accurate and clearly addresses the claim and corrections where applicable.

By following these instructions, you will truthfully, precisely, and succinctly synthesize the evidence for each claim, producing a clean, authoritative summary of notes and a bibliography of essential supporting documents/entities.
