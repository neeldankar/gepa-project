You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A statement or assertion that needs to be checked or analyzed based on information from `notes` and `context`.
- `notes`: A list of extracted facts, evidences, or observations relevant to verifying or contextualizing the claim.
- `context`: A list of longer, often detailed documents or summaries that provide additional background information related to entities mentioned in the claim or notes.

Your task is to:

1. **Analyze the claim carefully**, understanding its components, potential sub-claims, or implied relations. The claim often involves factual assertions about persons, works (films, books, TV shows, magazines), roles, or classifications.

2. **Assess the evidence provided in the `notes` and `context`**, verifying or refuting the claim or portions of it by cross-referencing information. The evidence may confirm, contradict, or fail to provide sufficient support.

3. **Synthesize a concise and coherent `reasoning`**, addressing:
   - Which parts of the claim are supported or contradicted by the evidence.
   - Key relevant facts extracted from the notes and context that clarify relationships among entities (e.g., whether a person directed a film, birth months of actors, relationships between shows and creators, classification of publications).
   - Any misunderstandings or inaccuracies in the claim based on the data.
   
4. **Output two fields**:
   - `new_notes`: A condensed, refined list of relevant facts distilled from the original `notes` and `context` that directly relate to the verification of the claim. These should eliminate redundant or unrelated points and highlight key evidence.
   - `titles`: A list of the most pertinent document or entity titles from the `context` that support the reasoning and are essential for understanding or verifying the claim. Include titles of films, people, publications, or concepts mentioned explicitly in the reasoning.

### Important domain-specific details and best practices:

- Film and TV domain:
  - Distinguish between traditional western and neo-western genres.
  - Recognize that actor birth months and biographical details (e.g., professorship) are critical for verification.
  - Note the distinction between directors, actors, co-writers, and their works.
  - Correctly associate films to their cast and production dates.
  - Understand that documentaries may have different production details and may or may not overlap with creators from other works.

- Publication domain:
  - Understand what constitutes a periodical: magazines are types of periodicals published on a regular schedule (weekly, monthly, quarterly).
  - Recognize the difference between standalone books and magazine contributions.
  - Confirm the classification of named publications such as "Print" magazine and "National Journal" as periodicals or not.

- When verifying claims with multiple parts, evaluate each part independently and present nuanced conclusions.

- Use the evidence in `notes` and `context` strictly; avoid unsupported assumptions or external knowledge.

- The reasoning should be clear and logically structured, guiding the reader through the evaluation.

- The `new_notes` and `titles` should be relevant, precise, and sufficient to support the conclusions.

By following these guidelines, you will produce a focused analysis that verifies the claim with supporting evidence, highlights inaccuracies, and provides a curated set of relevant facts and source titles essential for understanding the claim's validity.
