Task Description:  
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to generate a clear, concise, and focused output field called `query`. The `query` should consist of precise, targeted questions or interconnected question groups formulated to identify and retrieve the key missing facts or authoritative evidence required to fully verify or refute the `claim`.

---

Detailed Task Understanding and Domain-Specific Context:

1. **Nature of Inputs**  
- The `claim` is a factual statement involving key entities such as persons (with names and roles), creative works (films, music compositions, literary works), events (sports, awards, historical moments), and their interrelationships, comparisons, or attributions.  
- The `notes` are partial contextual evidence, extracted facts, or observations relevant to the claim but often contain ambiguities, contradictions, missing information, or uncertain attributions necessary for full verification. They may hint at knowledge gaps such as unclear authorship, disputed roles, or missing place associations.

2. **Critical Entities, Relationships, and Data Types**  
- Extract all explicitly named entities and their domain-specific roles (for example, identifying "Dynamo Kyiv" as a professional Ukrainian football club, or “Nelly Sachs” as a Nobel Prize in Literature laureate).  
- Identify relationships or dependencies, such as who coached whom, which school belongs to which district, which films were directed by whom, nationalities, birthplaces, record holders in sports, authorship of compositions or screenplays, and award histories.  
- In claims involving comparisons (e.g., which football ground hosted a final vs. other stadiums, which authors won awards first), include all entities under comparison and all attributes referenced.

3. **Detecting Knowledge Gaps and Ambiguities from Notes**  
- Scrutinize the notes for:  
  - Conflicting details (e.g., different schools being included/excluded in a district)  
  - Missing attributions (e.g., undisclosed composer, uncertain director credit)  
  - Geographic ambiguities (e.g., stadium located in one country or another)  
  - Uncertain biographical details (e.g., birthdates, nationalities)  
  - Missing comparisons or rankings (e.g., how many awards a person won compared to others)  
- These gaps form the core of what your query must seek to clarify.

4. **Formulating Effective Verification Queries**  
- Your `query` should specifically target the missing, ambiguous, or contradictory facts identified and avoid overly broad or irrelevant questions.  
- Where multiple interrelated facts are needed for full validation, logically combine related questions into one cohesive query to maximize retrieval efficiency.  
- Use explicit domain terminology and incorporate all key entities and attributes from the claim.  
- For comparative claims, ensure the query covers all entities and facets involved in the comparison (e.g., listing all member schools and confirming district membership for all).  
- Questions should aim to extract precise factual data: names, dates, roles, places, relationships, counts, or rankings.

5. **Domain-Specific Factual Context to Reference in Queries**  
- **Film Domain:** Titles, director(s) (including co-directors), screenplay authorship, cast members (especially debut films), nationalities of people involved, and award recognitions.  
- **Music Domain:** Composer names (full names), opus/catalog numbers, gender, nationality, correct association to compositions.  
- **Sports Domain:** Player names with roles (e.g., defender, coach), team names, event and competition details (dates, locations), medal or title records, nationalities.  
- **Literary Domain:** Author identities, Nobel Prize or other awards (win status and year), source materials for adaptations.

6. **General Query Construction Guidelines**  
- Keep queries concise yet comprehensive to cover all crucial verification points.  
- Avoid vague or generic queries that may retrieve irrelevant data or fail to pinpoint missing facts.  
- Use precise language referencing all entities and attributes mentioned in the claim.  
- When multiple documents or pieces of evidence are necessary, combine logically related questions into a single query for efficiency.  
- Maintain logical ordering and grouping of related questions.

---

Generalizable Strategy Summary:  
- Parse the `claim` to identify all critical named entities, their roles, and interrelationships.  
- Analyze the `notes` to find contradictions, insufficiencies, or ambiguous attributions.  
- Formulate a targeted query that explicitly requests only the missing or unclear information vital for confirming or refuting the claim—such as the correct composition authorship, definitive birthplace or nationality, exact membership of a group, authentic hosting venue, or award winner identity and count.  
- If the claim involves comparisons or multiple candidates, include all relevant entities and attributes in the query.  
- Strive for clarity and directness, ensuring the query is tightly focused on resolving the critical gaps.

---

Examples of Effective Queries:  

- *For a musical claim:*  
  "Who composed the composition titled 'Rhapsody, Op. 1'? Is the composer male and Hungarian? Is Johann Christoph Pepusch associated with this work?"  

- *For a film-related claim:*  
  "Which actresses starred in 'The Death and Life of John F. Donovan'? For each actress, what was her debut film? Were any of these debut films directed by American directors?"  

- *For a sports-related claim:*  
  "What are Angelique Kerber's nationality and birthplace? Who was the defending champion of the 2005 China Open Men's Doubles? What is that player's nationality? Did both compete professionally in tennis?"  

- *For a school district membership claim:*  
  "Which high schools compose the Sewanhaka Central High School District? Is Concordia Junior-Senior High School a member? What is the identity of the fifth school along with Sewanhaka High School, New Hyde Park Memorial High School, and Floral Park Memorial High School? Do all these schools serve students in Nassau County, New York?"

By strictly following these instructions, your generated queries will focus sharply on the core evidence needed for verifying claims in varied domains—maximizing retrieval precision and enabling clear confirmation or refutation.
