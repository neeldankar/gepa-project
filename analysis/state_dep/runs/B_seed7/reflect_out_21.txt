Task Description:  
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a single output field named `query`. This `query` must be a precise, focused, and comprehensive question or set of closely linked questions designed explicitly to identify the critical missing facts, clarifications, or authoritative evidences needed to verify or refute the entire `claim`.

---

Context and Domain-Specific Details:

1. **Input Characteristics**  
- The `claim` is a factual statement involving named entities such as people, creative works (films, books, music pieces), sports teams or individuals, awards, dates, places, and relationships between these entities.  
- The `notes` summarize factual context about these entities and claims but often highlight knowledge gaps, ambiguous details, contradictory information, or incomplete data critical for full verification.

2. **Domain Nuances**  
- **Film Domain:** Pay attention to film titles, directors (including co-directors or sole directors), screenwriters, actors (notably debut roles), nationalities or countries related to films and persons, source materials of adaptations, and award details. Some ambiguity often arises about directing credits, source authorship, or cross-national affiliations.  
- **Music Domain:** Focus on composer names (including full names), opus numbers, work titles, and composer attributes such as nationality and gender. Disambiguate cases where multiple composers or attributions exist.  
- **Literature Domain:** Critical to confirm exact authorship, prize winnings (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), and authoritative relationships between original literary works and their adaptations.  
- **Sports Domain:** Key entities are players, coaches, team memberships, medals, roles (e.g., defender, coach), event participation (by date and title), and nationality or birthplace. Identification of defending champions or titleholders is also a common fact point.

3. **Key Entity and Relationship Extraction**  
- Identify and explicitly mention all entities appearing in the claim, including their roles (person as player, coach, or laureate; films as adaptations or original works; teams and leagues; etc.).  
- Clarify relationships or interactions: e.g., who coached whom, who composed which composition with what opus number, which person won what award at what date, nationality or birthplace of individuals, league memberships, and sponsor relationships.  
- When multiple entities are compared or linked, incorporate all relevant sides into the query.

4. **Dealing with Ambiguities and Incomplete Notes**  
- From the `notes`, detect where critical evidence is missing or ambiguous: unknown durations of collaborations; uncertain nationality or birthplace; unclear authorship or directorial credit; unknown award or medal holder status; or uncertainty about team memberships or sponsorships.  
- Formulate the query to directly target these gaps.  
- When multiple plausible entities exist, request disambiguating details such as full names, dates, or official titles.

5. **Constructing Comparative or Composite Queries**  
- If the claim involves a comparison or asserts relationships involving multiple linked entities (e.g., awards between competing persons, membership overlap between multiple teams, sponsorship of leagues with named clubs), explicitly include all such entities and attributes.  
- Request ordered or ranked factual information if needed to verify superiority, affiliations, or timelines.  
- Bundle logically related questions into a single comprehensive query to maximize retrieval efficiency and minimize ambiguity.

6. **Query Construction Constraints and Guidelines**  
- Ensure queries are concise without sacrificing completeness; avoid vague or overly broad phrasing.  
- Use precise terminology and explicitly name all key entities, attributes, and relationships relevant to full validation of the claim.  
- Queries should directly expose the critical missing facts or confirm ambiguous facts that prevent definitive verification or refutation.  
- Group questions logically (e.g., all nationality queries together, all award queries together).  
- Avoid redundant or irrelevant information in queries.

---

General Strategy Summary:  
- Carefully parse the `claim` to extract all critical entities, roles, and inter-entity relationships.  
- Review the `notes` for incomplete, contradictory, or missing information relevant to those entities and relationships.  
- Formulate a focused, precisely worded `query` that requests authoritative data targeting all identified knowledge gaps needed to verify or refute the claim fully.  
- When the claim includes comparisons, collaboration, or relational assertions among multiple entities, ensure the query encapsulates all those elements cohesively.  
- Keep phrasing direct, unambiguous, and as compact as possible without sacrificing necessary detail.

---

Examples:  
- If the claim states an ambiguous composer and nationality for a piece, your query should identify the composer’s full name, nationality, and gender linked unambiguously to that piece.  
- For a claim about actors in multiple films with debut and nationality distinctions, the query should request the list of actresses, the films starred in, each debut film of each actress, and the nationality of directors of those debut films.  
- For sports claims involving players, teams, championships, or sponsors, queries should clarify player nationality and roles, team membership in specific leagues, dates and winners of championships, and sponsorship details for each league mentioned.

By adhering to these detailed instructions, your generated queries will be exactly targeted to retrieve the essential facts, comparisons, and authoritative references needed to fully verify or disprove claims across domains such as film, music, literature, and sports.
