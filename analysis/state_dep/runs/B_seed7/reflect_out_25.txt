Detailed Task Instruction for Verifying and Refining Factual Claims Based on Notes and Context

You will be given three inputs related to a factual claim:

1. `claim`: A factual statement concerning people, places, institutions, films, songs, or other entertainment or academic topics. The claim may include multiple factual subclaims or assertions.

2. `notes`: A list of extracted sentences or statements about the claim. These notes may overlap, repeat information, contain inaccuracies, omissions, or imprecise details relative to the claim.

3. `context`: A collection of authoritative encyclopedic paragraphs, document excerpts, or entity profiles providing verified background information that can be used to check, refine, or correct the claim and notes.

Your goal is to produce two outputs:

- `new_notes`: A refined list of concise, factually accurate, and relevant statements synthesized from both notes and context, which explicitly support, clarify, or correct the claim and its components. These notes must:

  - Break down the claim into granular factual subclaims for methodical validation.
  - Cross-check each subclaim against the provided notes and authoritative context. Where disparities exist, prioritize the context for factual accuracy.
  - Identify and fix any factual errors, including inaccuracies in names, dates, roles, locations, or relationships.
  - Remove redundancies, outdated information, or unsupported assertions prevalent in the original notes.
  - Include explicit statements on any subclaims that are unverified or contradicted by evidence.
  - Ensure each note is a standalone, precise statement suitable for independent verification.
  - Avoid including any irrelevant or speculative content not substantiated by the notes or context.

- `titles`: A precise list of the exact titles or entity names from the provided context documents that collectively support or provide essential background for the validated claim and your `new_notes`. These should:

  - Match context titles or entity names exactly as given.
  - Fully cover all verified aspects of the claim.
  - Exclude unrelated, tangential, or unsupported titles.

Domain-Specific Nuances and Verification Focus:

- Claims typically revolve around specific and detailed knowledge about people (e.g., actors, musicians, academics), institutions (universities, governmental bodies), cultural works (films, TV shows, songs), or geographic and botanical information.

- Pay special attention to exact spelling and full correct names (including middle initials, common alternative names, or stage names).

- Verify and specify accurate dates, such as birth years, release years, tenure periods, founding or renaming years, or event dates.

- Attribute correct roles, credits, affiliations, or relationships, such as:

  - Distinguishing lead actors from supporting actors.
  - Identifying the first or notable chairmen or founders.
  - Recognizing the exact institutional type (e.g., public vs. private universities).
  - Confirming geographic details, including special cases like species distribution limited to particular islands or political boundaries.

- Avoid assumptions or conflations, such as mixing different people with similar names, or assigning unverified connections.

- When multiple context sources conflict on facts, give precedence to the most authoritative context document over notes.

- Explicitly note when the claim or subclaims cannot be substantiated, and do not insert unsupported information.

Recommended Reasoning Approach:

- Decompose the claim into distinct factual elements or subclaims for granular verification.

- Systematically check each subclaim first in notes, then against the richer context.

- Correct or clarify any detected factual errors or vagueness clearly and precisely.

- Synthesize a compact, redundancy-free new_notes reflecting only verified, relevant facts.

- Document any unverified or incorrect subclaims explicitly.

- Compile all essential context titles that corroborate each validated subclaim.

- Maintain higher factual precision and clarity even if this requires correcting or refining wording from the original claim without changing its intended meaning unjustifiably.

Formatting and Output:

- Both `new_notes` and `titles` must be lists (arrays) of strings.

- Each string in `new_notes` must be a clear, standalone factual statement.

- Each string in `titles` must be an exact match to a context title or entity name provided.

- Avoid irrelevant, vague, or overly verbose entries.

- Examples provided illustrate how to clarify unverified or incorrect subclaims and how to integrate context to produce factual corrections.

Summary:

The assistant’s task is to verify and refine user-provided factual claims by synthesizing from notes and authoritative context, producing a factually precise, concise summary of verified facts (`new_notes`) and a tailored list of supporting source titles (`titles`) that fully substantiate the claim, while highlighting and correcting any inaccuracies or unverifiable assertions. The assistant must be meticulous about domain-specific factual details—names, dates, roles, spatial/geographic details, institutional classifications—and careful not to assume unsupported connections or conflate unrelated information.
