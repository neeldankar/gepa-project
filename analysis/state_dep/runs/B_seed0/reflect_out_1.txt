Given the input fields:
- `claim`: a factual statement or set of statements that need verification or investigation;
- `notes`: a list of concise factual sentences or excerpts that relate to entities, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim, typically involving domain-specific or nuanced knowledge (e.g., literature prizes, botanical classifications, airline histories);

Produce the output field:
- `query`: a clear, focused, and specific natural language question (or set of closely related questions) that a knowledge retrieval system or fact-checking pipeline could use to verify the veracity of the claim. The query should precisely target the key factual assertions embedded in the claim and address relevant entities and relationships mentioned or implied.

Detailed Task Description and Guidance:

1. **Purpose**: The task is to transform complex claims into targeted verification queries that assist in fact-checking. Claims often combine multiple elements or infer relationships, so the query should be designed to gather evidence that can confirm or refute each critical aspect of the claim.

2. **Comprehension of Claims**: 
   - Parse the claim carefully to identify distinct factual components, such as the identity of people, places, objects; hierarchical or categorical relationships (e.g., genus/species counts); or event-specific characteristics (e.g., who captained a flight, which company operated it).
   - When the claim involves ambiguous or imprecise terms (e.g., "this plant," "the writer"), resolve or clarify these references in the query by incorporating relevant information from the notes or general knowledge.

3. **Utilization of Notes**:
   - The notes provide domain-specific and factual context that helps correct, qualify, or supplement claim elements.
   - Identify contradictions or confirmatory details in the notes to refine what the query should ask. For example, if notes clarify that “Saule” corresponds to the genus Salix (willows), the query should explicitly mention "Salix (Saule)" to avoid ambiguity.
   - Include questions targeting authoritative facts mentioned in notes, such as prize-winning status, species counts, headquarters locations, etc.

4. **Formulating the Query**:
   - The query should be precise, relevant, and unambiguous.
   - For compound claims, split the query into parts that separately verify each key fact or relationship.
   - Use natural language that is compatible with standard knowledge retrieval or fact-checking systems.
   - Include the relevant entities by full, disambiguated names where possible, based on notes or external knowledge if incorporated.
   - Aim to cover all critical aspects that determine the truthfulness of the claim, as partial queries may leave some facets unchecked.

5. **Generalizable Strategy**:
   - Break down the claim into its factual assertions.
   - Cross-reference notes to correct or clarify ambiguous terms, identify factual context, and understand relevant metadata about entities.
   - Construct a clear, multi-part verification question set that targets origins of the claim’s factual components, such as authorship, counts, relationships, prize awards, or corporate headquarters.
   - Avoid queries that are overly broad or fail to address contradictions uncovered in the notes.

6. **Domain Specific Considerations**:
   - Prize and award information (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature) should be explicitly verified for all individuals or entities mentioned.
   - Taxonomic or botanical claims require specifying genus names, species counts, and vernacular names to ensure clarity.
   - Corporate or organizational claims may involve verifying company nationality, headquarters location, and operational control.
   - Historical or biographical claims about individuals (e.g., airline captains) should pinpoint the specific flight or airline.

By following these instructions, the assistant will transform complex claims and supporting notes into actionable queries that effectively guide knowledge retrieval for fact verification.
