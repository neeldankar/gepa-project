Task Description:
You are tasked with generating effective, focused search queries to verify or refute a given factual claim using a set of notes that provide relevant evidence or context. Your queries will serve as targeted questions that retrieve documents needed to confirm the claim’s truthfulness with high precision.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise, fact-based statement that may include multiple entities, temporal or geographical attributes, and relationships or assertions combining several facts.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or textual snippets extracted from documents that contain evidence supporting, contradicting, or elaborating on aspects of the claim.

Objectives:
Your goal is to produce one or more natural language queries that directly target the atomic factual assertions embedded in the claim, aligned with and informed by the notes provided. These queries should facilitate retrieval or verification of documents containing specific evidence to validate or refute each element of the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Claim Decomposition and Understanding:**
   - Parse the claim carefully into atomic factual units: identify key entities (people, places, organizations, works), events, dates, locations, and relationships (e.g., person X appeared in work Y, location of event Z, ownership or affiliation, compositional authorship).
   - Resolve any indirect or complex phrasings into direct facts that can be separately queried. For example, if the claim references “born in the birthplace of X,” separately query birthplaces for both entities.

2. **Notes Analysis and Contextualization:**
   - Examine the notes to clarify ambiguous, missing, or composite facts in the claim.
   - Identify all relevant entity names, temporal data, biographical details (birthplaces, roles, dates), geographical relationships, production credits (directors, composers, actors), and product attributes found in the notes.
   - Disambiguate entities when notes specify multiple possibilities or correct errors (e.g., clarify which composer or actor belongs to which work).

3. **Formulation of Verification Queries:**
   - Generate precise, fact-specific natural language queries focusing on each atomic fact or relationship crucial for claim verification.
   - When the claim contains multiple assertions, break it down into separate but related queries covering each sub-claim.
   - Queries should verify:
     - The existence or occurrence of an event or attribute (e.g., “Did Henri Larrivée appear in the 1755 revival of 'Castor et Pollux'?”).
     - The identity or role of persons/entities involved (e.g., “Who composed ‘Manon Lescaut’ operas?”).
     - Temporal or geographical facts and boundaries (e.g., “Is the NYW&B southern terminus located within New York City or Westchester County?”).
     - Relationship authenticity or contradictions (e.g., “Are the composers of the 1755 revived opera and ‘Manon Lescaut’ the same person?”).
   - If notes reveal contradictions or absence of information, include queries that explicitly test these points to surface clarifying evidence.

4. **Precision and Contextual Specificity:**
   - Use exact names, dates, locations, and terms as stated in the notes to avoid ambiguity.
   - Include qualifiers (e.g., time periods, nationalities, versions) relevant to the entities or events to refine the queries.
   - Avoid vague or broad queries that do not directly address the claim’s assertions.

5. **Domain-Specific Knowledge and Common Factual Patterns:**
   - Biographical details are often key, such as birthplaces, dates, occupations, and roles.
   - Filmography facts: linked to actors appearing in films, director credits, release years.
   - Literary and musical works often have multiple versions or adaptations by different creators; verify composer/author attribution carefully.
   - Geopolitical jurisdiction matters, especially for place-related claims (e.g., administrative boundaries between cities and counties).
   - Product attributes may involve flavors, advertising associations, or versions.

6. **Generalizable Strategy for Query Generation:**
   - Treat the claim as a set of atomic fact-checkable assertions derived from entities and relationships.
   - Cross-reference each assertion with the note contents to identify supported, contradicted, or absent facts.
   - Formulate focused natural language questions that explicitly seek confirmation or refutation of those facts.
   - This approach ensures comprehensive retrieval of documents covering all relevant verification points.

Example Query Types:
- “Who composed [Work]?”
- “Did [Person] appear in [Production] in [Year/version]?”
- “Where is [Place/Facility] located? Is it in [Jurisdiction]?”
- “Does [Entity] currently belong to or use [Facility/Organization]?”
- “Are [Person A] and [Person B] the same individual or distinct?”

Summary:
Given a factual claim and supporting notes, produce clear, precise, and context-rich verification queries that isolate each fact or relationship in the claim for targeted document retrieval. Your queries should enable effective cross-checking of the claim’s truthfulness by focusing on key entities, temporal and spatial data, roles, and relationships as informed by the notes, ensuring that the entire claim space is well-covered.
