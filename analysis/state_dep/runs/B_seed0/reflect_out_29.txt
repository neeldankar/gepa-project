You will be given three fields as input:
- `claim`: a statement or assertion that may have multiple components requiring factual verification.
- `notes`: a collection of extracted sentences or points relevant to verifying or analyzing the claim.
- `context`: a list of document titles and their summarized content or excerpts that provide background knowledge potentially relevant to the claim.

Your task is to analyze these inputs and generate two output fields:

1. `new_notes`:
   - Produce a concise, coherent, and non-redundant set of notes derived from the input `notes` and supported by `context`.
   - These notes should directly address the claim by summarizing the factual evidence found, clearly indicating what aspects of the claim are supported, refuted, or lack sufficient evidence.
   - Remove duplicated or repetitive information and focus on clarity and relevance.
   - When possible, include explicitly stated factual details from the `context` that underpin or contradict the claim.
   - Avoid speculation beyond the information provided, and highlight any missing or unsupported elements when verifying parts of the claim.

2. `titles`:
   - List the titles of documents from the `context` that are most relevant to substantiating or refuting the claim.
   - Include all key entities (persons, works, species, places, or concepts) mentioned in the claim or notes that appear in the context.
   - Aim to retrieve all gold standard or essential documents relevant to the claim to support a complete and accurate assessment.
   
In your reasoning (though not output directly), your approach should:

- Break down the claim into components or sub-claims.
- Use the `context` to verify facts such as:
   - Authorship or creators of works related to the claim.
   - Nationalities or origins of individuals mentioned.
   - Biological species' native habitats or characteristics.
   - Dates and production details of works or events.
- Cross-validate these facts with the `notes` to confirm consistency or identify contradictions.
- Document explicitly when evidence for a claim component is missing or inconclusive.
- Ensure accurate linkage of entities (people, titles, species) from the claim and notes to corresponding context documents for reliable referencing.

Additional domain-specific considerations:

- Recognize that music-related claims often involve band members, song titles, album names, and release dates.
- Film-related claims typically rely on actor filmographies, production details, and nationality or origin of actors.
- Botanical or biological claims depend on precise habitat or distribution information of species.
- When evaluating nationality or origin in claims, pay attention to geopolitical or cultural specifics mentioned (e.g., British vs. Australian).
- Avoid confusing similarly named entities (e.g., 'Vines' the band vs. 'Vine's Expository Dictionary').

The outputs should enable someone to understand the factual judgment of the claim, supported by well-chosen evidence and references to relevant source materials.
