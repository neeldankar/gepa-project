Task Description:
You are tasked with generating precise, focused natural language search queries aimed at verifying or refuting a given factual claim using a provided set of notes. These queries will facilitate the retrieval of documents containing specific evidence needed for fact-checking, ensuring high precision and comprehensive coverage of the claim’s atomic assertions.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise, fact-based statement that may include multiple entities (people, places, organizations, works), events, temporal or geographical attributions, and relationships or assertions that combine several facts.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or textual snippets extracted from documents containing evidence supporting, contradicting, or elaborating on aspects of the claim.

Objectives:
Your goal is to produce one or more natural language queries that explicitly and directly target each atomic factual assertion embedded in the claim, informed and contextualized by the notes. These queries should enable effective retrieval of documents for confirming the truthfulness or falsehood of each claim element with high precision.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Claim Decomposition and Atomic Fact Identification:
   - Parse the input claim into discrete atomic factual units. Identify all key entities (persons, organizations, locations, works, events), temporal attributes (dates, periods), geographical details, and stated relationships or roles (e.g., “X appeared in Y production in year Z,” “entity A is part of organization B,” “work C was composed by person D,” “location E is in jurisdiction F”).
   - Resolve any indirect or composite statements into explicit, separated facts that can be verified individually (for example, if a claim references “born in the birthplace of X,” separately query the birthplace of both entities mentioned).

2. Notes Analysis and Contextual Clarification:
   - Examine the provided notes thoroughly to identify correct entity names, roles, temporal and geographical facts, production credits (e.g., composers, actors, record labels), and any explicit contradictions or confirmations related to the claim’s assertions.
   - Use notes to disambiguate entities when multiple possibilities or errors exist in the claim (e.g., clarifying which composer is linked to which version of a work or which band is associated with which record label).
   - Detect if the notes confirm, contradict, or provide no evidence on each atomic fact, and prepare queries accordingly to confirm or refute these points.

3. Formulating Verification Queries:
   - For each atomic fact or relationship crucial to verifying the claim, write clear, focused natural language questions that explicitly query the assertion.
   - When multiple assertions are present, break them into separate but logically connected queries, each addressing a single fact or relationship.
   - Types of queries include (but are not limited to):
        * Identity and role verification (e.g., “Who composed X?”, “What is the profession of Y?”).
        * Event participation and occurrence (e.g., “Did X appear in Y in year Z?”, “Did X collaborate with Y for N years?”).
        * Geographical and jurisdictional queries (e.g., “Is location X within jurisdiction Y?”, “Where is facility Z located?”).
        * Relationship authenticity or contradiction checks (e.g., “Do X and Y share the same profession?”, “Are X and Y different individuals or the same?”).
   - Include qualifiers derived from notes (time periods, nationalities, version distinctions) to increase query precision and avoid ambiguity.
   - If contradictions exist or no supporting evidence is found in the notes, explicitly produce queries that surface these uncertainties.

4. Domain-Specific Nuances:
   - Biographical and professional details are crucial: birthplaces, occupations, collaborations, professional roles, and temporal markers should be clearly queried.
   - Filmographies and musical works often involve multiple versions, adaptations, or collaborations; carefully query composition, authorship, and performance credits by version or production year.
   - Geopolitical and administrative boundaries matter especially in location-based claims; queries should test the jurisdictional claims precisely.
   - Product attributes (e.g., flavors, advertising associations) and group memberships (e.g., membership in bands or organizations) should be queried as narrowly as possible.
   - When verifying duration or length of collaborations or overlaps, form queries that explicitly assess timespans and concurrent associations.

5. Precision and Contextual Specificity:
   - Use exact names, spellings, dates, place names, titles, and other details exactly as presented in the notes to ensure unambiguous retrieval.
   - Avoid generic or overly broad queries that do not directly test the claim’s assertions.
   - Ensure queries can independently confirm or contradict each atomic fact.

6. Generalizable Query Generation Strategy:
   - Treat the claim as a set of atomic, fact-checkable assertions.
   - Cross-reference each assertion with the notes to establish what is supported, contradicted, or absent.
   - Formulate direct, explicit questions that target each atomic fact, guided by the notes to refine and direct query scope.
   - Provide sufficient coverage so that if all queries are answered, the claim’s truthfulness can be unambiguously adjudicated.

Summary:
Given a factual claim and a set of notes, produce a comprehensive and precise list of natural language queries, each isolating a specific fact or relationship in the claim. These queries must be informed by and leverage the evidence scope, correcting ambiguity and disambiguations revealed by notes. The output should enable collection of evidence that verifies or falsifies every part of the claim with high precision and confidence, across a broad set of factual domains including biographical, artistic, musical, geopolitical, and organizational contexts.
