Task Description:
You will be given a concise, factual claim and a list of notes containing factual sentences or snippets extracted from documents relevant to verifying the claim. Your task is to generate one or more precise, natural-language search queries that effectively target the key facts, entities, events, attributes, and relationships in the claim. These queries will be used to retrieve or verify documents that confirm or refute each core assertion of the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A short, fact-based statement or assertion that may include multiple related facts or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or short information pieces extracted from one or more documents. These notes serve as supporting evidence, conflicting information, or contextual background pertinent to the claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. Understand and decompose the claim:
   - Identify the main entities (people, places, works, products), attributes (birthdates, roles, flavors, release years), and relationships (acted alongside, voiced, produced by, release order).
   - Break down compound or multi-part claims into atomic factual units for targeted verification.
   - Resolve indirect or inferential references in the claim into direct factual relationships; for example, instead of "born in the birthplace of X," identify the birthplace of both entities explicitly.

2. Leverage the notes to enrich and clarify:
   - Extract relevant factual information about entities, dates, film titles, roles, productions, or relationships present in the notes.
   - Use notes to identify corrections, disambiguations, or missing facts that affect claim verification.
   - Detect contradictions or gaps revealed in the notes to guide formulation of verification queries.

3. Formulate precise verification queries:
   - Queries should explicitly ask about each atomic fact or relationship necessary to verify the claim.
   - Address each part of the claim separately if multiple assertions are present.
   - Incorporate entity disambiguation, e.g., specify full names, film years, or production companies to avoid ambiguity.
   - Include relational queries such as "Did Actor X star alongside Actor Y in Film Z?", "Was Film A produced by Company B?", or "Was Person C born before Person D?"
   - When notes reveal contradictions or uncertainty, include queries explicitly designed to confirm or refute those uncertainties.

4. Contextual specificity and domain knowledge:
   - Use exact names, spellings, and titles as found in the notes (e.g., full names of actors, exact film titles with release years).
   - Apply domain-specific knowledge where relevant:
     * Biographical facts: birthplaces, birthdates, occupations.
     * Filmography: actors’ roles, co-stars, directors, production companies, film genres, release years.
     * Product facts: flavors, advertising relationships.
   - Time-related facts (e.g., release dates, birth years) should be explicitly addressed to verify chronological claims.

5. Generalizable verification strategy:
   - Parse the claim into clear, discrete factual units.
   - Cross-reference these units with the notes to identify which facts are supported, contradicted, or missing.
   - Develop natural, targeted, and fact-specific queries that foster retrieval or verification of all relevant supporting or refuting documents.
   - This ensures maximal evidence coverage and accurate verification of each assertion.

Examples of types of queries to generate:
- Verification of roles:  
  "Did Travis Oates voice Piglet in the 2011 Winnie the Pooh movie?"  
  "Did Gary Oldman star alongside Demi Moore and Meryl Streep in a 1995 American romantic drama?"
- Verification of production or release details:  
  "Was the 1995 film 'The Scarlet Letter' produced by Cinergi Pictures?"  
  "Was 'The Jungle Book' released before the 2011 Winnie the Pooh movie?"
- Verification of birthdates or birthplaces:  
  "Was Darren Stanley Hayes born before Darren Middleton, lead guitarist of Powderfinger?"
- Verification of compound or relationship claims by breaking into individual factual queries to cover each aspect explicitly.

Purpose:
These queries are intended to guide information retrieval systems to fetch documents that either substantiate or refute the claim comprehensively. Precision, disambiguation, and coverage of all factual units in the claim maximize verification effectiveness and retrieval success.

Summary:
Given a claim and related factual notes, output fact-specific, verification-focused natural language queries targeting key entities, relationships, and temporal facts. Use domain-specific insights, clarifications, and corrections from the notes to structure queries that uncover complete and accurate evidence for claim validation.
