Task Overview:
You will be given a concise factual claim and a set of notes containing factual information related to the claim. Your goal is to generate one or more natural language search queries that aim to verify the factual correctness of the claim by retrieving or validating evidence against the information contained in the notes. These queries will be used as search inputs to retrieve relevant documents or evidence to confirm or refute each part of the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A fact-based assertion that often includes specific entities (persons, places, works), attributes (birth dates, nationalities, occupations, flavors), events (film releases, TV show air dates), and relationships (who starred in what, birthplaces, roles played, product attributes). Claims can be compound, containing multiple assertions.
- `notes`: A list of sentences or snippets extracting factual information relevant to the claim. Notes may provide clarifications, corrections, disambiguations, temporal details, or deny certain relationships or facts present in the claim.

Detailed Task Description:
1. Parse the Claim:
   - Identify core factual units within the claim: entities, attributes, relationships.
   - Resolve indirect references or relational claims into direct facts for verification (e.g., when birthplaces are referenced via others).
   - Break compound claims into atomic factual assertions that each require separate validation.

2. Analyze the Notes:
   - Identify named entities (people, films, series, products, brands).
   - Extract temporal data (release years, premiere dates, birthdates).
   - Detect asserted relationships in the notes (actor-film role, director-film association, birth months).
   - Note contradictions, corrections, or lack of evidence found in the notes.
   - Include domain-specific context such as film genres (western, neo-western), television series roles, product flavors, nationality and occupational roles (actor, professor, operative).

3. Formulate Verification Queries:
   - Create natural language questions that directly target each factual assertion for verification.
   - Separate multiple assertions into distinct but related queries.
   - Include questions that disambiguate or test contradictory claims explicitly (e.g., “Did Person X star in Film Y?”, “Is Film Z directed by Person A?”, or “Was Actor B born in Month M?”).
   - Use exact names, titles, and date qualifiers from the notes to avoid ambiguity.
   - Tailor questions according to domain context: 
     * For filmography claims: verify actor roles, director credits, release/air dates.
     * For biographical claims: confirm birth dates/places, nationality, secondary occupations.
     * For product claims: confirm attributes like flavor variants or advertising associations.

4. Precision and Contextuality:
   - Avoid overly broad or generic questions; queries should specifically target claim assertions with contextual detail.
   - Incorporate corrections or contradictions in notes by including explicit verification queries.
   - Generate queries sufficient to retrieve all relevant gold evidence documents necessary for claim verification.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Decompose the claim into discrete facts.
- Cross-reference notes to understand which facts are supported, contradicted, or missing.
- Generate focused queries that aim to retrieve or verify those individual facts.
- Queries should be comprehensive enough to enable retrieval of evidence for each atomic fact involved.

Domain-Specific Insights:
- Biographical details: Birthplaces, birth months/years, nationalities, occupations (actor, professor, operative).
- Filmography and TV shows: Actor starring roles, director credits, series premiere dates, film release years, genre classifications.
- Product attributes: Flavors, advertising figures or campaigns.
- Corrective or contradictory information must be addressed explicitly in query form.
- Exact naming conventions and temporal qualifiers ensure precision and relevance.

Output Format:
- `query`: One or multiple natural language questions clearly formulated to verify the individual factual components of the claim, informed by the notes.

Summary:
Your output should be a set of precise, verification-focused natural language queries that target the claim’s distinct factual elements using the contextual clues and detailed information found within the notes. These queries aid in retrieving all pertinent verification evidence, ensuring thorough validation or refutation of the claim.
