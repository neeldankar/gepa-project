Task Description:  
You are given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to analyze these inputs to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`. 

Input Fields:  
- `claim`: A statement that may contain factual assertions to be verified or contextualized. It may include references to people, places, events, dates, objects, or other specific entities and relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of sentences providing extracted relevant information from external sources or previous processing steps. These often contain supporting, contradictory, or clarifying facts related to the claim, sometimes referencing specific entities, attributes, or relations.  
- `context`: A list of encyclopedic style short descriptions or document snippets referencing entities mentioned in the claim or notes, often with detailed background information.

Output Fields:  
1. `new_notes`: A curated and concise list of key factual points distilled from the given notes and context that are relevant to verifying or explaining the claim. This should:  
   - Confirm or refute key factual aspects of the claim with evidence.  
   - Include clarifications or corrections of misinformation in the claim.  
   - Summarize critical entity relationships and attributes that affect the truth or understanding of the claim.  
   - Remove redundancies and irrelevancies present in the original `notes` while preserving important evidence.  
   - Be written in clear, factual, neutral language, suitable for understanding the claim’s factual accuracy.  

2. `titles`: A list of document or entity titles extracted from the `context` that are most relevant to the verification or explanation of the claim. These should:  
   - Include all key entities directly involved in the claim.  
   - Include any additional entities or documents necessary to understand the claim’s context or background.  
   - Exclude unrelated or marginally relevant titles.

Important Domain-Specific and Task-Specific Guidelines:  
- Claims often involve biographical data (dates, professions, educational ties), geospatial relations (locations of cemeteries, parks, historical sites), product and platform origins (computer models, software platforms, game titles), or historical and scientific facts.  
- Context documents may include ambiguous or similarly named entities; carefully distinguish between homonyms or similarly named entries (e.g., similarly named cemeteries, people).  
- Where claims combine multiple assertions (e.g., a biographical fact plus an educational link), address each sub-claim separately in reasoning and notes.  
- When the claim or notes present errors or inaccuracies (e.g., wrong profession, mixing two distinct entities), explicitly correct or clarify with evidence from context.  
- Always ground your `new_notes` in the provided `context` or `notes`; do not introduce unverifiable facts.  
- Use the `notes` as a starting point but prioritize factual accuracy and completeness over mere repetition or listing.  
- For `titles`, include all relevant subject headings that would be needed to retrieve or understand all parts of the claim.  
- In reasoning steps (for your internal process), confirm how these pieces fit together to support or refute the claim, but your outputs should only present the refined notes and titles.

Generalizable Strategy:  
1. Parse the claim into discrete factual assertions.  
2. Cross-reference claim assertions with available notes and context details.  
3. Extract and condense relevant facts that either directly support or refute the claim.  
4. Identify all key entities mentioned or implied, ensuring their corresponding context documents are represented in the `titles`.  
5. Ensure clarity and precision in wording, avoiding any irrelevant or redundant information.

By following these instructions, your output will offer a clear, structured, and evidence-based factual summary that aids in verifying or explaining the claim, while highlighting essential documents for further reference.
