Task Description:
You will receive a concise factual claim and a set of notes that contain factual statements, context, or potential refutations related to the claim. Your objective is to generate precise, effective natural language search queries that can be used to verify the truthfulness of the claim by (1) querying the notes for relevant evidence, or (2) retrieving additional documents that confirm or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A short factual statement that may include multiple linked facts, entities, events, or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or snippets extracted from various sources that relate to the entities and assertions in the claim. These may provide confirmations, contradictions, or no relevant evidence.

Guidelines and Detailed Instructions:

1. **Thoroughly Analyze the Claim**  
   - Identify all key elements such as people, places, works (films, teams, products), dates, roles, and attributes mentioned in the claim.  
   - If the claim uses indirect or complex references (e.g., a person "born in the birthplace of X"), resolve them into explicit, verifiable facts (e.g., exact birthplaces of both individuals).  
   - Parse the claim into atomic fact units whenever it contains multiple assertions to verify each independently.

2. **Use the Notes to Clarify and Contextualize**  
   - Review the notes to extract corroborating or conflicting information related to the claim’s entities and assertions. These may include:  
     * Biographical data (birthplaces, nationalities, dates of birth)  
     * Filmography details (film titles, release years, actors, directors, voice roles)  
     * Product features and advertising attributes  
     * Corrections or disclaimers that contradict or clarify the claim  
   - Use names, dates, and relationships exactly as presented in the notes to avoid ambiguity.

3. **Formulate Verification-Focused Queries**  
   - For each atomic fact or relationship, compose one or more targeted natural language questions aimed at confirming or refuting it.  
   - When the claim includes multiple linked facts, separate them into discrete queries each targeting a single assertion.  
   - Include questions that test for the presence or absence of links between entities (e.g., whether a particular actor starred in a film, whether a character is associated with a real person, whether a known alias corresponds to a professional team name).  
   - Ask explicitly about any contradictions or lacking evidence identified in the notes (e.g., "Is X associated with Y?", "Did Z direct the film W?").

4. **Precision and Contextualization—Avoid General Queries**  
   - Use the exact terminology, entity names, and relationships from the notes and claim (including qualifiers like dates, nationality, or film versions) to disambiguate entities.  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries that may retrieve irrelevant documents.  
   - Focus queries on the core factual claims essential to verify the claim’s overall truthfulness.

5. **Niche and Domain-Specific Knowledge Integration**  
   - Claims often draw from domains such as:  
     * Film and entertainment: actors, directors, voice actors, character names, release years, film titles, nationality of stars  
     * Sports: player names, teams, nicknames or colloquial team names, match details, goal scorers, tournament data  
     * Product attributes: flavors, advertising associations, brand history  
   - Pay close attention to corrections or disambiguations in notes (e.g., a person identified as an American football player instead of a soccer player) and generate queries reflecting these critical distinctions.  
   - Where real names and aliases or fictional character names are involved, separate queries to identify:  
     * The real identity behind an alias  
     * Whether that alias/character is related to the films or people in question  
   - For biographical or filmographic claims, queries should check birthplace, roles (star, director, voice actor), co-occurrence in films, and year associations to triangulate evidence.

6. **Generalizable Strategy for Query Generation**  
   - Break down a complex claim into elemental factual statements.  
   - Cross-reference the notes to determine which facts are supported, contradicted, or unsubstantiated.  
   - Generate fact-specific questions aimed explicitly at verifying each component via document retrieval or note querying, maximizing evidence retrieval.  
   - Cover all potentially ambiguous or disputed points exposed by notes, so no part of the claim is left unverifiable.

Summary:
Given a claim and related notes, your task is to produce a set of precise, targeted natural language search queries. These queries must be designed to retrieve or verify documents that confirm or refute each atomic fact composing the claim. Leverage all biographical, filmographic, sports-related, or product attribute details in the notes to formulate verification-driven, disambiguated, and well-scoped queries. This ensures that the retrieved evidence and verification process is comprehensive and factually grounded.
