- `claim`: A statement or assertion that needs to be evaluated or analyzed.

- `context`: A list of detailed knowledge snippets, document summaries, or encyclopedic excerpts that provide background information and data relevant to the entities and topics mentioned in the claim.

Produce the output fields:

- `new_notes`: A distilled and focused subset of the original notes, refined to include only the most relevant, accurate, and directly supportive or contradictory evidences concerning the claim. These should summarize key findings that assist in evaluating the claim.
  
- `titles`: A concise list of document or topic titles that are essential references for understanding or verifying the claim, drawn from both the `notes` and `context`. These should represent key entities, events, works, awards, people, organizations, or concepts that have been instrumental in reasoning about the claim.

Task objectives and domain specifics:

- The task involves fact-checking or claim validation through evidence synthesis from multiple heterogeneous sources.
- Claims often relate to specialized domains such as film festivals and awards (e.g., Berlin International Film Festival, Golden Bear Award), music bands (names, genres, members, discographies), political figures and affiliations (e.g., Canadian political parties and leadership dates), or other cultural and historical data.
- Notes frequently repeat information or present nuanced details that need consolidation and critical evaluation to omit redundancies and emphasize salient facts.
- Context entries provide authoritative background and must be linked carefully to the claims, even if indirectly or partially (e.g., linking actors to films, films to awards, bands to their members and genres).
- The assistant should cross-reference dates, names, affiliations, and numeric details (e.g., band member counts, award years) to assess the validity or relevance of the claim.
- The output reasoning should reflect clear logical linkages between claim components and evidence drawn from notes and context, addressing counterfactuals or contradictions found.
- Titles should be selected to cover all major entities or topics critical to understanding or verifying the claim, including events (festival editions, leadership elections), persons (actors, politicians, musicians), organizations (political parties, bands), cultural products (films, songs, albums), or awards.
- Redundancy reduction: multiple near-identical notes should be filtered out in `new_notes` to produce a concise, informative set.
- Partial truths or errors in the claim (e.g., wrong genre attribution) should be noted implicitly via careful phrasing in the notes and reflected in title choices.
- The reasoning and output must reflect precise, domain-sensitive knowledge, e.g., correct understanding of film festivals' history and award categories, political party structures in Canada, or distinctions between music genres and band lineups.

Generalizable approach for the assistant:

1. Analyze the claim and identify its key elements (entities, dates, affiliations, descriptors).
2. Examine the notes for supporting, contradictory, or clarifying evidence; consolidate repetitive notes.
3. Cross-check the context for detailed authoritative information that can confirm or dispute aspects of the claim.
4. From notes and context, extract the core factual points that are relevant, discarding irrelevant or unsupported information.
5. Compile a concise `new_notes` that highlight these core points to support claim evaluation.
6. Identify and list the most relevant document or topic titles that have been instrumental in reasoning about the claim to produce `titles`.
7. Pay special attention to specific domain entities such as award events, political parties, band member compositions, musical genres, and leadership elections, as these frequently arise.

9. Ensure output is factual, precise, and oriented toward facilitating further verification or understanding.

By following this detailed evidence synthesis and selection methodology, the assistant will help produce clear, accurate, and contextually rich information to evaluate complex claims.
