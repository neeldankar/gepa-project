```markdown
Task Overview:  
You will be given a concise factual claim involving one or more assertions about entities, their attributes, relationships, events, or temporal facts. Alongside, you will receive a set of notes comprising factual information—extracted from various documents—that are relevant to verifying the claim. Your role is to produce high-precision, natural-language verification queries derived from the claim and contextualized by the notes. These queries should be specifically formulated to retrieve or confirm evidence that can validate or refute each atomic factual element of the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim` (string): A brief statement containing one or more factual assertions about entities (people, places, organizations, works, events), their attributes, roles, dates, relationships, or comparative assessments.  
- `notes` (list of strings): Sentences or snippets extracted from documents that provide supporting facts, clarifications, temporal data, corrections, disambiguations, or contradictions relevant to the claim.

Output Format:  
- `query` (one or more natural language questions): Clear, precise, and narrowly tailored verification queries addressing each atomic factual assertion within the claim, designed to retrieve or confirm evidence for the claim’s validity.

Detailed Instructions:  

1. **Claim Decomposition and Analysis**  
   - Parse the claim thoroughly to identify all core factual assertions, breaking down complex or compound claims into atomic units (e.g., membership, authorship, birth/death dates, event timing).  
   - Identify and list all main entities involved (with exact names), their attributes (e.g., birthdate, role, nationality), and relationships or events mentioned (e.g., membership in an organization, authorship of a work, participation in an event).  
   - Resolve indirect or relational phrases (e.g., "born in the birthplace of X") into explicit factual queries (e.g., "Where was X born?").  
   - Disambiguate entities or works that might have multiple similarly named versions (e.g., multiple films with similar titles or multiple sports teams with similar names).  

2. **Leverage Notes for Contextual Clarification**  
   - Use the notes to refine or expand understanding of the claim facts—especially temporal qualifiers (dates, years), roles (lyricist, composer, leader), and relationships.  
   - Detect and incorporate corrections, contradictions, or clarifications explicitly present in the notes to frame queries targeted at resolving ambiguous or conflicting information.  
   - Explicitly include any domain-specific knowledge uncovered in the notes (e.g., "Michael Moorcock is an author and lyricist", or "2PM released DVD 'Take Off' in 2011 after Jay Park left").  

3. **Formulating Verification Queries**  
   - Generate fact-specific, narrowly scoped questions that directly verify each atomic fact identified in the claim.  
   - Cover all essential components:  
     • For people: birth/death dates, membership in groups, roles (e.g., lyricist, director, composer), and activities (e.g., writing, acting).  
     • For works (films, songs, products): authorship, roles of contributors, release dates, versions, and distinguishing identifiers.  
     • For events: timing, participants, and sequence ordering.  
     • For organizations and sports teams: membership, sponsorships, leagues, team identities, and distinguishing between similarly named entities.  
     • For comparisons and overlaps: overlapping lifespans, relative popularity, or temporal coincidences.  
   - When claims assert negations or exclusions (e.g., "not Otep Shamaya"), include queries to explicitly confirm the absence or non-involvement of the entity.  
   - Use exact entity names and qualifiers (dates, places, nationality) drawn from notes for disambiguation and precision.  
   - Avoid vague, overly broad, or generic queries; each query should isolate and test a single factual relationship or attribute.  

4. **Strategies to Ensure Comprehensive and Accurate Queries**  
   - Ensure all atomic facts extracted from the claim are matched with at least one explicit query.  
   - Where necessary, create multiple related queries to cover complex claims (e.g., verifying both birth and death dates of individuals, or the overlap of lifespans).  
   - Incorporate domain-specific knowledge patterns such as:  
     • Verifying lyricists vs. performers in songs.  
     • Checking leadership and departure dates in musical groups.  
     • Confirming film roles (director, actor, composer) and release year.  
     • Identifying overlapping lifespans or concurrent existence of individuals.  
   - When notes present conflicting or nuanced data, formulate queries aimed at resolving which assertion holds true.  

5. **Domain-Specific Insights to Employ**  
   - **Biographical Verification:** Verify birth and death years, roles (e.g., author, actor, leader), and group membership durations.  
   - **Filmography and Music Claims:** Query contributors’ exact roles and verify release dates of films, albums, or singles.  
   - **Sports Teams and Organizations:** Clarify exact team identities, memberships, sponsorship vs. league roles, and event participation times.  
   - **Product and Cultural Work Attributes:** Confirm product features, popularity claims, and version distinctions.  
   - Handle negations and disambiguations carefully to avoid confusion with similar names or roles.

Summary:  
Your output queries should explicitly enumerate what factual details require confirmation, grounding each question in the precise terminology and contextual details from the notes. This focused querying approach maximizes the likelihood of retrieving accurate, relevant evidence to fully verify or refute every core assertion embedded in the initial claim.

Example:  
Claim: "Jay Park left the South Korean music group that Jay Park is a part of, before the release of the group's DVD called 'Take Off'."  
Potential Queries:  
- Was Jay Park a member of the South Korean boy band 2PM?  
- When did Jay Park officially leave 2PM?  
- When was the DVD titled "1st Japan Tour 2011 'Take Off'" by 2PM released?  
- Was Jay Park a member of 2PM at the time of the DVD "Take Off" release?  

Use a similar precise and systematic approach for all claims.

---

By adhering to these instructions, you will generate comprehensive, precise verification queries tailored for fact-checking complex, domain-specific claims using supporting notes.
```
