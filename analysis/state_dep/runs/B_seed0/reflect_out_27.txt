Task Description:
You are provided with a concise factual claim and a list of notes containing factual information relevant to the claim. Your objective is to generate natural-language verification queries that directly target the core facts and relationships in the claim by leveraging the information and context present in the notes. These queries are intended to be used to retrieve or verify evidence documents that confirm or refute the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise statement containing one or more factual assertions involving entities, attributes, relationships, or events.
- `notes`: A collection of factual sentences or snippets extracted from documents. These notes provide evidence, clarifications, corrections, or contextual background related to the claim’s content.

Output:
- `query`: One or more precise, fact-specific natural language questions designed to verify each key factual assertion within the claim by querying the relationships and details referenced or implied in the notes.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Guidance:

1. **Claim Analysis and Decomposition**  
   - Carefully parse the claim into atomic factual units or assertions.  
   - Identify all main entities (people, places, works, organizations), attributes (birthplaces, roles, dates), and relationships (membership, authorship, participation) described.  
   - Resolve indirect references or complex relational claims into direct, checkable facts.  
     For example, if the claim refers to “born in the birthplace of X,” identify and verify the birthplaces of both entities.

2. **Leverage Notes for Fact Clarification and Expansion**  
   - Use the notes to disambiguate entities, verify relationships, and gather temporal or contextual qualifiers (years, nationalities, versions).  
   - Detect explicit contradictions or corrections within the notes (e.g., misattributed roles, mistaken sponsorship).  
   - Note any distinctions or confusions between similarly named entities or works referenced in the note (e.g., different football clubs, similarly named films).  

3. **Formulating Verification-Focused Queries**  
   - Generate queries that directly target verification of the claim’s atomic facts, such as:  
     • Membership or affiliation of an entity to a group or league.  
     • Identification of sponsors or organizational roles.  
     • Confirmation of roles in films (director, actor, composer).  
     • Temporal sequencing (e.g., which film was scored first by a composer).  
     • Popularity or recognition comparison when claims involve relative assessments.  
   - Break down complex or compound claims into multiple, related questions addressing each core fact or relationship.  
   - Include explicit entity disambiguation in queries (e.g., confirming identity to avoid confusion between entities with similar names).

4. **Precision and Contextualization**  
   - Use exact names, roles, and terms as provided in the notes to avoid ambiguity.  
   - Include relevant qualifiers such as dates, places, nationalities, or versions to ensure precise retrieval.  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; queries should be narrowly tailored to test the validity of specific claim elements.

5. **Generalizable Strategy for Fact Verification Queries**  
   - Dissect the claim into clear factual units.  
   - Cross-reference the notes to understand which assertions are supported, contradicted, or unconfirmed.  
   - For each unit, formulate a natural language query likely to retrieve supporting or refuting evidence.  
   - This ensures comprehensive coverage of the claim’s verifiability and maximizes relevant evidence retrieval.

Domain-Specific Insights:
- **Biographical Details:** Queries often involve verifying birthplaces, birth years, or occupational roles of persons named in the claim.  
- **Filmography:** Many claims relate to films, including verifying actors’ roles, directors, composers, or film release dates.  
- **Sports Teams and Leagues:** Verify league memberships, sponsorships, and team identities, especially resolving confusions between similarly named clubs and clarifying organizational roles of sponsors vs. leagues.  
- **Product Attributes and Popularity:** Confirm product features (e.g., flavors or variants), or compare popularity and cultural reach, especially for games or cultural works.  
- The notes may contain explicit refutations or ambiguous information; generate queries that test these points directly to resolve contradictions.

Summary:
Given a claim and a set of notes, your role is to generate a set of focused, clear, fact-specific verification queries that will facilitate thorough checking of each factual element within the claim. These queries should make explicit what facts need confirmation and help retrieve comprehensive evidence, thus enabling accurate claim validation.
