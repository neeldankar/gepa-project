Task Description:
You will be given a concise, factual claim that often contains multiple related assertions, and a set of domain-specific notes — factual sentences or snippets extracted from documents pertinent to verifying the claim. Your goal is to generate precise, natural-language search queries that comprehensively target the key facts, entities, relationships, attributes, and temporal details embedded in the claim. These queries will facilitate retrieval of supporting or refuting documents, enabling thorough claim verification.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A short, fact-based statement potentially containing multiple linked facts or relationships. It may reference people, films, shows, products, roles, dates, or other entities with specific attributes or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or short extracts from one or more documents. The notes may support, contradict, or provide background to the claim. They often include domain-specific data such as actor filmographies, character descriptions, production details, genre classifications, birthdates, or release years.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Claim Decomposition and Entity Parsing:
   - Analyze the claim to identify all main entities such as persons, films, TV shows, products, or characters.
   - Extract attributes like birthdates, birthplaces, roles played, production companies, release years, or genre classifications.
   - Identify and explicitly break down compound or multi-part claims into atomic verification units (e.g., separate queries about individual actors, films, production details).
   - Resolve indirect references to direct factual relationships. For example, instead of vague phrases ("born in the birthplace of X"), explicitly identify the birthplaces or dates of each entity mentioned.

2. Leveraging Notes to Enhance Clarity and Accuracy:
   - Scrutinize notes to extract relevant, domain-specific factual information such as actor roles, cast overlaps, film genres, production companies, and other metadata.
   - Use notes to resolve ambiguities, fill missing facts, or detect contradictions that impact verification.
   - If notes reveal discrepancies or lack of evidence for parts of the claim, prepare queries specifically aimed at confirming or refuting these uncertain points.

3. Formulating Precise, Fact-Specific Verification Queries:
   - For each atomic fact or relationship in the claim, write clear, explicit queries targeting that unit.
   - Queries must explicitly mention full entity names, film release years, production details, or distinguishing characteristics to avoid ambiguity (e.g., specify "Max Gail" not just "Gail", or "1996 '101 Dalmatians' live-action film" rather than vague titles).
   - When the claim references relationships (e.g., co-starring, voice acting, production roles), formulate relational verification queries such as:
     * "Did Actor X star alongside Actor Y in Film Z?"
     * "Was Film A produced by Company B?"
     * "Was Person C born before Person D?"
     * "Is Character K featured in Film/Series Q?"
   - Include queries addressing timelines explicitly when the claim references dates, years, or chronological order.

4. Applying Domain-Specific Knowledge:
   - Biographical data may require queries about birthdates, birthplaces, occupations, and known career highlights.
   - Filmography verification involves checking actor roles, cast lists, character-actor mappings, and production/genre information.
   - For product-related claims, confirm flavors, branding, advertising partnerships, or release order.
   - Use common domain knowledge where applicable, such as recognizing that Cruella de Vil is from "101 Dalmatians" and thus referencing the correct title and year.

5. Generalizable Verification Strategy:
   - Parse and decompose the claim into distinct factual assertions.
   - Cross-reference these with contextual notes to identify confirmation, contradiction, or gaps.
   - Generate targeted, domain-aware natural language queries for each fact to ensure full retrieval coverage.
   - Formulate queries in natural language that mimic how a verification search would be conducted (e.g., interrogative sentences phrased for factual look-ups).

6. Query Scope and Coverage:
   - Include all relevant verification points, even if notes suggest negative or contradictory evidence, to ensure comprehensive search coverage.
   - When possible, create queries that capture the presence or absence of facts (e.g., "Is Junaid Khan a judge on Music Icons?" rather than just "Who are the judges?").
   - For claims involving multiple entities or relationships, produce separate queries per entity or relationship.

Example Query Types:
- Role and casting verification:
  * "Did Travis Oates voice Piglet in the 2011 Winnie the Pooh movie?"
  * "Did Gary Oldman star alongside Demi Moore and Meryl Streep in the 1995 American romantic drama 'The Scarlet Letter'?"
- Production or release details:
  * "Was the film 'The Scarlet Letter' (1995) produced by Cinergi Pictures?"
  * "Was 'The Jungle Book' film released before the 2011 Winnie the Pooh movie?"
- Biographical verifications:
  * "Was Darren Stanley Hayes born before Darren Middleton, lead guitarist of Powderfinger?"
- Character-actor mappings:
  * "Is the character Cruella de Vil featured in the Disney animated film '101 Dalmatians' (1961)?"
- Compound relational claims broken down atomically:
  * "Was Meesha Shafi a judge on the show 'Music Icons' as of April 2013?"
  * "Was Junaid Khan a judge on 'Music Icons' or a host for the 4th Lux Style Awards?"
  * "Did the producer and singer of the band Aaina join as a judge on 'Music Icons'?"

Purpose:
Your queries should be designed to enable retrieval of authoritative documents that confirm or refute the claim’s assertions. They must maximize precision and clarity, avoid ambiguity by detailed disambiguation, and ensure all relevant factual elements within the claim are fully searchable.

Summary:
Given a claim and related factual notes, generate fact-specific, verification-driven natural language queries that cover all entities, attributes, relationships, and temporal points within the claim. Leverage provided notes and domain knowledge to identify potential contradictions or gaps and create queries accordingly, ensuring comprehensive evidence discovery for claim validation.
