Task Description:

You are given a concise, fact-based claim and a corresponding collection of notes containing factual sentences or snippets extracted from relevant documents. Your objective is to generate precise, verification-focused natural language queries that can be used to check the truthfulness of the claim or to retrieve additional documents necessary for its verification.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A single or compound factual statement involving one or more entities, events, attributes, or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences, corrections, context, or refutations relating to the claim. These notes often contain critical detail needed to accurately frame verification queries.

Output:
- `query`: One or more natural language questions designed to verify each atomic factual assertion within the claim. These queries must explicitly focus on the key entities, dates, roles, attributes, and relationships in the claim and target evidence drawn or provable from the notes.

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Parse the Claim into Atomic Facts**
   - Break down the claim into its smallest verifiable units (e.g., identifying each entity, event, attribute, relationship).
   - Resolve indirect or implicit references in the claim into direct, explicit facts where possible (e.g., if the claim involves “born in the birthplace of X,” identify the actual birthplaces of both entities to test).

2. **Leverage the Notes to Refine and Focus Queries**
   - Cross-reference entities, dates, titles, and relationships in the claim with those mentioned in the notes.
   - Use notes to disambiguate or correct claim components (e.g., correcting mistaken director or actor attributions).
   - Identify contradictions, missing facts, or additional facts revealed by the notes to formulate queries that explicitly test these points.

3. **Formulate Verification-Oriented Natural Language Queries**
   - For each atomic fact, create a direct question verifying the associated claim aspect.
   - If the claim contains compound assertions, generate multiple, related queries that each verify one key fact or relationship.
   - Use exact names, dates, terms, and qualifiers found in the notes to ensure precision and avoid ambiguity.
   - Include entity disambiguations or relational verifications when there is potential for confusion (e.g., distinguishing between two similarly named persons or confirming a person’s involvement in a specific work).
   - Queries should be narrowly targeted and contextual, avoiding overly generic or broad questioning.

4. **Address Domain-Specific Considerations**
   - Biographical facts: Verify birthplaces, dates, occupations, and participation in events or organizations.
   - Film and TV: Verify actor roles, character names, series premiere dates, season numbering, and director attributions. Confirm if an actor actually played a given character or participated in a specific series/seasons.
   - Sports and Racing: Verify championship winners by season number and year, competitor participation in series, nationality of participants.
   - Educational institutions: Verify membership or affiliation of schools in a district, locations served (e.g., census-designated places).
   - Product attributes: Verify features such as flavors, product variants, advertising endorsements.

5. **General Strategy**
   - Distill the claim into its smallest factual units.
   - Use the notes to detect which facts are supported, contradicted, or missing.
   - Generate one or more targeted natural language questions that directly address each atomic fact needing verification.
   - The queries should enable maximal retrieval or confirmation of relevant documents or evidence to verify or refute the claim.

6. **Verification Questions Should:**
   - Explicitly mention all key entities (using exact names as in notes).
   - Include relevant temporal qualifiers (e.g., season number and year, dates of participation).
   - Clarify relationships (e.g., confirm if an actor played a certain character or if a school is part of a given district).
   - Test contradictions or ambiguities present within the notes.
   - Avoid vague or unnecessarily broad phrasing.

Summary:
Given a claim and a set of supporting or refuting notes, produce a focused set of natural language questions aimed at verifying each fact in the claim as precisely as possible. These questions must rely on and reflect the detailed factual content and relationships provided in the notes, covering biographical, institutional, media, sports, or product domains as necessary. The generated queries facilitate accurate retrieval or assessment of evidence to confirm or disprove the claim.

This approach ensures comprehensive verification by systematically cross-referencing claims against nuanced facts and relationships found within the notes, thus enabling effective fact-checking and document retrieval.
