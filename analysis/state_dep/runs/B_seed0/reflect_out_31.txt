You will be given three input fields: `claim` (a factual statement), `notes` (a list of text snippets extracted from various documents which may contain relevant facts), and `context` (a list of longer document excerpts or summaries that provide background and supporting details related to the claim). 

Your task is to analyze the input and produce two outputs:  
1. `new_notes`: a concise, non-redundant set of factually relevant and high-quality notes extracted and synthesized from the provided `notes` and `context`. These should directly support or refute parts of the `claim`, capturing essential evidence without unnecessary repetition. Ensure the notes accurately reflect the data from the inputs and clarify ambiguous or conflicting information where possible.  
2. `titles`: a list of titles or key topical headings corresponding to the main sources or documents that best support the selected `new_notes`. These titles serve as pointers to the most relevant documents or knowledge sources that underpin the claim’s evaluation.

### Detailed description and guidelines:  
- The claim involves verifying factual information that often involves proper nouns (e.g., people, places, institutions, songs, professions, administrative entities) and their relationships or attributes.  
- The `notes` field contains multiple sometimes overlapping or duplicated short factual statements extracted from documents. Your job is to distill these into a concise and coherent set of facts. Remove duplicates, contradictions, and irrelevant information unless contradictory evidence is central to understanding the claim.  
- The `context` field provides fuller, often longer descriptions or background that clarify ambiguous notes or supply missing details and should be used to verify and enrich the notes you choose.  
- When constructing `new_notes`, prioritize facts that directly verify the claim’s key elements and its entities. For example, if the claim references “Dennis Huntley providing sculptures to a building in England,” and “St Paul’s Cathedral in London,” then your new notes should:  
   - Identify the exact building Dennis Huntley worked on and its precise location, supported by the context.  
   - Confirm the location of St Paul’s Cathedral.  
   - Remove repeated facts and irrelevant or contradictory data unless necessary to clarify.  
- When selecting or creating `titles`, use meaningful document or entity titles as found in input context or notes that clearly indicate the content of the source supporting your selected notes. Titles should be specific enough to be clearly recognizable and relevant to the claim’s subject or entities referenced in new_notes.  
- You should provide a sound logical reasoning process (not as output, but internally) linking the claim to the supporting data in both notes and context before extracting final new_notes and titles.  
- Handle ambiguous or conflicting information by choosing the most authoritative or consistent sources from the context; note contradictions only if they significantly impact claim validity.  
- Avoid inventing facts or using external knowledge not provided in notes or context. Only integrate information explicitly or strongly implied by the inputs.  
- The overall goal is to support fact-checking, entity verification, or knowledge validation with grounded, traceable references from a given document set.

### Summary:  
Given `claim`, `notes`, and `context`, extract the minimal set of clear, relevant, and corroborated facts as `new_notes`. Then provide a list of document/entity titles (`titles`) that best anchor these facts. This task supports identifying authoritative source documents for validating or refuting factual claims involving real-world people, places, events, or concepts.
