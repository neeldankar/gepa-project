Task: Given a concise, factual claim and a set of notes extracted from documents relevant to verifying that claim, generate precise, natural-language verification queries. These queries are intended to accurately target the core factual units, entities, attributes, events, and relationships embedded in the claim. The queries should guide document retrieval systems to find evidence that supports or refutes each atomic factual assertion within the claim.

Input:
- claim: A short, fact-based statement or assertion possibly containing multiple related facts or compound relationships.
- notes: A list of factual sentences or informational snippets from one or more documents connected to the claim, serving as supporting evidence, contradictions, or contextual data.

Goal:
Craft exact, fact-specific, verification-focused queries that:
- Decompose complex or compound claims into their elemental factual units.
- Explicitly target each relevant named entity (people, places, works, products), specific attributes (dates, roles, production details), and stated relationships (co-actors, biographical facts, production companies, release order).
- Use domain-specific disambiguation to ensure queries retrieve unambiguous, relevant documents.
- Cover all core facts necessary to confidently verify or refute every part of the claim.

Key Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Information:

1. **Decomposition and Entity Resolution**:
   - Break multi-part claims into atomic, verifiable parts. For example, if a claim involves an actor appearing alongside others in a film produced by a certain company in a given year, separate queries should verify each fact individually: casting, co-stars, production details, and release year.
   - Resolve vague or indirect references. For example, if the claim references “the German Frank Sinatra,” identify exactly who that nickname refers to before querying about awards or biographical facts.

2. **Factual Categories and Query Types**:
   - **Biographical facts**: birthdates, birthplaces, roles/occupations.
   - **Filmography/Product details**: film titles with release years, actors’ roles, co-stars, directors, production companies, genre, release order.
   - **Awards and Achievements**: verify award wins/losses for named individuals and comparisons between them.
   - **Publication facts**: publication type (book, magazine article, periodical), publication year, author contribution.
   - **Relational facts**: Did Actor A star alongside Actor B in Film C? Was Person X the first chairman at Institution Y? Are two entities both classified as public universities?
   - **Temporal facts**: verifying chronology explicitly, e.g., birth order, release dates.

3. **Leverage Notes for Disambiguation and Completeness**:
   - Use notes to find exact spellings, full entity names, correct titles, and distinguishing features (e.g., film year, university location).
   - Integrate clarifications or corrections found in notes to avoid ambiguous or misleading queries.
   - Identify gaps or contradictions in notes that require explicit verification queries to confirm or deny uncertain facts.

4. **Query Construction Best Practices**:
   - Queries should be written as natural, precise, fully formed questions focused on single facts or tightly related fact sets.
   - Explicitly mention full names, titles, and relevant qualifiers to avoid ambiguity (e.g., “Morgan State University,” “Print magazine,” “2011 Winnie the Pooh movie”).
   - When comparing facts (e.g., awards won), form queries that directly ask for comparisons or individual tallies.
   - If multiple entities or claims are involved, create separate queries for each entity’s fact and queries that explicitly link their relationships for compound claims.
   - Queries should maximize coverage — aim to cover every assertion present in the claim independently.

5. **Generalizable Verification Strategy**:
   1. Parse the claim deeply: identify entities, attributes, relationships, and temporal facts.
   2. Cross-reference with notes to confirm known facts, detect unknown or conflicting information.
   3. Compose a set of precise queries that check each atomic fact individually.
   4. Where uncertainty exists due to incomplete or conflicting notes, include queries to confirm/refute.
   5. Use domain knowledge to frame queries logically and completely within the subject area.

6. **Examples of Effective Queries**:
   - "Did Travis Oates voice Piglet in the 2011 Winnie the Pooh movie?"
   - "Was Reverend Lyttleton Morgan the first chairman of the board of trustees at Morgan State University?"
   - "Are Morgan State University and the University of Idaho both classified as public universities?"
   - "Was the 1995 film 'The Scarlet Letter' produced by Cinergi Pictures?"
   - "Did Nivek Ogre, member of Skinny Puppy, participate in the one-off side project Rx?"
   - "Has the individual known as German Frank Sinatra won more music awards than Nivek Ogre?"

---

Summary:

Your output must be a coherent set of natural-language verification queries that explicitly focus on each core factual assertion of the input claim, informed and refined by the input notes. Disambiguate entities with full names and qualifiers, decompose compound assertions into atomic facts, and utilize domain knowledge to create queries that ensure comprehensive verification coverage. The queries you generate will be used to retrieve accurate evidence for validating or invalidating each part of the claim.
