You will be given two input fields: 
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that may involve complex relationships among people, events, works (e.g., films, albums), or organizations. 
- `notes`: a list of text snippets (facts, data points, or contextual information) relevant to the claim.

Your task is to produce a single output field:
- `query`: A precise, focused search query that when used in a document retrieval system will effectively retrieve relevant documents covering all key aspects of the claim.

Detailed task description:

1. **Purpose:** The generated query will be used to verify or refute the claim by retrieving documents that confirm or deny its components. Therefore, the query must cover all essential facets of the claim.

2. **Input analysis:**
   - Break down the `claim` into its core factual components or entities (e.g., persons, works, dates, locations, roles, events).
   - Use the `notes` to identify which elements in the claim are already partially or fully corroborated, contradicted, or unconfirmed by the provided information.
   - Pay special attention to domain-specific entities such as:
     - Named entities: people (actors, musicians), teams, companies.
     - Works of art and media: films, TV shows, episodes, albums, songs, books.
     - Events: sporting events, award shows, specific broadcasts.
     - Roles and relationships: cast roles, sponsorships, locations.
   - Also note temporal information (dates, years), aliases, and variant naming conventions that appear in `notes`.

3. **Formulating the query:**
   - The query should explicitly mention the core entities and relationships necessary to verify the claim.
   - It should aim to retrieve documents addressing all claim components, not just fragments.
   - When there are multiple entities/entities relationships, craft the query to reflect their intersections or connections (e.g., "2015 Kansas State Wildcats" AND "AutoZone sponsorship" AND "Liberty Bowl").
   - When there is ambiguity or partial confirmation, the query may include questions aiming to disambiguate or confirm these points (e.g., "Did actor X star in film Y?" "Was company Z the sponsor of event W in year Y?").
   - Avoid generic or overly broad queries that fail to focus on the claim’s key attributes.
   - Include alternative names or variants if needed (e.g., "American Dad episode 'Lost In Space'").

4. **General strategy:**
   - Use the notes to identify missing or weakly supported aspects of the claim to prioritize in the query.
   - Aim for precision, using dates, full names, or specific event titles where possible.
   - Structure the query as a natural language question or a keyword phrase tailored to retrieve relevant documents.
   
5. **Examples of domain knowledge reflected in queries:**
   - Sports: referencing team names, bowl games, sponsorship brands, and event years.
   - Music and TV: linking album names, song titles, TV show programming blocks, and episode titles.
   - Film and literature: referencing actors, character portrayals, film titles, novels, and their adaptations.

By following the above, the output `query` will be a focused search query optimized for verifying complex factual claims through document retrieval.
