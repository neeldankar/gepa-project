Task Description:
You are given a textual claim and a list of notes that provide factual information related to the claim. Your goal is to generate an effective search query (or a set of queries) that can be used to verify the claim using the information contained in the notes or to retrieve relevant documents needed for verification.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A concise, fact-based statement or assertion that may combine multiple facts or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or short snippets extracted from one or more documents. These provide evidence, context, or refutations related to the claim.

Output:
- `query`: One or more natural language questions designed to verify the truthfulness of the claim by querying the content and relationships mentioned in the notes. The queries should focus on the key entities, events, attributes, and relationships explicitly or implicitly stated in the claim and cross-checked against the notes.

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Understand the claim carefully**: Identify the main entities, attributes, and relationships described in the claim. In cases where the claim contains indirect references (e.g., “born in the birthplace of X”), resolve these to direct facts that can be checked (e.g., birthplaces of both entities).

2. **Use notes to clarify and expand the claim**: Analyze the notes to identify relevant facts that relate directly to the claim’s key assertions. These often include:
   - Names of persons, places, or works referenced.
   - Temporal information (e.g., years, release dates).
   - Relationships such as birthplaces, roles (actor, director), attributes (flavor, ranking).
   - Disambiguations or corrections from the notes (e.g., corrections of director or actor roles).

3. **Formulate verification-focused queries**:
   - Queries should ask directly about the key facts or relationships necessary to verify the claim.
   - When the claim contains multiple assertions or compound facts, break them into separate but related questions.
   - Include entity disambiguation and relationship verification, such as confirming if two people share the same birthplace, if a person directed a film, or if a product has a given feature.
   - If the notes show contradictions or lack of evidence, include queries that test these points explicitly, e.g., “Did Actor X appear in Film Y?” or “Is Film Z directed by Person A?”

4. **Be precise and contextual**:
   - Use the exact names and terms found in the notes for entities and works.
   - Include relevant qualifiers like dates, nationalities, or versions to avoid ambiguity.
   - Avoid generating overly broad or generic queries that do not focus on key claim assertions.

5. **Generalizable strategy**:
   - Parse the claim into atomic factual units.
   - Cross-reference with the notes to identify which facts are supported, contradicted, or missing.
   - Generate natural-language queries that aim at resolving the truthfulness or factual status of each atomic fact.
   - This approach ensures maximum retrieval of relevant evidence documents for claim verification.

Domain-specific Insights:
- Claims often involve biographical details (birthplaces, dates, occupations).
- Claims may relate to filmography: actors starring in films, directors of films, film release years.
- Claims can concern product attributes like flavors of soft drinks or advertising associations.
- Notes may explicitly correct or refute claim elements, or indicate absence of evidence.
- The resulting queries must reflect these factual domains and relationships with precision to guide retrieval systems effectively.

Summary:
Given `claim` and `notes`, produce verification-oriented, fact-specific queries that directly target the core assertions of the claim using the contextual and factual information in the notes. These queries enable retrieval or verification of all relevant documents and help confirm or refute each part of the claim.
