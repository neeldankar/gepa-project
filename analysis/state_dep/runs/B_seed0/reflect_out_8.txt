Given three structured inputs: 
- `claim` (a statement or assertion to be verified or analyzed),
- `notes` (a list of extracted or summarized factual statements relevant to the claim), 
- `context` (background information and detailed descriptions of entities, events, or items referenced in the claim and notes),

your task is to produce two outputs: 
- `new_notes` (a concise, refined set of factual statements that directly support or refute the claim, extracted and synthesized from the given notes and context), and 
- `titles` (a list of relevant document or entity titles that would serve as authoritative or primary sources related to the claim, notes, and context).

Instructions and Approach:

1. **Understand the Claim in Detail:**  
   Parse the claim carefully to identify the key entities, relationships, temporal or causal conditions, and the logical assertions within it.

2. **Cross-Reference Notes Against Claim:**  
   Examine the given notes—usually a list of factual statements or observations—to identify which facts are directly relevant to the claim’s components. Filter out redundant or less relevant information to curate a focused and minimal set of key facts that support clear verification of the claim.

3. **Use Context as Authoritative Background:**  
   Utilize the context documents to verify the accuracy and timeline details, confirm relationships between entities, or clarify domain-specific or nuanced points that the notes alone might not resolve unambiguously. Context can provide authoritative background information, e.g., biographies, release dates, definitions, or detailed descriptions of works or events.

4. **Temporal and Factual Accuracy:**  
   Pay particular attention to temporal ordering of events or performances (e.g., release dates, premiere dates, participation periods), categorical distinctions (e.g., opera acts vs. film structure), and membership or participation status in groups (e.g., band members or actors).  
   These domain-specific factual considerations are critical for verifying claims involving complex temporal or membership relationships.

5. **Synthesize New Notes:**  
   Produce `new_notes` as a concise, logically coherent set of factual sentences drawn from and confirmed by both notes and context that directly bear on evaluating the claim. Avoid repetition and extraneous details. Each fact should be precise and factually verifiable.

6. **Provide Relevant Titles:**  
   Produce the list `titles` containing the names of authoritative documents, entities, or topics that encompass the core factual grounding for the claim and refined notes. These typically come from the context or note sources and should include all key entities or works referenced in your new notes to ensure comprehensive coverage for factchecking.

Summary of Domain-Specific Knowledge Embedded in Task Examples:

- **Actors and Films/TV Shows**:  
  Understanding actor participation, voice roles, release and premiere dates, and genre categorizations for movies and TV series seasons; the temporal sequence of performance engagements across different media.

- **Opera and Film Structures**:  
  Knowledge that operas are traditionally divided into acts, whereas films typically do not have formal acts; understanding influence and inspiration relationships between works.

- **Musician and Band Membership**:  
  Awareness of band membership status, song authorship and performance roles, distinctions between solo artists and band members, and the context of song performances.

The assistant should apply rigorous cross-validation of all factual claims by triangulating the notes and context, center the response on relevant facts to confirm or refute the claim, avoid unnecessary detail, and systematically identify authoritative titles to include.
