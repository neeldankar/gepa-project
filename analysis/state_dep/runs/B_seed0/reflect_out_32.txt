Task: Generate precise, natural-language verification search queries to confirm or refute a concise factual claim based on given notes extracted from relevant documents.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A short, fact-based statement potentially comprising multiple related factual assertions or relationships involving entities, attributes, events, or temporal facts.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or snippets drawn from documents. These may support, contradict, clarify, or provide context to the claim.

Purpose:
The queries you formulate will be used to retrieve or verify documents that either substantiate or challenge each independent factual assertion present in the claim. The goal is to maximize coverage of all key factual units for comprehensive verification.

Detailed Task Description and Instructions:

1. Understand and Decompose the Claim:
   - Parse the claim thoroughly and identify **all core factual units**: 
     * Entities (people, places, works, organizations, products, etc.)
     * Attributes (birthdates, birthplaces, professions, roles, specific titles, flavors, release years)
     * Relationships and events (acted alongside, coached, produced by, named, won award, product release chronology)
   - Break down compound or multi-part claims into atomic factual queries to isolate each factual element for verification.
   - Resolve any indirect or inferential references into direct factual questions. For example, if the claim says "born in the birthplace of X," explicitly identify the birthplaces of both entities and confirm their equivalence.

2. Leverage Notes for Enrichment and Disambiguation:
   - Use notes to extract or confirm relevant facts, including:
     * Entity disambiguation: full names, exact film titles with release years, proper organizational names.
     * Cross-entity or relationship clarifications (e.g., confirming which actors starred in which films).
     * Contradictions or inconsistencies indicating uncertainty or error in the claim.
   - If notes reveal corrections or contradictions (e.g., roles or titles wrongly assigned), craft queries that explicitly test those contradictions.
   - Detect missing or ambiguous information from notes that necessitates verifying elements the claim assumes.

3. Formulate Precise Verification Queries:
   - Generate natural-language queries that ask directly about the atomic factual units.
   - Each query should be focused, unambiguous, and explicit; e.g.:
     * Facts about entities: "Was X born in Y?", "Is X the lead guitarist of band Y?"
     * Role verification: "Did Actor X voice Character Y in Film Z (year)?"
     * Relationship verification: "Did Actor X star alongside Actor Y in Film Z?"
     * Production and release facts: "Was Film A produced by Company B?", "Was Film A released before Film B?"
     * Temporal verification: "Was Person A born before Person B?", "Was Event X after Event Y?"
     * Contradictory facts: "Was Person X a trainer?" (if notes say no)
   - When a claim involves multiple assertions, generate one or more queries per assertion.
   - Use exact names, titles, and dates as found in notes or known domain conventions to avoid ambiguity.
   
4. Domain-Specific and Contextual Awareness:
   - For **biographical facts:** focus on birthplaces, birthdates, occupations, awards, nationality.
   - For **filmography or entertainment-related facts:** focus on precise film titles (including release year), cast lists, roles/characters, production companies, sequels/prequels.
   - For **sports-related claims:** focus on team affiliations, coaching roles, awards, nationalities.
   - For **product or brand facts:** flavors, advertising partnerships, launch years.
   - Address temporal facts explicitly to clarify chronological claims and factual sequences.
   
5. Addressing Contradictions and Ambiguities:
   - If notes contain contradictions or negate some parts of the claim, draft queries that clearly delineate these contested facts.
   - Include verification queries that seek to find if conflicting elements can be reconciled or disproven (e.g., "Was Jim Thorpe a trainer?" – when notes say no).
   
6. Query Style and Coverage:
   - Use natural language, clear and direct question formats suitable for knowledge retrieval systems.
   - Ensure thorough coverage of the claim’s components so no factual assertion is left unchecked.
   - Avoid general or vague searches; prioritize precision by incorporating disambiguating details such as dates, full names, and exact titles.
   - When the claim mixes different facts about entities or events, treat each fact independently.

Summary:
Given a concise factual claim and related notes, your goal is to create fact-specific, verification-oriented, natural-language queries targeting each distinct entity, relation, attribute, or temporal element in the claim. This approach ensures comprehensive retrieval of evidence supporting or refuting the claim’s entirety and resolves contradictions or ambiguities found in the notes.

Examples illustrating the approach:
- "Did Jim Thorpe name Eddie Mahan the greatest football player of all time?"
- "Was the 2007 film '28 Weeks Later' a sequel to '28 Days Later'?"
- "Was Jeremy Renner in the cast of both '28 Weeks Later' (2007) and 'Avengers: Age of Ultron' (2015)?"
- "Are Alaska and Texas both states within the United States of America?"

Your queries should be directly grounded in facts extracted from or relevant to the notes, clarifying the claim's verifiability with precise, atomic fact-checking questions.
