Task Description:  
You are given a concise factual claim along with a list of notes containing relevant factual snippets, contextual evidence, or contradictions related to the claim. Your objective is to produce one or more precise natural language search queries that can be used to verify the truthfulness of each core assertion within the claim using the information provided in the notes or to retrieve additional evidence for verification.

Input Format:  
- claim: A precise, fact-based statement that may consist of multiple interconnected facts and relationships involving entities such as people, places, events, films, or products.  
- notes: A list of factual sentences or short excerpts extracted from one or more documents, providing evidence supporting, clarifying, contradicting, or refuting the facts stated in the claim.

Detailed Instructions:  

1. **Analyze the Claim into Atomic Facts**  
   - Carefully parse the claim into its elementary facts or assertions focusing on entities (people, places, works), attributes (birth dates, birthplaces, roles, dates), and relationships (e.g., who directed which film, who defeated whom in a sports event, who began competing at what age).  
   - When the claim uses indirect references or relational expressions (e.g., “the grandfather of X,” “born in the birthplace of Y”), resolve these to concrete individual facts that can be verified explicitly (e.g., identify “grandfather of X,” identify Y’s birthplace, then check the place of birth of the other person).

2. **Cross-Reference with Notes to Identify Relevant Facts and Gaps**  
   - Utilize the notes to identify:  
     - Exact names, dates, and titles that appear in the claim.  
     - Confirmed attributes or relational facts (e.g., birthdate of an individual, film release years, sports player statistics).  
     - Contradictions or absent information that prevent verifying elements of the claim.  
   - Extract and keep note of corrections or disambiguations found in the notes that may influence the queries, such as:  
     - Clarification of who acted or directed in certain films.  
     - Identification of familial relationships or linking of aliases/spellings.  
     - Temporal clarifications like birth dates necessary for age comparisons.  

3. **Formulate Focused Verification Queries**  
   - For each atomic fact or relationship, generate a direct question aimed at verifying that element against the notes or external documents.  
   - Break complex or compound claims into multiple, clearly related query questions.  
   - Include disambiguation where necessary, incorporating exact names and qualifiers to avoid ambiguities (e.g., full names, associated entities like films or events, specific years).  
   - Target queries at extracting or confirming evidence for:  
     - Roles and identities (e.g., "Was X the lead vocalist of Y band?" or "Did Actor X star in Film Z?")  
     - Temporal facts (e.g., "Was Person A born before Person B?" or "Did Event C occur in Year D?")  
     - Relationship verifications (e.g., "Is Person X the grandfather of Person Y?" or "Did Player A defeat Player B in Tournament C?")  
     - Attribute existence or correctness (e.g., "Does Product X have flavor Y?" or "Is Film Z directed by Director W?")  

4. **Ensure Precision and Contextual Relevance**  
   - Use exact names, dates, and terms as presented in the notes to maximize retrieval of relevant documents and avoid generic or vague queries.  
   - Include qualifiers such as nationality, roles, or event contexts to sharpen query focus.  
   - Avoid overly broad queries that cannot pinpoint the necessary verification evidence.

5. **Generalizable Verification Strategy**  
   - Decompose the claim into independent verification points.  
   - Use notes to confirm or identify missing facts or contradictions.  
   - Generate natural language queries that directly correspond to each verification point, enabling retrieval systems to gather or check relevant evidence efficiently.  

Domain-Specific Insights:  
- Claims often include biographical data concerning birthplaces, birthdates, relationships (e.g., grandparent-grandchild), and roles (e.g., actor, director, musician).  
- Film-related facts may include actors, directors, release years, and role attributions requiring accurate disambiguation.  
- Sports-related claims frequently involve match outcomes, opponents, event names, and starting ages or career milestones.  
- Product-related claims might concern product attributes like specific flavors or advertising associations, which need to be checked explicitly.  
- Notes may contain direct corrections or highlight contradictions; queries should be designed to test these points when appropriate.  

Summary:  
Given a factual claim and contextual notes, produce precise, atomic verification queries that directly address each key fact or relationship in the claim. These queries should be framed to optimally leverage the identified facts, corrections, and entities in the notes, enabling effective retrieval or evidence checking to confirm or refute the claim fully.
