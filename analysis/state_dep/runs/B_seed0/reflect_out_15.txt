Task Description:

You are given three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A factual statement that may combine multiple pieces of information.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or observations relevant to the claim, extracted from various sources.
- `context`: A list of detailed background information and document excerpts relevant to entities, events, or places mentioned in the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Output Requirements:

1. `new_notes`:
   - Synthesize and condense the most relevant, accurate, and directly supporting factual statements from the `notes` and the `context` to confirm or refute the `claim`.
   - Identify any misconceptions, inaccuracies, or ambiguities in the `claim` by clarifying key facts.
   - Include only distinct, concise points that are well-supported by the input data.
   - When applicable, separate related but distinct facts clarifying different parts of the claim.
   - Avoid repetition and prioritize facts that resolve contradictions or address complex relationships within the claim.
   - Retain proper names, dates, and qualifiers to maintain factual precision.
   
2. `titles`:
   - Provide a list of relevant document or entity titles (e.g., Wikipedia article titles) that tightly correspond to the key facts, entities, or subjects involved in validating or invalidating the claim.
   - Aim to cover all necessary topical areas mentioned or implied by the claim and notes, including geographic locations, people, organizations, films, or other domain-specific entities.
   - Include any broader or related categories essential for understanding or fact-checking the claim.
   - Titles should be concise, standardized names that reflect the referenced documents or concepts within the `context` and `notes`.

Reasoning and Methodology:

- Carefully analyze the claim and cross-reference it with the `notes` and `context` to discern which facts are accurate, relevant, or contradictory.
- Identify nuanced relationships such as:
   - Person-film-director connections and their roles (e.g., co-writer vs actor).
   - Jurisdictional relationships in geography (e.g., counties, cities, provinces).
   - Institutional affiliations (e.g., colleges within counties).
- Clarify ambiguous or mixed-up claims by parsing multiple entities and their distinct attributes.
- Use detailed domain-specific knowledge implicitly shown in the input, including:
   - Understanding of film credits (director, writer, actor).
   - Recognition of political/administrative boundaries and hierarchies.
   - Familiarity with college affiliations and their geographic locations.
   - Awareness of entity roles and their relevance to the claim.
- Avoid assuming facts not supported by the `notes` or `context`.
- Present a clear, factual summary (`new_notes`) reflecting the truthfulness and exact scope of the claim.
- The choice of `titles` should reflect all relevant key entities, ensuring comprehensive topical coverage.

In summary, your response should reflect a rigorous fact-checking process by disentangling complex claims into verified supporting statements and identifying the relevant topical domains via the titles list.
