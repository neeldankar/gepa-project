Task Description:  
You are given a claim and a set of notes (textual evidence or relevant document excerpts) related to that claim. Your goal is to produce a precise, targeted search query that will help verify or refute the claim by retrieving the most relevant additional documents or evidence. The query should be designed to explicitly resolve the factual points in the claim that remain uncertain or warrant corroboration, leveraging the information already present in the notes.

Input Format:  
- claim: A single factual or composite statement to be verified.  
- notes: A list of strings containing factual statements or excerpts from documents relevant to the claim. These provide partial evidence or context about the claim, but may not fully resolve all its assertions.

Output:  
- query: A clear, concise, and focused natural language question or query that will guide retrieval of supporting or contradicting documents necessary for conclusive verification of the claim.

Key Instructions and Domain-Specific Details:  
1. Aggregate Evidence: Use the notes to identify what parts of the claim are already supported, contradicted, or unclear. Your query should focus on that uncertainty or ambiguity rather than restate already established facts.  
2. Multi-faceted Claims: If the claim includes multiple facts or entities (e.g., associations between persons, places, organizations, roles, or events), formulate a query that captures all relevant aspects or clarifies the relationship between these entities.  
3. Geographical and Organizational Precision: Pay special attention to geographic locations (counties, cities, venues) and organizational ownership or affiliations, as misstatements or subtle differences here are common points for verification. For example, distinguishing whether a location is part of a city versus a county, or the exact venue name and its ownership, is critical.  
4. Person Identity Resolution: When the claim involves persons with similar or ambiguous names, the query should explicitly clarify their identity or role (e.g., are two similar named individuals the same person, and what positions did they hold at specific times?).  
5. Clear Formulation: The query should be in plain, natural language. Aim for a question form that a search or retrieval system would understand as requesting precise, factual information.  
6. Avoid Redundancy: Do not include information in the query that is clearly resolved and supported by the notes. Instead, focus on missing or contradictory aspects, or those that need confirmation.  
7. Use Relevant Entities from Notes: Use the entities, titles, or terminology found in the notes to compose the query, as these are likely to align with authoritative documents or sources.  
8. Domain Examples:  
   - For railway claims, verify station names, terminus locations, and county/city boundaries.  
   - For sports team claims, clarify coaching staff identities and their roles in specific seasons.  
   - For entertainment venue claims, confirm venue names, ownership, and location specifics.  

Overall Strategy:  
First, analyze the claim in detail and cross-check it against the notes to identify gaps in evidence. Then, generate a short and specific question designed to retrieve the missing documents or information necessary to fully verify the claim. The query should balance completeness and precision to maximize effective document retrieval for claim verification.
