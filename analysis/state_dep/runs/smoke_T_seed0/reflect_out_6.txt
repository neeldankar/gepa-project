Task Description:
You will be given three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to produce two fields as output: `new_notes` and `titles`.

The inputs are:

- **claim**: A factual statement whose veracity or completeness may be evaluated.
- **notes**: A list of preliminary, extracted facts or observations related to the claim, which often include confirmations, refutations, or gaps based on the claim and the given context.
- **context**: A list of background information snippets extracted from relevant documents or knowledge sources that may support or contradict the claim.

Your outputs must be:

- **new_notes**: A refined, concise, and factually accurate set of notes derived from analyzing the claim, notes, and context together. These notes should:
  - Clearly confirm or refute parts of the claim using information from the `notes` and `context`.
  - Highlight key factual findings or discrepancies.
  - Exclude irrelevant, unsupported, or speculative information.
  - Address any ambiguities or missing information that prevent verifying parts of the claim.
  - Be phrased as stand-alone factual statements or direct clarifications directly relevant to the claim.

- **titles**: A small set (3 to 5) of the most relevant and authoritative document or article titles from the `context` that are essential for fact-checking the claim. These titles should:
  - Directly relate to the main entities, events, or facts mentioned in the claim.
  - Help locate the strongest sources for supporting or contradicting the claim.
  - Include titles of specific articles about people, events, locations, or datasets mentioned.
  - Avoid irrelevant or too broad titles not connected to the claim.

Guidelines and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Entity Matching & Name Variants**: Pay close attention to name variants, alternate spellings, or homonyms (e.g., "Höss" vs. "Hoss") to avoid false matches or dismissals. Claims often revolve around specific individuals or roles (e.g., Auschwitz commandants, professional tennis players, musicians).

2. **Factual Verification over Assumptions**: Never assume facts that are not present or explicitly indicated in the `context` or `notes`. If birthdates, ages, or relationships are missing, clearly state that the claim cannot be verified.

3. **Temporal and Hierarchical Knowledge**: When claims involve timelines, ages, or rankings (e.g., “younger than,” “longest serving”), verify with specific dates or durations when available. If dates are missing for comparison, note this explicitly.

4. **Domain-Specific Knowledge**: 
   - For historical or WWII-related claims, consider established SS command structures, known Auschwitz personnel, and distinct documentation like the Auschwitz Protocols.
   - For sports-related claims, refer to event results, player birthdates, and career facts (e.g., tennis tournament winners, player careers).
   - For music-related claims, use band memberships, formation years, and birthdates of artists for verification.

5. **Improving Note Quality**:
   - Reduce redundancy by merging similar notes.
   - Prioritize details that directly address claim components.
   - Remove speculative notes or repeated information.
   - Highlight any contradictions or confirmations supported by multiple notes or context entries.

6. **Titles Selection**:
   - Extract from `context` only those titles that would best facilitate fact-checking the claim.
   - If exact matches are missing, select closest authoritative or comprehensive titles.
   - Avoid including irrelevant or tangential titles even if present in the context list.

7. **Response Structure**:
   - Provide a short reasoning summary (optional internally) to justify note selections.
   - Present `new_notes` as a list of concise, well-formed statements.
   - Present `titles` as a list of exact document/article names, ideally properly capitalized and formatted.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Cross-reference claim components with both notes and context to confirm accuracy.
- Identify and compile concrete facts supporting or refuting claim elements.
- Clearly delineate verified knowledge from information gaps.
- Select the most relevant documents based on subject, person, event, or domain specificity.
- When uncertain, explicitly note lack of evidence rather than infer.

Your output should enable a fact-checker or researcher to quickly grasp the verified facts about the claim and locate the primary documents needed for in-depth verification.
