Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: a `claim` (a factual or semi-factual statement that needs verification or elaboration) and `notes` (a list of relevant factual points, context sentences, or evidence related to the claim). Your task is to produce a `query` that could be used to retrieve documents or information to verify, elaborate, or refute the claim using the given notes as context.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A single text statement that makes one or more factual assertions or implications.
- `notes`: A list of textual facts, statements, or pieces of evidence related to entities, events, dates, or relationships mentioned in the claim. These notes often include multiple overlapping or partially contradictory facts or clarifications, sometimes with timings, roles, or geographic details.

Output Requirement:
- Generate a single, clear, and concise `query` that directly addresses the core factual uncertainties or verification points in the claim, making use of relevant facts or gaps indicated in the notes.
- The query should be well-focused on key entities, their relationships, timelines, or origin/role details to maximize retrieval of the most relevant supporting documents.
- The query can be compound, addressing multiple related focal points if necessary (e.g., “Did X do Y? If not, who did?”).
- Avoid unnecessary or vague questions; the query must target the central factual claims or contradictions implied by the claim combined with the notes.
- The query should implicitly or explicitly tie together the main subjects/entities in the claim using contextual hints from the notes, e.g., connecting an organization’s founding date, roles of people, or origin of groups.

Domain-Specific/Task-Specific Details:
- Often involves verifying biographical details, such as professional roles (player, manager, group membership), timeframes (years active, founding dates), or origin (country/region).
- Needs to disambiguate entities with similar or overlapping identities (e.g., band names or artist names from different countries).
- Aims to clarify chronological and organizational relationships (e.g., did a player from a certain team at X years later become manager of Y team?).
- Recognizes that some notes provide direct factual negations or deny the existence of connections, which should influence focused querying on alternatives or clarifications.
- Queries should therefore sometimes ask for identification of alternative or related entities if the claim’s suggested link is not supported.
- The task involves a multi-document retrieval setting with "gold" documents relevant to different aspects of the claim. Queries should therefore aim to retrieve all relevant documents by covering all key points or ambiguities.

Generalized Strategy:
1. Carefully analyze the claim for its main assertion(s) and entities involved.
2. Cross-reference with the notes to identify supporting, contradicting, or missing information.
3. Identify the core factual questions implied by the claim and gaps revealed by the notes.
4. Frame a precise information retrieval query that targets those core questions in a single or compound format.
5. When necessary, include follow-up or clarifying questions about related entities if the claim’s direct link is not verifiable from the notes.
6. Keep queries factual, specific, and tailored to the entities, timeframes, and relationships highlighted or negated in the notes.

Example Patterns:
- “Did [person X] who [performed role/timeframe/event] in [entity Y] later [perform other role/event]?”
- “Is [entity X] confirmed to have [property/relationship] mentioned in the claim?”
- “Which [entity type] has/had the relationship described, given conflicting or missing data about [entities A and B]?”
- “Who managed/played for [entity] during [time period], and what were their related roles?”

Use this approach to produce the best possible retrieval query for each claim-notes pair.
