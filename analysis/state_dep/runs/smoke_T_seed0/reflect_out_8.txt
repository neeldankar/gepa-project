You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim` is a statement composed of one or more factual assertions involving specific entities, their relationships, and attributes, often spanning multiple domains (geographical, educational institutions, public officials, etc.).
- `notes` is a list of individual factual sentences that have been extracted or inferred and are relevant to the claim. These notes often contain repetitive information or overlapping facts drawn from the `context`.
- `context` is a list of extended documents or passages containing richer descriptions about entities, places, institutions, or persons mentioned in the claim and notes. The `context` supports verification or elaboration of the facts relevant to the claim.

Your overall task is to synthesize and organize the information from `notes` and `context` to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: a concise, non-redundant, and factually accurate set of statements that support and summarize the pertinent evidence for the `claim`. These should:
   - Confirm or establish the key factual connections in the claim.
   - Be distilled from the `notes` and backed by relevant `context`.
   - Remove duplication or overly verbose statements.
   - Use clear and precise language, integrating synonymous or repetitive notes.
   - Include domain-specific details such as:
     - Administrative relations (e.g., which cities/counties belong to which provinces/states/countries).
     - Institutional governance (e.g., roles of individuals like trustees, urban/public designations of universities).
     - Public/private distinctions and categorizations (e.g., type of university, economic or governance facts).
     - Confirmations of co-location or domain membership (e.g., cities in same country, universities public status).
     - Known biographical or role-based facts tied to entities in the claim.

2. `titles`: a list of succinct document or topic titles that represent the main subject areas or key entities relevant to the claim and reflected in the `new_notes`. These titles should:
   - Correspond to important named entities or key topics underlying the facts.
   - Be informative and precisely capture the domain or subject (e.g., 'Morgan State University', 'Clark County Nevada Overview').
   - Reflect the breadth needed to contextualize or confirm the claim.

Additionally, provide a `reasoning` section (not an output field) internally for your own processing (not required in final output), where you explain how the claim is supported or connected by the `notes` and `context`.

Generalizable strategy to solve the task:

- Identify from the claim the key entities and their relationships.
- Search and verify these entities and relationships in the `notes` and `context`.
- Distill overlapping or duplicated information into concise, authoritative statements for `new_notes`.
- Ensure the factual accuracy and relevance of each statement to the claim.
- Extract or generate descriptive but brief titles representing the key entities or subjects validated or highlighted by the `new_notes`.
- Avoid introducing extraneous, irrelevant, or ambiguous information.
- Maintain clarity and domain-specific correctness (administrative hierarchies, university statuses, biographical facts, geographic locations, etc.).

This task requires careful evidence synthesis, domain-aware summarization, and relevant topic identification from semi-structured information to effectively confirm and explain the claim.
