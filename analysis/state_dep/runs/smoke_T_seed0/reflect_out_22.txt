Task Description:

You will be given three inputs per task:
- `claim`: a statement or assertion involving multiple entities, events, roles, or facts that require verification.
- `notes`: a list of statements potentially supporting or refuting the claim; these may contain relevant or irrelevant information, redundancies, or varying levels of precision.
- `context`: detailed excerpts or summaries that provide authoritative information on the entities, events, or topics referenced in the claim.

Your primary goal is to analyze and verify the claim using the provided notes and context, producing two outputs:
1. `new_notes`: a carefully curated, concise subset of the original notes that clearly and accurately support or refute the claim based on the context. The new notes must:
   - Focus on factual accuracy and clarity.
   - Highlight precise details such as correct dates, roles, events, achievements, or attributions.
   - Avoid redundancy, irrelevant information, speculation, or unsupported inference.
   - Include the most relevant statements that directly confirm or disprove key parts of the claim.

2. `titles`: a list of succinct, descriptive titles corresponding to the primary source documents or key contextual topics critical for understanding or verifying the claim. These titles should:
   - Reference main entities, events, or themes tied to the claim.
   - Use official or recognized names (e.g., correct film titles, team names, event years, or person full names and roles).
   - Include multiple titles if the claim involves more than one principal topic or document.
   - Be concise yet informative enough to identify the core context needed for verification.

Detailed Guidelines:

1. Claim comprehension and fact verification:
   - Identify all core entities (people, teams, institutions), roles, events, dates, and actions in the claim.
   - Check the `context` excerpts for authoritative information (e.g., who did what, when, and under what circumstances).
   - Observe domain-specific nuances, such as:
       * Differentiating similarly named individuals.
       * Distinguishing between sports codes (e.g., American football vs. soccer).
       * Correct attribution of roles or achievements (e.g., who named someone, who scored a goal, who directed a film).
       * Confirming historical timelines precisely.
   - Evaluate the `notes` against context facts for corroboration or contradiction.

2. Selecting and refining `new_notes`:
   - Reject repeated or tangential notes.
   - Summarize or rephrase for precision and clarity where appropriate.
   - Address all major claim components (e.g., if the claim makes multiple assertions, each should be tackled).
   - Strictly avoid including unsupported claims or conjectures.
   - Retain mention of critical domain-specific info, such as:
       * Medal wins or coaching roles in sports.
       * Film direction vs. writing credits in entertainment.
       * Scientific term coiners and their professions in academic domains.
   - Reflect on temporal aspects or biographical data that support or negate the claim.

3. Generating `titles` accurately:
   - Titles must mirror the key subject matter of the contexts essential for claim verification.
   - Use well-established, formal titles, such as:
       * Person biographies by full name with their professions or key recognitions.
       * Official film titles with original language or translation, including release year if helpful.
       * Sports team season summaries or event titles including year.
   - Avoid generic or vague names; specificity benefits downstream use.

4. Domain-specific considerations and examples:
   - In sports claims, specify teams, events, coaching years, player positions, and scoring details.
   - For entertainment domains, clarify roles such as director, writer, actor, or producer, and distinct projects.
   - For scientific or biographical claims, include professional titles, co-creators or colleagues, term coinage origin, and academic affiliations.
   - Use contextual differences (e.g., a person who named a player “greatest” versus someone who coached a team) to disambiguate.

5. Strategy Overview:
   - Parse the claim into discrete factual components (entities, dates, roles).
   - Locate authoritative information in `context` corresponding to each component.
   - Cross-validate claims with `notes`, filtering out irrelevant or unsupported statements.
   - Compose `new_notes` that comprehensively but concisely address the claim's truth or falsehood.
   - Select `titles` for core documents or topics that provide necessary verification.
   - Deliver these outputs clearly and succinctly, facilitating accurate fact-checking and context retrieval.

This instruction ensures precise fact verification across diverse knowledge domains, combining detailed domain expertise with systematic evidence cross-checking, note refinement, and contextual document identification.
