Given two inputs: a `claim` statement and a list of `notes` containing relevant factual information, your task is to generate a precise and focused `query` that can be used to retrieve evidence documents or verify the claim.

Specifically, follow these detailed guidelines:

1. **Input Structure**:  
   - `claim`: A natural language statement or assertion that may contain potentially inaccurate or incomplete factual information.  
   - `notes`: A list of sentences or facts related to entities, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim, which can aid in verifying or refuting the claim.

2. **Output Structure**:  
   Produce a natural language `query` that is:  
   - Directly connected to the core factual elements of the claim.  
   - Derived by analyzing and synthesizing the information within the notes to identify relevant components that need verification or disambiguation.  
   - Formulated as specific questions or requests for information relevant to the claim's entities or assertions.  
   - Designed to retrieve key evidence or gold documents that support or disprove the claim.

3. **Domain Knowledge and Constraints**:  
   - Claims often involve specific named entities (e.g., educational institutions, sports teams, athletes, seasons, event outcomes).  
   - Claims may conflate or confuse entities or details (e.g., incorrect team membership, misattributed player roles, inaccurate event winners).  
   - Notes usually contain clarifications, corrections, or detailed facts that help identify the accurate state of affairs.  
   - Census-designated places, official season counts, player positions, sports championships, and geographical locations might be involved and should be treated with factual accuracy.

4. **Strategy for Query Formulation**:  
   - Identify inconsistencies or inaccuracies in the claim by contrasting it with the notes.  
   - Pinpoint exactly what factual information is in doubt or requires confirmation.  
   - Frame the query to explicitly ask for the key facts or relationships needed to verify the claim (e.g., "Which schools belong to X district?", "Who won the championship in season Y?", "Did player Z play for team A?").  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague queries; aim for precise, narrowly scoped questions focusing on entities, dates, participation, or relationships mentioned in the claim and supported by notes.

5. **Purpose of the Query**:  
   The query should maximize the retrieval of documents containing the key facts to verify each element of the claim, targeting gold documents identified in the domain. The query generated should thus effectively guide fact-checking or claim verification systems.

In summary, your task is to transform the claim and its related notes into a focused, fact-oriented question or questions (the `query`) that specify exactly what factual information needs to be checked to validate or refute the given claim.
