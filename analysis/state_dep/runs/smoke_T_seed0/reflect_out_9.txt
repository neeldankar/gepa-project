Task Description:

You will be provided with three fields as inputs:
- `claim`: a statement or assertion that may be factual or not, often involving named entities, specific events, dates, or roles.
- `notes`: a collection (list) of supporting or refuting statements, facts, or evidences relevant to verifying or analyzing the claim.
- `context`: a list of detailed contextual summaries or document excerpts containing relevant information about the entities, events, or topics mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to analyze these inputs carefully and produce two outputs:
1. `new_notes`: a concise, relevant subset or refinement of the `notes` that clearly supports or refutes the claim based on the `context`, emphasizing factual accuracy, clarity, and coverage of the key points.
2. `titles`: a list of informative, descriptive titles corresponding to the main documents or topics within the `context` that are essential to understanding or verifying the claim. The titles should clearly relate to the key entities, events, or themes involved.

Guidance and Detailed Instructions:

1. **Deeply verify the claim against the context and notes:**
   - Identify the core entities, timelines, roles, events, or facts within the claim.
   - Check the `context` excerpts for relevant facts, noting historical dates, roles, participant identities, or event outcomes.
   - Examine `notes` for corroborating or contradictory evidence.
   - Use this integrated information to judge the claim's validity or clarify its components.

2. **Extract and refine `new_notes`:**
   - Select statements most directly relevant to verifying or disproving the claim.
   - Summarize or rephrase if needed to increase clarity and factual precision.
   - Exclude redundant, irrelevant, or unsupported statements.
   - Maintain factual correctness, avoid speculation or unsupported inferences.
   - Emphasize key facts: e.g., exact dates, correct attribution of roles/actors/participants, outcome of events, and proper identification of entities.

3. **Generate accurate, descriptive `titles`:**
   - Titles should reflect the key entities and events necessary to understand the claim.
   - Use official or recognized names of people, groups, films, matches, or works mentioned in the `context`.
   - Include multiple titles if multiple main topics or documents are essential.
   - Ensure that titles are concise yet informative, and represent the main sources relevant to the claim.

4. **Domain-specific notes and strategies:**
   - Claims often relate to specific domains such as sports events (e.g., FIFA Confederations Cup), entertainment (films, voice actors), military history (regiments, battles), or biographical details.
   - Pay close attention to:
     - Proper disambiguation of similarly named persons (e.g., multiple individuals named “Nate”).
     - Accurate attribution of roles (e.g., who scored in a match, who voiced a character).
     - Dates of formation or inception of units or events.
     - Distinguishing between different kinds of football: American football vs. soccer/association football.
     - Specific titles of films, events, or teams, especially with year or subtitle disambiguators.
   - Cross-reference information for consistency (e.g., a player not mentioned in scoring but claimed to have scored).

5. **Generalizable approach:**
   - Parse claim to identify entities and claims about them.
   - Search context to find authoritative, detailed information about these entities.
   - Match notes’ contents to confirm or reject parts of the claim systematically.
   - Select key note entries that best summarize this verification.
   - Select titles representing core contextual documents critical for verification.
   - Present results succinctly and clearly for easy downstream use.

This approach will ensure accurate fact verification, precise note selection, and contextual document identification, supporting effective claim assessment across diverse knowledge domains.
