Task Description:

You will be given two inputs:  
- a `claim` — a statement or assertion whose truth or validity is to be checked via external information.  
- `notes` — a list of textual pieces of evidence, facts, or documents relevant to the claim. The notes often contain key domain-specific factual information needed to verify or refute the claim.

Your goal is to generate a `query` that will help retrieve additional relevant documents or information to verify the claim more comprehensively.

Key Details and Domain Knowledge for the Task:

1. **Inputs**:  
   - `claim`: Typically a complex factual statement involving entities, locations, dates, ownership, events, or relationships.  
   - `notes`: A set of statements extracted from related documents or knowledge bases. These notes usually cover some, but not all, essential aspects of the claim. They often clarify or contradict parts of the claim.

2. **Output**:  
   - Produce a concise but specific natural language query composed to help retrieve more documents or information that:  
     - targets what is missing or ambiguous in the notes relative to the claim,  
     - focuses on key entities, events, dates, or places related to the claim,  
     - covers the areas where verification is incomplete or uncertain,  
     - aims to help retrieve all essential documents that confirm or deny the claim.

3. **Reasoning Strategy**:  
   - Carefully analyze which parts of the claim are supported or refuted by the notes, and which parts remain unverified or unclear.  
   - Identify the key entities, factual assertions, and relationships mentioned or implied in the claim.  
   - Cross-reference these with entities and facts present in the notes to highlight coverage gaps.  
   - Formulate a query that explicitly addresses these uncovered or partially verified aspects to retrieve remaining relevant documents.  
   - Craft a clear, specific, and focused question, using factual elements (e.g., event names, entity roles, locations, dates) anchored in the claim and notes.

4. **Domain-Specific Considerations**:  
   - The claims often involve factual details about:  
     - Sports events and venues (e.g., UEFA Euro finals, stadiums, sports teams),  
     - Railroads and geographical jurisdictions (e.g., railway companies, station locations, counties and cities),  
     - Publications and individuals associated with halls of fame or awards, including publication frequencies.  
   - Understanding geographical and organizational hierarchies (e.g., football clubs and their home stadiums, railway terminus locations, locations within counties or cities).  
   - Temporal validity of facts (e.g., did a Hall of Fame exist at the stated time?)  
   - Correctly connecting entities that may seem related but are geographically or organizationally distinct.

5. **Examples of Good Queries**:  
   - Queries that question the location or ownership of venues or organizations in relation to the claim.  
   - Queries that ask for missing entities or events (e.g., specific stadiums or railway termini).  
   - Queries that clarify temporal details or publication frequencies when these affect the claim’s validity.

6. **Avoid**:  
   - Queries that are overly broad or vague and unlikely to pinpoint needed documents.  
   - Including redundant details already confirmed in the notes unless clarifying ambiguity.  
   - Queries that do not target the verification gaps or factual discrepancies arising from the claim and notes.

Summary:  
Given a `claim` and supporting or contradicting `notes`, produce a precise verification-oriented query that identifies and targets unresolved factual aspects needed to fully confirm or refute the claim through further information retrieval.
