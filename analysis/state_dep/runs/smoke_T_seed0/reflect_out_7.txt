Task Description:
You will be given a factual claim (a statement or assertion that may be true or false) and a list of related notes containing snippets of evidence or information extracted from various sources. Your task is to construct one or more precise, focused natural language search queries that, when used, would retrieve authoritative and specific documents or passages capable of conclusively verifying or refuting the claim.

Input Format:
- claim: A concise factual statement or assertion requiring verification.
- notes: A list of strings providing information related to the claim. These notes may confirm, deny, or be insufficient to fully verify the claim. Often, these notes highlight partial evidence, missing data, or discrepancies.

Output Requirements:
- Output only the field "query" containing one or multiple well-crafted natural language search queries or keyword phrases.
- Queries must be explicitly targeted to retrieve definitive facts or authoritative sources that would fill any existing gaps or ambiguities in the notes with respect to the claim.
- For multi-part claims, break down queries by each critical element or sub-claim for independent verification.
- Queries should focus on key entities, relationships, attributes, dates, events, roles, or affiliations directly relevant to the claim and unverified by the notes.
- Use clear, concise, grammatically correct language tailored for use as effective search inputs.
- Avoid broad, vague, or irrelevant queries — prioritize specificity and precision to identify definitive source documents.
    
Reasoning Strategy (Implicit in Query Construction):
- Analyze the claim to identify all its factual components (people, places, dates, events, roles, products, organizations, etc.).
- Compare these components against the notes to determine which parts are already confirmed or refuted and which remain uncertain or unverified.
- Identify missing critical details (e.g., exact dates, full names, official titles, precise locations, true affiliations, documented roles, or technical attributes) needed to complete verification.
- Formulate targeted queries aimed at retrieving authoritative sources or official records explicitly addressing these missing or ambiguous components.
- When dealing with claims involving people, include full names, roles, time frames, and associated events or affiliations to ensure unambiguous retrieval.
- For products, media, or organizations, specify relevant versions, geographic context, time periods, or other distinguishing features.
- If the claim merges multiple factual assertions, separate queries should individually target each for clarity.

Domain-Specific Details and Best Practices:
- For claims about persons (e.g., actors, musicians, professors, coaches), queries should target biographical data (birth dates, academic roles, professional positions), memberships, and geographic associations with precise temporal references where applicable.
- For organizations, sports teams, or events, specify relevant seasons, conferences, or group memberships to avoid ambiguity.
- For cultural products (films, music, products), include names, roles (e.g., director, co-director), geographical release or production contexts, and related entities or media.
- For botanical or ecological claims, specify scientific names, habitat locations with geographic specificity, and conservation status or protected areas.
- Focus queries on key gaps identified in the notes, e.g., when notes confirm a location but not establishment date, or mention affiliations but omit roles or timelines.
- Queries should aim to retrieve documents or passages beyond the current notes, to expand the evidence base and conclusively verify or refute the claim.

Summary:
Your role is to rigorously analyze the claim and the supporting or contradictory notes, identify unresolved factual elements, and craft succinct, effective search queries targeting authoritative documentary evidence that would conclusively verify or disprove the claim. Use precise natural language queries broken down into components corresponding to each claim element, keeping local contextual or domain specifics in mind, and ensure queries are well-formed for practical use in real-world information retrieval systems.
