Task Description:  
You are given a factual claim expressed as a single sentence or phrase alongside a list of "notes" — extracted information snippets from various sources relevant to the claim. Your goal is to craft one or more precise, focused natural language search queries designed to retrieve authoritative documents or passages that will conclusively verify or refute the claim by filling any gaps or addressing ambiguities not resolved by the notes.

Input Format:  
- claim: A single factual statement (sentence or phrase) whose truth needs verification.  
- notes: A list of strings providing related facts, evidence, or information from diverse sources that may support, contradict, or be insufficient for verifying the claim.

Output Requirements:  
- Provide only the output field named "query," containing one or more carefully formulated natural language questions or keyword phrases.  
- Queries must directly target key unresolved elements or missing details in the notes necessary for full verification.  
- If the claim contains multiple distinct sub-claims or components, break down the queries accordingly so each part can be independently verified.  
- Queries should focus on definitive facts such as identities, roles, key relationships, specific dates or time frames, affiliations, or unique attributes mentioned or implied by the claim.  
- Use clear, concise, grammatically correct natural language, suitable for search engines or document retrieval systems aiming to surface authoritative, specific documents.  
- Avoid vague, overly broad, or tangential queries that do not contribute to clarifying the claim’s veracity.

Reasoning & Query Construction Strategy:  
- Analyze the claim by decomposing it into its key factual components (people, roles, events, dates, products, organizations, media titles, etc.).  
- Compare these components against the notes to identify which elements are confirmed, contradicted, or insufficiently evidenced.  
- Identify precisely what factual information is missing or ambiguous. For example, unknown full names, uncertain roles, unclear timelines, unverified affiliations, or disputed events.  
- Formulate targeted queries that seek authoritative primary or secondary sources to confirm those missing or ambiguous elements.  
- When the claim names persons, include full names, roles, and specific time frames to disambiguate and retrieve correct documents.  
- For claims involving media (films, songs, products), specify exact titles, release years, versions, geographic regions, or related collaborators to avoid ambiguity.  
- For organizational or event claims, specify particular seasons, conferences, titles, or memberships to focus the search effectively.  
- Structure queries to separately verify each sub-claim to handle compound claims transparently.

Domain-Specific Details and Examples:  
- Film/Media Claims: Include director names, co-directors or writers, release years, nationalities, notable roles or character names, and production details (e.g., soundtrack producers, production companies).  
- Person Claims: Verify professions, nationality, specific roles (director, actor, producer), and temporal elements (e.g., “at the time of directing X film,” “during the 1992 film festival”).  
- Product/Brand Claims: Clarify versions by region or variant (e.g., flavor profile), production history, and official titles.  
- Organizations/Events: Confirm leadership roles during precise seasons/events, conference groupings, and membership affiliations.

Additional Guidance:  
- Your queries should enable retrieval of high-quality, authoritative sources (e.g., official filmographies, production credits, reputable databases, biographies, official event records).  
- Avoid redundancy or irrelevant general background searches; focus strictly on filling verified knowledge gaps identified from the notes.  
- If notes already confirm part of the claim, do not query that again unless to resolve a remaining ambiguity (e.g., confirming nationality of a director already named).  
- Use questions or keyword phrases that a researcher would naturally input into trusted search engines or specialized databases for fact verification.

By following this strategy and domain expertise, your query outputs will efficiently guide retrieval to conclusively verify or refute the provided claim.
