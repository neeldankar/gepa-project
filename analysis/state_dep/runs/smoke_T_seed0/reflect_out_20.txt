You will be given three fields as input:

- `claim`: a statement that makes one or more factual assertions involving people, places, organizations, events, or concepts.  
- `notes`: a list of sentences or short paragraphs containing raw relevant evidence or information extracted from documents related to the claim. These notes may include multiple perspectives, repeated information, or partial answers. You must interpret and synthesize this information carefully.  
- `context`: a list of background documents presented as factual summaries or descriptions, often including named entities such as people, organizations, events, and their pertinent attributes and historical details. These provide authoritative, encyclopedic knowledge that can confirm or refute the claim or clarify ambiguities.

Your task is to analyze the claim in light of the provided `notes` and `context` to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: a concise, curated list of statements extracted and paraphrased from the given `notes` and `context` that directly support the verification (support or refutation) of the claim. The `new_notes` should be factual, focused on the core entities and relationships necessary to understand the claim, free of irrelevant detail, and avoid redundancy. They should form a coherent mini-explanation or evidence base regarding the claim.

2. `titles`: a list of relevant document titles or named entities corresponding to the authoritative sources in the `context` that best cover the main people, places, organizations, or concepts essential to understanding and verifying the claim. These should help identify the key knowledge sources that underpin your reasoning.

General guidelines and domain-specific points:

- The `claim` often involves historical or contemporary facts, including roles or relationships between people (e.g., presidents, correspondents, authors), organizations (e.g., school districts, magazines), movements (e.g., revolutionary groups), and publications (e.g., periodicals).  
- Assess carefully the relationships and contradictions between entities (such as claims made by one person disproved by another, or inclusion/exclusion of schools in a district).  
- When discrepancies appear in `notes` about factual details (e.g., which schools belong to a district), use the `context` as an authoritative source to resolve or clarify the claim's accuracy.  
- Distinguish between similar or related terms carefully — e.g., periodical vs. magazine vs. serial; names of schools vs. informal references; persons with conflicting attributes or historical roles.  
- Use synthesized reasoning to explain how the `new_notes` and `titles` support or contradict the claim, focusing on the core facts involved.  
- Avoid including non-essential information, general commentary, or unrelated biographies unrelated to the claim and its verification.  
- The final `titles` should be a minimal, accurate set of documents highlighting the main entities or concepts, ideally matching the gold standard coverage whenever possible.

By applying this methodical, entity-centered, fact-checking and summarization approach, you will produce precise, relevant, and useful outputs that confirm, refute, or clarify complex factual claims grounded in trustworthy background data.
