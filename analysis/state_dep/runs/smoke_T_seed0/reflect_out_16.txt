Instructions for the assistant:

You are given two input fields:
- `claim`: A sentence or statement that connects entities, events, or facts, often making a factual assertion that requires verification or exploration.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets containing evidence or contextual information relevant to the claim. These snippets may mention entities, locations, dates, or other data points connected to the claim.

Your task is to produce the output field:
- `query`: A precise search or verification question derived from the claim, formulated so that it targets the key factual elements and relationships embedded in the claim. This query should be structured clearly to facilitate retrieval of relevant documents or evidence that confirm or refute the claim.

Detailed guidance and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Identify Core Entities and Relations:**  
   Extract from the claim the main entities (people, places, films, organizations, events) and the key relationships or attributes linking them. For example:
   - Locations associated with entities (e.g., the buildings connected to a sculptor)
   - Common properties or conditions applying to multiple entities (e.g., same country, same actor involvement)
   - Temporal or sequential links (e.g., film sequels and actors in them)

2. **Use the Notes to Identify Named Entities and Attributes:**  
   Since notes contain evidence snippets, use them to:
   - Confirm specific names of entities mentioned vaguely or indirectly in the claim.
   - Determine specific attributes (e.g., the exact cathedral associated with an artist, exact locations, years, or film titles).
   - Fill gaps or clarify ambiguous claims. For example, if the claim references "the 2007 sequel to 28 Days Later," notes identify the film as "28 Weeks Later."

3. **Formulate the Query to Target All Entities and Their Connection:**  
   Queries should:
   - Explicitly mention all named entities or equivalent terms identified in notes that relate to the claim.
   - Ask about the specific relationship asserted by the claim (e.g., “Are both located in England?”, “Which Welsh actor appeared in both?”, “Are two locations in the same country?”).
   - Avoid vague phrasing or leaving out key entities.
   - Align with the intended retrieval of corroborative documents covering all elements (e.g., actor names, film titles, specific buildings).

4. **General Strategy:**  
   - First, parse the claim to understand the fact pattern or assertion (geographical co-location, identity of a person, shared attributes, etc.).
   - Use the notes to identify explicit named entities and contextual facts that disambiguate or support the claim’s components.
   - Construct a clear and concise question that combines these elements to form a focused information retrieval query.
   - Ensure that the query is answerable by the evidence in the notes and consistent with the claim’s logic.

5. **Examples:**  
   - For a claim about multiple locations: “Are [building A] and [building B] both located in [country/region]?”  
   - For a claim about shared corporate or geographic affiliation: “Are [company location] and [airport location] in the same country?”  
   - For a claim about actors in films: “Which [nationality] actor appeared in both [film 1] and [film 2]?”

In summary, your output query must be a well-structured, specific verification question distilled from the claim and enriched by the facts in the notes, designed to retrieve documents that collectively validate all key aspects of the claim.
