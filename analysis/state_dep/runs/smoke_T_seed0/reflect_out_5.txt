Task Description:  
You will be given two input fields: a `claim` (a statement or assertion that makes one or more factual or relational statements) and `notes` (a list of relevant factual snippets or evidence related to the claim). Your goal is to produce a field called `query`—a concise, specific, and targeted question or query that could be used to verify or fact-check the `claim` based on the information in `notes`.

Detailed Instructions:  
1. **Input Format:**  
   - `claim`: A textual statement containing several factual assertions or complex claims often involving named entities (people, films, political parties, events, awards, dates).  
   - `notes`: A list of textual facts or evidence snippets that provide relevant background or detailed information related to the claim.

2. **Output Format:**  
   - Produce a single, precise `query` that:  
     - Directly addresses the core factual content or relationship asserted in the `claim`.  
     - Is answerable or verifiable by the information in the `notes`.  
     - Integrates key entities, dates, and domain-specific terms to avoid ambiguity.  
     - Formulates a meaningful fact-check or verification question rather than a vague or overly broad one.

3. **Core Goals and Best Practices:**  
   - **Extract the essence of the claim:** Identify the main claim components that require verification (e.g., a person’s birthdate and leadership role, a film’s award and genre classification, a film character’s association, etc.).  
   - **Use domain-specific knowledge gleaned from notes:** Incorporate relevant facts such as event dates, award names, film titles, film genres, political party names, and affiliations known from the notes or implied by them, reflecting the niche context (e.g., "Golden Bear at the Berlin International Film Festival," "New Democratic Party of Canada," or "family comedy action adventure films").  
   - **Ensure factual clarity:** The query should clearly ask if a certain fact or relationship holds true, in a way that a document retrieval or fact verification system can easily work with (i.e., a natural language fact-checking question).  
   - **Address multi-part claims:** If the claim contains multiple assertions (dates, persons, events, categorizations), incorporate all relevant components into the query while maintaining clarity and conciseness.  
   - **Avoid unsupported assumptions:** Base the query only on information inferable from the notes or clearly stated in the claim; do not invent facts or speculate beyond the provided evidence.

4. **Examples of Strategies to Follow:**  
   - Translate the complex claim into a precise question focusing on key entities and relationships (e.g., "Did any musical film win the Golden Bear at the 50th Berlin International Film Festival featuring an actor who was a U.S. Navy combat veteran in World War II?")  
   - When the claim centers on a person's identity and role associated with specific dates and party affiliations, the query should reflect those details clearly (e.g., "Who is the Canadian politician born on January 2, 1979, who became leader of the New Democratic Party on October 1st?").  
   - When dealing with film genre classifications or character associations, specify the film title, character name, and genre terms in the query to verify classification or relationship (e.g., "Are '101 Dalmatians' (1961) and G-Force officially classified as American family comedy action adventure films?").

5. **Domain Specificity and Factual Nuance:**  
   - Recognize and include domain-specific nuances like:  
     - Specific film festivals and awards (e.g., Berlin International Film Festival, Golden Bear award).  
     - Political party names and their youth wings or provincial sections (e.g., New Democratic Party, Ontario New Democratic Youth).  
     - Film classification categories (e.g., family comedy action adventure) and official character associations.  
     - Historical or biographical context of individuals (e.g., military service in WWII, birthdates, dates of leadership succession).  
   - Accurately reference dates and numeric information as these are critical to the fact-checking query.

Summary:  
Your output is a fact-checking or verification question (`query`) that captures and clarifies the main points of the `claim` using the factual context from the `notes`. This query will later be used to retrieve or check documents, so it must be precise, clear, and sufficiently detailed to guide fact verification efforts effectively.
