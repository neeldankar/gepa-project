Task Description:

You will receive three inputs:

- `claim`: A statement asserting one or more factual points, often involving specific named entities (people, places, organizations), dates, roles, events, or facts. The claim may be fully, partially, or not factual.

- `notes`: A list of statements, evidences, or assertions relevant to the claim. These may contain supporting or refuting information, overlapping or redundant details, and varying degrees of precision.

- `context`: A list of detailed summaries or excerpted documents presenting authoritative background information about the entities, events, or topics mentioned in the claim and notes.

Your goal is to carefully analyze and integrate all these inputs, and produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: A concise, refined subset of the original `notes` focused strictly on relevant, factual, and well-supported information that directly verifies, clarifies, or refutes the claim. This refinement requires:

   - Extracting core facts relevant to the claim’s entities and assertions.

   - Rephrasing or summarizing for clarity and accuracy where appropriate.

   - Removing redundancy, irrelevant or tangential information.

   - Preserving all key factual details, including exact dates, correct roles, accurate entity disambiguation, and specific outcomes/events.

   - Avoiding speculation, unclear assertions, or unsupported statements.

2. `titles`: A list of succinct, descriptive titles derived from the `context` documents. These titles must:

   - Clearly reference the main entities, events, or topics necessary for understanding or verifying the claim.

   - Use official, recognized, or commonly accepted names, including exact event titles (with year or subtitle), proper names of people or organizations, or accurate domain-specific terminology.

   - Include multiple titles if multiple context documents are central to verifying or comprehending the claim.

   - Be concise but informative, facilitating quick identification of the key background sources relevant to the claim.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Guidelines:

1. **Claim Analysis and Verification:**

   - Parse the claim to identify all named entities (people, places, teams, products, regiments), roles (e.g., player, voice actor, military rank), key events (matches, battles, film productions), dates, and factual assertions.

   - Use the `context` to find authoritative and detailed information about these entities and events, verifying facts such as:

     - Exact dates and event outcomes.

     - Correct attribution of roles (e.g., who scored or who voiced a character).

     - Disambiguation of similarly named individuals or groups.

     - Correct identification of countries, locations, or organizations.

     - Distinctions between close or commonly confused entities (e.g., two cemeteries with similar names or two different football codes).

     - Production details and affiliations in entertainment contexts (e.g., film titles, release years, voice actor credits).

   - Cross-check the `notes` against `context` to confirm their accuracy and relevance in supporting or refuting the claim.

2. **Note Refinement and Selection:**

   - Select only those statements from `notes` that have clear support or contradiction in `context`.

   - Prioritize high-precision information that directly addresses elements of the claim.

   - Summarize or refactor note entries to increase clarity and factual correctness.

   - Remove duplicated or overlapping notes.

   - Exclude notes that speculate beyond available evidence or are not relevant to core claim components.

3. **Title Generation:**

   - Titles must reflect the main contextual documents essential for validating or understanding the claim.

   - Use well-known official or standard naming conventions.

   - Include any necessary disambiguation (e.g., film title with year, full event name, specific location identifiers).

   - When no direct perfect match exists in context, create descriptive yet concise titles that encapsulate the key entities or events featured in the documents.

4. **Generalizable Strategy:**

   - Systematically parse the claim into discrete factual units.

   - Map entities and events to authoritative context excerpts.

   - Verify facts by cross-referencing notes and context.

   - Eliminate irrelevant or imprecise information.

   - Condense supportive info into clear, factual `new_notes`.

   - Identify and name critical context documents with clear, standardized titles.

Domain-Specific Considerations:

- **Sports:** Pay attention to the exact sport (e.g., American football vs. soccer), event formats (e.g., Men's Doubles, Mixed Doubles), player roles, scoring details, defending champions, partner changes, tournament editions, and venue locations.

- **Military History:** Correctly identify regiments, divisions, battles, army roles, formation years, and disambiguate similarly named military units.

- **Entertainment (Films, Video Games):** Note correct release years, production companies, official titles, voice actor credits and roles, platform origins, and geographic production origins.

- **Geography and Cemeteries:** Distinguish between similarly named locations or cemeteries, especially when they are geographically adjacent or commonly confused. Confirm burial locations precisely.

- **Individuals:** Disambiguate people with similar names, verify nationality or origin (e.g., Germans vs. Texans), roles (e.g., professional player vs. military rank), and contextual timelines.

- **Computing Platforms:** Focus on origin company countries, platform release dates, and market context.

This comprehensive approach ensures the assistant produces factually accurate refinements and informative titles that support effective, domain-appropriate verification of claims.

Remember: Always ground your output strictly in the provided `context` and validated `notes`. Avoid adding unverified information or assumptions, and maintain clarity, conciseness, and factual precision in both outputs.
