Given an input consisting of a `claim` (a factual statement or assertion) and a set of supporting or contextual `notes` (relevant documents, facts, or evidences), your task is to produce a `query` field. The `query` should be a concise, fact-seeking question formulated to effectively guide retrieval or verification of key missing or critical information needed to evaluate the `claim`.

Detailed requirements:

1. **Input format**:  
   - `claim`: a factual statement that may be complex and contain multiple facts or entities.  
   - `notes`: a list of textual evidences or relevant facts related to the claim. These include specific domain-related and niche factual details about people, places, events, or objects mentioned in the claim.

2. **Output**:  
   - Produce a single, clear, and focused `query`—a question (or series of interconnected questions, combined with "and"/"or" as needed) that addresses the key unknown or ambiguous parts of the claim. The query should target what remains to be verified or what additional supporting documents are necessary for definitive claim validation.  
   - The query should combine multiple related information needs in the claim into one meaningful question when appropriate (for instance, confirming both authorship and role of a person, or location and ownership of a venue).

3. **Understanding domain-specific and niche factual knowledge**:  
   - The notes and claims often reference very specific factual knowledge such as:  
     - The precise authorship of songs or lyrics, including specific lyricists, band members, and their roles.  
     - Historical roles, positions, and family relations in sensitive historical contexts (e.g., Auschwitz commandants, negotiations).  
     - Locations and ownership structures of entertainment venues including residencies, concert halls, corporations owning hotels and casinos.  
   - The query should acknowledge the complexity and specificity of these domains by explicitly naming key people, places, events, and relationships necessary to retrieve.

4. **Generalizable strategy for query construction**:  
   - Analyze the claim for multiple facets that need verification—e.g., who, what role, when, where, ownership, or connections.  
   - Check notes for what is already confirmed or contradicted and identify gaps or missing entities that remain unverified or partially referenced.  
   - Formulate the query to explicitly target these outstanding knowledge gaps in a precise question format.  
   - Use phrasing such as "Who are the credited...", "Is X involved in...", "Where is X located and who owns...", or "Was the grandfather of X involved in..." depending on claim scope.  
   - Ensure the question(s) are clear, specific, and free from speculative language.

By following these instructions, your produced queries will be well-tailored for document retrieval or fact verification tasks involving complex, domain-specific claims and detailed evidentiary notes.
