Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a precise `query` that can be used to verify or fact-check the given `claim` against the evidence contained in the `notes`.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A single factual statement or assertion about entities, events, or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of multiple informative snippets or extracted factual sentences relevant to the claim.

Output Requirements:
- Produce a concise, unambiguous natural language question (`query`) that directly targets the key factual elements of the `claim`.
- The query should be designed to retrieve or verify relevant documents or evidence supporting or refuting the claim.
- The query must mention critical specific entities, dates, locations, and relationships apparent in the claim and supported by the notes.
- The query should correctly disambiguate any potentially confusing references (e.g., clarify which band or album, name clarifications, or event details) that appear in the claim.
- The query must reflect the relevant domain or context derived from the notes (e.g., music groups, albums, sports events, stadiums).

Domain and Task-Specific Details:
- The factual domain spans popular music (bands, album releases, band formations, departures), sports events (football finals, stadiums, football clubs), and related venues.
- Claims often involve temporal relations (e.g., if a person left a band before a certain event), formation dates of entities, or geographic locations tied to events or groups.
- Notes may contain multiple near-duplicate snippets confirming the same facts.
- Disambiguate entities when claims involve multiple similarly named bands or venues.
- Queries should aim to retrieve the minimal and gold-standard documents necessary to confirm the claim.
- When the claim contains possible contradictions or errors, the query should focus on the key factual points that verify or disprove the described relationships.
  
Generalizable Strategy:
1. Identify the core factual assertions in the claim (who, what, when, where).
2. From the notes, pinpoint the relevant entities, dates, and events related to those assertions.
3. Formulate a direct question asking whether the claim’s assertion about these entities and events is true, specifically including all disambiguators used in the notes.
4. Ensure the query is self-contained and can be used for precise document or evidence retrieval.
5. Avoid extraneous information or overly broad questions; keep the query focused on claim validation.

Example Template:
If the claim states: "[Person] left [Group] before [Event/Release]."
And notes support that [Person] left [Group] at date X and [Event/Release] occurred at date Y > X,
Then the query should be:
"Did [Person] leave [Group] before the release of [Event/Release]?"

If the claim states involvement of multiple bands or albums:
"Was [Band A] formed in [Year]? Was [Band B], the band that released [Album], also formed in [Year]?"

If addressing location and association:
"Is [Stadium/Event Location] located in [Place A] and does it currently house [Club/Team]?"

Final Note:
Queries should be optimized to retrieve all and only the essential documents validating the claim, maximizing recall and precision relevant to the gold documents annotated in the feedback.
