Task Description:
You are given a `claim` (a factual statement or assertion) and a set of `notes` containing extracted relevant information, analysis, or background knowledge related to the entities and concepts in the claim. Your task is to produce a precise `query` (or set of queries/questions) that can be issued to an external knowledge base or search system to retrieve documents or data necessary to verify the truthfulness of the claim.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A factual statement involving entities, events, or attributes.
- `notes`: A list of textual points summarizing relevant known facts, contradictions, or missing information pertinent to the claim. These notes identify gaps, ambiguities, or fact-checking requirements about the entities or relationships mentioned in the claim.

Output Format:
- `query`: One or more well-formulated, focused questions or queries designed to retrieve authoritative documents or data that address the key factual uncertainties identified in the notes. These queries often include named entities, dates, titles, birth information, or status relevant to the claim's verification.

Key Instructions and Domain-Specific Details:
1. Focus on Fact-Checking:  
   Your queries must target the core fact-checking needs revealed by the notes. For instance, if the claim concerns birth order or origin, formulate queries that ask specifically about birth dates, birthplaces, or nationality of the named individuals.

2. Handling Named Entities:  
   When the claim references specific people, events, competitions, breeds, or other specialized terms:
   - Ask for authoritative identification or classification (e.g., is X a breed or an individual dog name?).
   - Ask for details about any relationship or participation in named events (e.g., did person A compete in event B?).
   - If there is confusion about entity roles (e.g., lead guitarist of a band), query for the identity and relevant details of that entity.

3. Addressing Ambiguities and Missing Data:  
   Use the notes to identify what information is missing or contradictory. Formulate queries that explicitly seek this missing data, such as dates, rankings, origins, or associations.

4. Domain-Specific Entities and Examples:  
   - Sports: Player origins, event participation, defending champions, event years and categories.
   - Dog Breeds: Distinguish between individual dog names and recognized breeds.
   - Music: Band names, individual roles (lead guitarist), song titles, birth dates for chronological comparison.

5. General Strategy for Query Construction:
   - Break down complex claims into multiple fact-focused questions if necessary.
   - Include all relevant named entities in queries to maximize retrieval precision.
   - Formulate simple, direct questions rather than complex or compound interrogatives.

6. Aim for Retrieval of Gold Standard Documents:
   Your queries should be designed to retrieve the authoritative documents needed to confirm or debunk the claim as identified by the feedback (e.g., specific people’s bios, event records, breed registries, discographies).

By adhering to this approach, you will produce queries that effectively support downstream fact verification processes using external knowledge sources.
