Task Description:

You will be provided with three fields as input:
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that connects or compares entities, people, places, or events.
- `notes`: A list of short factual snippets or sentences that provide supporting information relevant to the claim.
- `context`: A list of detailed documents or excerpts from knowledge sources (e.g., encyclopedic entries) providing background information on the entities or concepts in the claim and notes.

Your goal is to analyze these inputs and produce the following output fields:
- `new_notes`: A concise list of factually accurate and relevant notes distilled or synthesized from the original notes and context that directly support or clarify the claim.
- `titles`: A list of key document or entity titles (from the context or notes) that are essential sources or references for verifying or understanding the claim. These usually correspond to Wikipedia-style article titles related to people, places, films, or topics mentioned in the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understand the Claim:
   - Carefully parse the claim to identify the core factual contention, such as verifying birthplace, occupation, or relationships between entities, or confirming whether certain conditions hold true.
   - Identify the entities and concepts mentioned (people, places, films, professions, etc.).

2. Validate with Notes and Context:
   - Cross-reference the notes and context to find evidence supporting or refuting the claim.
   - Look for direct confirmations such as documented birthplaces, professions, film roles, or notable attributes.
   - Leverage detailed context material to verify factual background, especially for places (e.g., geographic, demographic data), people (biographies, occupations), and works (films, publications).

3. Synthesize Supporting Notes:
   - From the accumulated evidence, create new, concise notes that accurately capture the key supporting facts.
   - Avoid repeating redundant information.
   - If any part of the claim cannot be verified due to lack of evidence, explicitly acknowledge this in the new_notes with a neutral statement.

4. Identify Relevant Titles:
   - Extract the most relevant document or entity titles from the context and notes that underpin the claim verification.
   - These titles should be meaningful, commonly recognized entities related to the claim (e.g., person names, film titles, place names).
   - Ensure completeness by covering all key entities or concepts needed to understand or validate the claim.

5. Generalizable Approach:
   - Parse claim entities and relationships.
   - Verify assertions using provided notes for concise facts.
   - Confirm details and add depth using context documents.
   - Distill supporting facts into clear, non-redundant new notes.
   - Compile a comprehensive set of titles referring to key supporting documents.

6. Domain-Specific Notes:
   - Birthplaces are often corroborated by matching personal biographies and geographical entries.
   - Occupations and roles (e.g., film director, producer, screenwriter) should be verified from person biographies or film credits.
   - Film or event-related claims require titles referencing the work and associated individuals.
   - When multiple individuals are referenced, ensure clarity about their distinct roles and relations.
   - If information about an entity in the claim is missing from notes and context, state this explicitly.

7. Writing Style:
   - Express new_notes as factual, concise sentences.
   - Use neutral and precise phrasing.
   - Avoid speculation beyond what is evidenced by notes and context.

Example Output Structure:
```
### new_notes
[<list of concise, supportive fact statements>]

### titles
[<list of relevant article or entity titles>]
```

This instruction ensures the assistant provides focused, evidence-based supporting notes and relevant source titles to facilitate claim verification with a transparent and systematic method.
