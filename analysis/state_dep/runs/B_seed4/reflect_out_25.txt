Task Description:
You will be given an input with two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to generate a focused and precise `query` field that will help verify the claim or retrieve key missing information necessary for a thorough evaluation of the claim.

Definitions:  
- `claim`: A factual assertion or a set of related factual assertions concerning specific entities (people, bands, works, events, dates, attributes, roles, etc.) and their relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of sentences or short paragraphs providing supporting, contextual, or background information related to the claim. These notes may be overlapping, repetitive, or partially contradictory, reflecting the current state of retrieved knowledge.

Objective:  
Create a concise, clear, and factually-targeted **natural language question** or **factual query** designed to:  
- Confirm or refute the core factual points of the claim,  
- Resolve ambiguities or contradictions found between claim and notes,  
- Retrieve critical, missing details or evidence needed to fully verify the claim.

Key Knowledge and Domain-Specific Considerations:  
1. Identification of Core Entities and Elements:  
   Extract key factual components from the claim, including but not limited to:  
   - Names of individuals (e.g., musicians, actors, directors, athletes)  
   - Titles of works (e.g., operas, films, albums)  
   - Dates or years of important events (e.g., premieres, competitions)  
   - Specific roles or character names  
   - Relationships, collaborations, or achievements specified or implied  
   
2. Use of Notes to Analyze Evidentiary Gaps:  
   - Cross-check claim facts with notes to see which facts are already supported, contradicted, or missing.  
   - Detect contradictions or ambiguities (e.g., conflicting premiere dates, uncertain origins, disputed inspirations).  
   - Identify whether all entities mentioned in the claim have sufficient supporting data; if not, highlight these gaps.

3. Query Construction Requirements:  
   - Formulate the query as a natural language question or clear factual request that specifically targets unresolved fact(s).  
   - Focus only on the most relevant, unverified or ambiguously supported aspects of the claim to avoid redundancy and maintain clarity.  
   - When multiple unresolved sub-claims or related facts are strongly connected, combine them into one comprehensive query for efficient retrieval.  
   - Ensure query precision to enable retrieving documents that verify core claim components (e.g., full names, detailed event dates, relationship specifics).  
   - Avoid generic or overly broad queries; queries should directly support claim verification.

4. Strategy to Solve the Task:  
   - Break down the claim into atomic or related factual assertions.  
   - Map these factual assertions against the notes for coverage and contradictions.  
   - Identify which facts remain unsupported, ambiguous, or contradictory.  
   - Formulate a targeted query that explicitly requests information to confirm, clarify, or deny these facts, and that covers critical details missing in the notes.  
   - When multiple fact components are closely linked and pivotal, incorporate all into a single clear question.  

Example Guidance:  
- If claim contains a person’s involvement in an event (e.g., a tennis match result), ask explicitly for opponent details and event outcome to confirm the claim.  
- If claim includes conflicting dates across data sources, frame the query to ask for authoritative, verified dates.  
- If a claim compares achievements or awards between entities, the query should request a comparative summary of award records for those entities.  
- For claims involving inspiration or origins, ask specifically about the factual links or source inspirations to resolve contradictory accounts.

Summary:  
Given a `claim` and associated `notes`, your output `query` must be a focused natural language request that:  
- Identifies the key missing or ambiguous fact(s) from the claim-notes comparison  
- Requests precise, factual information needed for claim verification  
- Resolves contradictions or clarifies ambiguities  
- Covers all critical related sub-claims within one clear, concise query where applicable  
- Avoids redundancy or irrelevant broadness

This process ensures that the generated queries are optimized to retrieve the exact high-value documents or information fragments that will complete the verification process for the claim with minimal retrieval overhead.
