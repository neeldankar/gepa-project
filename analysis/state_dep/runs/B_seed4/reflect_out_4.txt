- `claim`: a factual assertion or set of assertions that needs to be verified or supported.

- `context`: a set (list) of document summaries, excerpts, or metadata (including titles) related to the topic areas of the claim; documents may have overlapping or nuanced information concerning entities, works, dates, or events referenced in the claim.

Your task is to carefully analyze the claim in conjunction with the notes and context, then produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: A concise, focused subset and/or reformulation of the original `notes` that directly relate to verifying or supporting the claim. These should:

   - Emphasize key facts or evidence confirming, refuting, or leaving unverified each component of the claim.
   - Exclude redundant, speculative, ambiguous, irrelevant, or off-topic information.
   - Clarify or consolidate repeated information.
   - When the claim has multiple parts or assertions (e.g., about multiple individuals, works, or events), address all parts with respective relevant facts.
   - Pay close attention to domain-specific details such as:
     - Identification of works (films, albums, operas, teams) by precise titles and dates.
     - Attribution of roles or attributes (e.g., voice actor for a character, composer of an opera, coach of a team).
     - Relationships between entities (e.g., an album by a band formed by a musician, involvement in a particular event or game).
     - Temporal information including years or periods to discriminate between similarly named works or events.
     - Naming variations or alternate titles.
     - Accolades or achievements (e.g., Nobel prizes, medals won) and their context such as nationality or event.
   - If evidence is insufficient to verify the full claim, explicitly indicate this with accurate partial support or definitive negatives.

2. `titles`: A list of document titles from the `context` that are most relevant and authoritative to verify or support the claim. Selection should be based on:

   - Direct factual relevance to the entities, events, or topics in the claim.
   - Covering all crucial elements of the claim, especially when it references multiple topics or assertions.
   - Prioritizing so-called "gold standard" or key documents known to be fundamentally connected to the claim if they are present.
   - Precision in picking exact titles when multiple overlapping or similar documents are present (e.g., distinguishing operas with the same name by different composers).
   - Including documents that clarify or resolve contradictory or ambiguous claims in notes.

Additional domain-specific and strategic considerations:

- Claims often involve creative works (films, operas, albums), individuals (actors, musicians, athletes) with notable achievements, or specific teams and events. Associate claims with corresponding domains precisely.
- Avoid selecting extraneous or background documents unless they directly contribute to claim verification.
- Use relationships and hierarchies between entities (e.g., a composer’s family members, team coaches, or actors’ nationalities) to confirm or disprove assertions.
- Track temporal distinctions carefully to avoid conflating similarly named works or events from different eras.
- When claims combine multiple independent facts (e.g., an accolade and a team affiliation), support or refute each with appropriate notes and ensure titles cover all these facets.
- Clearly signal when verification cannot be completed due to lack of supporting evidence, but still provide the best possible partial or contradicting facts.
- Repetitive notes should be merged or deduplicated.
- Employ logical deduction based on domain-specific knowledge present in notes/context to validate or refute premises inherent in the claim.

Your final output format must be:

- `new_notes`: a list of concise, directly pertinent sentences supporting your fact-checking analysis.
- `titles`: a list of strings containing the document titles from `context` best suited for verifying the claim.

The purpose is to assist in accurate, evidence-based claim verification along with retrieval of the most critical source documents to support or refute the claim.

Be precise, factual, and ensure your selections trace clearly back to the claim components and supplied evidence.
