Task Description:
You will be given inputs containing two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your role is to generate a concise, clear, and factually precise **query** to retrieve information necessary to verify the claim or fill critical evidence gaps. The `claim` is a factual assertion or set of related assertions about specific entities, events, dates, roles, or attributes. The `notes` are a collection of sentences or short paragraphs offering supporting information, context, or potentially conflicting data related to the claim.

Your objective is to produce a single, focused search query or information request that will:

- Confirm or refute the main factual points in the claim,
- Resolve any contradictions or ambiguities found in the notes,
- Retrieve missing key details essential for comprehensive claim evaluation,
- Seek the crucial supporting documents or evidence traces yet unretrieved.

Key Domain-Specific Details to Consider:

1. **Entities and Attributes to Identify:**
   - People: names (full where possible), roles/characters played, awards, professions.
   - Works: titles of films, books, TV shows, documentaries, video games.
   - Dates: release, publication, event dates, and timing relations between works.
   - Relationships: who did what, chronology among multiple works, casting, authorship.
   - Specifics such as roles played in works, exact publication years, and whether outlets (magazines, journals) are periodicals.

2. **Evidence Gaps and Ambiguities to Address:**
   - Missing names of key people or works.
   - Unknown or uncertain release/publication dates.
   - Conflicting or unverified claims about roles, chronology, or award status.
   - Unconfirmed relationships or factual dependencies (e.g., whether two entities coexist as periodicals).
   - Details that would link disparate facts into a coherent verification.

3. **Use of the Notes:**
   - Cross-check against the claim’s components to spot which facts are confirmed by the notes.
   - Highlight which aspects remain unsupported, ambiguous, lacking explicit evidence, or contradicted.
   - Avoid redundant queries for facts already established or clearly disproven.
   - Combine queries when multiple closely related facts need retrieval for verification efficiency.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Break down the claim into its essential factual components.
- Examine the notes to identify which parts have been confirmed and which remain unverified or ambiguous.
- Formulate a focused question or factual query targeting all unretrieved or unclear sub-claims relevant to claim validation.
- Combine related sub-queries into one logically integrated question when feasible (e.g., asking about release dates of two related films and casting details in one query).
- Keep queries succinct yet comprehensive enough to avoid multiple follow-ups.
- Prioritize clarity and precision to support retrieval of definitive evidence.
- Frame queries in natural language but targeted as fact-finding requests.

Additional Nuances from Examples:

- When the claim references a person’s role in multiple works, query to identify the person, confirm roles, and verify the link between the works.
- When dates or temporal order claims appear (e.g., film A after film B), request exact release years and relative chronology.
- For publication claims involving articles or books in magazines or journals, verify the publication year and the nature of the publication(s) (periodical or otherwise).
- When ambiguous or contradictory information about award status, actors, or adaptations arises, specifically seek to confirm or deny these status claims.
- When claims refer to works or people absent in the notes, explicitly query for those entities to retrieve the missing documents.

Output Format:

- Deliver a single well-structured, natural language query or information request.
- Avoid overlong or compound queries that may dilute focus.
- The query should encompass all key unresolved factual details needed for a final assessment of the claim.

Summary:

Your instruction is: Given a `claim` and accompanying `notes`, generate one clear, fact-seeking query that:

- Targets all unresolved factual components critical to verifying the claim,
- Addresses contradictions, ambiguities, or missing details,
- Is focused, concise, and comprehensive enough to retrieve the most relevant information or evidence sources needed.

This will facilitate effective retrieval of key documents and decisive fact verification.
