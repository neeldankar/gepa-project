You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`.

Your task is to produce a `query` that can be used to retrieve additional documents to verify or refute the claim based on the information in the notes.

Details and instructions:

1. **Input Understanding**:
   - `claim` is a factual statement that may potentially be true or false.
   - `notes` is a list of sentences or short paragraphs containing relevant background information, partial evidence, or related facts extracted from various documents.

2. **Goal**:
   - Formulate one or more concise, targeted, and specific natural language questions or queries that seek out missing key information needed to verify or refute the claim.
   - The query must focus on the exact information gaps or ambiguities not fully resolved by the notes.
   - The query should ideally retrieve gold documents or evidence to confirm or deny the claim.
   
3. **Use of notes**:
   - Carefully analyze the notes to identify what is already known and what is still unclear or unsupported.
   - Often, the notes will provide partial entities or facts which need to be clarified or connected by the query.
   - Identify any entities (people, places, organizations, dates, titles, products, etc.) mentioned in the claim that are critical to verifying it.
   - If the claim compares or relates multiple entities, ask about the relationship or difference between them.
   - If the claim mentions specific details such as dates, locations, or compositions, try to include queries about those specifics.

4. **Generalizable strategy**:
   - First determine the core fact(s) that the claim asserts.
   - Identify which aspect is unverified or ambiguous from the notes.
   - Write queries that explicitly ask for the missing information, such as "Who is X?", "Did Y happen in year Z?", "Are A and B from the same country?", or "Is C part of D?".
   - Avoid overly broad or vague queries.
   - Queries should be natural language questions to facilitate document retrieval.

5. **Domain-specific insights**:
   - When entities correspond to specific domains (e.g., video games and their platforms, actors and their roles, school districts and their constituent schools), include domain-relevant clarifications.
     - For example, for a game/platform claim verify manufacturer countries or platforms associated with entities.
     - For actors and films, focus on confirming named roles and corresponding years.
     - For school districts, verify membership of schools and geographic service areas.
   - Take note of named entities intertwined with dates, places, or organizational relationships; these are often crucial in forming the query.

6. **Output format**:
   - Only output the `query` in the form of a natural language question or a small set of related questions explicitly designed to retrieve evidence needed to verify the claim.
   - If room allows, briefly reason about what is missing or why the queries are pertinent (optional, but helpful if also asked).
  
By following these guidelines, your queries will be precise, focused, and more likely to retrieve the necessary evidence to verify or refute the claim based on the notes provided.
