You will be provided with three inputs:

- `claim`: a factual statement (or multi-part statement) whose truthfulness or accuracy must be verified or supported.
- `notes`: a list of extracted sentences summarizing relevant factual information from various documents.
- `context`: a list of document titles and their summaries, excerpts, or metadata, which may include official or authoritative sources related to the claim.

**Your task:** Perform a detailed critical analysis of these inputs to produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`  
   - Select and/or paraphrase a concise subset of the provided notes that directly and unambiguously relate to verifying or supporting each factual component of the claim.  
   - Focus strictly on factual, relevant information that confirms, refutes, or leaves the claim unverified based on solid evidence.  
   - Eliminate any redundant, irrelevant, speculative, contradictory, or overly general information not helpful for claim verification.  
   - For claims with multiple parts, systematically address each part separately with matching relevant notes.  
   - When evidence is incomplete for full verification, clearly state this while presenting all partial, reliable evidence found.  
   - Prioritize precise, domain-specific facts: e.g., identities, roles, relationships, dates, alternate titles, nationalities, production credits, awards, or sponsorships as relevant.  
   - Explicitly highlight any errors, misattributions, or contradictory evidence found related to the claim, grounding all conclusions in the notes.

2. `titles`  
   - Select a focused list of document titles from the provided context that are the most authoritative and directly relevant for verifying the claim.  
   - Titles must correspond explicitly to the key entities mentioned in the claim (individuals, films, albums, universities, events, products, etc.).  
   - Prefer titles that exactly match the claim references, including primary or "gold standard" documents (e.g., official filmographies, biographies, main works) when available.  
   - Ensure coverage of all significant components of the claim through the chosen titles.  
   - Avoid titles that are tangential, generic, or loosely related.  
   - If key gold standard documents mentioned in the claim are missing in context, note their absence but base your selection on available documents.

**Domain-specific and task-specific guidance:**

- Claims often involve specific works (films, albums, novels, etc.) or notable individuals (actors, musicians, directors, athletes), including their roles, associations, and biographical data. Relationships and correct attributions (e.g., lead vs. supporting roles, nationality, alternate work titles) are crucial.  
- Details such as variant or alternate titles, release years, and production or award credits are often pivotal to verification. Be attentive to these nuances.  
- When the claim involves multiple entities or assertions, dissect the claim deliberately and verify each one against corresponding notes.  
- Explicitly recognize and highlight contradictions or inaccuracies in the claim (e.g., wrong nationality, incorrect titles, non-existent associations) grounded in evidence.  
- If evidence neither fully confirms nor denies the claim but offers relevant partial insights, provide this nuance carefully and clearly.  
- Exclude any speculative, irrelevant, or unrelated information, even if superficially connected to the claim.

**General strategy for accuracy and precision:**

- Decompose the claim into its fundamental factual components or assertions.  
- Identify and isolate notes that directly support or refute each component.  
- Cross-reference notes with contextual document titles to choose the most relevant authoritative sources for verification.  
- Remove redundancies, contradictions, and non-pertinent details from the final new_notes output.  
- Select titles in the output that comprehensively cover the claim’s key subjects.  
- Clearly state when critical authoritative sources referenced in the claim are missing from the context but proceed with available information.

**Output formatting:**

- Return `new_notes` as a list of concise, factual sentences focused exclusively on the evidence relevant to verifying the claim.  
- Return `titles` as a list of strings with the selected document titles exactly as provided in the input context.

The goal is to produce factually precise, focused evidential notes and a compact, authoritative set of source documents that enable robust verification or refutation of the claim.
