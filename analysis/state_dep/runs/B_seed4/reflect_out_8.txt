Task: Given a factual claim (field `claim`) and a set of relevant extracted notes (field `notes`) corresponding to various related entities or events, produce a precise and focused search query (field `query`) that would efficiently retrieve supporting evidence or documents needed to verify the claim.

Detailed Description:  
1. The `claim` is a complex factual statement involving entities such as people, institutions, films, events, and temporal or relational assertions (e.g., chronological order, award wins, participation in works).  
2. The `notes` constitute extracted factual snippets or statements that contain partial or complete information relevant to verifying the `claim`. These notes often reference specific entities (e.g., names of actors, film titles, award ceremonies), dates (e.g., years of establishment, release or premiere dates), and relationships (e.g., actor cast overlaps, award recipients). The notes may also include contradictory or clarifying details to help assess the claim’s validity.  
3. The primary goal is to transform the `claim` and the supporting contextual `notes` into a precise, multi-part natural language `query` that:  
   - Explicitly targets the key entities mentioned in the claim and noted in the `notes`.  
   - Focuses on the factual predicates required to confirm or refute the claim, such as:  
     * Which entity did "X" participate in or is associated with?  
     * What are the founding/release/award dates for specific entities?  
     * Is there any overlap or relationship between specific actors, films, or events?  
     * Verification of chronological order or sequence of events.  
   - Covers all relevant aspects required to fully validate or falsify the claim, avoiding ambiguity or generality.  
4. The `query` should be explicit and sufficiently detailed to guide retrieval systems to fetch all necessary documents or evidence pertaining to:  
   - Entities (persons, institutions, films, festivals, TV shows, awards) especially those directly referenced in the claim and notes.  
   - Dates or timeframes significant to the claim’s predicates.  
   - Relationships (e.g., participation, award wins, overlap of cast members, historical ordering).  
5. Reasoning prior to crafting the `query` involves:  
   - Identifying the critical facts or entities that need verification.  
   - Determining what questions these facts answer within the claim.  
   - Formulating precise queries that target those explicit questions rather than vague or partial inquiries.  

Generalizable Strategy From Examples:  
- Parse the claim into elemental factual assertions (e.g., "X completed postdoctoral training at institution Y established before Z", or "an actor was in Film A and also performed in Film B after Event C").  
- Use the notes to confirm which specific entities, dates, or events appear relevant and should be focal points of the query.  
- Formulate the `query` as a set of questions aiming to retrieve documents about these entities and their relationships, including temporal ordering when required.  
- Ensure the query asks for all information needed simultaneously if multiple predicates are involved (e.g., institution founded years + person-institution relationship + other institution’s founding year).  
- Avoid leaving out any entity or date that figures prominently in the claim or notes and would be necessary to confirm or refute it.  

By following the above, the assistant will produce targeted, comprehensive, and unambiguous queries designed to effectively retrieve all crucial evidence related to verifying complex factual claims involving multiple entities and temporal or relational dimensions.
