Task Description:  
You will be given an input that contains two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a single `query` field — a clear, concise, and fact-focused search query or information request designed to help verify the claim or to obtain missing critical facts needed to fully evaluate the claim.  
  
Definitions:  
- The `claim` is a factual assertion containing one or more entities (people, organizations, shows, events, etc.), dates, roles, relationships, or achievements. These often involve domain-specific details such as athlete names, actor names, character names, film or TV series titles, albums, competition years, and other granular facts.  
- The `notes` field is a collection of factual sentences or short paragraphs related to the claim. The notes can have overlapping, repeated, ambiguous, or even contradictory information, and may not cover all details needed to verify the claim completely.  
  
Objective:  
Formulate a natural language query that:  
- Targets the core factual components of the claim (key entities, dates, achievements, roles, event details).  
- Explicitly focuses on any missing or ambiguous information revealed by comparison to the notes.  
- Seeks to resolve contradictions, clarify unclear points, or obtain evidence that is not yet confirmed by supplied notes.  
- Is sufficiently comprehensive to cover closely related sub-facts or multiple connected aspects of the claim if they affect overall claim verification.  
- Is concise, clear, non-redundant, and rich in relevant domain-specific factual detail to maximize retrieval of relevant documents.  
  
Domain-Specific Considerations:  
- Key entities include people’s full names (actors, athletes, singers, directors), team names, event titles, album names, character names, dates of events, and specific occupations or roles (e.g., judge, goalie, producer).  
- Claims often involve relationships such as membership in teams, cast participation, roles played, event participation, career milestones, or historical attributions (e.g., who “led” a strike force, who “joined” a show as a judge).  
- Multiple related entities or roles mentioned together should be queried in a single comprehensive question to capture interconnected facts.  
- When notes contradict or fail to confirm parts of the claim, highlight the ambiguous or contradictory elements as core targets for the query.  
- Include requests for verification of overarching labels or nicknames if such assertions remain unconclusively supported (e.g., “Is Manchester United known as the Red Devils?”).  
  
Generalizable Strategy to Develop Queries:  
1. Parse the claim into distinct fact assertions focusing on: entities, relationships, dates, roles, and achievements.  
2. Compare each fact against the notes to identify:  
   - Confirmed facts (well-supported by notes, no need to query)  
   - Missing facts (entities, roles, dates not documented)  
   - Contradicted or ambiguous facts needing resolution  
3. For the missing or ambiguous facts, generate precise natural language questions that:  
   - Explicitly request all critical missing details together to minimize iterative queries, e.g., “Who was X during Y event, and did X join Z?”  
   - Clarify contradicting facts by requesting authoritative information, e.g., “Was Junaid Khan a judge on Music Icons or just a host?”  
   - Verify identity or involvement of key persons or entities when claims conflated or misattributed roles.  
4. Avoid repeating confirmed facts or non-essential background to maintain brevity and focus.  
5. When a claim mixes several elements (e.g., a group of judges plus an additional member, or a nickname plus player transfers), bundle relevant verification questions instead of splitting.  
  
Examples of Query Focus From Prior Cases:  
- Verification of official nicknames or common labels for organizations (e.g., “Is Manchester United FC known as ‘the Red Devils’?”)  
- Identification of specific people fulfilling roles at specific times (e.g., “Who was the Leicester City goalie in 1928-29 season?”)  
- Confirmation of actor appearances in certain films (e.g., “Did Diane Keaton star in The Death and Life of John F. Donovan?”)  
- Clarification of judge membership on TV shows (e.g., “Was Junaid Khan a judge on Music Icons?”)  
- Verification of who joined a team or group and when, especially if notes list conflicting or incomplete memberships.  
  
Final Output:  
A single, well-constructed, fact-seeking query phrased as a natural language question or compound question, designed specifically to uncover authoritative factual evidence that:  
- Confirms or denies the central assertions of the claim  
- Fills in critical fact gaps not covered or ambiguous in the notes  
- Resolves contradictions or unclear details  
- Includes multiple connected facts in one query when appropriate.  
  
This precise and focused approach enables efficient retrieval of missing reference documents and comprehensive assessment of claim accuracy.
