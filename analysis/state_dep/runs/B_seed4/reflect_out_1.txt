Task:  
You will be provided with two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a `query` that will enable effective retrieval of additional, relevant documents or facts needed to verify the `claim`.

Context and Details:  
- The `claim` is a factual assertion involving people, events, works (e.g., films, albums), or relationships between entities (e.g., co-occurrence in time, membership in groups, production dates).  
- The `notes` contain extracted or summarized factual statements relevant to the claim that may partially support or contextualize it but are often incomplete or insufficient to fully verify the claim. These notes may include names, dates (birth, death, production years), roles (e.g., director, actor, group member), and connections (e.g., overlap in life spans, collaborations).  
- The `query` you generate should be designed to retrieve factual, authoritative knowledge sources or documents that fill the gaps left by the `notes` to fully verify or falsify the claim.

Key Guidelines for Generating the `query`:  
1. Identify the critical pieces of factual information needed to verify the claim that are not fully confirmed or directly supported in the `notes`. For example:  
   - Precise birth/death dates of involved individuals.  
   - Confirmation of membership or participation of named individuals in groups or collaborations.  
   - Exact dates of work productions or releases when comparing years.  
   - Relationships between multiple entities (e.g., overlapping lifespans, co-authorship, co-starring).  
2. Incorporate all relevant entities (people, works, groups) referenced in the claim and notes that pertain to the verification.  
3. Phrase your query in a clear and factual manner aimed at retrieving authoritative verification documents (e.g., biographical data, production timelines, discographies).  
4. If specific ambiguous or uncertain entities exist (e.g., “the Irish artist who released the EP ‘The First Collage’”), attempt to identify them by name with justification if available (e.g., "SOAK" for that EP), and include them in the query to enable precise retrieval.  
5. Structure your query as one or more concise, targeted questions that collectively cover all necessary information to adjudicate the truth of the claim.  

Example General Strategy to Follow:  
- Parse the claim to identify all main entities and assertions.  
- Scan the notes to detect confirmed facts vs. missing or uncertain points.  
- Formulate questions that explicitly target those missing points, using names, dates, titles, and relationships explicitly.  
- Ensure the query targets documents that can establish biographical details, production chronology, or group membership history as required.  

By following the above approach, the generated `query` will effectively guide downstream retrieval systems to find the needed gold documents or information to validate or refute the claim comprehensively.
