Task Description:
You will receive an input containing two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate an appropriate `query` field based on these inputs.

- The `claim` is a statement or set of statements that assert certain facts or relationships involving specific entities, events, or attributes.
- The `notes` field is a list of sentences or short paragraphs providing factual information, context, or supporting evidence related to the claim. These notes may contain overlapping, repeated, or partially contradictory information.

Your objective is to formulate a concise, clear, and factually precise **search query** or **information request** that would help verify the claim or retrieve the missing important details necessary to fully evaluate the claim.

Key points and domain-specific knowledge to consider when constructing the query:

1. Identify the key entities, dates, events, and relationships mentioned or implied in the claim. These often include:
   - Names of people (athletes, actors, designers, etc.)
   - Titles of works (TV series, games, tournaments)
   - Dates of events (premieres, competitions)
   - Roles or character names (e.g., "Gaius Baltar")
   - Specific achievements or attributes (e.g., "started competing at age five")
   
2. Use the notes to:
   - Clarify ambiguous or missing details in the claim (e.g., identifying the exact opponent who lost a competition)
   - Highlight any contradictions or uncertain facts in the claim that need resolution (e.g., conflicting premiere dates)
   - Identify which key entity or fact from the claim is not yet verified or is deficient in the notes

3. The query should be formulated as a natural language question or a clear factual query aiming to retrieve information that:
   - Confirms or denies the main factual points in the claim
   - Gathers key missing data or the most critical information needed to comprehensively evaluate the claim
   - Specifically targets the missing supporting documents or evidence traces as identified by the feedback (e.g., some entities or facts remain unretrieved)

4. Avoid redundancy in the query; focus on the most relevant sub-claims or facts that remain unverified or unclear.

5. The query should be comprehensive enough to cover multiple related facts if they are closely linked and pivotal for the claim's validity.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Break down the claim into its core factual components.
- Cross-check these components with the provided notes to detect which supporting information is present and which is missing or ambiguous.
- Formulate a query designed to retrieve the missing or ambiguous facts explicitly.
- Where multiple sub-claims or entities are involved, combine related queries into one clear question when possible, to efficiently retrieve multiple missing facts.
- Maintain clarity, relevance, and focus on fact-verification in your queries.

Example considerations based on prior examples:
- If the claim involves an event and a person’s involvement (e.g., “Who did Johan Kriek defeat in 1982 Australian Open?”), the query should explicitly ask for this fact.
- If there are conflicting dates or ambiguous phrases (e.g., “joined in 2010” vs “premiered in 2006”), the query should request clear confirmation of premiere dates or join dates of people or series.
- If the claim compares popularity or recognition (e.g., chess vs game designer’s games), the query should request evidence or metrics illustrating these comparisons.
- Queries should request precise entity-level details (full names, event titles, premiere dates, roles played) to help retrieve the exact documents needed.

In summary, your instruction is: Given the `claim` and supportive `notes`, generate a focused, fact-seeking `query` that aims to fill in the evidence gaps, resolve contradictions, or clarify ambiguous details necessary for full claim verification.
