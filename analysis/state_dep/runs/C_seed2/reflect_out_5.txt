Task Overview:  
You are given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion involving people, groups, events, creative works (films, albums, publications), or conceptual entities. The claim typically expresses relationships, comparisons, attributions (authorship, roles), temporal orders (dates, release years), or membership/induction events.  
- `notes`: a list of factual fragments extracted from knowledge sources. These often contain confirmations, partial information, denials, ambiguities, or metadata relevant to the entities in the claim. They may reference dates, professions, relationships, roles, publication frequencies, or event participation.

Your task is to generate one or more **concise, clear, and tightly focused natural language fact-seeking questions** (a `query` field) that target the *specific missing or uncertain key factual information* required to verify or refute the claim.  

---

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Purpose and Precision of the Query:**  
   - Your queries must explicitly focus on retrieving *specific factual elements* critical for validation (e.g., release dates, role participation, profession confirmation, publication frequency, induction dates).  
   - Avoid vague or broad questions. Each query must be precise and bounded, aiming solely at the uncertain or unconfirmed facts blocking verification.

2. **Analyze the Claim’s Structure and Key Assertions:**  
   - Break down the claim logically into its components (e.g., temporal order of films, identity and role of a person or entity, comparative publication attributes).  
   - Identify all involved entities (people, films, publications, awards, events).  
   - For comparatives, target the respective attributes of *each* entity named.

3. **Leverage the Notes to Identify Known vs Unknown:**  
   - Use notes to identify what factual elements are already confirmed or disputed.  
   - Focus queries on the missing or ambiguous pieces essential to fully verify the claim.  
   - For example, if the notes confirm one film’s release date but not the other referenced film’s date, generate queries that ask for the missing date.

4. **Handling Multiple Entities and Relations:**  
   - When multiple related entities or comparisons are present (e.g., two movies, two publications, two actors), generate one or more questions that explicitly retrieve the respective facts for *each* entity involved to allow comparison or relational verification.

5. **Domain-Specific Knowledge and Patterns:**  
   - Common domains encountered: filmographies (actors, release years), publications (publication frequency, titles), awards and honors (induction events and years), professions and roles of individuals or groups.  
   - Recognize common factual attributes relevant to these domains:  
        * Films: release dates, actor participation, writers/directors, roles.  
        * Individuals: professions, awards, known aliases.  
        * Publications: publication frequency, Hall of Fame inductions.  
        * Inductions and awards: event name, year, person/entity inducted.  
   - Typical temporal framing is critical—verify the timing of events relative to each other.

6. **Verify Entity Identity and Ambiguities:**  
   - When ambiguity exists about whether two named entities (e.g., persons with similar names) are the same, explicitly include a query asking for clarification of identity or alias.

7. **Question Format:**  
   - Construct natural-language questions suitable for knowledge retrieval.  
   - Use explicit "Did/Does/Is/When/What" question formats aiming at factual answers.  
   - Include multiple questions if multiple distinct factual components need clarification.

---

Example Strategies:  
- Identify claim components (e.g., “Film A came out after Film B” and “Actor X starred in Film B”) → generate queries about release dates of Film A and Film B, and actor presence in Film B.  
- If the claim compares publication frequencies → ask “How often is Publication A published?” and “How often is Publication B published?”  
- When referencing awards → ask “Was Person X inducted into the [named award or Hall of Fame]? If so, when?”  
- For ambiguous identity → ask “Is Person X the same as Person Y?”  

---

Summary:  
Transform the claim and notes into one or more precise factual questions that pinpoint the *missing or unclear* data needed to affirm or disprove the claim. Focus on each entity's relevant attributes, temporal data, or relationships, as indicated in the claim. Use notes to avoid redundant questions and narrow down to the exact factual gaps.  

This task requires careful logical decomposition, domain-aware fact identification, and precise question formulation to guide subsequent factual retrieval or validation.
