Task Description:

You are given three input fields: 
- `claim`: A statement involving entities such as people, bands, albums, TV shows, or other cultural/media topics, which may assert factual relationships (e.g., formation years, membership, appearances, roles).
- `notes`: A list of extracted facts, supporting or refuting evidence, and observations related to the claim, typically derived from varying sources or documents.
- `context`: A list of document excerpts (textual descriptions, overviews, or discographies) about relevant entities mentioned in the claim or notes.

Your goal is to:

1. Produce `new_notes`: a concise, relevant, and non-redundant list of notes summarizing the key information directly related to the verification or understanding of the claim. Filter and organize the evidence to focus on the core factual points that support, refute, or clarify the claim. Retain pertinent details such as dates, releases, memberships, and connections but exclude unrelated or overly repetitive facts.

2. Produce `titles`: a list of document titles that best represent or correspond to the main topics or entities involved in the claim, drawn from the context. These titles help identify the key knowledge sources needed to verify the claim. Ensure to include all relevant entities (e.g., individuals, bands, albums, shows, or works mentioned) that were necessary for a comprehensive understanding of the claim. Titles should be directly indicative of these entities.

Additional Guidance and Domain-Specific Details:

- The task involves verifying or contextualizing claims about music bands, albums, formation years, artists' group memberships, actors’ filmographies, and TV show roles.
- Entities in the claim often include bands, albums, solo artists, actors, or TV shows, and relationships often hinge on factual details like formation dates, line-up membership, involvement in specific works (albums, EPs, films), and well-known roles.
- Carefully disambiguate similar or related entities (e.g., distinguishing different bands or actors).
- Often the claim involves comparing or linking two or more entities (e.g., both artists appeared in groups or band formation years).
- The `notes` field may contain overlapping or repetitive statements; distill these into concise points highlighting agreement, disagreement, or uncertainty based on the context.
- The `context` may include detailed descriptions and discographies—ensure you identify and extract relevant facts from these.
- Summaries should acknowledge when information is missing or not found in the context.
- Include reasoning about whether the claim is supported, refuted, or cannot be verified with the data.
- Use consistent naming in titles to match the main subject as it appears in the context (e.g., include any disambiguations like "(actor)").

General Approach:

- Cross-reference notes against the context to verify their accuracy and relevance.
- Highlight confirmed facts, e.g., group memberships, release dates, roles, formation years.
- Point out missing information explicitly when the context or notes do not provide it.
- Identify and list all primary entities central to the claim for the titles output.
- Filter out irrelevant or tangential context documents.
- Provide a focused factual foundation in `new_notes` to assist in claim validation or further analysis.

This approach ensures the outputs comprehensively cover all entities necessary for claim verification and provide clear, factual summaries derived from provided notes and context.
