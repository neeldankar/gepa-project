Task Overview:
You will be given two inputs:
- `claim`: a statement or assertion that involves specific entities (people, teams, products, works, etc.), events (dates, launches, premieres, seasons, etc.), relationships (roles, ownership, participation, etc.), or attributes (flavors, genres, dates, durations, etc.).
- `notes`: a list of factual evidence, background information, or contextual data that relates to the claim. These notes often contain detailed domain-specific facts and precise temporal, geographic, and contextual markers.

Your Objective:
Generate a `query` — a carefully crafted set of factual, focused questions designed to verify all significant assertions embedded in the `claim` using relevant facts from the `notes`. The queries should illuminate the core facts needed to determine the claim’s truthfulness or accuracy.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Deeply Analyze the Claim:
   - Identify and clearly extract all named entities (e.g., artists, sports teams, actors), specific events (e.g., founding dates, release dates, seasons), attributes (e.g., flavor, genre), and relationships (e.g., role, performance timeline, management history).
   - Break down complex claims containing multiple assertions into distinct parts. Each part should be verified separately.

2. Scrutinize the Notes:
   - Identify and correlate key factual data points that support or refute parts of the claim.
   - Pay close attention to domain-specific details (e.g., music artist roles like songwriter vs. performer; sports details like player tenures and manager seasons; film and TV production details including release and premiere dates).
   - Use temporal sequencing to confirm event order when relevant (e.g., release dates of movies/series, playing career timelines, founding years).

3. Formulating Verifying Queries:
   - For every distinct assertion in the claim, generate one or more precise, targeted questions.
   - Questions should check:
     * Existence and identity of entities or events (e.g., “Which Canadian baseball team was founded in 1969?”)
     * Relationships and roles (e.g., “Did the identified player manage the Tampa Bay Devil Rays, and when?”)
     * Temporal order and context (e.g., “Was Anomalisa released before or after the Fargo premiere?”)
     * Attributes of entities or events (e.g., “What is the main flavor of Solo soft drink?” or “What is the genre of the 2017 film Wonder Woman?”)
   - Use terminology precise to the domain: e.g., “goal scorers” in sports, “songwriters” and “performers” in music, “premiere date” for TV or film, “manager seasons” in sports teams.
   - Include disambiguation or specification questions when multiple entities or dates may cause confusion.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions — each question should directly support verifying a specific component of the claim.

4. Structuring the Query Output:
   - Present queries as a numbered list of distinct, standalone questions.
   - Ensure all essential elements of the claim are queried, leaving no major assertion unverified.

5. Use of Domain Knowledge:
   - Understand typical domain conventions — e.g., soft drinks’ common flavors, standard baseball managerial career progressions, typical film/TV release patterns.
   - Leverage this understanding to ask the most relevant questions and anticipate the key facts the notes might provide.

Outcome:
By following this approach, you will generate comprehensive, fact-focused queries that enable thorough retrieval and verification of all critical aspects of the claim. This ensures high fidelity fact-checking and knowledge validation, with a clear mapping from claim components to precise information needs.

Summary Checklist for Queries:
- [ ] Identify and query every entity, event, attribute, or relationship mentioned.
- [ ] Break compound claims into separate questions for each assertion.
- [ ] Confirm temporal and contextual sequence where relevant.
- [ ] Use domain-specific terminology precisely.
- [ ] Avoid ambiguity by clarifying roles, identities, and dates.
- [ ] Ensure no major fact in the claim is left unqueried.

Example application:
If the claim involves a soft drink’s flavor and linked advertising, ask separately:
1. What is the main flavor of the specific soft drink?
2. Is there evidence of an advertisement linking another product with a particular song?
3. What is the flavor or key attribute of that advertised product?

If the claim involves sports teams and managers:
1. When was the team founded?
2. Who played on the team in the specified years?
3. Which players later managed another specified team, and when?

If the claim involves actor involvement and timelines:
1. Who acted in the specified film and what genre is it?
2. Which actors appear in other referenced films/shows?
3. What are the premiere or release dates to establish event sequences?

This detailed methodology will ensure your generated queries are precise, comprehensive, and effective for fact verification based on the provided notes.
