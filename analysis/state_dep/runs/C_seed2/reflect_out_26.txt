Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce an output field called `query`.

- The input `claim` is a factual or verification statement that needs to be confirmed or refuted.
- The input `notes` are supporting facts, observations, or evidences related to the claim. These are typically extracted from various documents and may include verification details, ownership statements, attribution facts, or popularity metrics.

Your Task:
Generate a focused, precise, and effective `query` that can be used to retrieve supporting documents or information to verify the claim. This `query` should be designed to maximize recall of relevant documents while minimizing noise, targeting key entities, relationships, or factual points necessary to validate the claim.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Considerations:
1. The `query` should typically be a natural-language question or a phrase that directly targets fact verification relevant to the claim. 
2. Use specific named entities mentioned in the claim and notes (e.g., person names, organizations, venues, titles of works or shows, brands).
3. Include core relationships or attributes expressed in the claim which can be directly addressed by documents (e.g., ownership, authorship, location, popularity comparisons).
4. When the claim involves comparisons (e.g., popularity between two games), ensure the query reflects that comparative nature explicitly.
5. When the claim involves authorship, credits, or identification of contributors, tailor the query to ask for authorship or credits.
6. When the claim involves venues, ownership, or organizational relationships, frame the query around confirmation of those connections.
7. The query should be specific enough to avoid overly broad document retrieval but broad enough to cover all required facets of the claim.
8. Where multiple potential key documents are expected (e.g., multiple entities need verification), the query can combine these elements logically.
9. Reflect context from notes to enrich or clarify the query, especially when notes mention entities or evidence not fully explicit in the claim.
10. Avoid redundant or vague queries such as simply restating the claim verbatim. Instead, produce an information-seeking query that would naturally lead to authoritative document retrieval.

General Strategy:
- Analyze the claim to identify key factual assertions.
- Extract named entities, attributes, relationships relevant to verifying the claim.
- Integrate the information from notes to focus the query on documented evidence.
- Formulate a natural, direct question or query phrase targeting those key elements.
- Ensure the query targets retrieval of documents sufficient to confirm or refute the claim.

Example Pattern:
- If claim is about authorship: "Who are the credited writers or authors of [work]?"
- If claim is about venue and ownership: "Is [Event] performed at [Venue] located in [Property], and who owns [Property]?"
- If claim is about popularity comparison: "What are the global popularity metrics for [Entity1] compared to [Entity2]?"

By following these detailed guidelines, your outputted `query` will effectively aid in identifying the documents necessary to verify the veracity of the claim.
