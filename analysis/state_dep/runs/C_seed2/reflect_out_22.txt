Given an input with two fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a `query` that can be used to retrieve additional, relevant documents necessary to verify or refute the claim.

- The input `claim` is usually a complex factual statement or assertion involving specific entities (such as people, organizations, events, or concepts) and relationships between them.
- The `notes` field provides contextual evidence, background information, or fact snippets related to the claim. These notes may confirm, partially confirm, contradict, or be silent about parts of the claim.
- Your main objective is to carefully analyze both the `claim` and the `notes` to identify:
  - What key factual components are verified, contradicted, or missing from the notes.
  - What specific pieces of information, entities, or relationships remain unclear or unsupported.
  - What precise questions or information gaps need to be resolved to better verify or refute the claim.

- The `query` you generate should be a focused, clear, and concise natural language question or set of questions that:
  - Target the missing or uncertain aspects needed for claim verification.
  - Aim to retrieve documents or sources containing relevant factual data (e.g., about persons, their associations, roles, origins, events, timelines, or relationships).
  - Incorporate relevant named entities or key terms extracted from the `claim` and `notes` to guide targeted document retrieval.
  - Avoid redundancy and overly broad or generic questions.
  
- When formulating the query, consider domain-specific conventions, such as:
  - For entities related to sports, ask about team affiliations, participation in specific events, or player statistics.
  - For musical groups or labels, inquire about origin, collaborations, or timeline details.
  - For persons connected with films or historical events, focus on roles, character names, or portrayals in specific productions.
  - Use the contextual clues in the notes to detect what information is already known (e.g., marking off known scorers or band origins) and what is unknown or disputed.

- Your overall approach:
  1. Parse and understand the claim’s key factual assertions.
  2. Cross-check these against the provided notes to identify confirmed facts, contradictions, and missing details.
  3. Identify the minimal set of facts or entities requiring further verification.
  4. Formulate a concise and precise natural language query or a small set of queries that, if answered, would resolve the outstanding uncertainties about the claim.

Your output must have a single field `query` with a question that targets the unresolved or unclear information essential for verifying the claim based on the inputs.
