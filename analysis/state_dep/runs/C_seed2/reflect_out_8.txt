Task Description:

You will be given three input fields: 
- `claim`: a statement involving one or more entities and possibly their relationships or attributes.
- `notes`: a list of sentences containing factual assertions, observations, or partial information relevant to verifying or clarifying the claim. These notes may contain duplicates or overlapping facts.
- `context`: a list of detailed passages or document summaries, often encyclopedic, that provide background information, key facts, and connections relating to the entities or topics mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to produce two output fields:
- `new_notes`: a concise, deduplicated, and accurate subset of `notes` that are directly relevant to assessing the claim given the context, including clarifications about the validity or evidence supporting or contradicting the claim.
- `titles`: a list of titles or topic names of documents or passages from the `context` that are the primary and most pertinent sources supporting or clarifying the information in your `new_notes`. 

Key Details and Instructions:
1. **Claim Verification and Reasoning**:  
   - Interpret the claim carefully, identifying all factual components (e.g., identities, relationships, timelines, attributes).  
   - Use the `context` passages to cross-check or enrich information regarding the claim.  
   - Identify any absence of information or conflicting information in the `notes` or `context`.  
   - Highlight when a claim or part of a claim is supported, refuted, or unverified based on the available information.

2. **Filtering and Summarization of Notes**:  
   - Eliminate duplication and contradictory notes.  
   - Include only those notes that directly help understand or verify the claim.  
   - Optionally, add clarifying or corrective statements if the notes do not fully support the claim. For example, if no evidence exists for a part of the claim, explicitly note that.

3. **Selection of Titles**:  
   - Choose titles of the most relevant documents or context passages that contain the crucial factual information backing your `new_notes`.  
   - Your selection should aim to cover all key entities or events directly relevant to verifying the claim.  
   - Titles are often names of persons, events, works, or topics (e.g., "Rudolf Höss", "1982 Australian Open – Men's Singles", "Amy Tan").

4. **Generalizable Strategy**:  
   - Parse claim into atomic facts.  
   - Cross-reference claims with `context` to confirm accuracy.  
   - Filter `notes` to include only facts supporting or clarifying those atomic facts.  
   - Explicitly state when information is missing or unsupported.  
   - Choose document titles corresponding to factual anchors in your reasoning, ensuring coverage of all relevant aspects of the claim.

5. **Domain-specific Knowledge Embedded**:  
   - Names and spelling variants matter (e.g., "Rudolf Höss" may appear as "Rudolf Hoess").  
   - Historical context matters (WWII, Nazi concentration camps, tennis tournaments, authorship).  
   - Negotiation roles, commandant vs. negotiators, or dates and events (e.g., match finals) are critical.  
   - Authorship vs. authorship of specific works should be distinguished carefully.  
   - Sporting achievements, such as defeats or starting ages, require careful contextual confirmation.

Your outputs should be concise yet sufficiently detailed to stand as clear summaries supporting the fact-checking of the claim. Always refer back to your reasoning to justify the selection of notes and titles.

Example format of outputs:  
- `new_notes` as a list of curated, relevant note sentences.  
- `titles` as a list of relevant document or passage titles from the context that support `new_notes`.
