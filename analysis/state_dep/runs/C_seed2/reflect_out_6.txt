Task Description:

You will be given three input fields for each task instance:

- `claim`: A statement that asserts one or more factual points or relationships that require verification or exploration.
- `notes`: A list of strings containing preliminary findings, observations, or partial verifications related to the claim. These notes typically reflect what has been gathered or confirmed from disparate sources or initial searches relevant to the claim.
- `context`: A list of strings representing background information, excerpts, or knowledge snippets that may pertain to entities, events, or concepts in the claim.

Your objective is to produce two outputs, `new_notes` and `titles`, that together guide the next iterative step of evidence gathering and verification for the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Understand the Claim**  
   - Carefully parse the claim to identify its key factual components and implicit assertions.
   - Note if the claim contains multiple sub-claims or conflated information that needs disentangling.

2. **Analyze the Provided `notes` and `context`**  
   - Review the existing notes for coverage, gaps, contradictions, and any partially verified facts that support or refute parts of the claim.
   - Examine the context to check if relevant entities or topics mentioned in the claim are present and whether crucial confirming or disconfirming information is available.
   - Identify which aspects of the claim are fully or partially verified, which remain unsupported due to missing or ambiguous information, and if there are any misunderstandings or misattributions in the notes/claim.

3. **Derive `new_notes`**  
   - Propose a focused and prioritized set of next investigative notes or fact-checking points that should be gathered to corroborate or debunk unresolved components of the claim.
   - These should typically include key searches for entities, clarifications of ambiguous references, or verification of claims not yet conclusively supported.
   - Avoid redundancy by not repeating already confirmed points unless necessary for clarification.
   - When claims conflate distinct entities or facts, generate notes that clearly separate these for fact-checking.

4. **Generate `titles`**  
   - Suggest concise, clear titles or topics for the next best documents, articles, or search queries that will directly contribute to evidence retrieval.
   - Titles should be specific to relevant people, works, locations, or events related to the claim.
   - They should reflect both confirmed and unconfirmed components, covering those parts of the claim needing further investigation.
   - Avoid overly general or unrelated titles; focus on what clarifies or verifies the claim.

5. **General Reasoning Approach**  
   - Approach the task as an iterative fact-checking and evidence retrieval step, where the existing notes/context form your basis for determining what is known and unknown.
   - Treat the notes as a summary of prior search or fact-finding attempts, and the context as potentially rich but possibly incomplete background information.
   - Your outputs (new_notes and titles) should be logically derived from a careful comparison of the claim, notes, and context, aiming to guide an effective next retrieval step.
   - Address errors such as conflations or misconceptions explicitly by separating factually distinct entities or claims in your notes and titles.

6. **Domain-Specific Considerations**  
   - Recognize that the claims often involve:
     - Biographical details (e.g., actors, directors, historical figures)
     - Works of art or media (e.g., films, documentaries, songs)
     - Locations and architectural facts (e.g., cathedrals and their locations)
     - Voice actor and character associations in media franchises
     - Attribution of authorship or directorship in creative works
   - Fact-checking often requires tracking multiple similar or ambiguous entities (e.g., multiple cathedrals named "St Paul's Cathedral" in world locations) and disambiguating them.
   - Claims may combine unrelated facts or misattribute roles; your response should help disentangle these to guide accurate fact verification.

Summary:  
Given a claim, initial fact notes, and background contexts, your task is to produce refined, targeted next-step notes and retrieval titles that address unresolved or ambiguous parts of the claim. Your outputs guide an effective iterative evidence retrieval and fact verification process, enabling resolution of complex or partially verified claims.
