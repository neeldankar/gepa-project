Task Description:
You are given two input fields:  
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that may reference people, groups, events, works (e.g., films, albums), or concepts.  
- `notes`: a list of extracted factual fragments, evidence, or metadata related to entities mentioned in the claim. These notes can include confirmations, denials, ambiguities, or relevant domain knowledge.

Your goal is to generate a `query` field, which should be one or more concise, clear, fact-seeking questions designed to retrieve the key pieces of information necessary to verify or refute the claim.

Detailed instructions and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Purpose of the Query:**  
   The query must explicitly target the critical factual elements needed to validate the claim. This usually means asking about specific attributes, counts, relationships, professions, dates, or appearances related to the entities mentioned in the claim.

2. **Scope and Granularity:**  
   - If the claim compares quantities or attributes (e.g., "twice as many albums"), generate queries that ask for the relevant counts or comparative data for each entity involved.  
   - If the claim concerns professions or shared roles, ask about the precise professions or occupations of the named individuals.  
   - If the claim links appearances, roles, or participation in events/media, query the identity and relevant credits (e.g., did person X appear in show Y at time Z).  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; each query must be focused on retrieving the specific fact(s) that would confirm or disprove the claim.

3. **Multiple Entities:**  
   When multiple entities are involved (e.g., bands, people, works), generate queries that explicitly retrieve information for each entity mentioned, especially when comparative or relational facts are concerned.

4. **Handling Ambiguities:**  
   If the claim or notes reveal ambiguity (e.g., uncertainty about whether two names refer to the same entity), encode this into the query by asking clarifying questions, e.g., "Is entity A also known as entity B?"

5. **Use of Notes:**  
   Use the notes to identify what is known and what is missing. The query should target the missing or uncertain knowledge that blocks verification. For example, if notes state one entity's facts are known but not the other's, the query should focus on the latter.

6. **Example strategy:**  
   - Break down the claim logically (e.g., entity attributes, relations, timeframes).  
   - From the notes, identify which assertions are already supported or refuted, and which knowledge gaps remain.  
   - Construct precise questions aimed solely at the remaining gaps.  
   - When applicable, include multiple questions to cover all required information for full verification.

7. **Query Format:**  
   Output the query as a natural language question or set of questions, which can be used to retrieve or look up factual documents or knowledge snippets relevant to verifying the claim.

---

Summary:  
Transform the `claim` and `notes` inputs into one or more tightly focused, clear, fact-seeking questions that efficiently identify the necessary information to verify the claim, by querying the relevant entities, attributes, and relationships indicated by the claim and unresolved by the notes.
