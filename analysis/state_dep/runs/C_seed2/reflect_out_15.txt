Given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a precise and effective search `query` that can be used to verify or refute the truthfulness of the `claim` using the factual information contained in the `notes`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understanding Inputs:
   - `claim`: A statement or assertion about specific entities, events, or relationships that needs verification.
   - `notes`: A list of factual sentences or pieces of information relevant to one or more components of the `claim`. These notes contain key entities, dates, roles, relationships, or factual claims that can support or contradict the claim.

2. Objective:
   - Produce a concise, yet specific natural language `query` that targets the core factual components necessary to verify the claim. This query should prioritize retrieval of the most relevant documents or data points necessary to confirm or disprove the claim.

3. Domain-specific considerations:
   - Entities may include people (with birth and death dates), organizations (founders, roles), artistic works (films, albums, books), and educational institutions (school districts, schools).
   - When the claim cites overlapping lifespans or biographical data, your query should include full names, birth/death years, and roles where relevant to confirm co-existence or timelines.
   - When the claim references collaboration or creative attribution (e.g., co-writing or directing a film), your query should mention the specific work, creators, and their roles.
   - For educational institutions or organizations, your query should focus on official compositions or memberships, and geographic/locality information if relevant.
   - The goal is to capture essential factual elements that link the claim’s components, highlighting relevant named entities, relationships, dates, and attributes needed to retrieve validating sources.

4. Query construction strategy:
   - Identify the key factual elements in the claim that need to be proven or disproven, such as:
      * Overlapping lifespans/dates of individuals
      * Roles or titles (e.g., “creator,” “founder,” “actor”)
      * Relationships between entities (e.g., director and film, school and district)
      * Specific named entities (people, institutions, works) mentioned in notes and claim
   - Formulate a clear, direct question or phrase that explicitly links these key elements.
   - Avoid ambiguity or overly broad terms; incorporate distinguishing details such as dates, full names, or role descriptors from the notes.
   - Ensure the query is specific enough to retrieve the critical evidence (documents/entities) mentioned in the notes, which are the known gold sources for verification.

5. Output format:
   - Provide the output with the field name `query` only.
   - The query should be a natural language question or phrase that clearly focuses on verifying the claim’s factual assertion.

Example transformations include:
- From a claim about overlapping lifespans of named individuals, generate a query asking whether those individuals with named birth/death years had overlapping lifespans.
- From a claim about creative collaboration on a particular work, generate a query asking whether the named individuals co-created or had the stated roles for that work.
- From a claim about the composition of an organization or district, generate a query asking which specific entities officially belong to or serve the stated geographical or functional area.

This task requires synthesizing scattered factual notes into succinct verification queries that precisely connect the claim’s key components while leveraging all the domain-specific information present.
