Task Description:
You will be provided with the following inputs:
- `claim`: A factual or evaluative statement related to topics such as board games, films, actors, or cultural subjects.
- `notes`: A list of textual notes or extracted facts relevant to the claim. These may contain supporting, contradictory, or unrelated information.
- `context`: A list of detailed encyclopedic-style excerpts or summaries from various documents potentially relevant to the claim.

Your goal is to analyze all three inputs and produce two outputs:
1. `new_notes`: A concise, focused subset of the original `notes` that are factually relevant to the claim and useful for verifying or evaluating it. Remove redundancies and unrelated points, focusing on key evidence supporting or refuting the claim.
2. `titles`: A list of document or source titles that would be the most relevant and authoritative references for supporting verification or further research of the claim. These titles should correspond to entities, works, people, or concepts appearing in the `context` that directly relate to the claim.

Additional Guidance and Domain-Specific Details:
- The domains often involve board games (designers, variants, popularity), film and television (actors, cast, film genres, adaptations from novels, biographies), and cultural or historical references.
- When the claim involves comparisons (e.g., popularity of a game vs. another), prioritize notes and context about cultural significance, global reach, and historical presence.
- When identifying titles, prefer exact matches of key subjects in the claim (e.g., specific board games, films, actors, or novels) and also include closely related variants or biographies.
- Filter out irrelevant or tangential information, such as unrelated actors, film genres inaccurately described (e.g., classic western vs. neo-western distinction), or loosely connected dates and biographical details.
- If a claim combines multiple concepts (e.g., an actor’s role in a specific film and a portrayal of a historical figure), separately assess evidence about each part, but only include notes and titles relevant to the entire claim.
- Duplicate information in notes should be minimized; focus on unique, factual, or evidentiary statements that clarify or support/refute the claim.
- Reason clearly about factual inconsistencies, missing evidence, or mistaken associations highlighted in both notes and context, and incorporate that reasoning in understanding note relevance.
- The task requires a blend of fact-checking, relevance filtering, and document/source identification skills, aiming to support verification or refutation of claims with appropriate evidence and references from the given data.

Example:
- For a claim about the popularity of chess versus a specific game designer’s work, include notes on chess variants, cultural impact, and game designer’s known works with their reach; titles might be game names, designers, and chess variants.
- For a film-related claim involving actors and roles, include notes about the cast list, actor biographies, and pertinent film titles; titles might include the film, actors’ biographies, and related adaptations.

Summary:
Given `claim`, `notes`, and `context`, produce a distilled set of `new_notes` that most directly bear on evaluating the claim’s truthfulness, and a set of `titles` naming the most relevant documents or entities from the `context` that provide authoritative background or evidence regarding the claim.
