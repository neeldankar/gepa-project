Given the input fields:
- `claim`: A complex factual claim or statement involving people, events, or entities.
- `notes`: A list of factually relevant sentences or snippets extracted from source documents that may support or refute the claim.

Your task is to produce the output field:
- `query`: A concise, fact-checking focused question or set of questions that would, when answered, help verify the truthfulness of the `claim` against reliable sources.

Detailed Task Description:
1. Carefully analyze the `claim` to identify the key entities (persons, places, events), relationships, and factual assertions involved.
2. Use the `notes` to find factual evidence points addressing these assertions. The `notes` generally contain multiple overlapping or paraphrased statements about the claim's subject matter.
3. Summarize and synthesize these evidentiary points into one or more clear, precise queries that target the essential factual disputes or confirmations implicit in the claim.
4. The queries should be designed to:
   - Retrieve or verify critical facts from authoritative sources,
   - Resolve ambiguous or complex references within the claim (e.g., identifying “the person who claimed X” or “the player who was defeated by Y”),
   - Cross-check dates, roles, relationships, names, and events mentioned.
5. The queries should be comprehensive enough to cover all components of the original claim, while remaining as concise as possible.
6. Queries often combine multiple factual checks into one question, tied by conjunctions like “and,” or may be multipart questions.
7. The purpose of the query is to:
   - Guide retrieval of supporting or contradicting documents,
   - Facilitate fact verification by clarifying which facts need confirmation.

Domain-Specific and Niche Details:
- The claims can involve complex chains of identity and relationships (e.g., “the person who was defeated by X” or “the man who Ruby Hart Phillips was at odds with”).
- Sports claims often require linking athletes, tournaments, match outcomes, and biographical details (e.g., ages players began competing).
- Geographical claims may involve hierarchical containment (e.g., cities within counties, colleges within cities) and multiple entities sharing locations.
- Historical/political claims may refer to events, political figures, and conflicting journalistic accounts, necessitating disambiguation between similarly named or related persons and precise timeline checking.
- The notes often paraphrase or restate facts multiple times; you must identify the factual consensus or contradiction in these.
- Some claims mix direct factual statements with indirect or relational qualifiers that must be explicitly clarified in the query.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Break down the claim into atomic factual components requiring verification.
- Cross-reference each fact with the evidence in `notes`.
- Frame a combined question that covers all these facts and relationships clearly, mentioning key names, dates, events, and roles explicitly.
- Aim to include critical identifiers and cross-links to avoid ambiguity (e.g., specify “1982 Australian Open Men’s Singles final,” “College of Southern Nevada in Clark County, Nevada,” or “the President of Cuba in 1940 who claimed that…”).
- Avoid vague or overly broad queries; be as explicit as possible based on the claim and supporting notes.

In summary, generate a clear, fact-checking query that encapsulates the claim’s key assertions by synthesizing relevant evidence from the notes, facilitating precise fact retrieval and verification.
