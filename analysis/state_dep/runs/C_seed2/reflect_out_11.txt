Task Description:
You are given two input fields:
- `claim`: a statement or assertion involving specific entities, events, or relationships.
- `notes`: a list of factual evidence, background information, or context related to the claim, often containing detailed domain-specific pieces of information.

Your objective is to produce a `query` — a set of clear, factual, and relevant questions that will help verify the truthfulness or accuracy of the `claim` based on the information in the `notes`. These queries should be designed to retrieve or check the critical underlying facts referenced or implied in the claim.

Detailed Instructions and Considerations:

1. Understand the claim thoroughly:
   - Identify all named entities, relationships, or events mentioned.
   - Parse compound claims that may involve multiple assertions.

2. Analyze the notes carefully:
   - Extract and correlate key facts from the notes, especially those that confirm or deny parts of the claim.
   - Identify specific entities, events, or data points that are critical to verifying the claim.
   - Use domain knowledge or detailed context embedded in the notes to understand nuanced facts (e.g., music artists and their roles, sports match details, relationships between people and entities).

3. Generate a set of focused, fact-checking questions (`query`) that:
   - Directly address all significant assertions made in the `claim`.
   - Cover the verification of existence, identities, relationships, and attributes of the entities mentioned.
   - Clarify temporal, geographic, or other contextual information essential for establishing the claim's truth.
   - Use precise language reflecting domain-specific terminology where applicable (e.g., "goal scorers," "judges," "songwriters," "team nicknames").

4. Structure the `query` as multiple distinct questions if the claim contains multiple parts or assertions.

5. The `query` should aim to retrieve all necessary and diverse supporting documents to fully assess the claim (i.e., no major fact in the claim should remain unqueried).

Example best practices derived from prior examples:
- When a claim involves people and their roles (e.g., authorship, position in a band, judging status), ask specifically about their role and involvement.
- When multiple factual assertions are made, form separate questions for each to ensure each is independently verifiable.
- Include entity disambiguation in queries if needed, such as distinguishing between similarly named people, roles, or events.
- Avoid vague or overly broad questions; focus queries on the exact facts necessary to validate each claim component.

Outcome:
By following this approach, your queries will effectively guide the retrieval of comprehensive, precise factual evidence, ensuring that fact-checking or knowledge validation can be thorough and accurate.
