Given input fields:
- `claim`: a factual or factual-claim statement involving specific entities, relationships, or events.
- `notes`: a list of sentences extracted from knowledge sources that provide partial or complete background information relevant to the claim.

Produce output field:
- `query`: a precise, targeted search query (or series of queries) that would retrieve documents or sources necessary to verify or refute the claim.

Task explanation and guidance:
1. **Goal:** From the claim and provided notes, infer the minimal, specific questions that must be answered to fact-check or validate the claim. This involves explicitly identifying key entities, relationships, attributes, or comparisons in the claim that need external verification.

2. **Understanding the claim:**
   - Identify all proper nouns (people, bands, songs, companies, events).
   - Parse comparative or relational claims (e.g., "X won more awards than Y", "X was the captain of a plane operated by Y", "X and Y are members of band Z").
   - Recognize any implied or explicit factual assertions that could be verified by authoritative documents.

3. **Using the notes:** 
   - Extract the key facts and knowledge points referenced.
   - Note which entities or relationships have partial coverage or confirmation from the notes.
   - Recognize gaps where information is missing or ambiguous (for example, absence of awards info, or confusion about company nationality).

4. **Formulating queries:**
   - Queries should focus on retrieving knowledge sources that can address any missing or ambiguous items critical to fully validate the claim.
   - Structure queries as precise questions or keyword combinations targeting the key entities and relationships.
   - Include queries that ask for:
     - Identity or roles of individuals.
     - Membership or participation in groups/bands.
     - Awards or achievements.
     - Company headquarters or nationalities.
     - Specific performances in songs or events.
   - When comparisons are made (e.g., awards count), include queries to obtain data on both sides of the comparison.

5. **Examples of domain-specific factual knowledge relevant to this task:**
   - Band members and side projects (e.g., Nivek Ogre is a Skinny Puppy member with the Rx side project).
   - Song performers and vocalists.
   - Airline companies and their headquarters.
   - Flight captains and associated flights.
   - Awards earned by artists or musicians.
   - Corporate nationalities and geographic headquarters.
   - Recognition that missing information from notes should be explicitly requested in queries.

6. **Generalizable strategy:**
   - Carefully read and decompose the claim into its factual components.
   - Align known facts from notes to those components.
   - Identify what critical information is missing or unconfirmed.
   - Compose queries that retrieve documents which fill those gaps or confirm the facts.
   - Ensure the queries cover all elements needed to confirm or refute the entire claim.

Summary: Your main task is to transform given factual claims, supported by partial note-based context, into a set of targeted queries designed to retrieve authoritative knowledge about the key entities and facts involved, enabling accurate fact verification. Include domain-specific understandings of music groups, awards, corporate details, and performers to inform query formulation.
