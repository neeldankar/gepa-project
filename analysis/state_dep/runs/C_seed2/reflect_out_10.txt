Task Description:  
You are given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual assertion involving one or more entities such as people, groups, events, works (films, albums), or concepts. The claim attempts to state a factual relationship, attribute, or occurrence.  
- `notes`: a list of factual fragments, extracted evidence, or metadata related to the entities or assertions in the claim. These notes may contain confirmations, refutations, partial evidence, or domain knowledge relevant to verifying or disproving the claim.

Your goal is to produce a `query` field: succinct, clear, and fact-seeking question(s) aimed precisely at retrieving the key information needed to verify or refute the claim.

---

Detailed instructions and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Purpose and Focus of the Query:**  
   The generated query must explicitly ask about the specific factual elements central to verifying the claim. These include exact counts, classifications, relationships, attributes (such as profession, awards, dates), or participations (e.g., whether a person appeared in a film, or whether a film won a particular award). The query always targets discrete facts needed to prove or disprove the claim’s components.

2. **Taxonomic and Hierarchical Accuracy:**  
   If the claim involves taxonomic or hierarchical terms (e.g., genus, subfamily), verify and clarify these terms before making comparisons. Ask about the precise taxonomic classification and then about counts or attributes at the correct rank to avoid comparing incompatible categories.

3. **Entity-Specific and Comparative Queries:**  
   When multiple entities or groups are referenced, generate queries that request relevant facts about each entity or group independently and comparatively if the claim hinges on comparison (e.g., number of species for each, awards won by multiple people, professions of several individuals). This ensures clarity and completeness.

4. **Handling Ambiguities or Name Confusions:**  
   If there is ambiguity (e.g., confusion between similarly named items or if the claim seems to confuse one entity with another), include queries that clarify identity and relationships, such as: "Is entity A the same as entity B?” or “Is film X the same as film Y?”

5. **Use Notes to Identify Gaps and Known Facts:**  
   Leverage the notes to distinguish what information is already established and what remains unknown or ambiguous. The query should not duplicate confirmed facts but rather target unresolved pieces blocking verification. For example, if Nelly Sachs' Nobel Prize win is known but the screenplay writer’s status is unclear, the query should target information about the screenplay writer only.

6. **Precision and Brevity:**  
   Queries must be direct and narrowly focused on verification-relevant facts. Avoid overly broad or vague questions that do not clearly correspond to a key factual element of the claim.

7. **Suggested Query Types by Domain or Claim Type:**  
   - **Taxonomy/biology:** questions on classification hierarchy, species counts, genus/subfamily ranks.  
   - **Film/Entertainment:** questions on film titles, award winners (including event, year, and award type), screenplay writers, actors’ biographies (military service, professions, awards).  
   - **Literary awards:** questions on award winners, affiliations between works and creators, whether claims about shared awards/professions hold.  
   - **Comparisons:** counts or statuses for each relevant entity for direct factual comparison.

8. **Multi-question Queries:**  
   When verification depends on multiple factual elements, produce multiple questions clearly distinguished, covering each necessary piece of verification information comprehensively.

---

Example approach to formulating the query:  
- Break the claim into atomic factual assertions (e.g., "Caesalpinioideae is a genus," "Nelly Sachs won a Nobel," "Film X won an award Y," "Actor A was a Navy combat veteran").  
- Cross-reference each assertion with notes to identify which are already supported, contradicted, or unknown.  
- Formulate direct questions targeting missing or ambiguous facts (e.g., exact taxonomic rank, award recipients, identity of screenplay writer, presence of military record).  
- When appropriate, include confirmatory or clarifying questions about entity identity to resolve ambiguities.

---

**Query Format:**  
Generate one or more clear, focused natural language questions suitable for querying a factual knowledge source or database to efficiently retrieve the precise facts needed to verify the claim.

---

By following these instructions, you will create highly precise, targeted queries that enable efficient, accurate verification or refutation of complex factual claims involving multiple entities and nuanced domain knowledge.
