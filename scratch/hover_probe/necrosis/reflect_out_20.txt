Task Description:
You are given three inputs: `claim` (a factual statement or assertion), `notes` (a list of factual statements relevant to verifying or contextualizing the claim), and `context` (a list of detailed source documents or text passages providing background information). Your task is to produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: a concise, well-curated set of notes synthesizing critical factual evidence from the input `notes` and relevant parts of the `context`, focusing on verifying or refuting the claim. These should summarize key factual points, clarify discrepancies or inaccuracies in the claim, and highlight important domain-specific details uncovered from the evidence. Avoid repeating redundant or extraneous information.

2. `titles`: a small list of descriptive titles of relevant reference documents or topics that relate to the claim and evidence. These titles should capture the main entities, locations, concepts, or records involved in the claim and verification process.

Guidelines and Domain Knowledge for Performing the Task:

- The `claim` typically involves verifying factual accuracy, dates, locations, historical names, or relationships between entities (e.g., band formation date and place, river origin and former names, company or band associations).

- The `notes` often contain both correct and incorrect information. You must identify and highlight the correct facts supporting or disproving the claim, noting any factual errors.

- The `context` contains richer, source-level detailed information (e.g., official descriptions or historical accounts) which helps resolve ambiguities or verify key facts. Use it to confirm or refute points in the claim and notes, especially when the notes have contradictions or incomplete data.

- When reconciling dates, geographic locations, or naming history, ensure precise differentiation between similarly named places, entities, or time periods (e.g., distinguishing Linchuan District in Jiangxi province vs. river origin in Zhejiang province).

- If a claim is partially correct (e.g., correct location but incorrect year), reflect that nuance by explicitly stating which parts are accurate and which are not.

- If some aspects of the claim cannot be verified or lack data in notes/context, explicitly indicate these gaps in your output.

- Summarize important domain-specific details relevant to the claim:
   - Band formation years vs. claimed dates.
   - Official licensing or location of broadcasters like WCJB-TV.
   - Historical naming conventions of rivers or geographic regions.
   - Founders or frontmen relations to record labels or bands.
   - Geographical distinctions between districts or regions.

- Titles should be factually relevant and concise; typically key entities, places, or concepts mentioned in the claim and discovered in notes/context that are necessary to assess the claim.

- Avoid vague or generic titles; prefer specific ones that directly connect to the claim validation.

- Your reasoning should document critical fact-checking steps and identify supporting or contradictory evidence.

- `new_notes` and `titles` form a distilled, evidence-based synthesis that could be used for final claim verification or generation of a knowledge summary.

In brief, your task is a focused fact verification and synthesis exercise grounded in disparate evidence, producing evidence-summarizing notes and key document/topic titles that capture the essence and resolution of the claim.
