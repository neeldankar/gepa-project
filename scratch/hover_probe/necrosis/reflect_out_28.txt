Task Description:
You will be given two textual inputs: a `claim` and a list of `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` consisting of one or more information-seeking questions. These queries should focus precisely on the factual elements needed to verify the truthfulness of the claim based on or relative to the evidence and context provided in the notes.

Key Objectives:
- Identify the key factual assertions, entities, relationships, temporal elements, and geographic or spatial aspects within the given claim.
- Use the notes to determine which facts supporting or disproving the claim are present, partial, or missing.
- Formulate clear, focused, and discrete interrogative queries that target the minimal and most relevant sets of facts needed for verification.
- Ensure queries are phrased to retrieve authoritative, fact-based answers suited for confirming or refuting each critical aspect of the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Analyze the `claim` fully:
   - Extract all key factual components: named entities (people, bands, organizations, places, works of art/media), dates or periods, spatial relationships (e.g., "more southern than"), and roles or relationships among these entities (e.g., "fronted by", "created by", "directed", "wrote").
   - Identify if multiple related facts must be separately validated to avoid conflation or ambiguity.

2. Examine the `notes` carefully:
   - Determine what relevant facts are confirmed by the notes, which are contradicted, which are absent, or which require further verification.
   - Detect any contradictions or incomplete information that needs resolution.
   - Recognize domain-specific details such as band formation years, band members and their roles, record labels and creators, film directors, writers, and the relationship between creative works (e.g., adaptations, screenplays).

3. Crafting the query:
   - The queries must ask explicit, fact-focused questions on:
     * Verification of each entity's identities, roles, and relationships mentioned in the claim.
     * Temporal facts such as formation dates, release years, or event chronology.
     * Geographic or spatial relationships, especially when relative positioning is stated.
     * Connections between creative personnel and their works (e.g., "Who directed and wrote [Film]?").
     * Clarifying ambiguous pronouns ("him") by specifying which individual is referred.
   - Phrase each question so that the answer can be supported by authoritative documents or factual data.
   - Avoid compound questions that bundle multiple distinct facts together; instead, separate them for precision and clarity.
   - Include enough questions to cover all essential factual components needed to fully verify or refute the claim.

4. Domain-specific insights to include:
   - Bands: formation year, origin location, band members with specified roles (frontman, bass guitarist, creator of associated labels).
   - Persons: possible multiple professional roles (actor, developer, director, writer), and their individual works and connections to others.
   - Films and TV series: director, writer, screenplay author, lead actors, adaptation sources, year of release, and broadcasting networks.
   - Geographic relationships often rely on relative location descriptors; queries should explicitly check these.
   - When multiple works share similar or overlapping titles (e.g., "True Crimes"), clarify which work and person is referenced.

5. Reasoning and goal:
   - Your queries should target resolving any ambiguities, contradictions, or missing data.
   - The aim is to enable downstream retrieval of authoritative evidence that directly supports or disproves each core factual assertion in the claim.
   - Strive for minimal yet comprehensive coverage of all critical claim components.

Example query formulations:
- "What year and city did [Band Name] originate?"
- "Who is the frontman/creator of [Record Label]?"
- "Did [Person A] direct and write [Film Title]?"
- "Which network aired [TV Series]? Who starred and developed it?"
- "Is [Person A] credited as the writer of [Film Title]?"
- "Is [Band A]'s origin geographically more southern than [Band B]’s origin?"

Summary:
Your output should be a set of clear, targeted factual questions that collectively retrieve all necessary information to conclusively verify or refute the claim, leveraging both the direct and the indirect context provided by the notes. Incorporate domain-specific knowledge about entertainment (music, film, TV), geography, and named entity roles to ensure full coverage of complex or multi-faceted claims.
