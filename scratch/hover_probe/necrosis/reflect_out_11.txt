Task Description:
You will be given three fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to produce two new fields: `new_notes` and `titles`. 

- `claim`: a factual or factual-sounding assertion or statement that may or may not be supported by the given information.
- `notes`: a list of factual statements, observations, or evidences extracted from various documents that may support, contradict, or are otherwise relevant to the claim.
- `context`: a list of short summaries, descriptions, or excerpts from relevant documents or sources that contain detailed information about entities, events, or places related to the claim.

Your outputs:

1. `new_notes`:
   - Create a concise, relevant subset or refined summary of the original `notes` that directly address the truthfulness or veracity of the `claim`.
   - Include notes that provide evidence supporting or refuting the claim, or that clarify ambiguous or missing information.
   - Highlight key factual data points that are most pertinent to evaluating the claim's accuracy.
   - Remove redundant, irrelevant, or unsubstantiated notes.
   - Synthesize insights where appropriate, so the notes jointly explain why the claim is supported, refuted, or unsubstantiated based on the evidence.

2. `titles`:
   - Extract a minimal yet comprehensive list of document or entity titles from the `context` that are most relevant for the claim verification.
   - These titles should represent key actors, locations, events, or concepts referenced in the claim or critical to understanding it.
   - Aim to include all relevant titles that contributed important evidence in your reasoning for the `new_notes`.
   - The titles should reflect the breadth of evidence considered, ideally covering all major points that support or counter the claim.

Additional Details and Guidelines:

- The claim often involves named entities (people, places, bands, districts, events), relationships between them, or specific factual assertions such as roles, origins, memberships, or historical names.
- The `context` can include detailed descriptions of people’s careers, geography, band memberships, and historical or political details.
- Your reasoning should carefully disambiguate entities with similar names or overlapping attributes and assess claims using geographic, temporal, or role-based logic.
- Pay special attention to the specificity of claims (e.g., “known for negative roles in a particular show” vs. “known for negative roles in general”) and ensure the notes reflect this nuance.
- When entities are referenced that appear in multiple documents or contexts, consider all relevant ones to ensure comprehensive coverage.
- Use evidence from the notes and context to confirm or refute the claim; where evidence is missing, clearly state the lack of supporting information.
- The goal is to provide a distilled, factually accurate summary of evidence (`new_notes`) and a focused set of source titles (`titles`) that a fact-checker or reader could consult for verification or deeper understanding.
- Avoid speculation or unsupported conclusions; base everything on the provided notes and context.
- The style of `new_notes` should be factual, neutral, and explanatory, aiding in claim verification.

Generalizable Approach:

- Read and understand the `claim`.
- Review and cross-examine the `notes` and `context` for direct and indirect evidence.
- Identify and discard irrelevant or non-contributory information.
- Synthesize a clear summary (new_notes) that explains the claim’s support or refutation.
- Select relevant source or entity titles from context that underpin this summary.
- Maintain clarity and completeness in the notes and titles output to allow verification without re-examining the entire input.

This task requires accuracy, domain knowledge application (names, places, music/bands, TV shows, geography, history), and the ability to summarize and select supporting facts precisely.
