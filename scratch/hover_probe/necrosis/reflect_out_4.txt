Task Description:
Given three sets of inputs — a `claim` (a factual statement that may be partially correct or incorrect), a list called `notes` (short factual points or excerpts from sources relevant to the claim), and a longer list called `context` (detailed textual passages or descriptions from source documents related to the claim) — your task is to generate two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. `new_notes`: A concise, accurate, and factual summary extracted or synthesized primarily from the `notes` but informed by the `context` that directly addresses the veracity or key elements of the input `claim`. These should include:
   - Confirmed facts that support or partially support the claim.
   - Identified corrections or contradictions to the claim, specifying errors such as wrong dates, locations, affiliations, or attributes.
   - Avoid repetition or redundant information by selecting the clearest and best-supported notes.
   - When multiple sources clarify or correct the claim, prioritize those with consistent, authoritative wording.
   - Focus on domain-specific factual corrections (names, dates, affiliations, roles, locations, broadcasting networks, formation years, known collaborators, etc.).

2. `titles`: A list of relevant document titles, article names, or entity names that best represent the sources backing the `new_notes`. These titles should:
   - Reflect the entities, events, or topics that confirm or dispute parts of the `claim`.
   - Include titles corresponding to the main subjects in your summary, such as people (e.g., composers, actors), works (e.g., television series, music albums), organizations (e.g., TV networks), or geographic locations.
   - Cover all significant domains touched upon by the claim, as evidenced by the `notes` and `context`.
   - Strive for completeness by referencing all key documents involved in the claim's validation, highlighting any missing but crucial documents from the initially provided input if identified.

Generalizable Strategy:
- First, carefully analyze the claim to identify the key factual elements it asserts (e.g., names, dates, places, roles, affiliations).
- Then, cross-check each assertion against the `notes` for direct confirmations or refutations, using the `context` passages to enhance understanding or clarify ambiguous details.
- Distill the relevant notes into a non-redundant, factually accurate summary (`new_notes`) that highlights both truths and inaccuracies in the claim.
- Select and list the most relevant document or source titles (`titles`) from the `context` and `notes` that validate the summarized points.
- Consider the domain-specific nuances such as:
   - Dates of formation, birth, or events.
   - Correct broadcasting network for TV series.
   - Accurate professional roles or collaborations.
   - Distinctions between similarly named entities or shows.
   - Verification of whether a person holds a specific role (e.g., teacher vs. non-teacher).
- Be attentive to contradictions or errors in the claim (e.g., misattributions of a TV network, wrong formation year of a band).
- Avoid presenting overly detailed or lengthy outputs; keep `new_notes` focused and precise for use in fact-checking or knowledge-base construction.

Domain-specific details to consider:
- Geographic identifiers and their correct naming conventions (e.g., “Gainesville, Florida” as North Central town).
- Correct year of formation or establishment for bands, organizations, or teams.
- Accurate broadcast channel/network for specific TV shows or serials in the Indian television domain.
- The distinction between co-starring actors and their roles within named TV series.
- Professional roles of persons in musical or performing arts context (e.g., composer, teacher, violinist).
- Relations between artists or collaborators (e.g., violinists and composers they work with).
- Common errors such as mixing up shows’ broadcast networks or confusing formation years need particular attention.

The outputs will be used for fact verification, source validation, and summarization of nuanced factual claims. Your response should therefore maintain high factual accuracy, clarity, and relevance.

Input format:
- `claim`: A single textual statement.
- `notes`: A list of short factual sentences or excerpts.
- `context`: A list of longer textual passages from various documents related to the claim.

Output format:
- `new_notes`: List of synthesized, factually supported notes clarifying or correcting the claim.
- `titles`: List of key document or entity titles validating the `new_notes`.
