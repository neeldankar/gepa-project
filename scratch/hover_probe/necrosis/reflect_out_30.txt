Task Description:  
You will receive two inputs: a `claim` and a collection of `notes`. The `claim` is a concise factual assertion or set of assertions involving named entities (persons, bands, organizations, films, TV series, books, record labels, events, locations) and their relationships, attributes, timelines, and roles. The `notes` comprise text snippets offering partial, complementary, or conflicting factual information relevant to these entities or relationships.

Your goal is to derive a precise set of fact-focused, explicit interrogative queries that target the essential information needed to verify or refute every key factual element of the claim. These queries will guide authoritative document retrieval or human fact-checking, ensuring minimal yet comprehensive coverage of all claim components.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A factual statement(s) including entities, dates, roles, relationships, and assertions that require factual confirmation.  
- `notes`: A list of textual excerpts containing context, confirmations, partial evidence, or contradictions relevant to verifying elements of the claim.

Output:  
- A set of clear, discrete, unambiguous questions designed to retrieve authoritative facts.  
- Each question should focus on a single factual aspect or relationship to reduce ambiguity and maximize retrieval precision.

Core Requirements and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Dissecting the Claim into Key Factual Elements:**  
   - Identify every concrete fact in the claim:  
     - Named entities (people, bands, organizations, creative works, locations)  
     - Dates or years (release dates, formation years, broadcast dates)  
     - Roles and relationships (e.g., director, writer, actor, label owner, frontman, record label signatory)  
     - Adaptations or source relationships (e.g., film adapted from a novel by a named author)  
     - Associations or affiliations (e.g., band signed by a specific label, TV show aired on a certain network)

2. **Leveraging Notes to Contextualize and Detect Verification Gaps:**  
   - Use notes to confirm which facts are already affirmed, contradicted, or missing.  
   - Pay special attention to:  
     - Creative roles, distinguishing director, writer, developer, actor, etc.  
     - Band memberships, roles of each member, and label signings  
     - Broadcast platforms for TV series  
     - Correction of common confusions or conflations between people or works (e.g., differentiating between similarly titled films)  
     - Non-existent or incorrectly attributed entities or facts clearly debunked in notes

3. **Constructing High-Precision Interrogative Queries:**  
   - Frame question for each distinct factual unit:  
     - Verify entity existence and role accuracy  
     - Confirm specific relationships and affiliations  
     - Validate dates and timelines  
     - Clarify spelling and identity differences where confusion is possible  
     - Separate queries addressing potentially conflated facts  
   - Use question words explicitly: Who, What, When, Where, Did, Is, Are, etc.  
   - Avoid vague or compound queries; aim for granular, fact-checkable questions.

4. **Domain-Specific Query Patterns:**  
   - **Film and Literature:**  
     - Identify director(s), writer(s), and adapter(s) of films  
     - Confirm original source material and adaptation authorship  
     - Distinguish similarly titled works and their creators  
   - **Music Bands:**  
     - Confirm genre, formation year, member roles, and recording contracts  
     - Verify label owners and whether bands were signed to specific record labels  
   - **TV Series & Acting Credits:**  
     - Confirm developer(s), principal cast, and network/platform affiliations  
     - Distinguish between creators, writers, and actors where relevant  
   - **Individuals with Multiple Roles:**  
     - Query each role held by a person separately (e.g., actor vs. writer vs. developer)

5. **Reasoning and Clarifications:**  
   - Identify and isolate conflated claims into separate factual questions  
   - Flag and clarify internal inconsistencies, such as misattributed authorship or simultaneous contradictory role claims  
   - Ensure minimal queries cover all aspects needed to comprehensively verify the claim  
   - Ground queries to factual checks that can be answered by authoritative documentary sources or official records

6. **Examples of Effective Queries:**  
   - "Who directed [Film Title]?"  
   - "Did [Person] write the screenplay for [Film Title]?"  
   - "Was [Band] signed to [Record Label]?"  
   - "What instrument did [Musician] play in [Band]?"  
   - "When was [Film/TV Series] released or first broadcast?"  
   - "Is [Person] credited as a developer or star of [TV Series]?"  
   - "Was the film '[Title]' adapted from the novel '[Book Title]' by [Author]?"

7. **Output Style:**  
   - List queries clearly and separately  
   - Keep each question concise and explicit  
   - Avoid ambiguity and compound questioning  
   - Ensure completeness so the entire claim’s factual assertions can be thoroughly checked

This approach ensures high precision in document retrieval, minimizes ambiguity and conflation, and facilitates authoritative verification of complex claims containing multiple entities, dates, and relationships.
