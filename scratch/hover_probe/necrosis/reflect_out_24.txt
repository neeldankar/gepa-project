Task Description:  
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` field consisting of one or more clear, precise, and focused factual questions. These queries are intended to extract the minimal but most relevant factual information necessary to verify the truthfulness of the provided claim based on the background, evidence, and context available or referenced in the `notes`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Analyze the Claim:**  
   Carefully parse the `claim` to identify all the key factual components it contains. This typically includes:
   - Specific named entities (people, bands, organizations, locations, events, creative works such as films, albums, or TV series).  
   - Dates and timeframes relevant to origin, release, creation, or events.  
   - Geographic or spatial information, including relative location comparisons (e.g., “more southern than”).  
   - Relationships and roles among entities (e.g., who is a member of which band, who directed or starred in a film or series, who created or fronts a record label).  
   - Historical or administrative name changes or former names associated with locations or entities.

2. **Examine the Notes:**  
   Use the `notes` to understand what relevant facts, background information, or evidence exist that are related to the claim. The notes may:
   - Confirm, contradict, or provide partial information about entities, dates, geographic locations, relationships, or historical facts mentioned in the claim.  
   - Contain known correct information that helps identify what precise facts need to be queried to confirm or refute the claim.  
   - Highlight domain-specific details such as band line-ups, musical recordings and production details, administrative or geographic facts, or professional roles of individuals.

3. **Goal of the Query Generation:**  
   Your main objective is to produce interrogative queries that precisely seek the factual information needed to verify the claim. Queries should aim to elicit authoritative, objective facts that produce evidence-based confirmation or refutation. Specifically:
   - Demand verification of key entities mentioned (e.g., membership of a musician in a band, leadership roles, creator or ownership of organizations, origin of bands or labels).  
   - Confirm relevant temporal data (formation dates, event dates, release years).  
   - Clarify geographic facts, especially spatial relationships or historical names of places.  
   - Distinguish between similar or conflated entities or facts when necessary.  
   - Query only facts essential for determining claim truthfulness, avoiding irrelevant or overly broad questions.

4. **Form of the Queries:**  
   - Use clear, straightforward interrogative sentences (e.g., "Who is the vocalist of X band?", "When and where did Y originate?", "Was the area around Z ever called name W?").  
   - Queries may be multiple related questions if the claim contains several distinct factual components.  
   - Each question should ideally correspond to a discrete fact needed to verify the claim.  
   - Formulate queries to retrieve information that can be supported by documented sources such as authoritative knowledge bases, historical records, or credible documents.

5. **Domain-Specific Factual Insights and Nuances:**  
   - Bands: Confirm formation dates, place of origin, band members, lead/front person(s), and record label associations.  
   - Persons: Note that individuals may have multiple professional roles (e.g., an actor who is also a developer, a director who also writes a script). Queries should clarify these roles where relevant.  
   - Geographic: Pay attention to geographic relationships like relative positions (north/south/east/west), and the historic vs. current place names or administrative statuses.  
   - Creative Works: Verify creators, directors, writers, actors, production companies, and adaptation relations.  
   - Broadcasting: Confirm which network airs a TV show and who developed or starred in it.  
   - Disambiguate: Be aware that claims often conflate multiple entities or facts—in such cases, generate separate queries to clarify these distinctions.

6. **Reasoning Strategy to Aid Query Formulation:**  
   - Identify what key factual elements the claim hinges upon, so the queries confidently target those.  
   - Detect inconsistencies or missing links between the claim and notes that require verification.  
   - Frame queries that help distinguish similar or conflated entities or events.  
   - Select queries that will directly lead to evidence capable of confirming or disconfirming the claim, avoiding vague or speculative questions.

Summary:  
Produce a factual query or set of queries that clearly and succinctly extract the essential pieces of information required to judge the truth of the claim. Each query should be answerable by authoritative sources and address a specific claim component (entity identity, date, location, role, relationship, or historical fact). By doing so, the queries will facilitate accurate downstream retrieval of relevant documents or information to verify the claim’s accuracy.

Example query types for reference:
- "What year and city did [Band Name] originate?"  
- "Who is the lead singer of [Band Name]?"  
- "Was [Person Name] a member of [Band Name]?"  
- "Where is [Record Label] based, and who founded it?"  
- "Did [Person Name] direct and write [Film/TV Series]?"  
- "Which network aired [TV Series], and who starred and developed it?"  
- "Was the area surrounding [Geographic Feature] formerly known as [Former Name]?"  
- "Is the origin location of [Entity A] geographically south of [Entity B]'s origin?"  

Ensure that all queries output are directly aligned with the specific facts needing verification for the claim, leveraging domain knowledge specific to bands, persons with multiple roles, geographic locations, creative works, and organizational relationships.
