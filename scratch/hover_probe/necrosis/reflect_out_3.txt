You are given an input that consists of two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a concise and precise `query` field. This `query` should be a well-formed question that captures the central factual verification needed to assess the truthfulness or accuracy of the `claim`, based on the information provided in the `notes`.

Instructions:

1. Analyze the `claim` carefully:
   - Identify the key factual assertion(s) made in the claim that can be supported or refuted.
   - Focus on concrete entities, relationships, or historical facts mentioned (e.g., people and their roles, locations, organizations, events, dates).

2. Review the `notes` thoroughly:
   - The notes provide detailed, domain-specific factual evidence relevant to the claim.
   - Notes often clarify or contradict parts of the claim by giving background context, geographic information, temporal detail, or associations between entities.
   - Use the notes to understand ambiguity or uncertainty in the claim’s statements, such as verifying the authenticity of alleged relationships, historical facts, or titles.

3. Formulate the `query`:
   - Turn the central verification point of the claim into a clear, straightforward question.
   - Frame the question to directly test the main factual aspect(s) of the claim.
   - The question should be factoid, answerable by checking the notes or authoritative sources, not open-ended or opinion-based.
   - When the claim combines multiple facts (e.g., an individual holding two related positions), the query may combine those facts logically to maintain context.
   - Avoid overly broad or vague questions; keep them narrowly focused on the claim’s key assertions.

4. Reasoning (optional but recommended):
   - Optionally, produce a short reasoning step summarizing how the notes relate to the claim and why the query chosen properly tests the claim’s validity.
   - This can help ensure completeness and clarity but is not mandatory for final output.

Summary:
Given `claim` and supporting or contradicting `notes`, produce a fact-focused, specific, and unambiguous `query` question that captures the main factual verification task implied by the claim.
