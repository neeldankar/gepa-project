Task Description:
You are given three input fields: 
- `claim`: a declarative statement or assertion involving people, works (like TV series, films, albums), roles, or relationships.
- `notes`: a list of textual snippets (often partial or detailed factual statements) extracted from various documents related to entities or facts in the claim.
- `context`: a list of longer, often encyclopedic or detailed summaries about entities, works, or related topics connected to the claim.

Your task is to analyze the `claim` in light of both the `notes` and `context` to:
1. Assess the truthfulness or accuracy of the claim based on the factual evidence presented.
2. Condense and refine the information from the `notes` (and context when needed) into a coherent, concise set of `new_notes` that reflect the verified facts relevant to the claim, excluding redundancies or unsupported assertions.
3. Identify the most relevant document or entity titles from the information sources that underpin the reasoning and evidence for the claim's assessment, and provide this as the output field `titles`.

Detailed Requirements and Domain-Specific Guidance:
- Entities include persons (with their professional roles), TV series, films, music bands, albums, documentaries, and other media works. Many have nuanced roles or contributions (e.g., actor vs developer, vocalist vs bassist, director vs writer). Paying careful attention to these distinctions is essential.
- Works may have complex production or airing histories involving multiple people or organizations; e.g., a TV series having multiple actors, developers, or networks.
- The input may contain multiple similarly named or related entities; distinguishing among them using contextual clues is crucial (e.g., "Soul Mates" (TV series) vs "Soul Music" (TV series) vs "Soul Mates" (Babylon 5 episode)).
- Claims often make compound assertions involving individuals’ roles (like actor, director, writer, musician in specific bands). Validation requires precise instrumental or professional role delineations.
- Maintain precision in spelling of names and titles (e.g., “Mikael Åkerfeldt” vs “Michael Akerfeldt”), and ensure proper matching despite minor spelling variants.
- Use context information to verify or clarify ambiguities in notes or claims (especially when the notes are incomplete or ambiguous).
- When the claim implicates a relationship or attribution (e.g., who developed a show, who wrote a screenplay), highlight if evidence is lacking or ambiguous.
- The `new_notes` should be factual, concise, relevant, and avoid unsupported assertions. They must summarize the confirmed information that directly addresses parts of the claim.
- The `titles` list should include all relevant named entities or works that were pivotal in validating or refuting the claim, including people (with their full roles or titles), media works (with release years or descriptors), and any relevant organizations.
- The reasoning behind the outputs should reflect careful corroboration of the claim against the evidence, noting confirmations and contradictions, and clarifying unsupportable assertions.

Generalizable Solution Strategy:
- Parse the claim carefully to identify key entities, relationships, and assertions.
- Aggregate relevant facts from notes, noting contradictions, confirmations, and missing information.
- Cross-check and disambiguate entities and facts against the more detailed context entries.
- Synthesize a balanced and precise summary of verified facts in `new_notes`.
- Collect the titles corresponding to authoritative or relevant documents/entities that inform the conclusion.
- Flag any part of the claim that is not supported, ambiguous, or contradicted by the evidence, ensuring clarity in the refined notes.

The entire output should be structured as:
- `new_notes`: a list of verified, relevant factual statements that support or refute the claim.
- `titles`: a list of document/entity titles that underpin the reasoning and factual checks.

This task requires domain-specific knowledge about people’s professional roles, media productions, and accurate attribution of creative or performance contributions, as well as critical evaluation of evidence quality and completeness.
