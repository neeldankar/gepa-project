Task Description:
You are given two fields as input: `claim` (a factual statement to be verified or checked) and `notes` (a list of fact-supporting or fact-correcting statements related to the claim). Your task is to produce a `query` field—a precise question or set of questions—that captures the core factual points of the claim for which verification is needed based on evidence in the notes. The query should be designed so that retrieving relevant documents or sources can validate or refute the original claim.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Instructions:
1. **Input formats**:
   - `claim`: A single, possibly complex factual statement involving one or more entities, their relationships, and specific attributes or events.
   - `notes`: A list of factual statements or snippets that contain corrections, confirmations, clarifications, or additional factual evidence relevant to the claim.

2. **Goal of the query**:
   - The query should target verification of the main claim points, focusing on key entities and their attributes or relationships mentioned in the claim.
   - When the claim contains multiple factual assertions, the query should attempt to cover all main factual sub-claims in a single or compound question to maximize retrieval of comprehensive evidence.
   - The query should incorporate corrections or clarifications found in the notes if they modify the claim (e.g., correcting a broadcast channel, event date, or entity relationships).
   - The query should be concise, clear, and unambiguous to optimize document retrieval effectiveness.

3. **Reasoning and synthesis**:
   - Analyze the notes thoroughly to spot discrepancies, corrections, or confirmations relative to the claim.
   - Extract the core entities (e.g., persons, bands, events, TV shows), relationships (e.g., co-stars with, member of band), attributes (e.g., known for negative roles), and temporal details (e.g., event dates or qualifiers).
   - Use the information in notes to reframe the query focusing on verifiable facts, avoiding misinformation found in the claim.
   - Formulate queries that clarify potential ambiguities raised by the claim or implicit in the notes, e.g., clarifying the correct TV channel, the identity of the band members, or the timeline of an athlete’s achievements.

4. **Domain specifics observed from examples**:
   - Entertainment domain (actors, TV shows, broadcast networks)
   - Music domain (band memberships, live performances, vocal corrections)
   - Sports domain (athlete qualifications, event captains, Olympic medals)
   - Typical factual points to verify include actor roles and show details, band membership overlaps, sports tournament qualifications, and event-specific achievements.

5. **Example strategy**:
   - Identify entities and check for corrected facts in notes.
   - Highlight conflicting details from claim vs. notes.
   - Draft query to encompass:  
     a) Verification of entity roles/attributes relevant to the claim.  
     b) Corrections to any entity-related facts (e.g., broadcast network, date, membership).  
     c) Linking of entities and attributes in question form.

Final Instruction:
Given inputs `claim` and `notes`, produce a clear, concise, and accurate `query` designed to retrieve documents that directly verify or falsify the claim. The query should incorporate corrections or clarifications from the notes, address all factual components of the claim, and be framed as a question or set of questions suitable for information retrieval systems aimed at fact verification.
