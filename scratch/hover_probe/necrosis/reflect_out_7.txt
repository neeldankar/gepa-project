Task Description:

You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a `query` that accurately and precisely captures the essential factual verification question arising from the `claim` using the evidence and context contained in the `notes`.

Key Details and Requirements:

1. Inputs:
   - `claim`: A single, specific factual statement or assertion that may require verification or disambiguation.
   - `notes`: A list of text snippets or summary statements gathered from relevant documents, containing factual information related to entities, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim.

2. Output:
   - `query`: Formulate a concise, clear, targeted question or set of connected questions that a knowledge retrieval or verification process should answer to confirm or refute the claim based on the known facts in `notes`.

3. Approach:
   - Perform careful factual reasoning by cross-referencing the `claim` against details in `notes`.
   - Identify key entities, dates, locations, or relationships mentioned in the claim.
   - Note any implicit verification needs such as historical naming, association of persons with roles, or event participation.
   - Target the `query` so that it queries exactly the points of potential uncertainty or potential factual error emerging from the comparison of claim and notes.
   - Avoid ambiguity: the query should clearly isolate the claim’s contested or check-worthy fact.
   - If multiple distinct factual questions arise from the claim and notes, the query can explicitly combine these, linked logically (e.g., “Was X true, and was Y true?”).

4. Domain-Specific Guidance:
   - Claims often revolve around biographical, geographic, historical, or sports factual details, such as:
     - The identity or attributes of persons (e.g., roles in bands, sports teams, or historical figures).
     - Precise geographic or historical place names and their relationships.
     - Accurate identification of event participation, ranking, or titles (e.g., championships, tournaments).
   - Notes may repeatedly emphasize or clarify distinctions between similarly named entities or geographies.
   - A thorough understanding of distinctions among persons with similar or identical names is critical.
   - The query should often seek historical or temporal confirmation (e.g., “was X formerly known as Y?”) or role/relationship validations.

5. Quality criteria:
   - The query should enable retrieval of all relevant “gold” documents or sources needed to confirm the veracity of the claim.
   - The query should be constructed so that it facilitates precise, domain-relevant document retrieval or fact-checking.
   - It should avoid unnecessary wording or unrelated questions.
   - It should explicitly address contradictions or lack of evidence in the notes that affect the claim.

Summary:

Given a claim and supporting or contradictory factual notes, your task is to reason about the factual correctness of the claim, identify the core points requiring confirmation, and generate a clear, targeted factual query that precisely addresses those points, enabling retrieval or validation of all necessary information to verify the claim in the specific domain context.
