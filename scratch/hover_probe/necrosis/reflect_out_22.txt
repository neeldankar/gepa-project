Task Description:
You are given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a statement that makes one or more factual assertions. The `notes` field contains a list of facts, background information, or evidence relevant to verifying or contextualizing the claim. Your goal is to produce a clear, focused, and factually relevant `query` that can be used to retrieve or confirm information needed to verify the claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. Understand and parse the `claim` carefully. Identify the key entities, relationships, and factual assertions being made.
2. Review the `notes` to understand:
   - What information is already known or available.
   - What gaps remain to be filled to verify the claim completely or clarify ambiguities.
   - Which entities, events, or relationships are associated with the claim.
3. Your `query` must be a single coherent question or a closely related set of questions that:
   - Targets crucial missing or unclear information needed to validate or refute the claim.
   - Leverages specific details such as names, dates, relationships, or roles as identified in the claim and notes.
   - Is concise and unambiguous to maximize effective and precise document retrieval.
4. The reasoning behind your query should reflect how the claim is analyzed, which key points are targeted, and how the notes help shape the query.
5. The queries often involve verification of people’s roles, their relationships to events or groups, authorship or contribution to creative works, and clarifying overlapping identities or attributes between entities.
6. Avoid overly broad or generic queries; instead, focus on the precise fact or connection the claim makes or implies.
7. When multiple pieces of evidence are required (e.g., confirming two separate members’ roles), combine related questions appropriately in a single query to reflect that dependency.
8. Make sure the query is framed in a way that the retrieved documents will ideally include authoritative sources confirming or denying the claim's components.

Domain-Specific Details Observed:
- The director or writer roles for films and their adaptations and screenplay contributions.
- Specific attributes of musical artists, including exact roles in the band (e.g., bass guitarist vs lead vocalist/guitarist).
- Membership and songwriting status within musical groups, including differentiating between writers and performers.
- Importance of differentiating similarly named people or projects (e.g., "True Crimes" vs "True Crime").
- The contextual relevance of dates, group affiliations, and distinct works to correctly link claims and notes.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Parse the claim into discrete factual assertions.
- Cross-reference `notes` to recognize what is confirmed, denied, or ambiguously stated.
- Identify what information is critical but missing or uncertain.
- Formulate a targeted, specific, and clear retrieval question that isolates the uncertainty or verifies the entire claim.
- Use entity names and roles explicitly in the query to improve precision in document retrieval.

Example:
Given a claim about a film director’s dual role as writer and their association with a particular film, and notes clarifying screened roles and unrelated works, the query correctly asks: 
"Who directed the 2011 drama adapted from him and Rosemary Sutcliff, and did this director write the film True Crimes?" 

This approach helps retrieve documents focused on the pivotal individuals and verifies the composite assertion.

Summary:
Produce fact-checking queries from claims and contextual notes that maximize retrieval of evidence to confirm or deny the claim; ensure queries are narrow and focused on precise factual connections and roles as deduced from the claim and notes.
