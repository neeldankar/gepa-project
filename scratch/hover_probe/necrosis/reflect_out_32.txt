Task Description:

Given three fields:

- `claim`: a statement which may contain factual assertions about people, events, places, or entities.
- `notes`: a list of textual notes or evidence snippets related to the claim, potentially including corrections, elaborations, or supportive facts.
- `context`: a list of longer reference documents or knowledge passages relevant to the entities or topics mentioned in the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: a concise, focused summary or synthesis of the key evidential notes from the `notes` input, selecting or rephrasing those that directly support or refute the claim. Include clarifications, corrections (e.g., correcting a mistaken date), and relevant details from the notes that would help verify the claim accurately. Avoid redundancy and irrelevant information. The new notes should clearly provide evidence related to the claim’s key factual points.

2. `titles`: a list of titles/names of relevant reference documents (from the `context`) that best correspond to the important entities, topics, or references needed to verify the claim. This is like specifying which documents or articles contain crucial factual information tied to the claim and new_notes. The titles should accurately capture key people, places, organizations, or works related to the claim, as supported by the notes and context.

Additional Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Guidelines:

- The claim often involves factual assertions about people (musicians, actors, band members, etc.), media (TV shows, bands, songs, albums), places (cities licensing TV stations), and dates (formation years, release dates). Attention to specific domains like music, television, and entertainment is important.

- Clarify any incorrect or imprecise information found in the claim, especially times/dates, affiliations, or identities. For example, if the claim incorrectly states a band's formation year or a TV show network, correct that in `new_notes`.

- Distinguish between closely related but distinct entities (e.g., different bands, TV channels, or individuals). Confirm or deny relationships or co-memberships carefully.

- When the claim involves multiple entities (e.g., two musicians, two bands), explicitly confirm from notes/context whether they overlap or not.

- The provided `notes` can contain repetitions or near-duplicates. Synthesize or select the most clear and precise statements to avoid redundancy in `new_notes`.

- Use domain knowledge revealed in `context` to help identify which titles are most relevant for verification. These usually correspond to named entities or articles with exact or near-exact names of people, bands, TV shows, places, or phenomena cited in the claim and notes.

- The `titles` selection is crucial for grounding evidence. Include at least all key proper nouns, media titles, and relevant places. If the claim references band membership, include that band’s article title if present.

- The reasoning portion of the previous responses (assistant’s explanations) can guide clarity but is not required in the output; only produce `new_notes` and `titles`.

- Avoid adding extraneous or unrelated titles; keep focused on documents that best explain or support/refute the claim.

Overall Strategy:

- Carefully read the claim to identify factual components to verify (dates, affiliations, relationships, places).

- Review `notes` to extract accurate summaries and corrections; disregard duplicates and irrelevant or unsupported notes.

- Use `context` documents to identify authoritative reference titles relevant for the claim.

- Produce a minimal but sufficient subset of notes and titles that effectively enable verification or clarify the claim, reflecting corrections, confirmations, or negations made clear in the notes/context.

By following this approach, the assistant can provide accurate, concise new notes and a carefully chosen set of titles that together best support or refute the given claim.
