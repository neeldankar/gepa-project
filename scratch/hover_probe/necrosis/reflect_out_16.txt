Task Description:  
You will be provided with two inputs: a `claim` and a set of `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` or a set of factual questions. These queries must focus on the essential, fact-based information needed to verify or refute the claim using the content or references present in the notes.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: a factual statement or set of assertions containing entities such as people, organizations, events, dates, locations, creative works (films, TV series, music bands), and their attributes or relationships.  
- `notes`: a list of text snippets providing partial context, factual details, or evidence related to the entities or relationships mentioned in the claim. The notes may affirm, contradict, or provide incomplete information relevant to the claim.

Objective:  
Your primary goal is to craft an explicit, targeted, and concise set of interrogative queries to retrieve authoritative factual data that can directly verify each key component of the claim. This enables downstream information retrieval or human verification to effectively locate the minimal but complete set of relevant documents or evidence.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:  

1. **Identify Key Claim Components:**  
   - Extract all concrete factual elements from the claim, including:  
     - Named entities (persons, bands, organizations, films, TV shows, books, record labels, etc.)  
     - Dates or years (e.g., release years, formation dates)  
     - Roles and relationships (e.g., director, writer, actor, frontman, label signatory)  
     - Geographic or spatial references if any (e.g., relative city positions)  
     - Event adaptations (e.g., a film adapted from a novel by a specific author)

2. **Contextualize with Notes:**  
   - Use the notes to understand what information is confirmed, missing, or conflicting, especially focusing on:  
     - Authorship or creative roles (e.g., who directed, wrote, or developed a creative work)  
     - Band membership and recording contracts or labels  
     - Broadcast networks or platforms airing shows  
     - Correct identities and spellings (e.g., confirming which person is linked to which work)  
     - Common confusions addressed or disproven in the notes (e.g., non-existence of a "Supergrass Records")  

3. **Formulate Queries to Clarify Verification Needs:**  
   - Frame the queries as clear and precise questions aimed to establish or negate the following factual points for each element involved in the claim:  
     - Verification of entity existence and attribute accuracy (e.g., Is XXXX a rock band? Did YYYY direct the 2011 film?)  
     - Confirmation of specific factual relationships (e.g., Was band ZZZ signed by Mike Smith? Did Nicholas Boshier develop and star in Soul Mates?)  
     - Temporal validity (e.g., What year did band formation or film release occur?)  
     - Affiliations or broadcast channels involved  
     - Distinguishing potentially conflated entities or works (e.g., differentiating “True Crime” and “True Crimes”)  

4. **Interrogative Query Style:**  
   - Use 'Who', 'What', 'When', 'Where', 'Did' question formats, targeting specific, discrete facts.  
   - When multiple related facts are necessary, separate them as distinct questions to ensure clarity.  
   - Avoid broad or vague queries; focus on granular, testable factoids.

5. **Domain-Specific Insights for Query Generation:**  
   - Film and literature: Clarify directors, writers, adaptation sources, and film titles with correct spelling.  
   - Music bands: Confirm genre, band members, formation year, record label signings, and label owners/agents.  
   - TV series: Confirm principal cast, development roles, and broadcasting networks or platforms.  
   - Persons with multiple roles: For individuals involved as actors, writers, developers, or directors, confirm each role distinctly.  
   - Geographic relationships if mentioned: Verify the relative positioning or other spatial assertions.  

6. **Reasoning Considerations:**  
   - Identify internal inconsistencies or problematic assertions in the claim based on notes (e.g., wrongly attributed writing credit).  
   - Separate likely conflated facts or entities into multiple queries to minimize conflation.  
   - Focus on the minimal fact set necessary to affirm or deny the entire claim accurately.

Output Structure:  
- Provide a short set of focused, well-formed questions that cover every essential factual component of the claim in light of the notes.  
- Ensure these questions can be answered definitively by consulting authoritative documents or official knowledge bases.

Example Query Template for a claim about a film:  
- "What year was [Film Title] released?"  
- "Who directed [Film Title]?"  
- "Is [Person A] credited as a writer or developer of [Film Title]?"  
- "Was [Film Title] adapted from a work by [Author]?"

This approach ensures high precision in information retrieval, reduces ambiguity, and facilitates authoritative verification of claims involving complex interactions among multiple factual entities and relationships.
