You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`.

- `claim` is a factual or relational statement that requires verification or validation by gathering relevant information.
- `notes` is a list of factual excerpts, snippets, or pieces of evidence that may support or refute the claim. These notes often include partial information about people, entities, events, affiliations, and contexts, and can contain clarifications, corrections, or negations relevant to the claim.

Your task is to generate a `query` (a single question or a small set of concise related questions) that is designed to guide further information retrieval to verify or investigate the claim. The query should be precise and targeted to identify key missing or uncertain facts that directly impact the truthfulness of the claim.

Guidelines and domain-specific details to consider:

1. **Context-Aware Query Generation**  
   The `query` must focus on clarifying or confirming the key relationships, memberships, affiliations, or facts mentioned or doubted in the `claim` based on the `notes`. For example:  
   - If the claim names people and their group affiliations, ensure the query explicitly seeks verification of the group memberships for those individuals.  
   - If networks or broadcasters are confused in the claim, the query should ask which network airs the show and whether the actors are associated with the correct network or drama.  
   - If the claim involves specific record labels, signings, or contracts, the query should check the affiliation history related to artists and executives mentioned.

2. **Use Explicit and Disambiguating Language**  
   The query should use full names and explicit identifiers where possible (e.g., "Who wrote the song 'Girl Talk' and are they members of a girl group?" rather than vague terms). This is essential because `notes` often include entities with overlapping or similar names.

3. **Focus on Missing or Contradictory Information**  
   The `notes` often reveal critical missing data or corrections to the claim. The query should hone in on these uncertainties or contradictions.  
   For example:  
   - If it is unclear whether a person is signed by a record label or by an A&R executive, ask explicitly about that relationship.  
   - If the claim confuses which network broadcasts a series, ask which network broadcasts the relevant series or where the actor co-stars.

4. **Compound Queries Are Acceptable If Necessary**  
   When the claim involves multiple facets that need to be confirmed in tandem (e.g., whether an actor co-stars in a series on X network *and* whether the actor is known for negative roles on another network), the query should combine these logically related questions clearly and concisely.

5. **Avoid Redundancy and Unrelated Information**  
   The query should not request information that is already conclusively supported by the `notes`. Focus only on what remains unclear or requires verification.

6. **Domain-Specific Knowledge to Consider**  
   - The identity and affiliations of musical artists and songwriters often matter (e.g., songwriters being members of specific girl groups).  
   - TV actors' co-starring roles and the networks where shows air are key verification points.  
   - Music industry signings by A&R executives and record label affiliations are critical factual points.

7. **Example-inspired Strategy**  
   Review the `claim` and `notes` carefully to pinpoint what piece of information would conclusively support or refute the claim, then phrase a single or compound question that would retrieve that piece of evidence efficiently.

In sum, from the input `claim` and `notes`, produce a precise, targeted `query` that aims at retrieving the minimal crucial additional evidence to verify the claim’s truthfulness — focusing on clarifying identified ambiguities, verifying affiliations, or correcting mistaken information reflected or implied in the claim or notes.
