Task Objective:

You will be given three text inputs — a `claim`, a set of `notes`, and a `context` — all related to one or more domain-specific factual assertions involving entities such as musicians, bands, TV shows, actors, record labels, locations, events, or publications. Your goal is to produce two outputs, `new_notes` and `titles`, which together support, clarify, or correct the factual accuracy of the `claim` by synthesizing and cross-referencing the information available in the `notes` and the `context`.

Input Specifications:
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion involving verifiable details about one or more domain-specific entities (e.g., band formation years and locations, membership of actors in TV shows, songwriters and their group affiliations, record label creators).
- `notes`: A collection of short, often fragmented textual snippets drawn from various sources related to the claim’s entities. These may contain redundancy, partial or conflicting information.
- `context`: Longer, descriptive passages or encyclopedia-style entries providing authoritative background, history, or detailed facts about the entities, groups, releases, or events connected to the claim.

Output Requirements:

1. `new_notes`:
   - Extract, synthesize, and concisely restate only the factually correct, non-redundant pieces of information that directly support, clarify, or correct the `claim`.
   - Prioritize facts verified by both `notes` and `context` — use repeated or highly authoritative context to resolve conflicting data (e.g., formation dates, founding members, network affiliations).
   - Emphasize key connections involving multiple entities or fact clusters crucial to the claim, such as:
     - For music claims: band formation year, location (city, region), membership timelines, record label affiliations, album release and production details, live performances or concert releases.
     - For entertainment claims: actor roles, TV series titles, broadcasting networks or channels, songwriters or creators of works, and any associated organizations.
     - Geographical clarifications (e.g., relative locations of cities, states, or regions) relevant to the entities.
   - Remove irrelevant, contradictory, or unsupported information to maintain clarity and precision.
   - When domain-specific terminology or proper nouns are involved (e.g., network names like ABC2 or Star Plus; labels like Sire Records; punk subgenres), include them exactly and correctly.

2. `titles`:
   - Provide a comprehensive but focused list of key entities referenced in the claim or necessary for understanding it, drawn from both the notes and the context.
   - Include all major persons (musicians, actors, creators), bands, TV shows, record labels, organizations, venues, albums, or distinct works referenced.
   - Incorporate related entities needed for disambiguation or contextual completeness (e.g., specific TV networks, geographical locations, renowned collaborators).
   - This list should help with knowledge retrieval, verification, or provide essential background to fully grasp the claim and `new_notes`.

General Methodology:

- Carefully cross-check the `claim` against information in both the `notes` and the richer `context`. Only facts supported by multiple reliable sources should be adopted.
- Reconcile conflicting or ambiguous information in favor of the most authoritative or most frequently corroborated details, especially formation dates, places of origin, band membership changes, or broadcast details.
- Avoid introducing any unfounded, extraneous, or irrelevant data beyond what the inputs support.
- Distinguish clearly between similarly named entities or overlapping domains by specifying contextual details such as dates, locations, or production credits.
- Pay special attention to domain-specific factual accuracy, such as:
  - Confirming the exact formation year and city of a band or artist.
  - Verifying network affiliations and production details for TV shows or songs.
  - Establishing correct membership and songwriting credits in musical groups.
  - Clarifying geographic positioning of towns or cities when relevant.
- Use domain-precise terminology to foster factual and contextual clarity.
- Summarize complex or duplicated information in an integrated, concise way to best support or correct the `claim`.

Examples of Domain-Specific Details to Attend:

- Music domain: band origin city and region (e.g., Gainesville, Florida in North Central Florida), formation year (e.g., 1997 for Against Me!), member roles (e.g., Laura Jane Grace as founder), releases and record labels (e.g., Sire Records, Fat Wreck Chords), live concert DVD or album production details.
- Entertainment domain: Actor names and their roles, TV show titles and network broadcasters (e.g., WCJB-TV as ABC affiliate), songwriter and group relationships (e.g., Lisa Lopes and Tionne Watkins writing "Girl Talk" as part of TLC).
- Geographic context: positioning cities relative to one another (e.g., Chicago is more northern than many southern US locations).

By following these instructions, your outputs will provide a precise, authoritative, and clear set of synthesized facts (`new_notes`) and relevant key entities (`titles`) that enable accurate verification or contextualization of the claim.
