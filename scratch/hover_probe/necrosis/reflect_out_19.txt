Given two input fields, `claim` and `notes`, generate a precise, clear, and comprehensive `query` that can be used to verify or fact-check the claim using information from the provided notes.

Detailed task description and guidelines:

1. **Inputs**:
   - `claim`: A complex factual statement or assertion that needs verification.
   - `notes`: A list of relevant textual excerpts (facts, descriptions, or evidence) related to entities or events mentioned in the claim.

2. **Goal**:
   - Formulate a single query in natural language that is intended to check the veracity of the `claim` by confirming or disconfirming key pieces of factual information contained in it.
   - The query should be suitable for retrieving supporting or refuting evidence from a knowledge base or search system.

3. **Key points to consider**:
   - Extract all factual components from the claim that must be verified (e.g., persons, events, dates, roles, relationships, attributes).
   - Use the notes to understand the entities and relationships involved, especially niche or domain-specific knowledge such as:
     - Proper names, including correct spellings (e.g., "Christiaan Van Vuuren", not "Christian Van Vuuren").
     - Titles and roles (e.g., "skip" means captain in curling).
     - Precise event names, venues, and dates (e.g., "Art of Life 1993.12.31 Tokyo Dome concert").
     - Affiliations and memberships (e.g., band membership, broadcaster association).
     - Any specialized terminology (e.g., curling terminology, music production techniques).
   - Phrase the query so it covers **all** critical points necessary to fully verify the claim. This often means combining multiple fact checks into one query.
   - The query should avoid ambiguity, using explicit references from the claim and notes.
   - The query should be grammatically correct and fluent, mimicking typical natural language questions.

4. **Strategy**:
   - Carefully parse the claim to identify all factually relevant parts.
   - Use notes to confirm entity names, roles, and relationships, ensuring the query reflects accurate and disambiguated information.
   - Include questions about entities’ participation, roles, relationships, and attributes highlighted in the claim.
   - Make the query broad enough to retrieve all necessary documents or evidence to support or refute the claim.

5. **Outcome**:
   - A single, well-formed, focused question that directly targets the factual claims made.
   - The query should facilitate retrieval of all gold-standard documents or evidence needed for fact verification.

Example format of a good query:  
"Who starred in [TV series name], who was involved in its development, and which network aired the show?"

This instruction ensures the assistant generates queries that maximize coverage of the claim's factual components and relevance to domains such as entertainment, sports, music, or others, based on the notes provided.
