Task Description:

Given three input fields:

- claim: a factual statement or assertion often involving named entities, relationships, or events.
- notes: a list of textual snippets summarizing evidence or facts relevant to the claim, extracted from various documents.
- context: a list of detailed documents or excerpts providing background information about the entities or topics in the claim.

Your objective is to produce two output fields:

- new_notes: a concise, high-quality subset of the notes that best support or directly address the claim. This subset should include only the most relevant, accurate, and evidential sentences or snippets that substantiate or refute the claim as deduced from the context and notes. Remove redundancy and keep the notes focused on key factual points related to the claim. When there is ambiguous or unclear information (e.g., undefined references like "this group") or negative evidence (e.g., "no evidence found"), include those clarifications to explain why the claim is not fully supported.

- titles: a list of document or entity titles from the context that are most relevant and critical to understanding, verifying, and contextualizing the claim. These are usually proper nouns or named entities mentioned in the context that directly relate to the claim and the new_notes. Titles should correspond to authoritative sources or key entities referenced in the evidence, and cover all core topics involved in the claim.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. Named Entity Normalization & Identification:
   - Identify and preserve key named entities such as personal names, event names, organization or band names, albums, tournaments, or other domain entities relevant to the claim.
   - When entities are ambiguous (i.e., pronouns like "this group"), clarify in new_notes that the identity is unclear if that impacts evidence evaluation.

2. Temporal and Event Consistency:
   - Pay close attention to date inconsistencies or temporal mismatches (e.g., Sochi Olympics took place in 2014, not 2009).
   - When the claim involves times or events, note and include temporal clarifications in new_notes highlighting possible misalignments.

3. Veracity Assessment & Evidence Alignment:
   - Evaluate claims for truthfulness based on the evidence in the context and notes.
   - Include relevant confirming or negating facts, such as "X was signed by Y" or "No evidence supports X signed by Y".
   - Explicitly include instances of absence of evidence when pertinent.

4. Specificity & Conciseness:
   - Summarize and distill notes to the minimal but sufficient set that covers the claim’s key factual assertions and their validation.
   - Remove repetitive or irrelevant information.

5. Domain Examples:
   - Music Domain: correctly capture band names, album names, record labels, roles of individuals (e.g., A&R man, composer, teacher).
   - Sports Domain (curling example): capture qualifying events, team roles (e.g., skip as captain), Olympic events and dates.
   - Classical Music / Artists Domain: capture collaborations, roles (composer, teacher), notable works/events associated.

6. Output Format:
   - new_notes: List of strings capturing essential evidence summaries relevant to the claim.
   - titles: List of strings representing precise document or entity titles from the context underlying the claim.

Overall, your approach should mimic a focused evidence selection and summarization process, combining direct factual verification with clarifying contextual information, tailored to the domain and claim specifics. Present clear, targeted, and factually grounded outputs that enable efficient understanding and verification of the claim by a reader.
