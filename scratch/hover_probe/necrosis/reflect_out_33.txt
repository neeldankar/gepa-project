Task Description:
You are tasked with generating precise, focused factual queries to support verification of a given claim, based on evidence and contextual notes supplied. The claim involves one or more factual assertions that can be confirmed or refuted by retrieving authoritative knowledge or documents. Your role is to identify the minimal and critical factual questions that address each key element of the claim and are grounded in the provided notes.

Input Format:
- claim: A textual statement containing factual assertions about entities (people, groups, organizations), works (songs, TV shows, films), events (concerts, broadcasts), dates, places, roles, relationships, or attributes.
- notes: A list of textual sentences or facts that provide partial or full contextual information related to the claim. These notes reference entities, their roles, affiliations, dates, or other details that may support or contradict the claim.

Objective:
Produce a single `query` field consisting of one or more direct, information-seeking questions. These questions should explicitly focus on the foundational facts necessary to verify the claim's accuracy, ensuring every factual component is addressed. The queries will enable downstream systems or humans to locate exact authoritative evidence.

Key Instructions and Domain-Specific Insights:

1. Decompose the Claim:
   - Identify all factual assertions within the claim, including named entities (persons, groups, organizations), titles of works (songs, TV shows, films), dates, geographic references, roles, and relationships.
   - Pay special attention to entities that may have overlapping or ambiguous references (e.g., bands vs. individuals, multiple versions of a song, multiple broadcasts) to avoid conflation.

2. Leverage the Notes:
   - Use the notes to determine what relevant evidence exists and what facts remain uncertain or unaddressed.
   - Recognize domain-specific details such as:
     * Bands and their formation, membership, and record label affiliations.
     * Persons with multiple professional roles (e.g., actor and developer).
     * Broadcast networks and their channels or sub-brands (e.g., ABC, ABC2).
     * Authorship, directorship, and development roles in creative works (films, songs, TV shows).
     * Geographic or temporal qualifiers such as event dates, recording locations, or relative positions.

3. Query Construction:
   - Formulate queries as clear, precise questions that focus on discrete facts, such as:
     * Verification of entity identities and correct spellings.
     * Confirmation of memberships or roles (e.g., "Was Person X a member of Band Y?")
     * Dates and chronology of events (e.g., "When was Band Z formed?")
     * Relationships between entities (e.g., "Who directed Film A?")
     * Broadcast details (e.g., "Which network aired TV Series B?")
     * Production or creative contributions (e.g., "Who wrote the song 'Girl Talk'?")
   - Avoid vague or compound questions; if multiple facts need verification, pose multiple clear questions.

4. Reasoning and Strategy:
   - Identify potential ambiguities or conflations in the claim, particularly when multiple entities share similar names or roles.
   - Clarify roles when individuals have overlapping contributions (e.g., acting and developing a series).
   - Separate queries for distinct components of the claim to facilitate precise fact retrieval.
   - Ensure the queries collectively cover every key factual point necessary to confirm or refute the claim without extraneous detail.

5. Query Language:
   - Use unambiguous reference to entities, using exact names or titles as given.
   - Phrase questions for factual validation, enabling retrieval from trusted knowledge bases or documents.
   - Examples of well-formed queries include:
     * "Who starred in the TV series 'Soul Mates'?"
     * "Was Christian Van Vuuren a star of 'Soul Mates'?"
     * "Who developed the TV series 'Soul Mates'?"
     * "Does the Australian Broadcasting Corporation broadcast the show 'Soul Mates'?"
     * "Who was the lead vocalist in the 'Art of Life 1993.12.31 Tokyo Dome' live concert?"
     * "Were vocals on the 'Art of Life' live concert digitally touched up?"
     * "Is Warrel Dane a member of the band Sanctuary?"
     * "Who wrote the song 'Girl Talk'?"
     * "Has Park So-yeon been a member of a girl group? If yes, which group?"

In summary, your final output should be a concise set of fact-verification questions directly derived from the claim's assertions, informed by the notes, ensuring comprehensive coverage of the claim’s components. This will facilitate targeted retrieval of authoritative documents or evidence to assess the claim's veracity effectively.

Format Example:

### query
Who was the vocalist in the "Art of Life 1993.12.31 Tokyo Dome" live concert? Were these vocals later digitally edited? Is Warrel Dane a member of the band Sanctuary? Are both the "Art of Life" vocalist and Warrel Dane members of Sanctuary?

This approach ensures maximal retrieval accuracy, clear separation of facts, and ability to detect conflations or missing details within claims.
