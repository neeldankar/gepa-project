You will be given the following inputs: 

- `claim`: A factual or semi-factual statement that may involve multiple entities, relationships, or geographic/historical assertions.
- `notes`: A list of extracted fact-like sentences or partial evidences that relate to the `claim`. These notes can contain repetitive or overlapping information derived from larger context documents.
- `context`: A list of longer textual summaries or excerpts from source documents that provide background or detailed descriptions related to entities or topics mentioned in the `claim` and `notes`.

Your task is to analyze these inputs carefully and produce the following outputs:

1. `new_notes`:  
   - A distilled, concise, and factually accurate list of sentences derived from the `notes` and `context` that directly support or refute the `claim`.  
   - Eliminate redundancy, contradictions, or irrelevant information from the original `notes`.  
   - Include clarifications when necessary to resolve ambiguities in the claim using the context.  
   - The `new_notes` should explicitly address key entities, dates, roles, geographic or historical details relevant to the claim.  
   - The goal is to provide a coherent, evidence-based summary that directly informs whether the claim is supported or contradicted by the given data.

2. `titles`:  
   - A list of relevant document or entity titles (derived from the `context`) that are necessary to understand the claim and the evidence around it.  
   - Include all unique key subjects (persons, places, bands, districts, or any proper nouns relevant to the claim and evidence).  
   - The list should cover all principal entities, and any critical secondary entities who help contextualize or clarify the claim.  
   - Avoid irrelevant or unrelated titles not connected to the claim or evidence.

Additional domain-specific instructions and best practices:

- In cases involving musical artists and bands, carefully distinguish roles (e.g., guitarist vs bass guitarist vs vocalist), band memberships, and collaborations. Use exact name spellings and known band affiliations from the context.
- In cases involving geographic or administrative regions, carefully use accurate place names, historical naming changes, districts vs provinces, and clearly separate similarly named but distinct regions.
- When dealing with historical or biographical data, pay attention to dates, titles, and social or professional roles (e.g., teacher, composer, official posts).
- When the claim compares or relates two or more entities or persons, verify evidence for each entity separately and then jointly address the claim’s assertion or contradiction.
- The reasoning behind your outputs should consider whether the notes and context confirm, deny, or fail to support aspects of the claim, and the distilled `new_notes` should reflect that nuanced assessment.
- The `titles` you select should fully cover the essential source documents or subjects the evidence is drawn from to understand the claim’s validity.

By following this approach, you help ensure clear, precise, and well-supported outputs that serve as strong evidence dossiers for verifying complex multi-entity claims in specialized domains such as music, geography, and history.
