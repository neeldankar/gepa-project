Task Description:  
You are given two input fields: `claim` (a factual or partially factual statement containing named entities, relationships, or events) and `notes` (a list of textual evidences or documents potentially relevant to verifying or contextualizing the claim). Your objective is to generate a concise, precise, and well-scoped `query` that can be used to fact-check, verify, or retrieve missing information supporting or refuting the claim. The query should be formulated as a natural language question or set of questions that directly target the key points or facts stated in the claim by considering and cross-referencing the evidence in `notes`.

Guidelines and Domain-Specific Details:  
1. **Input Fields:**  
   - `claim`: A statement involving entities (e.g., people, organizations, TV shows, bands, geographic locations) often with specific factual assertions (e.g., dates of formation, roles, network affiliations). This statement may contain inaccuracies or unsupported assertions.  
   - `notes`: A set of snippets or sentences extracted from various sources, some of which confirm parts of the claim, others that contradict it or provide additional relevant context. The notes often include:  
     - Named entities involved in the claim (persons, bands, TV shows, organizations).  
     - Factual details like licensing locations, formation years, network broadcast channels, co-starring or development roles, and genre or role types (e.g., actor roles, band formation years).  
     - Conflicting data or clarification about the claim’s specific elements.

2. **Goal of the Query:**  
   - The query should *explicitly* address the key factual relationships claimed (e.g., "Is WCJB-TV licensed to Gainesville, Florida?", "Was Against Me! formed in 1991 or 1997?", "Did Nicholas Boshier co-star and develop Soul Mates?").  
   - The query should incorporate relevant entities and attributes critical for disambiguation or validation, including dates, roles, affiliations, or locations mentioned in the claim and clarifications in the notes.  
   - The query should target verification or disambiguation of contradictory facts revealed by the notes, not just restate the claim verbatim.  
   - If multiple facts or entities are involved in the claim, frame questions that jointly test those relationships.

3. **General Strategy:**  
   - Identify the primary entities and their relationships as stated in the claim.  
   - Compare these with the critical factual points or contradictions raised in the notes.  
   - Formulate a focused question that can be answered by retrieving or verifying specific documents or authoritative data supporting or refuting the claim.  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague queries; be specific and precise to the claim’s factual components.  
   - Include comparison where there is an ambiguity in the claim (e.g., differing formation years, different broadcasters).

4. **Examples of Entities and Relations Commonly Encountered:**  
   - TV series, their starring actors, directors, and broadcasting networks.  
   - Bands and their formation year and hometown.  
   - Licensing locations for TV stations.  
   - Acting roles (e.g., known for negative roles) versus show affiliations.  
   - Co-starring relationships between actors and the TV shows/networks they appear on.

5. **Query Output Format:**  
   - Provide a single or multi-part natural language question encapsulated in one or two sentences.  
   - The query should be structured so that an information retrieval system or a fact-checking process can use it to pull relevant evidence from additional documents or knowledge bases.  

Summary:  
Produce a targeted, context-aware question (`query`) based on the `claim` and augmented by the evidentiary context in `notes`. This query is meant to clarify, verify, or resolve discrepancies in the claim’s facts, particularly regarding key entities, dates, roles, or locations mentioned in the inputs. The focus is on maximizing recall and precision of required supporting documents by explicitly reflecting the factual points contested or asserted in the claim with grounding from the notes.
