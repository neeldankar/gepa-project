Given the input fields:
- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that involves named entities, relationships, or events.
- `notes`: a list of textual evidence snippets or context documents related to the claim.

Your task is to produce an output field:
- `query`: a clear, focused, and explicit question or set of questions aimed at gathering the essential missing information or clarifications needed to verify or investigate the claim based on the `notes`.

Additional detailed guidance:

1. **Purpose:**  
   The goal is to identify gaps, ambiguities, or uncertainties in the given claim that cannot be conclusively determined from the provided notes. The generated query should directly address these uncertainties or target key factual details that remain unclear or unsupported.

2. **Contextual and domain-specific knowledge:**  
   - Claims often reference specific people (e.g., musicians, band members), entities (e.g., record labels, bands), songs, or dates that may have multiple interpretations or require precise identification.  
   - Some claims hinge on specific roles or relationships (e.g., “the writer of the song X,” “the person who signed the band Y,” “the creator of entity Z”) which may be ambiguous without clarification.  
   - The notes may provide partial or conflicting evidence, or mention multiple entities with similar names or roles. The query should be designed to resolve these ambiguities by narrowing down the precise entity or fact referenced.  
   - Geographic, temporal, and membership attributes are often key elements to verify (e.g., origin dates and locations of bands, membership in groups, label signings).

3. **Reasoning and query formulation:**  
   - Your reasoning should identify what exact detail(s) cannot be confirmed from the notes and why.  
   - Formulate the query as a direct question or set of questions to retrieve or confirm these details explicitly.  
   - Prioritize clarity, specificity, and relevance: the query should be answerable and directly linked to verifying the claim’s truth.  
   - When multiple entities or interpretations are possible (e.g., multiple songs with the same title, multiple people named in the notes), highlight that the query should disambiguate which entity is referenced.  
   - Use proper names, titles, and roles drawn from the claim and notes when constructing the query. For example:  
     - “Who is the writer of the song ‘Girl Talk’ referenced in the claim, and were they a member of a girl group?”  
     - “Did Mike Smith, associated with Supergrass Records, ever sign the band Quietdrive?”  
     - “What is the origin date and location of the band Greek Fire? Who is the creator of Constantinople Records, and did they front Smashing Pumpkins?”

4. **Handling partial evidence:**  
   - If notes confirm some aspects of the claim but leave others ambiguous or unsupported, formulate your query to target only the missing or unconfirmed elements.  
   - Avoid asking for information already established in the notes unless it’s to clarify ambiguous references.

5. **Generalization:**  
   - Your approach can be applied to claims involving people, groups, dates, locations, memberships, creative works, or business relationships in the music domain (but applicable broadly).  
   - Focus on identifying and isolating the core factual points that require further evidence to verify the claim.

By following these instructions, you will create queries that effectively facilitate retrieving the most relevant and missing information needed to substantiate or refute the claims based on the provided evidence.
