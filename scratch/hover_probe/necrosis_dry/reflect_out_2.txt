Task Description:

You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. Input Fields:
   - `claim`: A statement or assertion related to specific factual information about people, groups, or entities (e.g., musicians, bands, composers, or other cultural figures). The claim often contains relationships or roles (e.g., who played what instrument, who signed which band, who taught whom).
   - `notes`: A list of textual statements that contain evidence or relevant facts that can support or refute the claim. These notes may be partially redundant or overlapping.
   - `context`: A list of detailed encyclopedia-style descriptions or extended excerpts about the entities or topics referenced in the claim. This context provides authoritative background information, often including precise roles, titles, affiliations, dates, and relationships.

2. Task Objective:
   Using the supplied `notes` and `context`, analyze the claim carefully to:
   a) Verify or refute the key factual assertions made in the claim.
   b) Select and consolidate the most relevant and factually accurate pieces from `notes` that directly support your reasoning related to the claim’s truthfulness.
   c) Identify the relevant authoritative source articles (or "gold documents") from the `context` supporting or negating the claim.

3. Output Fields:
   - `new_notes`: Produce a curated list of factually accurate, non-redundant, and directly relevant notes drawn from the original `notes` input. These should clearly support your reasoning or the claim's (in)accuracy. Remove irrelevant, contradictory without evidence, or unsupported statements.
   - `titles`: Provide a list of titles (entity names or article headings) from the context that are crucial references for assessing the claim’s truthfulness. They represent the authoritative evidence or "gold documents" confirming or denying the claim. These correspond to the most relevant context entries.

4. Important Domain-Specific Considerations:
   - The claims often involve precise domain-specific factual knowledge about music, including roles within bands (e.g., guitarists vs bass guitarists), recording contracts, label affiliations, and biographical details about musicians and composers.
   - The names in the claim and context sometimes involve alternative spellings, diacritics, or nicknames (e.g., “Michael Akerfeldt” vs “Mikael Åkerfeldt”). Carefully resolve these to the correct entities.
   - Distinguish between similar but non-identical labels or roles (e.g., "Supergrass Records" is a label related to the band Supergrass, not to Mike Smith, despite his association with Supergrass).
   - Validate information using both notes and context, prioritizing context as authoritative background.
   - Avoid including documents or notes that do not contribute directly to validating or refuting the claim, even if topically related.
   - The claim may contain compound assertions involving more than one entity; treat all components thoroughly in your reasoning and outputs.

5. Reasoning Strategy (implicit but important for your approach):
   - Read and understand the claim in detail, identifying entities and the factual assertions made.
   - Cross-reference claims against the provided notes and context:
     * Verify the roles, titles, relationships, or affiliations claimed.
     * Identify contradictions or lack of evidence for parts of the claim.
   - Select only the most relevant supporting or refuting notes and context titles that specifically relate to the claim’s key points.
   - Provide a concise but comprehensive reasoning (optional as in the examples but helpful for clarity), demonstrating how evidence from notes and context supports or refutes the claim.

6. Output Format:
   - Return only two fields: `new_notes` (list of selected notes) and `titles` (list of key context article titles).
   - Ensure the `titles` correspond exactly to entities/topics in the `context` that verify or debunk the claim.

Summary:
Your task is to rigorously fact-check complex claims about musicians, bands, and related cultural figures by selecting relevant evidential notes and authoritative sources from detailed contextual data, producing a concise, accurate, and relevant subset of notes and source titles to support your assessment of the claim’s accuracy.
