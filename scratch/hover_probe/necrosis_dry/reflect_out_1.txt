Task: Given a claim (a factual statement that may contain multiple assertions) and a set of supporting or related notes (pieces of information relevant to the claim), produce a precise and focused query (or set of queries) that can be used to verify or refute the factual accuracy of the claim.

Input format:  
- `claim`: A single sentence or short paragraph containing one or more factual assertions.  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets or sentences related to the claim. The notes contain factual information which may confirm, contradict, or partially support various components of the claim.

Goal:  
Formulate one or more clear and specific queries that target the key factual claims or details in the input `claim`. These queries should be constructed to retrieve authoritative evidence (documents, sources, or facts) needed to confirm or deny the truthfulness of each distinct factual component of the claim.

Key Points and Domain-Specific Details:  
- Claims often include multiple facts about people (e.g., band members, creators), dates (formation years), locations (origin cities, licensing towns), relationships (frontman vs. creator roles), or affiliations (broadcasters, record labels).  
- Notes can contain partial, redundant, or somewhat conflicting information, summarizing known facts or highlighting missing details to confirm the claim.  
- The queries should precisely isolate these distinct factual elements, such as:  
  * specific formation years of bands or entities  
  * geographic locations associated with bands or organizations  
  * roles of individuals (e.g., whether a particular person fronts a band or founded a label)  
  * broadcast details, such as which broadcaster airs a show  
  * any other verifiable facts mentioned or implied in the claim

Generalizable Strategy:  
1. Carefully analyze the claim to extract all discrete factual assertions that require verification.  
2. Cross-reference the notes to understand which aspects are confirmed, contradicted, or unknown.  
3. Identify any gaps in the evidence that need to be addressed by querying external sources.  
4. Craft concise and targeted natural language queries for each factual assertion, ensuring coverage of all elements relevant to validating the claim.  
5. Avoid unnecessary broad or ambiguous queries by focusing on the pivotal facts often related to people, dates, locations, and affiliations embedded in the claim.

Examples of effective query construction based on claims and notes:  
- "What year was the punk rock band Against Me! formed?"  
- "Is WCJB-TV licensed to Gainesville, Florida?"  
- "What is the origin year and location of the band Greek Fire?"  
- "Is Billy Corgan the creator of Constantinople Records, and does he front the Smashing Pumpkins?"  
- "Who starred in the TV series Soul Mates alongside Christiaan Van Vuuren?"  
- "Who developed the TV show Soul Mates?"  
- "Does the Australian Broadcasting Corporation air the show Soul Mates?"

The queries serve as focused requests for fact-checking essential claim components, supporting retrieval of all gold evidence documents needed for full validation.

Summary:  
Given a claim with multiple factual assertions and related notes, produce a set of clear, specific factual queries that collectively verify or refute the truth of each claim component, targeting retrieval of the key supporting evidence documents.
