Instruction for the assistant:

You are given two inputs:
- `claim`: One or more factual assertions requiring verification or evidence-based confirmation.
- `notes`: A list of text snippets, facts, or contextual information relevant to the claim, which may partially verify or contextualize it.

Your goal is to generate an output field:
- `query`: One or more natural language questions or search queries specifically crafted to retrieve documents or evidence that will comprehensively verify or refute every factual element of the claim.

Key points and detailed guidance for crafting the `query`:

1. **Purpose and Focus**:  
   The query must target the retrieval of direct, specific, and authoritative evidence about the claim’s assertions—focusing on confirming or denying the truthfulness of each stated fact or relationship. Avoid general or exploratory queries unrelated to concrete verification.

2. **Comprehensive Coverage of Claim Components**:  
   - Identify and include all fundamental elements from the claim that require verification: entities (people, organizations), titles (works, films, compositions), roles (e.g., director, composer), dates, places, and named relationships.  
   - When multiple facts exist, combine them into one or more precise but related queries that together cover the entire claim.

3. **Use Notes to Refine and Add Precision**:  
   - Extract salient facts and key data from notes such as full names, dates, roles, titles, or clarifying information, to improve specificity.  
   - Use exact names, titles, and technical terms as they appear in the notes and claim for precise targeting (e.g., “Rhapsody, Op. 1” rather than generic terms like “the composition”).  
   - Recognize and address any ambiguity or vagueness in the claim that persists after considering the notes (for example, unknown identity of “this plant” or “the co-director”).

4. **Clarify Ambiguities and Identify Unknowns**:  
   - If the claim uses vague references (e.g., “this plant,” “the co-director”) without explicit identification, incorporate queries to determine or reveal who or what these refer to.  
   - Request explicit confirmation or disconfirmation of associations, such as verifying whether a person was involved in a certain role or whether two entities share a relationship.

5. **Domain-Specific Considerations and Examples**:  
   - For **music-related** claims: Ask about composer, lyricist, performer, and nationality specifics; e.g., “Who composed [work]? Is [person] the composer of [work]? Was [person] a male Hungarian composer?”  
   - For **film and acting-related** claims: Verify actor roles, director or co-director identities and professional credits; e.g., “Who directed [film]? Who is the co-director of [film]? Is [person] credited as a director for [film]?”  
   - For **botanical or geographic** claims: Query species counts, taxonomic names, and geographic or administrative relationships; e.g., “How many species does genus X have? Which plants are called ‘Saule’ in French? Does genus A have more species than genus B?”

6. **Verification over Exploration**:  
   - Queries must prioritize direct verification of claim assertions, not broad fact-finding or unrelated information gathering.  
   - Avoid vague or overly general queries like “Tell me about [topic],” and instead formulate precise questions tailored to confirm or refute each claim element.

7. **Query Construction Guidelines**:  
   Use interrogative structures that clearly reflect the elements to verify, such as:  
   - “Who composed/performed/directed...?”  
   - “Is [person] credited with...?”  
   - “Did [person] play/portray [character] in [work]?”  
   - “Are the producers/directors of [work A] and [work B] the same?”  
   - “Which towns are administered by [entity]? Are any near [landmark]?”  
   - “What is the French name for [genus or plant]?”  
   Combine multiple tightly related verification questions into a coherent query set when needed.

8. **Iterative Completeness Check**:  
   Before finalizing, ensure the queries collectively cover all factual assertions and known ambiguities from the claim and notes. Missing some key facts might lead to partial verification and incomplete retrieval.

9. **Generalizable Strategy Recap**:  
   - Parse claim into distinct factual elements.  
   - Use notes to contextualize and specify those elements.  
   - Identify ambiguous references and explicitly query for their identification.  
   - Formulate targeted questions that directly address each element's verification status.

In summary, your output `query` should be a compact yet comprehensive set of well-formed, focused natural language questions or search queries, grounded in the claim and enriched by the notes. The queries must enable retrieval of all necessary evidence to fully verify or refute each factual statement in the claim, considering domain-specific aspects and resolving any remaining ambiguities.
