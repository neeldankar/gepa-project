Task Description:
You will be provided with a `claim` that makes a factual statement or comparison involving specific named entities, concepts, or events, and a set of `notes` that summarize or highlight relevant contextual information extracted from a collection of documents. Your task is to produce a `query` that can be used to retrieve additional relevant documents or evidence to verify the truthfulness of the `claim`.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A natural language sentence stating a fact or making a comparison/assertion about entities, events, or relationships.
- `notes`: A list of short context sentences or facts relevant to the claim, often referencing named entities, their attributes, or relationships, extracted from known documents.

Output:
- `query`: A precise, targeted search query or a combination of phrases designed to effectively retrieve key documents or evidence from the knowledge base. The query should focus on the critical entities, relationships, or facts central to verifying the claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. Carefully analyze the `claim` to identify the main entities (persons, places, organizations, objects), the key relationships or assertions made about them, and any qualifiers or comparisons involved.
2. Review the `notes` to understand what relevant contextual information is already known—such as confirmations or contradictions of entity attributes (e.g., breed status, burial locations, award counts) and their relationships.
3. Identify which specific pieces of evidence or documents are necessary to fully verify the claim, especially those that remain unverified or partially addressed in the notes.
4. Construct a search `query` incorporating:
   - The key named entities directly involved in the claim.
   - The specific attributes, events, or relationships central to the claim verification.
   - Any pertinent disambiguating details (such as nicknames, alternative names, related events/projects).
   - A format that can be understood by a retrieval system to locate focused supporting documents (e.g., concatenated keywords, exact phrase matches, or boolean combinations).
5. The query should be focused enough to retrieve the missing crucial documents or evidence identified in the feedback, without being overly broad.
6. Avoid redundancy; do not include terms or entities already fully confirmed unless needed to discriminate relevant documents.
7. It is acceptable to include multiple related entity names connected to the claim if they help in retrieval of documents that would verify or refute the claim (e.g., both the name of a person and the known affiliated locations or side projects).
8. The query should align with the style of evidence needed for verification, for instance:
   - When verifying entity attributes (e.g., dog breeds) focus on "entity + breed".
   - When verifying locations or relationships, include both entities and location names.
   - When verifying comparative awards or records, include lists or award terms combined with entity names.

Domain-Specific Knowledge Context:
- Entities may include proper names of individuals (artists, athletes, historical figures), animal breeds, locations (cemeteries, parks), musical bands, aliases/nicknames, or events.
- Notes often mention context-specific facts such as recognized breed status, burial cemetery names and their relationship to national park areas, and award histories.
- Claims often require establishing inclusion/exclusion or comparative relationships (both breeds or not, cemetery location within a park, more awards than another artist).
- The importance of linking nicknames or alternative names with canonical entities (e.g., "German Frank Sinatra" = Udo Jürgens) is critical for forming effective queries.
- Side projects or related affiliations (e.g., band side projects) may be pivotal in disambiguating entities.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Map claim entities and relationships to relevant keywords from notes.
- Identify gaps or missing documents from the feedback points.
- Generate concise, targeted queries designed to retrieve those missing documents.
- Use logical combinations of entity names, alternate names, and key attributes or relationships.
- Balance specificity and breadth to ensure maximum relevant retrieval.

Example of an effective query form (from examples):
- "Banana Joe V Tani Kazari" AND "Villanuco de Las Encartaciones" AND "dog breed"
- "George W. Wallace" AND "Golden Gate National Recreation Area" AND "cemetery"
- "Udo Jürgens awards" AND "Nivek Ogre awards"

Your output must be a standalone search query string that directly supports verification of the claim.
