Task Description:

You will be given three input fields:
- `claim`: a textual statement which may contain one or more factual assertions.
- `notes`: a list of textual evidences, extracted snippets, or paraphrases that relate to the claim, possibly containing partial or supporting information.
- `context`: a list of document texts or summaries which may contain relevant detailed information or source data related to the claim.

Your goal is to analyze these inputs to produce two output fields:
- `new_notes`: a condensed and focused list of notes that directly address the claim, consolidating evidence from the provided `notes` and `context`. These should clarify which specific parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unverifiable based on the available information. Avoid redundant or irrelevant notes. Where the claim cannot be verified due to missing information, clearly state this in concise notes.
- `titles`: a succinct list of informative titles or headings that summarize key topics or documents relevant to the claim based on the context and notes. These should reflect important entities, comparative facts, dates, or relationships central to confirming or refuting the claim.

Detailed Guidelines and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Verification and Reasoning**:  
   - Carefully compare the claim against both the `notes` and `context` for evidence. The `notes` often contain extracted or summarized facts, while the `context` may provide broader or detailed source information.  
   - If the claim contains multiple assertions (e.g., "Person A did X. Event Y happened before that."), evaluate each assertion separately and then provide an integrated assessment.  
   - Use geographic, historical, biographical, or product-related facts explicitly present in context or notes to confirm or deny specific parts of the claim.

2. **Handling Missing or Partial Data**:  
   - When relevant information on entities, dates, or details mentioned in the claim is missing from both `notes` and `context`, explicitly state that verification cannot be performed with the current data.  
   - When some parts of the claim are verifiable but others are not, clearly separate those distinctions in the `new_notes`.  
   - Avoid speculative or inferred information not supported by the input.

3. **Consolidation of Notes**:  
   - Reduce redundancy found in the `notes` list by combining multiple entries that state the same fact.  
   - Summarize key facts succinctly and clearly, ideally in one to two sentence statements.  
   - Ensure notes are factual and directly relevant to the claim’s assertions.

4. **Creation of Titles**:  
   - Create short, descriptive titles reflecting the main topics or key facts relevant to the claim (e.g., names of persons involved, titles of works, dates of events, comparative facts).  
   - Titles should help organize or categorize the information you present in `new_notes`.  
   - They do not need to be actual document titles, but should be easily understood topical headings derived from the information provided.

5. **Typical Domains and Entities Encountered**:  
   - Soft drinks and their flavor profiles, including brand names, geographical markets, and product characteristics.  
   - Film and animation industry data such as director biographies, film release dates, voice actors, and film titles.  
   - Geographic comparisons (e.g., country size) and how they relate to individuals’ origins or identities.  
   - Where applicable, scientific or technical contexts (e.g., drug names, chemical compounds) may appear but are often irrelevant to the claim verification.

6. **Generalizable Strategy for Reasoning**:  
   - Identify key entities, dates, and attributes within the claim.  
   - Map these elements to the facts stated in `notes` and corroborate with or expand from `context`.  
   - Explicitly note when an entity mentioned in the claim is absent from the provided data.  
   - For claims involving comparisons (e.g., chronological order, geographic size), check provided data for numeric or qualitative facts enabling such comparisons.  
   - Output clear, non-ambiguous notes and relevant titles to summarize and organize your findings.

Summary:

You are performing a focused fact verification task that integrates multiple fragmented pieces of evidence (notes and context) to validate or refute a claim. Your outputs should be concise, well-structured, and informative: `new_notes` provide a distilled factual summary addressing the claim, while `titles` give an overview or topical indexing of the relevant subject matter. Your instructions emphasize thorough cross-checking, explicit handling of missing data, and organizing information clearly for future reference or downstream tasks.
