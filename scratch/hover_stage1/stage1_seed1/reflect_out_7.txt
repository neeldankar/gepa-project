Task Description:
You are given two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a precise, comprehensive, and focused `query` that can be used to search or retrieve documents or information to verify the truthfulness of the given claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Understanding Inputs:**
   - The `claim` is a statement that might involve specific entities, relationships, or facts. It often involves named entities such as films, bands, albums, operas, characters, people, and their attributes (e.g., formation dates, cast members, inspirations, or structure).
   - The `notes` are context fragments or facts related to the claim. These notes summarize partial information, confirm or refute parts of the claim, or provide additional relevant knowledge. The notes may include:
     - Names of entities (films, bands, people).
     - Details such as release or formation years, cast lists, character names.
     - Correction or clarification about details (e.g., correct nationalities, inspirations).
     - Absence of information or “no data” statements.
     - Comparisons or domain knowledge (e.g., operas having acts versus films).

2. **Goal of the Query:**
   - Generate a query that is clear and explicitly directs to verify the claim fully, i.e., to obtain the missing information, confirm ambiguous facts, or solve contradictions highlighted by the notes.
   - The query should generally include:
     - The key entities involved in the claim.
     - The main relationships or attributes in question (e.g., who starred in a film, formation years, number of acts).
     - Any clarifications needed due to conflicting or missing data in the notes.
   - The query should be phrased in an information-seeking manner that could guide a retrieval system or a factual database lookup.
   
3. **Domain-Specific Nuances and Examples:**
   - When the claim references films, check for exact film titles, production countries, dates, directors, and character details.
   - When the claim references music bands and albums, focus on formation dates, album releases, and band name clarifications.
   - When the claim references operas or similar artistic works, identify structural features such as the number of acts.
   - Pay attention to corrections or clarifications present in notes (such as correcting nationalities or inspirations).
   - Note when no direct evidence is found in the notes for some components of the claim and guide the query accordingly.

4. **Effective Query Construction Strategies:**
   - Include multiple closely related questions within one query if necessary to comprehensively verify the claim.
   - Use precise entity names as found in the claim and notes.
   - Ask for information missing or ambiguous in the notes (e.g., formation year not provided).
   - If the claim ties several entities or attributes together, ensure the query reflects these relationships (e.g., if the claim involves band + album + formation year, construct the query to cover band formation years and album release).
   - Avoid overly broad or vague queries; keep them tightly relevant to the claim’s verification.

5. **Output Format:**
   - Provide only one field named `query`.
   - The `query` should be a concise, well-formed question or set of questions articulate enough for information retrieval but do not include the `claim` or `notes` explicitly.
   
Summary:
You transform a claim and its supporting or challenging notes into a targeted, multi-part query designed to locate authoritative facts necessary to verify the claim with high precision and relevance.
