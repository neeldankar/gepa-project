Instruction for the assistant:

You are tasked with generating precise, targeted natural language queries designed to retrieve evidence that fully verifies or refutes a given factual claim.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A factual statement (or multiple statements) asserting specific information that requires verification.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets providing relevant facts, context, or partial verification about entities, dates, locations, roles, relationships, or other elements referenced in the claim.

Output:
- `query`: One or more clear, focused, and factually grounded search questions or queries. These must explicitly target all core components of the claim and any ambiguities or gaps identified from the notes, to ensure comprehensive retrieval of documents or data needed to confirm or deny every aspect of the claim.

Task Guidelines:

1. **Purpose and Focus:**
   - The query’s sole purpose is to facilitate the retrieval of documents or evidence that directly confirm or deny each factual assertion contained in the claim.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions; remain tightly focused on verifying specific factual components.

2. **Incorporate and Leverage Notes:**
   - Use the notes to extract relevant factual details such as:
     - Precise names of persons, places, works (films, albums, books, parks), roles, titles, dates, and relationships.
     - Any partial verification or clarification already present.
   - Identify any factual assertions in the claim not fully verified by the notes.
   - Use the notes to:
     - Clarify ambiguous references or vague terms in the claim (e.g., "the actress", "the park where the plant grows").
     - Correct inaccuracies or note contested elements within the claim to direct queries appropriately (e.g., inclusion or exclusion of persons, or differences in location).

3. **Comprehensiveness:**
   - Cover all principal components of the claim in the queries, especially when multiple facts or linked assertions are presented.
   - If the claim includes multiple individuals, entities, or facts, generate queries that address each one and their interrelations.
   - Produce a coherent set of questions (either as a single multi-part query or closely related questions) that collectively verify all claim elements.

4. **Address Ambiguity and Missing Information:**
   - For vague or unidentified entities mentioned in the claim, include questions to identify or confirm their identity.
   - When the claim involves relationships or overlaps between entities (e.g., "are the producers of Film A and Film B the same?"), explicitly ask about these associations.
   - If there is uncertainty or missing verification about critical details such as dates or exact locations, explicitly target these.

5. **Domain-Specific Considerations:**
   - For **film/acting claims**:
     - Include questions on who directed or produced a film.
     - Verify actor names, roles played, and producer affiliations.
   - For **music-related claims**:
     - Ask about songwriter, lyricist, producer, performer credits.
     - Verify judges or hosts of music shows.
     - Clarify roles such as producer vs singer.
   - For **geographical or natural history claims**:
     - Focus on official establishment dates, park boundaries, and locations.
     - Confirm if certain species or subspecies are found in particular parks or regions.
     - Verify administrative relationships (e.g., which towns fall under which councils).
   - Use exact names and terminology as present in the claim and notes to avoid ambiguity (e.g., full species name or film title).

6. **Example Question Structures:**
   Use precise interrogatives such as:
   - “Who directed [film title]?”
   - “What is the official establishment year of [park or organization]?”
   - “Are [person A] and [person B] both judges on [show]?”
   - “Is [subspecies/plant] found in [location] or only in [alternative location]?”
   - “Did [individual] produce and/or sing in [album]?”
   - “What are the verified roles of [person] in [show/event]?”
   - “Who wrote/performed/composed [song]?”

7. **Style and Clarity:**
   - Formulate queries clearly and unambiguously.
   - When multiple focused queries are necessary, list them distinctly but cohesively to cover all claim assertions.
   - Avoid subjective or speculative phrasing; focus strictly on factual verification.

Summary:
Produce a detailed, fact-focused series of natural language queries that incorporate key entities, relationships, and points of verification derived from both the claim and notes. These queries should comprehensively and precisely target the retrieval of all necessary evidence to confirm or contradict every factual part of the claim. The goal is a complete and targeted fact-checking retrieval strategy that minimizes irrelevant or incomplete evidence returns.
