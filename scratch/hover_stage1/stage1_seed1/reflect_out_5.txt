Task Description:

You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to generate an appropriate `query` that can be used to validate the claim by retrieving relevant factual information. 

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Understanding Inputs:**
   - `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that needs to be verified or investigated.
   - `notes`: A list of sentences or facts extracted from relevant documents. These notes provide supporting, contradicting, or contextual information about the claim.

2. **Output Requirement:**
   - Produce a clear, specific, and concise `query` that encapsulates the key factual question(s) raised by the claim.
   - The query should be phrased in a way that when answered, it helps verify the truthfulness or validity of the claim.
   - If necessary, the query can be multipart or clarifying, if the claim includes multiple related facts or a compound assertion that requires deeper investigation or disambiguation.

3. **Incorporate Domain-Specific Details and Context:**
   - Include key entities mentioned in the claim that appear in the notes, such as names of people, places, events, titles, dates, organizations, or other specific identifiers.
   - Incorporate temporal, geographic, or organizational context that is relevant to the claim (e.g., dates of service, locations of events, album release years, director names).
   - Reference the type of entity involved (e.g., album, regiment, sports team, stadium) to contextualize the query appropriately.

4. **Use Notes to Refine the Query:**
   - Use the facts present in the provided notes to confirm which entities or details are central, so the query precisely focuses on verifying the essential parts of the claim.
   - If the claim contains inaccurate or conflicting information that appears in the notes, phrase the query to explicitly test those points for clarity.
   - The query should be designed to retrieve the gold documents or evidence that confirm or refute the claim.

5. **General Strategy for Query Construction:**
   - Identify the core factual assertions in the claim.
   - Frame the query as a natural language question that asks whether these assertions hold true, e.g., "Did X happen?" / "Is X true?" / "Where/how did X occur?"
   - When multiple key points exist, consider forming compound or clarifying queries that help disambiguate or cover all fact-checking bases.
   - Avoid overly broad or vague queries; be as targeted as possible according to the claim and notes.

Example pattern (based on given exemplars):
- For claims about historical events linked to a film or person: "Did [entity], featured in [film or context], begin/exist/do X at time Y?"
- For claims about albums and songs by musicians: "Did [musician/artist] release [album] that includes a cover/performance of [song]?"
- For claims about venues, sports teams, and events: "Did [event] take place at [venue] located in [place], and is that venue currently used by [team/organization]?"

This task requires careful attention to detail and a factually precise output that can serve as a focused query to retrieve or verify evidence related to the original claim.
