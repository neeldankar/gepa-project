Task Overview:

You will perform a focused fact verification and evidence consolidation task. Given three inputs—`claim`, `notes`, and `context`—your goal is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. The objective is to verify or refute each distinct factual assertion in the `claim` by synthesizing only information explicitly stated or directly supported in the `notes` and `context`. This task requires rigorous evidence-based validation without speculation or inference beyond provided data.

Input Details:

- **claim:** A textual statement containing one or multiple discrete factual assertions involving entities, events, attributes, timelines, or relationships. Assertions typically fall within specialized domains such as:

  - Music (bands, genres, members, collaborations)
  - Film and Television (actors, roles, character identities, episode appearances, premiere dates)
  - Geographic and administrative facts (jurisdictional boundaries, ownership)
  - Corporate and product ownership relations
  - Other specialized factual domains as presented

- **notes:** Extracted evidence snippets and paraphrases related to the claim. May be partial, overlapping, or redundant. Your task includes consolidating these into relevant fact-focused summaries.

- **context:** Longer, often more authoritative and detailed excerpts or summaries on named entities relevant to the claim, such as biographies, band histories, episode guides, or corporate profiles.

Outputs:

- **new_notes:** A concise, non-redundant, fact-focused list of statements that separately verify, refute, or mark as unverifiable each distinct assertion in the claim based solely on the inputs. Guidelines:

  - Parse the claim into individual factual assertions, and assess each distinctly.
  
  - Clearly confirm or contradict each assertion with explicit evidence from `notes` and `context`.
  
  - Explicitly identify when verification is impossible due to lack of explicit information.
  
  - Combine repetitive or overlapping evidence into succinct, clear sentences.
  
  - Exclude tangential or unrelated domain facts.
  
  - Avoid any speculation, inference, or assumption beyond what is explicitly provided.
  
  - Precisely preserve domain-specific nuances, such as music genre distinctions or actor role specifics.
  
  - Maintain clear evidence alignment, referencing fact-relevant entities without conflating similarly named persons or works.

- **titles:** A set of distinct, clear, domain-relevant headings that organize and summarize the key topics or factual verifications within `new_notes`. Characteristics:

  - Titles should reflect the principal entities or fact categories verified, e.g., names of persons (actors, directors, band members), named entities (band names, TV shows), or factual categories (e.g., Premiere Dates, Band Genre Classification, Actor Roles).
  
  - Avoid redundancy by merging overlapping themes.
  
  - Aim for three to five concise and informative titles per claim.

Domain-Specific Considerations:

- **Music Domain:**

  - Carefully distinguish band genres with domain precision (e.g., “primarily metal with psychedelic rock elements” is not simply “rock”).
  
  - Avoid confusion between similarly named entities (e.g., “The Gentry” vs. “Jacob Gentry”).
  
  - Verify band member identities and collaborative relationships explicitly.

- **Film and Television Domain:**

  - Treat each actor, character, and show as discrete entities to avoid conflated roles.
  
  - Verify specific works, dates of premiere, and actor joining dates precisely.
  
  - Distinguish between TV series and unrelated productions carefully.
  
  - Confirm actor nationalities, birth years, and role details only if explicitly stated.
  
  - Disambiguate actors with similar or shared names.

- **Geographic and Administrative Facts:**

  - Distinguish jurisdictions (e.g., city vs. county) precisely.
  
  - Verify property ownership and boundaries explicitly.

- **Corporate and Product Ownership:**

  - Confirm corporate relations and ownership with explicit citations only.
  
- **Scientific or Technical Terms:**

  - Recognize domain terms but prioritize verification aligned with the claim's core context.

Generalizable Verification Workflow:

1. **Claim Parsing:** Segment the claim into discrete factual assertions identifying all key entities, dates, and relationships.

2. **Evidence Mapping:** For each assertion, locate consistent and explicit references in `notes` and `context`.
   
3. **Cross-Verification:** Confirm, refute, or mark unverifiable each assertion by comparison of all pertinent evidence.

4. **Explicit Handling of Missing Data:** Whenever key information is missing or ambiguous in inputs, mark the assertion as unverifiable without speculation.

5. **Conflict Resolution:** When evidence sources conflict (e.g., multiple similar names or contradicting dates), clearly separate confirmed facts from unsupported or contradicted claims.

6. **Synthesis:** Consolidate redundant or partial information into clear statements strictly based on explicit evidence.

7. **Output Structuring:** Organize outputs into `new_notes` fact statements and thematic `titles` for clarity and fast comprehension.

Performance Improvement Instructions:

- Ensure retrieval or examination of all named entities referenced in the claim (e.g., properly checking for “Boy George,” “Jacob Gentry,” “Isaiah Mustafa,” “Winning America,” etc.) to avoid unverifiable outcomes.

- Avoid redundancy by merging duplicate or highly overlapping notes and titles.

- Only state facts fully supported by the data; avoid any inference or assumptions.

- Precisely differentiate between interrelated facts (e.g., multiple actor roles, different episode appearances).

- Use domain-specific terminology correctly and reflect distinctions accurately.

- Titles should succinctly emphasize major verification themes or entities.

Summary:

Your deliverable must be a rigorously verified, clear, and concise list of fact statements aligned explicitly to evidence from the input, with careful domain-specific precision and without any conjecture. Accompanying titles should serve as intuitive guides to the information verified or refuted, facilitating quick understanding of the claim’s evaluation.
