You will be provided with three inputs:

1. `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that needs to be evaluated.
2. `notes`: a list of extracted evidential statements relevant to the claim. These are raw pieces of information that may support, contradict, or be unrelated to the claim.
3. `context`: a list of documents, entities, or background information that contain detailed, authoritative knowledge relevant to assessing the claim.

Your task is to produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`: a distilled, concise set of key evidential facts directly supporting or refuting the claim, synthesized from both the `notes` and `context`. These must:  
   - Focus narrowly on facts that clearly substantiate or contradict the claim.  
   - Exclude extraneous, redundant, or unrelated information.  
   - Include critical temporal (dates, event order), geographical (locations, jurisdictions), relational (roles, affiliations), and descriptive details essential for accurate evaluation.  
   - Present facts in precise, unambiguous language suitable for fact verification.  
   - Represent a synthesis and filtering of all evidence, not a verbatim copy of inputs.  

2. `titles`: a list of authoritative and relevant document or entity titles from the `context` that:  
   - Contain the evidence cited in `new_notes`.  
   - Cover essential entities (people, organizations, events, works) central to understanding or verifying the claim.  
   - Are comprehensive yet focused; include all crucial references but avoid irrelevant material.  

**Key domain-specific and task-specific considerations:**

- Claims often hinge on subtle factual distinctions such as:  
  - The precise composition or membership of organizations (e.g., specific schools within a school district).  
  - Temporal sequencing such as event or release dates, championship years, or tenure periods.  
  - Jurisdictional and geographical nuances (e.g., whether a place is a hamlet, a census-designated place, or part of a city or county).  
  - Attribution of creative or professional roles (e.g., who directed, composed, wrote, or played a role in a film or event).  
  - Correct identification of persons especially where multiple individuals share similar names or nicknames (e.g., distinguishing between several "Nathaniel" or "Nate" sports players in different disciplines).  
  - Clarifying relationships such as sequel, remake, or distinct events/entities with similar names.

- Evidence should be cross-checked for consistency and relevance: temporal details must align correctly; geographical designations must be precise; named entities must be verified not confused with similarly named unrelated entities.

- Use reasoning that explicitly compares timelines, roles, and geographic details to confirm or refute claims rigorously.

- Avoid restating notes or context verbatim; instead, synthesize by combining and filtering relevant facts into a coherent, evidence-based summary suitable for a human fact-checker.

- The goal is to produce a clear evidential summary (`new_notes`) and an authoritative citation list (`titles`) that together enable confident validation or refutation of the claim.

**Example strategies to apply:**

- When multiple similar names or entities appear, clarify which are relevant and which are not, ensuring no conflation.

- When claims involve events (e.g., sports tournaments, film releases), verify exact dates, outcomes, and participants to verify claim accuracy.

- When organizations or groups are involved (e.g., school districts, musical groups), list exact, official members or components as evidence.

- For geographic references, use exact place types and jurisdictions as recorded in authoritative context (e.g., distinguishing New Hyde Park CDP vs. Nassau County at large).

**Summary:**  

Synthesize and filter to create precise, factually accurate evidence that directly addresses the claim, identify authoritative sources from the context that support these facts, and communicate this in a clear, unambiguous manner suitable for thorough fact verification.
