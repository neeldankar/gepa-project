You will be given two fields as input:  
- `claim`: a complex factual statement that may consist of multiple parts concerning people, events, entities, or relationships.  
- `notes`: a list of sentences containing extracted relevant information that may or may not confirm or refute parts of the claim.

Your task is to produce a `query` field that formulates one or more clear, precise questions designed to retrieve evidence needed to verify the entire claim. The queries you generate will be used to search for documents or data to confirm or contradict the claim.

When generating the `query`, keep the following in mind:

1. **Decompose multi-part claims:** The input claim often contains multiple intertwined assertions (e.g., about a person’s identity, dates, affiliations, events, relationships). Identify these distinct factual components and phrase queries that address each part explicitly, either in a single combined query or as logically connected questions.

2. **Clarify ambiguous references:** If the claim mentions entities with possible name variants, ambiguous film or book titles, or unclear roles (e.g., “the writer” or “the actress”), your query should seek to resolve this ambiguity by asking for the relevant identity or attributes.

3. **Cross-validate names, dates, and affiliations:** Many claims involve verifying that a person with a specific birthdate held a position on a certain date, or that a political party is affiliated with a youth organization, or that a film’s screenplay was based on a novel. Include these factual checks explicitly in the query.

4. **Cover all claim parts:** Make sure your query encompasses all aspects of the claim; partial queries that omit important components lead to incomplete retrieval.

5. **Use concise, natural language:** Frame your queries as clear, direct questions suitable for retrieving documents or database entries, rather than verbose descriptions or statements.

6. **Incorporate domain-specific knowledge patterns observed:**  
   - For film claims, verify film titles correctly (distinguish similar or similar-sounding titles), screenplay bases, and notable contributors (writers, directors).  
   - For literature or awards, confirm recipients and their awards.  
   - For political claims, verify identities, dates of leadership changes, and organizational affiliations.  
   - For celebrity or biographical claims, explicitly query for debut works, director nationalities, and starring roles.

7. **Use conjunctions prudently:** If two or more factual elements are related in the claim, combine related queries with “and” or “also” to streamline retrieval, unless the elements are independent and better queried separately.

The ultimate goal is to produce queries that retrieve all necessary supporting documents or data sources to accurately assess the claim’s truthfulness.

Example guidance:  
- Given a claim referencing a politician’s birth date, party leadership date, and youth organization affiliation, the query should ask:  
  “Did [Politician Name], born [birth date], become leader of [Party] on [date], and is [Youth Organization] affiliated with [Party]?”  

- Given a claim about an actress’s debut and a film she starred in:  
  “Who is the actress who starred in [Film Name], and was her debut film directed by an American director?”

- Given a claim about a screenplay:  
  “Was the screenplay of the film [Exact Film Title] based on a novel? Also, have [Writer Name] and [Other Person] both won a Nobel Prize in Literature?”

Focus on generating precise, comprehensive queries that reduce ambiguity and enable retrieval of all relevant documents covering every claim component.
