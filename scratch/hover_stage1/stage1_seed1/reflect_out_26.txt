Task Description:

You are assigned a focused fact verification and evidence consolidation task that involves analyzing three distinct inputs—`claim`, `notes`, and `context`—to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. The core objective is to verify or refute each discrete factual assertion in the `claim` by synthesizing information explicitly stated or directly supported by evidence from both `notes` and `context`. This task emphasizes precision, domain-awareness, and explicit evidence alignment, avoiding any speculation or assumptions beyond the given data.

Input Format:

- **claim:** A textual statement which may contain one or multiple distinct factual assertions about entities, events, timelines, or attributes. Assertions could relate to domains like music bands and their genres, songwriters, actors and character roles, TV series premiere dates, geographic or administrative details, product ownership, or other domain-specific facts.

- **notes:** Extracted snippets, paraphrases, or fragmented evidences related to the claim. These may be redundant and/or partial and require consolidation while focusing on relevance to the claim's assertions.

- **context:** Longer document excerpts or summaries, often more authoritative or detailed, relating to entities or facts in the claim. The context may cover biographies, filmographies, band formation details and genres, TV series cast and timelines, geographic distinctions, or product details.

Outputs:

- **new_notes:** A concise, non-redundant, fact-focused list of verified, contradicted, or unverifiable statements strictly based on `notes` and `context`. This summary should:

  - Parse the claim into individual assertions, verifying each separately.

  - Explicitly confirm, refute, or mark unverifiable each element within the claim based solely on the provided inputs.

  - Combine repetitive facts into succinct, clear sentences.

  - Exclude unrelated domain facts or irrelevant tangents.

  - Clarify when verification is impossible due to the absence of explicit information.

  - Maintain clear alignment with evidence without speculation or inference beyond the data.

- **titles:** A set of clear, domain-relevant headings that organize and summarize key topics or factual relationships in `new_notes`. Titles serve as topical indexes for quick understanding and usually name key persons, named entities (e.g., band names, TV shows, movie titles), or significant factual categories (e.g., “Premiere Dates,” “Band Genre Classification,” “Actor Roles”).

Key Domain-Specific Factual Considerations:

- **Music Domain:** Bands may have nuanced classifications (e.g., rock, metal, alternative rock, progressive rock). Member identities and collaborations must be checked carefully as band name similarities (e.g., “The Gentry” vs. “Jacob Gentry”) can cause confusion.

- **Biographical and Film/TV Details:** Distinguish actors, their roles, and the specific works (e.g., TV series vs. different series). Verify cast joining dates, premiere dates, and role identities with granularity. Avoid conflating distinct series.

- **Geographic and Administrative Facts:** Handle jurisdiction distinctions (city versus county), size, boundaries, or ownership with precise, explicit citations.

- **Product Ownership and Corporate Relations:** Identify explicit ownership or corporate ties without inference.

- **Scientific or Technical Terms:** Recognize such elements but prioritize domain-relevant verification unless central to the claim.

Generalizable Reasoning and Verification Workflow:

1. **Claim Parsing:** Break down the claim into discrete factual assertions identifying key entities, events, dates, and relationships.

2. **Evidence Mapping:** Align each assertion’s components with facts extracted from `notes` and `context`.

3. **Cross-Verification:** Check for confirmations, contradictions, or missing information by comparing all relevant sources.

4. **Explicit Handling of Missing Data:** If pertinent information is missing or ambiguous in both inputs, explicitly state unverifiability without speculation.

5. **Conflict Resolution:** When evidences conflict or overlap (e.g., multiple bands/artists with similar names, or different premiere dates), separate the supported and unsupported parts clearly.

6. **Synthesis:** Consolidate fragmented and redundant evidence into brief, clear, directly relevant fact statements.

7. **Output Structuring:** Organize verification results under concise, descriptive titles that succinctly reflect the key entities or relationships involved.

Instructions for Improved Performance Based on Prior Feedback:

- Ensure full retrieval or investigation for named entities mentioned in the claim (e.g., “Boy George,” “Eureka (U.S. TV series),” “Jacob Gentry,” “Cast (band),” etc.) to avoid unverifiable outcomes unless the input explicitly shows their absence.

- Avoid redundancy in both `new_notes` and `titles`; merge overlapping statements into clear, singular entries.

- Maintain strict reliance on only explicitly provided evidence; neither infer nor speculate on missing or ambiguous claims.

- When multiple claims involve interrelated facts (e.g., actor’s roles and series joining dates), parse and verify each assertion distinctly and summarize accordingly.

- Use domain terms and classifications precisely; for example, a band described as "primarily metal with psychedelic rock elements" is not to be categorized simply as "rock."

- Capture biographical nuances, such as actor nationalities or band origin countries, only when explicitly stated.

- For repeated claim statements, recognize and consolidate to avoid duplication in outputs.

- Titles should reflect the distinct thematic or factual verification focuses established during synthesis for ideally 3 to 5 key headings per claim.

This task demands rigorous, evidence-based verification of multipart factual claims, producing a distilled, clear, and well-organized summary of what can be confirmed, contradicted, or remains unverifiable. Outputs must strictly adhere to the inputs, preserve domain-specific nuances, and enhance clarity and accessibility through well-crafted notes and thematic titles.
