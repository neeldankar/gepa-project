Task Description:

You will perform a precise and focused fact verification and evidence consolidation task centered around three distinct textual inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your objective is to evaluate the factual accuracy of the multi-faceted `claim` by analyzing relevant evidences and detailed information found in `notes` and `context`. You must produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

---

Inputs:

- **claim**: This is a textual statement that may contain multiple discrete factual assertions relating to persons, events, dates, places, entities, or products that need to be verified or refuted. The claim’s assertions may involve biographical details, geographic or jurisdictional facts, product and brand ownership, filmographies, animation productions, league memberships, or comparative historical data.

- **notes**: A set of fragmented snippets, paraphrased facts, partial evidences, or extracted references relevant to the claim. These may be redundant, overlapping, or incomplete and often summarize or highlight salient points tied to the claim. You must synthesize these carefully, detecting repeated or contradictory information.

- **context**: Longer textual extracts or document summaries containing authoritative or background information potentially relevant to entities or events mentioned in the claim. This often includes detailed biographies, production notes, league and team history, sponsor relationships, or geographic distinctions.

---

Goals and Verification Strategy:

1. **Decompose the Claim**  
   - Parse the claim into individual factual components. Each distinct assertion (e.g., a person’s nationality, film role, a team founding date, league membership, sponsorship status) must be considered separately.

2. **Evidence Mapping & Cross-Verification**  
   - For every claim component:
     - Search both `notes` and `context` for explicit, directly relevant facts.
     - Confirm if there is supporting evidence, explicit contradiction, or absence of data.
   - Avoid assumptions or inferred connections without explicit evidence.
   - When multiple entities or overlapping or competing facts exist, disentangle them to assess each component precisely.

3. **Handling Absence or Conflicts of Evidence**  
   - If the claim includes an entity or fact not found in either `notes` or `context`, state clearly that verification is not possible.
   - If some parts of the claim are supported and others are not, clearly separate these in `new_notes`.
   - Never speculate beyond data given or attempt to fill gaps with external knowledge not present in inputs.

4. **Domain-Specific Factual Areas to Consider**  
   - **Film and Animation**: Voice actors’ nationalities, character names, animated film release years, production credits, and film sponsorship or adaptation details. Distinguish singing voices versus speaking voices, and identify voice actors’ nationality or associated productions.
   - **Sports Teams and Leagues**: Founding years, team histories, league memberships, sponsor relationships, player tenure and managerial careers, especially in baseball franchises (e.g., Montreal Expos, Tampa Bay Devil Rays) or football clubs (e.g., Ellesmere Port teams). Distinguish Canadian versus US teams, league names, divisions, and sponsorship status.
   - **Geographic and Jurisdictional Details**: City versus county distinctions, league regional coverage, and club home locations.
   - **Product and Brand Facts**: Ownership, market presence, and corporate relationships where applicable.

---

Output Formatting and Requirements:

- **new_notes**:  
  - Synthesize relevant facts into clear, concise, factually accurate statements that directly address each claim assertion’s verification status (confirmed, contradicted, unverifiable).
  - Remove redundancy by merging multiple notes saying the same or similar facts.
  - Explicitly state when a claim component cannot be verified due to lack of data.
  - Avoid speculation or introduction of unrelated or tangential information.
  - Maintain textual clarity with one or two well-formed sentences per fact.

- **titles**:  
  - Create a set of brief, descriptive headings summarizing the main factual groupings or topics identified during verification.
  - Titles should reflect the core entities, events, or relationships involved (e.g., “Nala Voice Actors in The Lion King (1994)”, “Montreal Expos Founding and History”, “West Cheshire League Sponsorship”).
  - Use titles to organize and index key verification themes to facilitate understanding.

---

Generalizable Reasoning and Workflow:

- Begin by dissecting the claim into individual assertions.
- Cross-reference `notes` and `context` to collect factual data for each assertion.
- Reconcile contradictory facts and identify unsupported components.
- Produce clear, minimal, and evidence-aligned `new_notes` focused purely on verification.
- Generate meaningful, domain-relevant `titles` representing the topic clusters of gathered facts.
- Explicitly distinguish verified, contradicted, and unverifiable claim components during consolidation.
- Refrain from external knowledge or assumptions; rely solely on the provided inputs.

---

Additional Context for Effective Verification (Domain-Specific Details):

- Voice actor nationalities in specific animations (e.g., The Lion King (1994)) are critical to verify claims about actors’ origins and roles.
- Teams' founding dates (e.g., Montreal Expos in 1969) and player/manager career timelines must be supported explicitly.
- League affiliations and sponsorship (e.g., Football Conference vs. West Cheshire League, principal sponsors like Carlsberg) require exact matching against stated facts.
- Distinguish between similarly named entities (e.g., Ford Motors F.C. vs. Vauxhall Motors F.C.) and their respective league memberships.
- For movies, pay attention to distinctions between speaking voices and singing voices for characters, as these often have different actors.
- Sponsorship claims about leagues or competitions demand concrete evidence; avoid conflating league sponsors with league names or participants.
- Historical timelines and geographic jurisdictions should be handled strictly by evidence presented, without extrapolation.

---

Summary:

This task requires meticulous, evidence-focused fact-checking and synthesis of fragmented and overlapping information, producing a precise dossier assessing each claim component’s validity grounded strictly within the provided documents. Your outputs will offer a distilled, authoritative summary with clear topical categorizations, serving as a reliable verification reference.
