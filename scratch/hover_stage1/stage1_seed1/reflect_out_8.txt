Task Description:

You will be given three inputs:
- `claim`: A statement containing one or more factual assertions to verify.
- `notes`: A list of text snippets, extracted evidences, or paraphrases that relate to the claim; these may be partial, redundant, or overlapping.
- `context`: A list of document texts or summaries providing broader or detailed background, source data, or supporting/refuting information related to the claim.

Your goal is to examine the claim against the notes and context, then produce:

1. `new_notes`: A distilled and focused collection of fact-based notes that directly support, contradict, or indicate unverifiability of each part of the claim, based on the combined evidence of input notes and context. These notes should:
   - Explicitly address individual factual assertions within the claim, especially when the claim has multiple parts.
   - Combine redundant or repetitive information into concise summary statements.
   - Clearly distinguish verifiable facts from those for which no corroborating information exists.
   - Avoid speculation or inferred conclusions unsupported by the given data.
   - Clearly state when verification is impossible due to missing or incomplete information.
   - Focus on factual accuracy, relevance, and clarity to ensure each note directly relates to verifying the claim.

2. `titles`: A succinct set of descriptive headings or topics summarizing the key entities, events, dates, and relationships central to verifying the claim. Titles should:
   - Reflect main subjects derived from the notes and context (e.g., persons, films, geographic regions, scientific terms).
   - Summarize thematic clusters relevant to the claim verification.
   - Aid in organizing and understanding the evidence and reasoning.
   - Be short, informative, and easily comprehensible, not necessarily actual document titles.

Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Film Industry: Pay attention to film titles, release years, composer and director identities, actor involvement, and the chronology of works.
- Geography/Biology: Note native habitats, geographic comparisons (e.g. size, climate), species distributions, and environmental classifications.
- Historical Contexts: Relate persons, events, and productions to known dates and collaboration relationships.
- Soft Drinks and Consumer Products: Flavor profiles, brands, and geographic market information.
- When scientific or technical items appear, treat them as background unless directly relevant to the claim.

Verification Strategy:
- Break down the claim into discrete factual assertions.
- For each assertion, cross-reference notes and context for explicit supporting or contradicting evidence.
- Use specific dates, names, locations, and other concrete facts to confirm or deny each assertion.
- Identify gaps where no data is available and explicitly mark unverifiable points.
- When multiple notes state the same fact in different wording, condense into a single clear note.
- Avoid introducing external knowledge or speculation not present in inputs.

Output Quality:
- `new_notes` should be clear, non-redundant, and aligned exactly with the claim's content.
- `titles` should facilitate quick understanding of the core verification elements.
- If partial verification is possible, distinguish verified parts from unverifiable ones clearly.
- Maintain a neutral tone without bias toward confirming or denying unless supported.

This method ensures thorough, precise fact verification by integrating fragmented evidence into coherent summaries and thematic pointers, suited for domains including films, biographies, geography, and related factual areas.
