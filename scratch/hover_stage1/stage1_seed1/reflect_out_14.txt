Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: a `claim` and a list of evidence `notes`. Your goal is to generate a precise, targeted `query` (research question) that, when searched or queried against a corpus, will retrieve all relevant documents or evidence necessary to verify the truthfulness of the claim using the information in the notes.

Input Format:
- `claim`: a factual statement that requires validation.
- `notes`: a list of textual evidence snippets related to the entities, dates, roles, events, or facts mentioned in the claim.

Output Required:
- `query`: a concise, clear, and fact-focused interrogative sentence formulated as a research question. This question should explicitly encapsulate the core factual element(s) to be confirmed or refuted from the claim, optimized to maximize retrieval of all pertinent documents.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. Key Entity and Temporal Extraction:
   - Identify all key entities in the claim (e.g., people, places, companies, artworks, events).
   - Extract associated temporal details (dates, years, historical periods) relevant to these entities or their activities.
   - Examples:
     - For people: birth and death dates, periods of activity.
     - For artworks or media: release or premiere dates.
     - For companies or organizations: headquarters location, founding dates.
   
2. Contextual Attribute Linking:
   - Leverage the notes to connect entities with critical attributes such as:
     - Roles and titles (e.g., "director Francis Ford Coppola", "composer Jean-Philippe Rameau").
     - Locations or origins (e.g., "M7 Aerospace LP headquartered at San Antonio International Airport").
     - Temporal overlaps (e.g., overlapping lifespans or active years).
     - Collaborative or cast relationships (e.g., co-stars, composers, or producers).
   - Including these details increases the precision of information retrieval.

3. Capturing the Core Fact to Verify:
   - Parse the claim to identify the precise factual relationship or attribute to verify, such as:
     - Whether two or more entities shared the same timeframe or event.
     - Whether a person held a specific role or title.
     - Authorship, composition, or creation (e.g., who composed a work).
     - Geographical co-location or organizational affiliations.
     - Correct cast or production credits.
   - Your query must explicitly reference this core fact in a way that directly tests the truthfulness of the claim.

4. Maximizing Retrieval Precision and Coverage:
   - Ensure the query mentions all relevant key entities and incorporates significant relevant attributes identified from the notes.
   - Be specific enough to include all possible gold documents or evidence in the retrieval.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries that risk missing key documents.
   - For example, mention multiple creators, alternative titles, or detailed descriptors as needed.

5. Clarity, Brevity, and Completeness:
   - Formulate the query as a direct, focused factual question, succinct but complete.
   - Avoid redundant wording or excessive verbosity but do not omit any element crucial for accurate retrieval.
   - Use question formulations like "Did X do Y?", "Was X the author/composer of Z?", "Did entity A and entity B overlap in time?", or "Did event A occur at location B on date C?"

6. Generalizable Query Construction Strategy:
   a) Analyze the claim to establish the main fact being verified.
   b) Identify all entities and temporal details involved.
   c) Link each entity to known attributes or roles mentioned in the notes.
   d) Formulate a question that explicitly asks for verification of the factual relationship or attribute combining these components.
   e) Include multiple entities, roles, and dates if needed to ensure all necessary evidence is retrievable.

Additional Domain-Specific Insights from Examples:

- When verifying casting claims in films, include actor names, film titles, production companies, release years, and genre to capture all relevant documents.
- When verifying literary or historical claims involving categories like “poet-diplomat” or award winners, explicitly mention the category, the award (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), the nationality if relevant, and additional involved persons.
- When verifying music or media claims, specify album names, artists/bands, song titles, episode titles, air dates, and broadcasting schedules to contextualize the factual query.
- If the claim involves temporal co-occurrence (e.g., two people alive simultaneously), ask precisely if their lifespans or active periods overlapped.
- If verifying authorship or inclusion (e.g., who starred in which film), explicitly seek confirmation of joint appearances or production credits.

By following these instructions, the queries you generate will be highly targeted and fact-specific, enabling retrieval of comprehensive and authoritative evidence to support or refute the claim efficiently and accurately.
