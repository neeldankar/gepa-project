Task Description:

You will perform a precise fact verification and evidence consolidation task involving three distinct textual inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to analyze the claim, which consists of one or multiple discrete factual assertions, and systematically verify each by synthesizing only explicitly stated or directly supported evidence found in `notes` and `context`. Avoid any speculation, inference beyond the available information, or assumptions not explicitly backed by the provided data.

Inputs:

- **claim:** A textual statement possibly containing multiple distinct factual assertions. These assertions cover various domains such as music bands, their genres and discographies; individuals such as actors, athletes, musicians and their roles or achievements; TV or movie series and their premiere dates or cast joining details; geographic and administrative details like jurisdictions and boundaries; product ownership and corporate affiliations; and other domain-specific facts.

- **notes:** Extracted snippets or paraphrased fragments related to the claim. These can be partial, redundant, or fragmented and require consolidation. They are intermediate evidence items connected to the claim’s assertions.

- **context:** Longer, more detailed, authoritative excerpts or summaries relevant to the claim entities or facts. This includes biographies, discographies, official records, league histories, band histories, or other comprehensive factual records that may clarify, support, or contradict claim elements.

Output Requirements:

You must provide two outputs that strictly reflect verification outcomes based solely on the information within `notes` and `context`.

1. **new_notes:**  
   - Parse and break down the claim into separate factual assertions.  
   - For each assertion, explicitly state whether it is confirmed, contradicted, or unverifiable strictly from the `notes` and `context`.  
   - Consolidate repetitive or overlapping facts across `notes` and `context` into clear, succinct, accurate, and domain-appropriate statements.  
   - Exclude unrelated, off-topic information or extraneous details not directly relevant to the claim’s assertions.  
   - When information to verify an assertion is missing or ambiguous in the inputs, mark it as unverifiable without conjecture or inference.  
   - Use precise domain-specific terminology, and preserve nuanced distinctions (e.g., do not conflate “primarily metal with psychedelic rock elements” as simply “rock”).  
   - Avoid redundant entries; consolidate facts where overlap occurs.  

2. **titles:**  
   - Provide a small set (usually 3 to 5) of concise, domain-relevant, descriptive headings summarizing the main topical areas or factual relationships addressed in `new_notes`.  
   - Titles should reflect key entities (e.g., person names, band names, TV series), or factual categories (e.g., “Band Genre Classification”, “Actor Role Verification”, “Premiere Dates”, “Native American NFL Teams”).  
   - Titles serve as thematic indexes facilitating quick grasp of the verification focus and outcomes.  

Key Domain-Specific and Task-Specific Considerations:

- **Music Domain:**  
  - Bands can have subtle genre classifications (e.g., metal vs. psychedelic rock vs. alternative rock vs. progressive rock).  
  - Be cautious with name similarities that can cause confusion (e.g., “The Gentry” is distinct from “Jacob Gentry”).  
  - Verify band formation details, album and EP release counts, and aliases (e.g., one band performing under multiple names like “One Unique Signal” also performing as “The Telescopes”).  
  - Album release numbers must be fact-based; do not infer totals where data is missing.

- **Film and TV Domain:**  
  - Distinguish carefully between actors and their roles across different series or episodes.  
  - Verify cast joining dates, differentiating between multiple series (e.g., “Eureka (U.S. TV series)” vs. others).  
  - Confirm premiere dates and first appearances explicitly from evidence.  
  - Avoid conflating similar titles or franchises unless explicitly connected.

- **Geographic and Administrative Domain:**  
  - Distinguish between jurisdictional units clearly (e.g., city vs. county boundaries).  
  - Validate attributes like size rankings or ownership strictly on stated evidence.  
  - Ensure explicit citation for geographic relationships and not inferred or assumed boundaries.

- **Sports Domain:**  
  - Carefully confirm athlete participation periods, achievements, and team affiliations.  
  - Validate claims on championship wins, participations, and absence of competitors with precise season/year or event names.  
  - Distinguish individuals with similar names and nationalities rigorously (e.g., Finnish vs. Spanish rally drivers).  

- **Product and Corporate Ownership:**  
  - Only affirm ownership or corporate ties when explicitly mentioned.  
  - Avoid assumptions based on company names or inferred relationships.

Generalizable Verification Workflow:

1. **Claim Parsing:** Decompose complex claims into atomic factual assertions to be verified separately.

2. **Evidence Mapping:** Map each assertion’s entities and facts onto the corresponding `notes` and `context` snippets.

3. **Cross-Verification:** Identify whether assertions are supported, contradicted, or unverifiable by comparing all relevant provided information.

4. **Explicit Missing Data Handling:** When no explicit evidence is found in inputs, mark assertions as unverifiable with no inference.

5. **Conflict Resolution:** When there are conflicting data points, separate what is supported from what is contradicted clearly.

6. **Synthesis:** Merge fragmented or overlapping evidence into clear, concise, domain-appropriate statements reflecting verification status.

7. **Output Structuring:** Present `new_notes` and `titles` aligned clearly to help users quickly grasp firm factual conclusions and their topical groupings.

- Avoid repetition and redundancy in outputs by merging duplicative facts and titles.

- Stick strictly to explicitly provided evidence; do not speculate or infer beyond what is stated.

- For claims combining multiple interrelated facts (e.g., actor roles with joining dates), treat each fact independently and summarize verification clearly for each.

- Use precise terminology for domain-specific facts and avoid simplification that loses nuance.

- Consolidate repeated claim elements and evidence into unified, clear verification statements to improve clarity.

Summary:

This task requires meticulous analysis and verification of multipart fact claims by synthesizing fragmented, redundant, or partial evidence into concise, explicit verification summaries categorized under thematic titles. Your outputs must strictly reflect what is explicit in the inputs, maintain domain-specific accuracy and terminological precision, and facilitate quick comprehension of verified, contradicted, or unverifiable claim elements.
