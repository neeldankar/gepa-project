Task Description:
You will be given a `claim` — a factual statement or assertion — and a list of `notes` which contain extracted evidences, observations, or contextual information relevant to the claim. Your goal is to produce a precise and comprehensive `query` that could be used to verify the truthfulness of the claim by retrieving necessary factual documents or data.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A single factual statement or set of related assertions.
- `notes`: A list of sentences or observations related to the claim, including both supporting and contradicting evidence, missing information, or relevant context.

Output Format:
- `query`: A specific, structured, and actionable question or set of questions designed to retrieve documents or information necessary to conclusively verify the claim.

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Understand the Claim Thoroughly**: Break down the claim into its key factual components. Claims often include multiple interconnected facts (e.g., identities, roles, dates, relationships, institutions, events, temporal orders) that must be verified.

2. **Analyze Evidence in Notes**: Carefully evaluate the provided `notes` for:
   - Explicit evidence that confirms or contradicts elements of the claim.
   - Gaps where critical information is missing or insufficient to verify.
   - Dates, affiliations, role confirmations, origins, or chronological orderings relevant to the claim.
   - Contradictions within the notes that affect the claim’s validity.

3. **Formulate Verification Queries**:
   - When the notes lack direct confirmation, construct queries to seek the missing critical facts or evidence.
   - Queries should ask for all relevant entities or attributes required to verify the claim (e.g., identities of people, their roles, affiliations, establishment dates of institutions, event dates, sequence/timeline of actions).
   - For claims involving comparisons or temporal order (e.g., “prior to”, “after”), explicitly include requests for dates or timeline information.
   - For claims involving multiple related entities, include queries about all relevant entities to retrieve comprehensive evidence.
   - Include questions about contradictory evidence (e.g., “Is the airline headquartered in X or Y?”).

4. **Be Precise and Unambiguous**: Queries should be well-formed and specific enough to retrieve exactly the documents or information needed to verify each piece of the claim, avoiding vagueness.

5. **Incorporate Domain-Specific Knowledge**:
   - Roles such as “postdoctoral training”, “actor in film”, “captain of plane”, and affiliations like universities or airlines are common in claims.
   - Temporal relations are critical for claims that assert sequences of events.
   - The establishment or founding dates of institutions are commonly required for claims comparing timelines.
   - Identifying contradictions in nationality, company headquarters, or role presence is vital.
   
6. **Example Query Strategies**:
   - For verifying a person’s role and affiliation: “Did [Person] have the role of [Role] at [Entity], and was [Entity] located at or established in [Place/Time]?”
   - For timeline-related claims: “When did event/person X occur/do Y? When did event/person Z occur? Which happened first?”
   - For contradictory claims: “Is [Company] headquartered in [Location1] or [Location2]?”

7. **Use Queries to Retrieve Missing Documents**:
   - Identify which key entities or facts are missing and explicitly mention them in your query.
   - Consider the feedback on document retrieval to ensure queries help find all “gold documents” relevant to verifying the claim.

Summary:
Your queries effectively function as requests for the minimal and sufficient supporting evidence or context to verify or refute the claim fully, synthesizing multiple aspects such as identity, roles, temporal sequence, and institutional facts. Always base queries on logical gaps or contradictions identified in the provided notes.
