Task Description:

You are tasked with performing meticulous fact verification and evidence consolidation based on three input types—`claim`, `notes`, and `context`—to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. The `claim` is a textual statement that may incorporate multiple factual assertions requiring verification. `notes` consist of fragmented facts, paraphrases, or evidence snippets potentially supporting or contradicting the claim. `context` includes longer, often authoritative document extracts or summaries that provide detailed and reliable information relevant to the entities, events, dates, or attributes mentioned in the claim.

Your primary goal is to analyze and verify each distinct component of the claim by synthesizing evidence from both `notes` and `context`. The output `new_notes` must consolidate this synthesized verification clearly and succinctly; the `titles` should organize and summarize the key factual themes or entities underpinning the verification.

---

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Input Parsing and Claim Decomposition**  
   - Carefully parse the claim into discrete, verifiable factual assertions involving entities, events, dates, products, places, filmographies, or comparative facts.  
   - Identify all specific elements such as names of people, films, product brands, geographic locations, roles, ownership, timelines, and awards.

2. **Cross-Verification Strategy**  
   - Map each element from the claim to explicit factual assertions found in `notes` and `context`.  
   - Corroborate or contradict the claim components by rigorous cross-checking between the inputs, avoiding assumptions or speculative interpretations.  
   - Where evidence is absent in both `notes` and `context`, explicitly state unverifiability rather than inferring.  
   - Handle complex or overlapping entities separately (e.g., multiple film actors, directors, similar product brands, geographic jurisdictions) to avoid conflating facts.

3. **Synthesis and Consolidation of `new_notes`**  
   - Remove redundancy by merging repeated or similar facts into succinct, precise sentences.  
   - Strictly limit notes to information directly pertinent to claim verification.  
   - Explicitly indicate which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unverifiable based on the available evidence.  
   - Avoid speculation or introducing external knowledge not substantiated in the inputs.  
   - Use clear, factual language closely reflecting the content and wording of the input data.

4. **Creation of `titles`**  
   - Develop concise, descriptive headings that capture key thematic topics, entities, or relationships found in the verification (e.g., names of persons, film titles with release years, product brands, geographic features, timelines).  
   - Use titles as topical indexes to organize the notes and aid quick understanding of the verification focus.  
   - Ensure titles are domain-relevant and highlight the organizing principles of the consolidated notes.

5. **Domain-Specific Knowledge Essential for Task**  
   - Soft drink brands, flavor profiles, and their geographic market presence or ownership structures.  
   - Biographical and filmographic details: film directors, voice actors, actors’ nationalities, release years, and film titles (including animated films).  
   - Geographic facts involving city vs. county boundaries, jurisdictional status, area size comparisons, and related administrative structures.  
   - Ownership or operational relations between companies and locations (e.g., company-railway station relations).  
   - Scientific or technical information (chemical, biological terms) may appear but are peripheral to verification unless directly relevant.  

6. **Generalizable Verification Workflow**  
   - Identify and isolate key entities, events, dates, or descriptive attributes in the claim first.  
   - Check all available assertions in `notes` and `context` for explicit, matching facts concerning these claim elements.  
   - Detect and reconcile overlaps, contradictions, or corroborations in the inputs to build a coherent evidence profile per claim assertion.  
   - Clearly distinguish verifiable facts from unverifiable or contradicted components in the output notes.  
   - Organize `new_notes` thematically under the generated `titles` for clarity and structured presentation.

7. **Handling Missing, Conflicting, or Partial Data**  
   - If any claim element lacks support or mention in both `notes` and `context`, state that verification is not possible due to insufficient data.  
   - If some parts of the claim are verifiable and others are unsupported or contradicted, separate these explicitly in your notes.  
   - Avoid conjecture, assumptions beyond given evidence, or implicit connections not documented in inputs.

---

Summary:

Provide a focused, factually accurate, non-redundant summary that confirms, refutes, or declares unverifiable each part of the claim by synthesis from the input evidence. The `new_notes` must strictly summarize verified or contradicted facts relevant to claim components while omitting tangential or unverifiable data. The `titles` should succinctly index the key verified topics or entities, aiding swift comprehension of your verification results.

Maintain strict adherence to explicit evidence and domain-aware reasoning, using clear and precise language. Avoid speculation, redundancy, irrelevant detail, or assumptions outside the provided `notes` and `context`.

This task blends detailed evidence extraction, discrete factual parsing, domain-specific fact awareness (yielding knowledge on films, actors, geography, products), and strategic synthesis into a high-quality verification dossier suited for downstream factual analysis or retrieval applications.
