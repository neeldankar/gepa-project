Instruction for the assistant:

You will be given two input fields:  
- `claim`: A factual statement or set of statements whose truth or accuracy needs to be fully verified or refuted.  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets or pieces of information related to the claim, containing contextual or partial factual data that may support or contradict the claim.  

Your objective is to generate an output field:  
- `query`: One or more precise, factually grounded natural language questions or search queries that, when used to retrieve information, will yield all necessary evidence/documents to comprehensively verify or refute every factual component of the claim.

---

Detailed guidelines:

1. **Fundamental Goal**  
   Construct queries explicitly designed to retrieve documents or evidence that can conclusively support or disprove the claim’s assertions. The queries must deliberately cover *all* principal factual elements of the claim, enabling exhaustive verification.

2. **Comprehensive Inclusion of Key Facts and Entities**  
   - Extract key components from the claim such as:  
     - People (full names, roles, relationships)  
     - Organizations (clubs, companies, groups, councils)  
     - Creative works (songs, albums, films, DVDs, books) with exact titles  
     - Dates, seasons, years, events, and locations  
   - Incorporate and align relevant data found in the `notes` (like correct names, dates, roles, nicknames), ensuring queries use the exact terminology and phrasing from claim and notes to maximize precision.

3. **Bridging Gaps and Clarifying Ambiguities**  
   - Identify any vague or ambiguous references in the claim (e.g., "the goalie," "the town," "the actress") and explicitly request their identification or clarification as part of the queries.  
   - When the notes provide partial verification or introduce additional factual data that contextualizes the claim, integrate these to refine the queries so they target both verification *and* clarification needs.  
   - If the claim references relationships or overlap between entities (e.g., shared producers, nickname usage, player transfers), formulate queries to verify those direct associations explicitly.

4. **Domain-specific Details and Considerations**  
   - **Music-related claims:** Ask about membership of groups, songwriting credits (lyrics/music), performer/producer roles, release dates of works (albums, DVDs).  
   - **Sports-related claims:** Verify player roles (goalie, striker), team rosters for specific seasons, nicknames associated with clubs, transfer history, and sponsorship connections.  
   - **Geographical claims:** Determine administrative areas, official names of places, geographic proximities, and governing bodies (e.g., which towns a council administers).  
   - **Film/acting claims:** Confirm actor-character-role alignments, film producers, release and production dates.

5. **Query Structure and Style**  
   - Compose clear, direct, natural language questions or explicit search queries that can be used to retrieve highly relevant documents.  
   - Combine multiple related verification questions into a single compound query if appropriate for completeness and context.  
   - Use question forms such as:  
     - "Who is/was...?"  
     - "Did [person] play/portray [role/character] in [work]?"  
     - "Is [entity] credited with ...?"  
     - "Are the producers of [work A] and [work B] the same?"  
     - "Which towns are under the administration of [entity]?"  
     - "Is [nickname] officially associated with [club/team]?"  
     - "Did [player] participate in [season/year] for [team]?"  
     - "What company sponsored [event]? Is it based in [location]?"

6. **Focus on Verification vs. Broad Exploration**  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries.  
   - Ensure every query element is linked to a claim component requiring verification or clarification.  
   - The queries should directly target fact retrieval to confirm or deny the claim’s assertions, not general background information.

7. **Utilizing the Notes Effectively**  
   - Treat notes as partial or contextual evidence; use them to identify what has already been partially verified or what remains unknown.  
   - Use dates and names in notes to refine temporal and entity specificity in queries, e.g., if notes mention a DVD titled "Take Off" linked to 2PM and a known departure date, specify these details to precisely confirm timing.  
   - If notes mention entities or facts related but not explicitly stated in the claim, consider including queries to link those details back to the claim’s components.

---

Summary:

Your output should be one or more detailed, well-structured natural language questions or queries that collectively target every fact and relationship in the claim. They should explicitly utilize the names, dates, titles, and facts from the claim and notes, clarify ambiguous references, verify all actors, roles, nicknames, affiliations, sponsorships, and events, and do so in a way that will retrieve documents to provide comprehensive evidence needed to fully verify or refute the original claim.
