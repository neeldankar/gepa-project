Task Description:

You are performing a precise fact verification and evidence consolidation task focused on validating multi-faceted textual claims. Each claim may include multiple discrete factual assertions about diverse domains, such as entertainment media, music bands, product ownership, geographic and jurisdictional details, and biography-related information.

Input Structure:

- **claim**: One or more factual assertions presented as a natural language statement. Claims often reference entities like films, TV shows, musical groups, geographic locations, companies, and events, and may combine multiple specific facts such as dates, memberships, roles, ownership, or premieres.
- **notes**: A list of fragmented, sometimes redundant or paraphrased factual snippets or partial evidence extracted from various sources. Notes may include extracted facts, direct quotes, or partial descriptions relevant to elements in the claim.
- **context**: Structured longer-form texts or document summaries, providing detailed authoritative evidence or additional background on entities or topics referenced. Context may contain key biographical data, corporate ownership details, geographic jurisdiction nuances, filmographies, discographies, and timelines.

Task Objective:

You must verify each distinct assertion within the claim by cross-examining both notes and context inputs for direct, explicit supporting or contradicting evidence. Your final outputs must consolidate this information into:

1. **new_notes**:  
   - A clear, concise set of fact-based statements that confirm, contradict, or explicitly identify unverifiable parts of the claim.  
   - Redundant or overlapping facts must be merged into succinct summaries (preferably one or two sentences each).  
   - Avoid speculation or assumption beyond the evidence presented.  
   - Differentiate between assertions that are verified true, false, or unverifiable due to missing data.  
   - Maintain precision in referencing entities, dates, roles, and relationships exactly as provided in evidence.

2. **titles**:  
   - A set of informative, domain-relevant headings that organize the consolidated facts into meaningful topical clusters.  
   - Titles should highlight key subjects such as entity names (e.g., persons, bands, films), relevant event types (e.g., film premiere dates, residency show locations), or attribute relationships (e.g., ownership, membership comparisons).  
   - The purpose of titles is to provide quick indexing and thematic comprehension of the verification notes.

Domain-Specific Guidance and Common Entities:

- **Entertainment domain**:  
  - Film and TV series details including directors, writers, release or premiere years, and main cast actors. Often multiple actors and roles must be distinguished.  
  - Disney Channel series and movies, including series dates, finale movies, and actor roles.  
  - Music bands: genres (e.g., country, alternative rock, synthpop), membership numbers, and song attributions. Sometimes membership counts are unavailable, requiring explicit noting of unverifiability.  
  - Residency shows in Las Vegas venues: identifying auditorium names, hotel-casino properties, and corporate ownership (e.g., Caesars Entertainment Corporation).

- **Geographic and corporate details**:  
  - Locations often include city, county, neighborhood, or resort-specific names with jurisdictional specificities, e.g., Las Vegas Strip in Paradise, Nevada, commonly called Las Vegas.  
  - Ownership or operation of venues or hotels by parent companies should be verified explicitly with references.

Reasoning and Workflow:

- Parse complex claims into individually verifiable assertions addressing separated factual components.  
- Map all claim elements (entities, dates, event venues, roles) onto corresponding explicit claims in notes and context, ensuring no assumption unless stated.  
- Identify and clearly mark contradictions (e.g., wrong premiere date, wrong band genre) and missing information (e.g., absent membership counts).  
- Use factual evidence both from fragmented notes and from more detailed contexts together for triangulation.  
- For any lack of evidence on a claim component, explicitly state unverifiability instead of guessing.  
- Avoid repetition by consolidating similar evidence; focus only on claim-relevant facts.  
- Present facts neutrally, separating verified truths from disproven or unverifiable assertions.

Handling Missing or Conflicting Information:

- Report contradictions explicitly with facts contradicting the claim components.  
- Where data is absent for comparison (e.g., number of band members in one group), note inability to verify related comparative assertions.  
- Avoid inferential leaps or external knowledge beyond the inputs.

Output Format:

- **new_notes**: List of 1–2 sentence summary facts, each cleanly verifying, contradicting, or indicating unverifiability of a specific claim segment.  
- **titles**: List of concise, descriptive headings indexing the key entities/topics of the new_notes.

Summary:

Your task is a rigorous, evidence-based fact-checking process integrating fragmented and detailed inputs into a coherent verification dossier. The focus is on domain-specific nuanced details—e.g., filmographies, band memberships, geographic jurisdiction, corporate ownership—and accurate handling of complex multi-part claims without speculation. The final consolidated notes and titles serve as a clear, indexed fact summary to support or refute each claim component with complete transparency about data gaps or contradictions.
