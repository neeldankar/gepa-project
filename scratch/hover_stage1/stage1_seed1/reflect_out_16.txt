Task Description:

You are tasked with a focused fact verification and evidence consolidation task, where you analyze three types of inputs—`claim`, `notes`, and `context`—to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. The `claim` is a textual statement possibly containing multiple factual assertions that need verification. The `notes` are often extracted snippets, paraphrases, or partial evidences relevant to the claim, while the `context` comprises longer document texts or summaries potentially containing detailed source information related to entities, events, timelines, or attributes mentioned in the claim.

Your overarching goal is to verify or refute each distinct component of the claim by synthesizing and consolidating information from both `notes` and `context`, avoiding redundancy, speculation, or irrelevant content.

---

Detailed Guidelines and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. **Input Structure and Understanding**  
   - **claim**: A statement consisting of one or more assertions (e.g., about persons, events, dates, geographic locations, product characteristics, filmographies, or comparative facts).  
   - **notes**: Fragmented facts, paraphrases, or extracted evidences that may partially support or contradict the claim. This input can contain redundancy.  
   - **context**: Summaries or full-text snippets from diverse documents that may provide authoritative or more detailed information on the claim’s subjects.

2. **Verification Approach and Reasoning Strategy**  
   - Parse the claim into discrete assertions where possible and address each individually.  
   - Map claim elements (entities, dates, events, attributes) to facts in both `notes` and `context`.  
   - Cross-verify facts by probing both inputs together to spot confirmations, contradictions, or missing information.  
   - Explicitly note when parts of the claim cannot be verified because critical information about entities, events, or attributes is absent from both inputs.  
   - For geographic, historical, biographical, or product-related facts, use explicit details only—avoid assumptions or inference beyond the data given.  
   - When multiple entities or facts overlap (e.g., multiple actors in movies, overlapping dates, competing geographic references), carefully separate which parts are supported and which are not.

3. **Consolidation of `new_notes`**  
   - Remove redundancy by combining similar or repeated facts into succinct, clear statements—preferably one or two sentences each.  
   - Focus exclusively on information directly relevant to verifying the claim’s assertions, omitting irrelevant tangents or unrelated domain facts.  
   - Clarify precisely which elements of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unverifiable.  
   - If verification is impossible due to missing data or ambiguity, clearly state this instead of speculating.  
   - Maintain factual accuracy and align notes closely with evidence found in the inputs.

4. **Creation of `titles`**  
   - Generate concise, descriptive headings that summarize key topics, entities, or factual relationships uncovered during verification.  
   - Titles should be domain-relevant and reflect the organizing principles of your notes (e.g., names of persons, film titles with dates, product or geographic features, timeline relations).  
   - They serve as topical indexes for the summarized evidences and facilitate quick understanding of verification focuses.

5. **Domain-Specific Knowledge Embedded in the Task**  
   - Common facts relate to soft drink brands, flavor profiles, and geographic market presence.  
   - Biographical facts include film directors, voice actors, release years, animation movie titles, and actor nationalities.  
   - Geographic comparisons, including city versus county boundaries, size, and jurisdictional status, are frequent.  
   - Product ownership or company-railway station relations may appear, requiring careful geographic and corporate structure reasoning.  
   - Scientific or technical terms (e.g., chemical or biological entities) may appear but are often not central to verification.

6. **Generalizable Reasoning and Workflow**  
   - Identify key components (entities, events, dates) in the claim first.  
   - Search both `notes` and `context` for explicit assertions tied to these components.  
   - Distill duplicated claims and reconcile contradictions; produce a coherent evaluation per factual assertion.  
   - Explicitly separate verifiable from unverifiable or contradicted claim parts in `new_notes`.  
   - Group related notes under thematic headings synthesized as `titles`.

7. **Handling Missing or Conflicting Data**  
   - When the claim contains information about an entity or event absent in all inputs, clearly note that verification is not possible.  
   - If parts are verifiable but others are unsupported, separately highlight these distinctions.  
   - Avoid speculation or implied connections not grounded in provided evidence.

---

Summary:

Your task is a meticulous, evidence-based fact-checking and summarization exercise that integrates fragmented inputs into a concise, focused verification dossier. Your outputs must be:

- **`new_notes`**: A reduced, clear, and precise list of facts confirming, contradicting, or indicating unverifiability of the claim’s components based on the complete input set.  
- **`titles`**: A concise list of relevant topical headings that summarize and organize the key subjects, entities, or relationships essential to understanding the verification outcome.

This task requires strict adherence to evidence, careful parsing of multi-part claims, clear handling of data absence or contradiction, and domain-aware synthesis of facts to aid downstream understanding or retrieval tasks.
