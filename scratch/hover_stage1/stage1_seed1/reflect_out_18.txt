You are given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim` is a statement that makes one or more factual assertions about people, films, plants, or other domain-specific entities.
- `notes` is a list of sentences extracted or derived from knowledge sources that provide supporting or contradicting information relevant to the claim. These notes often include nuanced, domain-specific facts, clarifications, or corrections about names, dates, taxonomy, roles, or other factual content.
- `context` is a list of text snippets representing detailed background information, descriptions, or summaries of entities related to the claim. This includes encyclopedic or reference-style information about people, films, subfamilies, genera, or other topics mentioned in the claim and notes.

Your task is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

### Task Description and Goals

1. **Reasoning and validation:**  
   Analyze the `claim` critically against the `notes` and `context` to determine factual correctness, contradictions, and clarifications. Use the `notes` and `context` to support your reasoning—particularly for verifying entities’ existence, roles, titles, or attributes (such as award-winning status, release dates, taxonomic ranks, etc.).

2. **Generating `new_notes`:**  
   Condense and synthesize the most salient, relevant, and verified facts from the `notes` (and supported by `context`) that directly address and clarify key points in the `claim`.  
   - Eliminate redundancy and keep concise, factual statements.  
   - Include corrections or clarifications where the claim is inaccurate or misleading.  
   - Include distinctions between similarly named or related entities to avoid confusion.  
   - The `new_notes` should enable a reader to understand why the claim is true, false, or partially true, based on evidence.

3. **Generating `titles`:**  
   Identify the most relevant and appropriate document or entity titles from the `context` that provide authority or reference points for the claim and your `new_notes`.  
   - Titles should be clearly connected to the entities or facts involved in the claim (e.g., specific film titles, actor profiles, plant genus or subfamily names).  
   - Titles should be precise and correspond to comprehensive reference entries or articles likely representing the gold-standard sources for the given claim.  
   - Aim to retrieve all gold-relevant titles tied to key claim components for complete coverage.

### Domain-Specific and Niche Details to Note

- Film-related claims often involve actors and directors with overlapping or similar names (e.g., Sidney Poitier vs Damion Poitier), release dates, awards, or roles.  
- Botanical claims involve taxonomic ranks where confusion between genus, subfamily, or species counts can cause factual errors. Correct taxonomic hierarchy must be observed.  
- Actor credits, film release years, and director roles should be cross-validated; some actors may have no directing credits.  
- Award-winning status must be carefully differentiated between individuals with similar names.

### Generalizable Strategy for the Task

- Carefully decompose the claim into individual assertions.  
- Cross-reference each assertion against `notes` and `context`, verifying or falsifying with domain-specific evidence (such as known filmographies, botanical taxonomies, or biographical data).  
- Focus on clarifying distinctions to avoid conflating entities (e.g., actors with similar names).  
- Distill the `notes` to their most relevant and accurate points to highlight crucial clarifications or corrections to the claim.  
- Choose `titles` that represent the most authoritative, meaningful knowledge sources bearing directly on the claim’s truthfulness.

Your output should enable clear, evidence-backed understanding of the claim’s validity, provide concise supporting or corrective notes, and link to key reference titles to contextualize the evaluation.
