You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` field that formulates one or more clear, specific, and factually grounded questions that can be used to verify the truth or accuracy of the given claim by retrieving and checking relevant factual documents or evidence.

Detailed guidance:

1. **Input understanding**:
   - `claim` is a statement that makes one or more factual assertions.
   - `notes` is a list of sentences or short paragraphs containing potentially relevant facts, partial evidence, or clarifications related to the entities and topics mentioned in the claim.

2. **Output requirements (the `query`)**:
   - The `query` should be phrased as one or multiple direct, focused factual questions.
   - These questions should be designed to guide retrieval of authoritative sources or documents that directly confirm or refute the key factual elements of the claim.
   - The questions should specifically target:
     - Identification of key entities, such as names of people, places, works (films, bands, performances), or events mentioned in the claim.
     - Verification of important attributes about these entities referenced in the claim (e.g., birth date, profession, membership, location, roles in productions).
     - Relationships or links asserted by the claim (e.g., "Did Michael York star both in X and Y?", "Is King ov Hell’s band based in this Norwegian county?").

3. **How to use `notes` in crafting the query**:
   - Extract and incorporate key factual points from the notes relevant to the claim to focus or disambiguate the query.
   - Include any clarifying details from notes, such as alternative names, dates, locations, or conflicting information that may help differentiate or validate parts of the claim.
   - Use notes to identify which entities or facts require external evidence to confirm, so the query targets precisely those.

4. **General reasoning approach to form queries**:
   - Break down the claim into atomic verifiable facts if it contains multiple components.
   - For each fact, formulate targeted questions addressing:
     a) Who/what is involved (e.g., names of stars, bands, actors, films).
     b) Attributes or metadata (birth month, birthplace, role, membership, location).
     c) Relationships and events (e.g., starred in, was born in, was a member of).
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions; focus on fact-checkable specifics.
   - When a claim references entities with multiple possible interpretations or overlapping attributes mentioned in notes, include questions to disambiguate or clarify.

5. **Domain-specific considerations learned from examples**:
   - When verifying entertainment industry claims, include questions about actors’ or musicians’ involvement in specific productions, their biographical details (birth month/year, profession), and the locations tied to bands or events.
   - For geographic and administrative claims, be aware of administrative changes (e.g., counties changing names or borders), so queries might ask about historical or original locations.
   - Ensure queries explicitly request confirmation of cited roles or connections (e.g., “Did Michael York star in both X and Y?” rather than just “Who starred in X?”).

By following the above, your generated `query` will optimally support retrieval of factual evidence to verify the claim accurately.
