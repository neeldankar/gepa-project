Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: a `claim` and a list of evidence `notes`. Your goal is to generate a precise, targeted `query` (research question) that when searched or retrieved against, will help verify the truthfulness of the claim using the information in the notes.

Input Format:
- `claim`: a factual statement that needs to be verified.
- `notes`: a list of textual pieces of evidence related to the entities, dates, or facts mentioned in the claim.

Output Required:
- `query`: a concise, clear, and fact-focused question designed to retrieve all relevant documents or information needed to confirm or refute the claim.

Detailed Guidance and Domain-Specific Considerations:

1. Identify Key Entities and Temporal References:
   - Extract and focus on key entities (persons, places, works such as films, operas, companies, airports) from the claim.
   - Identify any temporal information (dates, periods) relevant to the entities or events.  
   Example: For people, their birth and death dates; for artworks, release or premiere dates.

2. Link Entities to Contextual Attributes:
   - Use notes to link entities to attributes such as lifespans, locations, composers, directors, release dates, or headquarters.
   - Include relevant roles or titles (e.g., "director Francis Ford Coppola", "composer Jean-Philippe Rameau", "M7 Aerospace LP headquartered at San Antonio International Airport") when formulating the query to improve specificity and retrieval precision.

3. Formulate the Query to Capture the Core Verification Aspect:
   - Queries should explicitly capture the factual relationship or temporal overlap relevant to the claim.
   - For example, if verifying co-existence in time, ask if both entities were alive or active during overlapping periods.
   - If verifying authorship or composition, ask about the composers or directors of the compared works.
   - If verifying geographical co-location, ask if both entities are located within the same geopolitical boundary (e.g., country).
   
4. Maximize Retrieval Coverage and Precision:
   - The query should mention all key entities and their significant attributes to retrieve all gold documents known to support or challenge the claim.
   - For example, mention multiple known creators or states if relevant.
   - Avoid vague or overly general queries that may miss critical documents.

5. Be Concise but Complete:
   - Queries should be succinct yet contain all necessary details (entity names, dates, roles, places) required for precise document retrieval.
   - Avoid unnecessary verbosity, but do not omit core elements crucial for retrieval.

6. Generalizable Strategy:
   - Parse claim to establish what fact is being verified (alive at the same time? composed by same/different people? located in same country? etc.).
   - Map claim entities to relevant facts in notes.
   - Construct an interrogative query that explicitly asks the factual question which, if answered, supports or refutes the claim.

By following the above, your generated queries will enable accurate and complete retrieval of necessary evidence documents, facilitating factual verification of claims.
