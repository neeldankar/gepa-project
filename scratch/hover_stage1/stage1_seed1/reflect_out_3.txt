You will be given two input fields: `claim` (a factual or relational statement involving specific entities, events, or concepts) and `notes` (a list of statements summarizing or clarifying factual information relevant to the claim, typically extracted from multiple sources). Your task is to produce a single output field called `query`.

The purpose of the `query` is to form a precise, fact-seeking question that targets the core factual verification needed to assess the truthfulness of the claim using the given notes. The query should:

1. Reference the key entities, relationships, and specifics mentioned in the claim, often naming particular people, groups, bands, songs, organizations, or concepts involved.
2. Reflect the central point of contention or fact that the claim asserts or implies, especially focusing on:
   - Whether certain entities share a specific property or relationship (e.g., membership in a band, collaboration with a record label, shared profession),
   - Whether a stated event or interaction occurred as described (e.g., co-working durations, song performances, professional overlaps).
3. Be formulated as a clear, explicit yes/no or verification question that can guide retrieval of all relevant supporting or contradicting information.
4. Avoid broad or vague formulations—focus on the specific factual details highlighted by the claim and clarified by the notes.

Additional important considerations based on domain-specific insights and the task nature:

- The claims often involve music bands, songs, musicians, record labels, professions, and relationships between individuals tied to scientific or cultural fields.
- Notes typically provide detailed but not always complete evidential context, including explicit denials of connections or professions, origins of bands, duration of collaboration, and membership status.
- The query must be sufficiently precise to retrieve all relevant documents or knowledge sources necessary to evaluate the claim, minimizing irrelevant or missing document retrieval.
- In cases where multiple entities are compared or related, the query should distinguish each explicitly, for example naming the specific band, person, or organization.
- Duration or time-based aspects mentioned in claims (such as "worked 3 years with") should be explicitly questioned if stated.
- If the claim implies intersections or overlaps (e.g., "both share profession X"), the query should explicitly ask whether both or multiple entities fulfill that criterion.
- If denial or negation is part of the claim, the query should frame the question to confirm or refute that negation clearly.

Generalizable strategy to create the query:

- Extract the core relationships or properties asserted by the claim.
- Cross-check entities and properties with the notes to ensure the query targets exactly the evidence at issue.
- Formulate a direct factual question using named entities and specific predicates or relations.
- Phrase the query to retrieve evidence from known related articles or documents about those entities (e.g., band names, song titles, personal names, organizations).
- Use natural but unambiguous language tailored towards fact verification rather than open-ended exploration.

Example patterns:

- "Did [Entity A] and [Entity B] both [perform action/hold property]?"
- "Are [Entity A] and [Entity B] members of [Organization/Band]?"
- "Did [Entity A] collaborate with [Entity B/Organization] for [duration/time]?"
- "Do [Entity A] and [Entity B] share the same profession or professional field?"
- "Is [Entity A] the performer of [Song] by [Band]?"

The ultimate goal is for the query to be a well-targeted, fact-based question that can maximize retrieval of relevant documents or knowledge needed to verify the original claim using the provided notes.
