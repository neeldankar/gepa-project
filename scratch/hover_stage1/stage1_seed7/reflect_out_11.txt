Given the input fields `claim` and `notes`, generate a concise, clear, and factual search query that effectively captures the core fact or relationship to be verified in the claim. The purpose of the query is to aid in retrieving relevant documents for fact-checking.

Key details and domain-specific information for this task include:  
- The `claim` is a factual statement involving people, places, works (such as movies, songs, albums), dates, or relationships between entities.  
- The `notes` contain supporting or refuting evidence extracted from multiple documents related to the claim, including named entities, dates, roles, geographic details, and work titles.  
- Often the claim involves verifying if a specific individual has a certain attribute, participation, or connection (e.g., an actor appearing in particular movies, musicians releasing certain songs/albums, individuals sharing a birthplace).  
- The query should explicitly mention key entities or titles involved in the claim (e.g., specific movies, albums, people) and focus on the core verification question (e.g., "Is there a Welsh actor who appeared in both X and Y?", "Was X born in Y?", "Did X release a studio album titled Z including song Y?").  
- When claims involve numeric or hierarchical facts (e.g., city sizes, birth dates), incorporate those details if they are central to the claim’s verification.  
- The query should be direct, interrogative, fact-focused, and phrased in a way that the retrieved documents will directly support or refute the claim.  
- Avoid adding extraneous details not relevant to the central fact-checking need.

Recommended approach:  
1. Identify the main entities (people, places, works) named in the claim.  
2. Identify the key relationship or fact to be validated (e.g., birthplace, participation, authorship).  
3. Use the evidence in the notes to understand how these entities and facts relate.  
4. Formulate a search query as a natural-language question that asks precisely about this relationship, including important contextual details (dates, titles, roles) when appropriate.  
5. Keep the query succinct but comprehensive enough to retrieve focused supporting or refuting information.

Example pattern for query formulation:  
- For actor and movie participation claims: "Is [person/actor] involved in [movie1] and [movie2]?" or "Is there an actor from [origin] who appeared in both [movie1] and [movie2]?"  
- For birthplace claims: "Was [person] born in [place/ city], which is [additional geographic/ demographic fact]?"  
- For music or album claims: "Did [musician] release the album [album name] in [year], including the song [song name]?"  

The aim is to generate queries that can be used for accurate fact-checking document retrieval based on the input claim and notes context.
