- `notes`: A collection of factual points, evidence snippets, or extracted information related to the claim. These notes often include data from multiple sources and may contain duplicates, redundant details, or partly overlapping information.

- `context`: A set of document summaries, excerpts, or descriptive passages that provide authoritative background on entities, events, persons, or concepts relevant to the claim. These include biographies, event descriptions, historical overviews, filmographies, or club histories depending on the domain.

Your task is to analyze these inputs and produce two outputs:

1. `new_notes`:
   - Extract and synthesize the key factual points from `notes` and supporting information from `context` that directly support, refute, or clarify the claim.
   - Remove duplicates, contradictions, and redundant entries within the original material.
   - Explicitly state the accuracy or inaccuracy of each claim element based on evidence.
   - Correct any factual inaccuracies or misattributions present in the claim, including proper disambiguation of similarly named persons, places, or events.
   - Provide clarifications where claims are ambiguous, partially true, or misleading.
   - Present information in a concise, well-organized, and domain-aware manner, suitable for quick fact-checking or verification.

2. `titles`:  
   - Provide a prioritized list of the most relevant and authoritative document titles, entity names, event names, or concepts from `context` that are crucial to verifying the claim.
   - These titles serve as pointers for further investigation and should reflect domain-specific importance such as historical figures, sports teams and seasons, film titles and filmmakers, or other relevant subjects.
   - Titles should be concise but sufficiently descriptive to uniquely identify the source or topic.

When performing this task, adhere to the following domain-specific guidelines and best practices:

- Prioritize accuracy in factual domains such as historical sports records (e.g., specific football seasons and players’ roles), film industry details (directors, actors, film production credits), and niche cultural topics (e.g., Norwegian black metal scenes or geographical specifics) whenever present.

- Organize the output logically, with clear, factual statements that enable both validation and possible debunking of the claim efficiently.

- Anchor clarifications in evidence-based reasoning, making explicit why a claim is supported, refuted, or partially correct.

- Make explicit if certain elements cannot be verified with the current data, to maintain transparency.

This structured approach allows for consistent, domain-aware fact verification and refinement of claims, balancing comprehensive evidence use with precision and clarity.
