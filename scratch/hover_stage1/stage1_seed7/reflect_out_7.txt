Task Description:  
You are given two input fields: a `claim` and a list of `notes`. Your task is to generate a `query` that can be used to verify or validate the claim based on the information in the notes. The `query` should be formulated as a focused, relevant question (or set of questions) that targets key factual points or relationships implied by the claim and supported or contradicted by the notes.

Detailed Instructions:  
1. **Input Understanding:**  
   - The `claim` presents a factual statement that needs to be verified. It often involves comparisons, attributions, identifications, or factual relationships between entities (e.g., people, events, organizations, or products).  
   - The `notes` provide supporting, contradicting, or clarifying factual information related to the claim. These are often multiple sentences or facts, many of which include domain-specific structured information such as player names and teams, game designers and their works, film directors and their credits, or tournament results.

2. **Output Requirements:**  
   - Produce a `query` that is a natural language question or a concise set of questions aimed at retrieving evidence to verify the claim.  
   - The `query` should be specific enough to guide information retrieval toward key authoritative sources or documents cited in the notes (e.g., identifying goal scorers and their teams for a football match, comparing popularity metrics between games, or confirming director credits for films).  
   - The query should capture the main entities and relationships involved in the claim and reflect the nuanced factual distinctions found in the notes.  
   - Avoid overly broad or vague queries; instead, focus on the core factual elements that will disambiguate or confirm the claim.

3. **Domain-specific Considerations:**  
   - If the claim involves **sports-related facts** (e.g., players, teams, matches, tournaments), frame the query to retrieve information about players involved, event outcomes, and team nicknames or colloquial names if mentioned.  
   - For **board games or game designers**, queries should target the global popularity, formal competitive scenes, community size, tournament existence, and recognition of specific games or designers referenced.  
   - For **films and directors**, focus on director credits, co-directors, film titles, and production details that verify roles and associations.

4. **Reasoning and Query Construction Strategy:**  
   - Analyze the claim to identify key entities and relationships mentioned.  
   - Cross-check these against the notes to understand the scope and nature of the claim — does it require comparison, confirmation of identity, or factual event details?  
   - Formulate the query as a question that, when answered authoritatively, will allow fact-checking of the claim.  
   - If the claim involves multiple related facts (e.g., identities of goal scorers and their teams), combine these into a single, coherent query rather than multiple unrelated questions.  
   - Use precise language and mention specific entities (names, dates, events) rather than general terms to improve retrieval accuracy.

5. **Examples of Good Queries:**  
   - For a claim about game popularity:  
     *"What is the worldwide popularity of chess compared to games designed by Leo Colovini, including number of players, tournaments, and sales?"*  
   - For a sports claim identifying players and team nicknames:  
     *"Who scored in Brazil's 3-0 victory over Spain in the 2013 FIFA Confederations Cup Final, what teams did those players represent, and is any professional football team colloquially known as 'Nathaniel Nate Jacks'?"*  
   - For director credits in films:  
     *"Who directed Finding Dory, and who co-directed A Bug's Life?"*

By adhering to this detailed task framework and domain-specific factual focus, your generated queries will better target the evidence needed to verify claims accurately and efficiently.
