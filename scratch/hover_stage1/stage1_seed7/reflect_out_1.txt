Task Description:
You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a `query` field based on these inputs. The `query` you generate should be a clear, concise, and targeted information-seeking question or questions that, when answered, would allow verification, evidence gathering, or fact-checking of the `claim` using the information found within the given `notes`.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A single text statement making factual assertions that may involve entities, events, dates, attributions (e.g., who directed or starred in a film, who composed an opera, what albums bands released, formation years of bands, etc.).  
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences or snippets relevant to the claim. These notes may contain partial, overlapping, or complementary information about the entities or facts involved in the claim, including domain-specific details (e.g., band formation years, film casts and directors, composer attributions, opera revivals).

Key Points and Domain-Specific Details:
- Claims often combine multiple factual elements about named entities (bands, films, operas, albums, persons, years).  
- Notes may mention multiple related entities that must be linked and disambiguated (e.g., band that released a specific album, actor/director roles, opera composers and their time periods).  
- Notes sometimes contain corrections, contradictions, or clarifications that can confirm or refute parts of the claim.  
- The claim verification depends on identifying and linking specific "gold documents" or key facts buried across the notes.  
- Examples include:  
  * Identifying the correct band that released an album and their formation years.  
  * Matching an actor to multiple films and confirming directors.  
  * Verifying composers of operas from different time periods and adaptations.  

General Strategy for Query Generation:
- Analyze the claim to isolate key factual relationships and entities that require evidence or confirmation.  
- Extract from the notes any relevant details about these entities (names, dates, roles, titles).  
- Frame the query to explicitly ask for these relationships or attributes, often by combining:  
  * Identification questions (e.g., who composed X, who starred in Y).  
  * Verification questions (e.g., are these two people the same/different, did someone hold a role in this film).  
- Phrase queries as natural language questions, aiming for specificity and clarity, to retrieve maximum relevant information.

Additional Guidance:
- The query should aim to retrieve all necessary supporting facts ("gold documents") that enable full validation of the claim.  
- Ensure queries capture all multi-faceted components of the claim (timeframes, people, titles, roles).  
- Avoid vague or overly broad queries that might miss critical entities or relationships.  
- Do not assume information beyond given notes; use notes to guide focus.  
- When multiple entities or facts are linked in the claim, produce compound or multi-part queries that address all key points.

Summary:
Given a claim and relevant supporting notes, generate precise, fact-focused queries that ask about the specific people, events, works, and their attributes involved, to support accurate verification or retrieval of facts subsuming all key aspects of the claim.
