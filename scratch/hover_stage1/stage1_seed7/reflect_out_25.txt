You will be performing a fact verification and clarification task, focused on verifying claims related to diverse but often domain-specific topics such as historical events, sports venues and organizations, music bands and members, school districts and their composition, and other specialized entities or events. 

For each query, you will receive three inputs:  
1. `claim` – a factual/historical statement that needs verification, correction, or clarification, often involving details about people, places, events, or organizations.  
2. `notes` – a collection of extracted factual points and evidence from multiple documents or sources relevant to the claim (sometimes containing duplicates or redundant statements).  
3. `context` – summaries or excerpts of documents, biographies, event descriptions, or relevant related entities that provide background or authoritative information related to the claim.

**Your goal** is to synthesize these inputs to produce two outputs:  
1. `new_notes`: A concise, organized, and accurate set of factual points distilled from both `notes` and `context`, which:  
   - Removes duplicates and redundant or irrelevant data.  
   - Extracts only the most relevant and evidence-backed details to directly support, refute, or clarify the claim.  
   - Corrects any factual errors or misattributions present in the claim (e.g., wrong geographical locations, misnamed entities, inaccurate timelines).  
   - States clearly whether the claim is supported, refuted, partially correct, or unverifiable based on the evidence.  
   - Provides clarifications where the claim is ambiguous or partly true, referencing supporting evidence.  
   - Focuses especially on domain-specific knowledge such as:  
     - Specific sports venues linked to historical events (e.g., UEFA Euro 2012 Final, specific stadiums and their host teams).  
     - Precise membership, formation details, or discography related to music bands (including Norwegian black metal bands, metal or rock guitarists and lineups).  
     - Accurate composition and naming of school districts and their constituent schools, including geographic and jurisdictional details.  
     - Historical or event-related people, places, dates, and organizations, with attention to common confusions or misattributions.  

2. `titles`: A prioritized list of concise, descriptive titles or names of key documents, sources, entities, or concepts crucial for understanding or verifying the claim. These should include:  
   - Primary sources or authoritative reports (e.g., official event pages, stadium profiles).  
   - Key named entities such as people (musicians, athletes), places (stadiums, districts), organizations (clubs, school districts), and events (finals, album releases).  
   - Names of documents or concept descriptors that frame the claim’s context and aid verification.  

**When generating your outputs:**  
- Cross-check and reconcile details from the `claim` against the combined `notes` and `context` inputs, resolving contradictions.  
- Disambiguate similar or commonly confused entities (people, places, events) and ensure names and titles are accurate and standardized.  
- Avoid introducing any facts that are not explicitly present or reasonably inferred from the provided information.  
- Present your `new_notes` in a logical, succinct structure suitable for rapid fact-checking or clarifying the claim.  
- In your `titles`, include only the most relevant and authoritative sources, prioritizing those that best support the claim’s verification.  
- Handle limited or missing information gracefully by explicitly stating unverifiability or insufficient evidence if needed.  

**Domain-specific guidance:**  
- For sports-related claims, ensure correct stadium names, locations, home teams, and event hosting details are correctly identified.  
- For music-related claims, distinguish between band members, their roles, birthdates, and associated acts precisely, given common confusion in minor or less-documented groups.  
- For educational institutions, pay close attention to official district boundaries, school lists, grade structures, and the nomenclature of schools and their locations, especially in New York or other U.S. states where districts and census-designated places are relevant.  

**Overall**, your response should provide a factually accurate, well-structured, and evidence-supported refinement and clarification of the claim, backed by selected authoritative titles that facilitate transparent validation and potential deeper inquiry.
