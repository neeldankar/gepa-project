Task Overview:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your job is to create a precise and focused natural language question or set of questions — referred to as the `query` — which directly targets the core factual assertions or relationships presented in the `claim`. These queries are intended to guide retrieval of authoritative evidence to verify or refute the claim.

Input Details:
- `claim`: A natural language statement containing one or more factual assertions that may include entities (people, places, things), roles or professions, temporal information (dates, timeframes), locations (country/state/city/region), or other relationships.
- `notes`: An array/list of textual evidentiary points that provide clarifications, corrections, confirmations, or contradictions relevant to the claim. These notes often highlight ambiguities, factual conflations, or nuanced domain-specific distinctions that are critical for fact verification.

Key Objective:
- Formulate one or more succinct, natural language question(s) that pinpoint the specific information needed to verify the claim based on the ambiguities or contradictions surfaced in the `notes`.
- Avoid vague or overly broad questions. Instead, focus on clearly disentangling the factual components of the claim that require verification.
- Your `query` should be crafted in fluent English, suitable for use in information retrieval or fact-checking systems.

Guidelines and Domain-Specific Details:
1. **Disentangle Multiple Assertions:**  
   Claims often combine multiple facts about different entities, roles, timelines, or locations. Separate these into distinct, focused queries as needed to precisely verify each fact or relationship.

2. **Leverage Notes for Clarification or Contradiction:**  
   The notes provide critical context such as:  
   - Distinctions between persons, characters, or entities with similar or overlapping names.  
   - Differentiations between an individual’s name and the name of a breed, role, or entity.  
   - Temporal changes in roles or titles (e.g., a person’s job at time X vs time Y).  
   - Geographic hierarchies or nested locations (city within state within country).  
   - Confirmed or refuted associations between films, characters, cast members, or voice actors.  
   Incorporate these clarifications explicitly by asking questions that reflect the ambiguity or correction.

3. **Focus on Key Factual Dimensions:**
   - Temporal specifics — e.g., was person X holding position Y at year Z?  
   - Positional or role verification — e.g., did actor A portray character B in film C?  
   - Identity disambiguation — e.g., is name X a breed or an individual dog’s registered name?  
   - Location-specific queries — e.g., is location A part of country B?  
   - Relationship or professional overlap — e.g., do two individuals share the same profession or roles?

4. **Question Types:**
   - Prefer direct yes/no questions or fact-check style queries ("Was X the Y at time Z?", "Do A and B share characteristic C?", "Is entity X located in Y?").  
   - Where the claim conflates multiple facts, form queries that separate each factual component clearly.  
   - When multiple entities or facts are involved, the query should explicitly reference all of them to clarify their relationships or distinctions.

5. **Natural Language and Clarity:**
   - Your queries must be fluent, grammatically correct, and easily understood by humans or automated systems.  
   - Avoid unnecessary complexity or verbose phrasing; be succinct but comprehensive enough to capture the essential fact to check.

6. **Strategic Reasoning for Query Formation:**
   - Identify the main factual claims embedded in the `claim`.  
   - Analyze the `notes` to understand whether the claim is contradicted, supported, or ambiguous.  
   - Use the notes to pinpoint which precise fact or relationship requires further evidence or clarification.  
   - Compose a question that directly targets that fact, anticipating that the answer will confirm or refute the claim.

Outcome:
Your well-crafted `query` will serve as a focused retrieval prompt, enabling the gathering of authoritative evidence to verify the claim efficiently and accurately.

Claim: "The French star of The Cutting Edge voiced Nala in an animated 1994 film."  
Notes: Indicate that Nala is voiced by American actresses in Disney's The Lion King (1994), no French actor or The Cutting Edge connection.  
Query: "Did a French actress who starred in The Cutting Edge provide the voice for the character Nala in the 1994 animated film The Lion King, particularly in the French-language version?"

By following these detailed instructions and domain nuances, you will reliably generate precise query questions suited to fact verification tasks involving complex, multi-faceted claims with supporting or refuting notes.
