Task Description:
You are given two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a `query` that is a clear, focused, and specific search question or set of questions designed to retrieve relevant documents necessary for verifying the truthfulness of the claim. The `query` should be constructed based on both the claim and the evidence or information found in the `notes`.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A declarative statement containing a factual assertion that needs verification.
- `notes`: A list of textual evidences, references, or fact snippets related to the claim, which may confirm, partially confirm, refute, or provide context around the claim.

Output:
- `query`: One or more concise but detailed and precise questions formulated to retrieve all required evidence to verify the claim’s accuracy.

Detailed Guidelines and Domain-Specific Details:
1. The purpose of the `query` is to support fact-checking by guiding retrieval of pertinent documents that either confirm or refute the claim.
2. Your query should explicitly reflect the underlying verification requirements implied in the claim and reinforced or complicated by the `notes`.
3. If the claim involves multiple parts or entities (e.g., two subjects linked to a property or two conditions to be true simultaneously), the query must clearly address each part to ensure comprehensive evidence retrieval.
4. The notes often include:
   - Names of people, places, works of art or film, events, or dates associated with the claim.
   - Contextual clarifications, such as corrected assumptions ("No evidence of X"), location details, occupational information, or relational facts.
5. To formulate the query:
   - Identify the core entities or subjects in the claim.
   - Identify the key properties, relationships, or events that must be verified.
   - Cross-reference with the notes to identify which entities or aspects need additional checking or clarification.
6. The query should be phrased as natural language questions (e.g., "Did X do Y?", "Where is X located?", "Who directed Y and does this person have Z property?").
7. When a claim has compound conditions (e.g., two actors starred in two different productions), ensure the query includes multiple related questions or a compound question that addresses these.
8. The queries must be specific enough to retrieve gold-standard documents relevant for fact-checking the claim but general enough to not exclude valid evidence.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Read the claim carefully to extract all factual assertions and entities.
- Analyze the notes for confirming or conflicting information.
- Identify any gaps or specifics requiring further retrieval to fully verify the claim.
- Formulate targeted queries structured as fact-checking questions that explicitly ask for evidence related to those extracted facts or contested points.
- Include all necessary sub-questions when the claim involves multiple facts or entities.

This approach ensures that the queries maximize retrieval of all necessary documents to verify the claim while minimizing noise or irrelevant results.

Example:
Claim: "An English actor starred in 'Hollywood Goes Classical' at the Dorothy Chandler Pavilion and also starred in 'Lethal Obsession'."
Notes: Indicate that Terry Ray starred in the play (American actor), Michael York (English actor) starred in both productions.
Query: "Did any English actor star in 'Hollywood Goes Classical' at the Dorothy Chandler Pavilion? Additionally, did that same English actor star in 'Lethal Obsession'?"

This example illustrates forming a query to capture both parts of the claim to retrieve all relevant documentation for fact-checking.

---

Summary of key points to always include in instructions:
- Input fields: `claim` (a factual assertion), `notes` (contextual evidence).
- Output field: `query` (a fact-checking question(s) designed to retrieve necessary evidence).
- Query must cover all aspects of the claim to ensure comprehensive evidence retrieval.
- Use insights from notes to identify verification needs and formulate the query accordingly.
- Query should be clear, specific, natural language questions suitable for document retrieval in fact-checking pipelines.
