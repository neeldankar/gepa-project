Task Description:

You will be given three input fields for fact-checking and evidentiary distillation tasks:

- `claim`: A statement or assertion that may combine one or more factual components. These components can include named entities (people, places, organizations, events, awards, or cultural works), relationships, roles, or classifications. Claims may be complex, composite, or involve implicit information.

- `notes`: A list of factually relevant sentences or fragments related to the claim. These notes may provide supporting evidence, clarifications, corrections, or refutations of different elements in the claim. They may contain partial, redundant, or contradictory information.

- `context`: A list of longer, descriptive documents or encyclopedic entries. These provide detailed background, definitions, biographies, historical accounts, or verification data related to topics, entities, or events mentioned in the claim.

Your outputs are twofold:

1. `new_notes`: Condense and refine the original notes into a succinct, coherent, and accurate subset that directly addresses the factual correctness of the claim. This refined summary should:
   - Resolve inconsistencies and clarify ambiguous points.
   - Exclude irrelevant, redundant, speculative, or unsupported assertions.
   - Highlight key factual points necessary to verify or understand the claim.
   - Distinguish between confirmed facts and unverified or false information.
   - When parts of the claim are partially supported and partially refuted, clearly separate these in the summary.

2. `titles`: A list of concise, precise document titles or topic headings drawn from or inferred from the context. These should represent the core entities, events, or thematic categories that substantiated your reasoning—such as names of people (with accurate professions, achievements, roles), geographic locations (with confirmed administrative hierarchies), events, awards (with distinctions between nominations, wins, or legends), organizations, or cultural artifacts referenced in the claim or notes.

Domain-Specific and Task-Specific Considerations:

- Named Entities:
  - People should be identified with precise roles, professions, or affiliations (e.g., "judge on Music Icons," "producer and singer of Aaina").
  - Clearly disambiguate between similar or related entities with similar names, e.g., differentiate "Junaid Khan" the singer/actor from "Junaid Khan" the judge.
  
- Geographic Hierarchies:
  - Confirm the exact relationship between locations (e.g., city, county, province/state, country).
  
- Awards and Accolades:
  - Carefully confirm distinctions such as winning, nomination, or mentions without confirmation.
  
- Entity Roles:
  - Distinguish carefully between related roles like "athlete," "coach," "trainer," "writer," or "screenplay author," avoiding conflation.

- Evidence Integration:
  - Use the notes and context together to verify claim elements; notes may contain direct assertions and context may offer deeper confirmation or background.
  - Combine indirect or implied evidence cautiously to draw well-supported conclusions.
  
- Partial Truth and Corrections:
  - If the claim mixes true and false elements, reflect each aspect clearly in the summary rather than presenting an all-or-nothing judgment.

Generalizable Strategy:

1. Carefully parse the claim into discrete factual components and key entities referenced.
2. Thoroughly sift through the notes to identify factually relevant data supporting or countering these components; remove duplications and irrelevant details.
3. Use the context documents to confirm, extend, or correct note-based information, focusing on accurate entity identities, roles, place hierarchies, award distinctions, and other domain-specific facts.
4. Summarize the verified information as a clear, concise subset of notes that resolve contradictions and avoid speculation.
5. Select or infer context document titles that contributed decisively to your verification and understanding of the claim.
6. Aim for an output that balances succinctness and completeness to facilitate effective downstream fact-checking and retrieval.

Remember that the overall goal is to produce a distilled factual summary and relevant topic titles that directly aid in validating the claim with maximum accuracy and clarity.
