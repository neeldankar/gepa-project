Task Description:

You are given three input fields:
- `claim`: a statement or assertion that combines one or more factual elements, sometimes complex or composite, potentially involving named entities, relationships, or categorizations.
- `notes`: a list of factually relevant sentences or fragments that refer to or clarify various details related to the claim. These notes may include corrections, clarifications, or evidence supporting or refuting aspects of the claim.
- `context`: a list of longer, encyclopedic or descriptive documents that contain detailed background information, definitions, or verification materials related to entities, events, or topics mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to produce two output fields:
- `new_notes`: a distilled, concise, and accurate subset of the notes that directly address the factual correctness of the claim. This summary should resolve inconsistencies, filter out irrelevant or redundant information from the original notes, and highlight the key factual points needed to verify or understand the claim. If some notes provide strong evidence refuting or supporting claim elements, prioritize those. Avoid including speculative or unsupported assertions.
- `titles`: a list of short, precise document titles or topic headings extracted or inferred from the context that most directly support the reasoning and factual validation of the claim. These should ideally reflect key entities, events, or thematic categories relevant to the claim (e.g., names of notable people, awards, geographic regions, organizations, or cultural artifacts).

Generalizable Strategy:
1. Understand the claim carefully and identify its key components — entities, roles, relationships, and assertions.
2. Examine the notes to differentiate factually relevant information from noise or contradictory details. Aggregate corroborating evidence and important clarifications.
3. Use the context to confirm or supplement note information, focusing on named entities or topics central to the claim.
4. Summarize the essential evidence into `new_notes` that sufficiently support or refute the claim without redundancy.
5. Extract or infer relevant document or topic titles from the context correlated with the verified facts and entities in your summary; ideally selecting documents that substantively contributed to your reasoning.
6. Ensure your output balances brevity and completeness to facilitate effective fact-checking and information retrieval.

Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Named entities might include people (with precise professions, awards, or affiliations), places (including administrative divisions, provinces, or cities), events, awards (e.g. Nobel Prize in Literature), television shows, or cultural productions.
- Pay attention to roles or titles (e.g., “judge on Music Icons”, “producer and singer of Aaina”), ensuring factual correctness about such roles.
- Distinguish between similar-sounding or related entities (e.g., differentiating between “Junaid Khan” as a singer/actor and as a judge) to avoid conflation.
- Confirm geographic hierarchies and jurisdictions carefully (e.g., county, city, province, country) and their relationships.
- When verifying claims about awards and distinctions, be precise whether someone has won, been nominated, or not confirmed.
- Understand that some evidence may be indirect or imply a conclusion rather than stating it explicitly; integrate these carefully.
- In cases where a claim mixes true and unconfirmed or false elements, your summary should clearly separate what is supported from what is not.

The instructions above incorporate the evidence evaluation, entity disambiguation, and context relevance selection strategies that you should apply consistently.
