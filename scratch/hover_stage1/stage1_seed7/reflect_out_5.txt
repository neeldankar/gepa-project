Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`.

- The `claim` is a factual statement that requires verification or exploration through relevant factual information.
- The `notes` field contains multiple short factual sentences or bullet points related to the entities, topics, or concepts mentioned in the claim. These notes may contain partial, indirect, or related information that can help verify the claim or clarify its components.

Your goal is to produce a `query` that is focused, precise, and formulated as one or more questions aimed at retrieving authoritative information necessary to verify or refute the claim based on the notes. The query should be constructed by:
- Identifying the key entities, relationships, or facts in the claim that need to be confirmed.
- Considering the relevant information presented in the notes to uncover what specific questions can fill knowledge gaps or confirm key aspects of the claim.
- Including any relevant constraints such as dates, locations, contextual qualifiers, or attributes specified in the claim.
- If the claim involves comparing two or more entities or concepts (e.g., numbers of acts, awards, geographic proximity), the query should explicitly ask for the comparative or confirmatory data needed.
- When multiple facets of the claim need validation (e.g., who, when, what), structure the query to reflect those different pieces of information, ideally in a single, coherent question or a minimal set.
- Avoid overly broad or vague queries. The query should be narrowly tailored to the claim's verification.
- Include relevant domain-specific terminology when it appears in the claim (e.g., awards like "Golden Bear," specific works like operas or films, administrative units, etc.).
- The output should not restate the claim but instead translate it into a research or fact-finding question(s).

Reasoning Strategy (Implicit for you to follow):
- Parse the claim into discrete factual assertions.
- Use the notes to identify which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or unverified.
- Identify what specific information is needed to retrieve documents potentially missing or crucial for full verification (also known as gold documents).
- Formulate a query that targets these missing or ambiguous knowledge gaps to enable retrieval of those documents.
- Your query should facilitate retrieving documents that directly help confirm or deny the claim components.

Examples of domain-specific details from past tasks to keep in mind:
- For geographic claims: local government area names, proximity relations (e.g., "near Higgin's Creek and Deua National Park"), and verification of administrative control.
- For film and award claims: names and years of festivals (e.g., "50th Berlin International Film Festival"), award names (Golden Bear), films' genres or notable attributes (musicals), film cast members and their biographies (e.g., military service).
- For works of art (operas, films): number of acts or specific structural elements, inspiration sources, directors, co-production countries, and correct titles.
- Recognize when claims involve indirect references or need to disambiguate similar names or related works.
- Recognize when data is typically unavailable or undefined (e.g., films usually don't have acts like operas), and reflect that in the query if relevant.

Your output format must be:

### reasoning  
(A concise explanation showing how you parsed the claim, what needs to be verified, and where gaps might exist to inform query construction.)

### query  
(The formulated focused factual query or question(s) designed to retrieve the missing or necessary information to verify the claim.)

Do not include the original claim or notes in your output.
