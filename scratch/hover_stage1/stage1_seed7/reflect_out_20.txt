Task Description:
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a `query` field that, if answered by searching external sources, would effectively verify the truthfulness of the entire claim based on the information in the notes.

Detailed Instructions:
1. **Understand the Claim**: The `claim` field often contains multiple interrelated factual statements involving entities such as people, events, products, dates, relationships, or characteristics. Carefully parse the claim to identify all essential components or sub-claims that must be checked to verify the entire claim.

2. **Leverage the Notes**: The `notes` field contains multiple pieces of relevant factual information, sometimes with redundancy. Use these notes to understand the context and formulate precise questions. The notes typically mention:
   - Specific entities (e.g., persons like Travis Oates, places, sports teams),
   - Dates (e.g., release years, founding years),
   - Relationships (e.g., actor-to-character, manager-to-team),
   - Product or media details (e.g., flavor of a drink, movie titles and years, associated songs or ads).

3. **Formulate the Query**: Construct the `query` as one or more clear, explicit questions that:
    - Directly cover all key facts in the claim that need verification,
    - Use explicit entities, dates, and relationships mentioned in the claim or notes,
    - Are phrased to maximize the chance of retrieving relevant authoritative documents (e.g., Wikipedia pages about movies, teams, songs, or persons involved).

4. **Principles for Effective Queries**:
    - Combine related sub-claims into a single query when they are logically connected ("Did Person X do Y in Year Z, and was Event A before that?").
    - When the claim contains multiple distinct factual points, use conjunctive queries to cover all points simultaneously, avoiding vague or overly broad queries.
    - Include full, specific entity names and context, such as:
      - For people: full name, roles,
      - For teams and sports seasons: team names, founding years, locations,
      - For cultural products: exact movie titles with release year, song titles, associated advertisements,
      - Product attributes like flavor should refer precisely to the named product.
    - Avoid ambiguous or incomplete queries that could miss critical documents.

5. **Domain-Specific Knowledge** (to incorporate when relevant):
    - Actors and voice roles in specific films (e.g., Travis Oates as Piglet in "Winnie the Pooh" 2011).
    - Historical release years and sequels of movies (e.g., "The Jungle Book" 1967 vs. 2003 sequel).
    - Founding years and geographic origins of sports teams, especially clarifying distinctions between American and Canadian baseball teams.
    - Managerial careers and their playing histories related to specific MLB teams (e.g., Tampa Bay Devil Rays managers).
    - Flavors and origins of soft drinks, including Norwegian products and their U.S. derivatives (e.g., Solo, Urge, Surge, Sunkist).
    - Associations between songs and product advertisements (e.g., the "Krupa" song linked to a specific soft drink).
  
6. **Expected Output Format**:
    - Provide a natural language question or series of questions that would guide evidence retrieval.
    - Ensure the query explicitly encompasses all elements of the claim requiring verification.
    - Queries should be concise yet comprehensive enough for retrieval of all relevant evidence.

Summary:
Your main objective is to transform the input `claim` and given contextual `notes` into a focused, entity-rich, and fact-comprehensive `query` aimed at collecting authoritative evidence to verify or refute the entire claim. Use the notes to identify gaps or key fact checks required and ensure your query targets these precisely. Avoid overly generic or incomplete queries, as they reduce retrieval success.
