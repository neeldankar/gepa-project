Task Overview:  
You are given two fields, `claim` and `notes`, and must produce a single field, `query`. Your goal is to generate one or more specific, natural language question(s) that precisely target the key factual components in the `claim` for verification, using the contextual clarifications or corrections provided in `notes`. These queries will be used to retrieve authoritative evidence to confirm or refute the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A statement or set of assertions containing multiple entities (people, places, organizations), roles, dates, or relationships that make fact-checking necessary.  
- `notes`: A list of sentences providing relevant context, clarifications, or contradictions related to the claim. These notes may highlight errors, ambiguous facts, temporal or role changes, conflations of entities, missing data, or geographic hierarchies.

Output:  
- `query`: One or more focused, natural language question(s) that directly address the key factual elements in the `claim` requiring verification. The queries must be clear, precise, and suitable for use in retrieving authoritative documents.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Guidelines:  

1. **Purpose of the Query**  
   - The query should isolate the core factual claim(s) from the possibly compound or conflated statement.  
   - It should aim to disambiguate, verify, or seek clarifications where the notes identify inaccuracies or incomplete information.

2. **Key Elements to Target**  
   - Names and roles: Confirm exact positions, titles, or professional roles at relevant times.  
   - Dates and temporal relationships: Question who held which role at which date, or who was born when.  
   - Locations: Clarify geo-hierarchies (city vs. county vs. state) or whether multiple locations/entities coexist in the same place.  
   - Relationships among entities: Check whether two or more named entities share a profession, affiliation, or characteristic.  
   - Avoid conflations: If the claim improperly combines facts from different entities or times, break queries into precise parts reflecting each fact.

3. **Formulating Effective Queries**  
   - Use clear, specific, natural English phrasing in question form, e.g.,  
     - “Was [Person A] the [Role] in [Year/Period]?”  
     - “Is [Location A] located within [Location B]?”  
     - “Did [Person A] and [Person B] share the same profession or role?”  
   - Prefer yes/no or direct factual questions that yield definitive supporting or refuting evidence.  
   - Where multiple related facts are being verified, multiple precise questions are acceptable, ideally linked or clearly separated.

4. **Use Notes to Refine the Query**  
   - Identify contradictions, missing data, or corrected facts in the notes to pinpoint what evidence is currently absent or unclear.  
   - Use notes to avoid queries that are too broad or vague (e.g., do not simply ask for all birthdates if the claim only questions relative age).  
   - Specifically address ambiguous claims or where the notes expose a likely conflation or confusion (e.g., nationality misattribution, mixing birthplace vs. birthplace of origin, role confusion).

5. **Examples and Best Practices from Prior Cases**  
   - If birthdates of two persons are relevant but unknown for one, query for both birthdates and the relative age comparison.  
   - When multiple colleges or locations might be conflated, query about presence/location of each and examine the connection to the person named.  
   - If a claim involves nationality, birth year, and roles for actors and notes contradict or clarify these, separate each fact into distinct queries targeting each point.

6. **Overall Approach**  
   - Read the claim carefully to parse all key entities and factual relationships asserted.  
   - Review notes thoroughly for contradictions, missing information, or clarifications.  
   - Formulate one or more succinct questions, precisely focused on verifying specific fact(s).  
   - Ensure queries are suitable for subsequent retrieval tasks that will find and confirm or refute the claim’s truth.  
   - Avoid excess or irrelevant details; keep queries natural, targeted, and unambiguous.

By following these instructions, you will produce precise, high-quality queries that effectively bridge the gap between the claim and the evidence needed to verify it.
