Task Description:
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a complex factual statement that may involve multiple components—such as names of persons (e.g., actors), titles of works (e.g., TV series, films), dates, roles, awards, or other biographical or production details. The `notes` field is a list of extracted factual statements or evidence from external documents relevant to the claim. These notes might include confirmations, contradictions, or absence of evidence for parts of the claim.

Your task is to produce a concise, well-scoped `query` string that can be used to retrieve additional supporting or contradictory documents to verify the claim. The query should be carefully formulated to:
- Target the key factual elements or entities involved in the claim (e.g., actor names, character names, titles of works, awards).
- Focus on the aspects that are ambiguous, unsupported, or disputed according to the notes.
- Include relevant disambiguations or clarifications inferred from the notes (e.g., distinguishing between similarly named works or roles, specifying precise award names or character titles).
- Incorporate temporal information if dates are relevant to the claim’s truth (e.g., premiere dates, years an actor joined a cast).
- Aim to retrieve missing but critical documents that would help fully verify or refute the claim, especially those documents identified as still needed in the feedback.
- Avoid redundancy by combining multiple interrelated components into a single coherent query where appropriate.

Additional Domain-Specific Context and Strategies:
- Claims commonly relate to entertainment industry data—actors, film or TV roles, award nominations, character names, series premiere dates, and nationality details.
- Key domain entities often include actor names, character names, film/TV show titles, and award names.
- Ambiguities frequently arise from similar or overlapping data (e.g., the same actor in different roles or shows with overlapping dates).
- The notes often specify contradictions or clarifications (e.g., disproving an actor’s nationality or correcting the timeline of events).
- The query should aim to retrieve any missing entities or documents noted in the feedback as still needed, e.g., secondary shows or lesser-known roles connected to the claim.
- When multiple components of the claim are checked, queries should connect them logically to retrieve documents that clarify their relationship or distinctions.
- The reasoning should reflect an understanding of these nuances, but the final output is only the `query`.

Example Approach:
- Identify the principal entities in the claim (e.g., actor, TV series, role).
- Analyze notes to see what has been confirmed or disproved and where gaps remain.
- Formulate a query that requests data linking the entities or clarifying contested details (e.g., Is actor X nominated for award Y for role Z in series A? When did series B premiere relative to actor X joining it? What is the nationality of actor X who appeared in productions M and N?).
- Make sure the query is clear, focused, and precise enough to fetch the required documents to verify the claim fully.

Summary of Instruction:
Given the `claim` and the extraction-based `notes`, produce a single clear, precise, and comprehensive `query`. This query should aim to retrieve the documents containing missing or clarifying information needed to verify the claim’s truth, focusing on the interplay of actors, productions, roles, dates, and awards critical to the claim. Use the notes and prior feedback insights to target unresolved or ambiguous factual elements in the claim.
