Given the fields `claim` and `notes`, produce the field `query`.

Task Description:
You are verifying or investigating the factual accuracy of the given `claim` using the provided `notes`, which contain relevant supporting or refuting evidence. Your goal is to formulate one or more precise, focused **natural language question(s)** (the `query`) that directly target the core factual components or relationships in the `claim`. These queries will be used to retrieve authoritative documents to confirm or refute the claim.

Key Guidelines and Domain-Specific Details:
- The `claim` may contain multiple entities, roles, dates, locations, or assertions combined together. Your `query` should disentangle these components and seek clarity on ambiguous or potentially conflated facts highlighted by the `notes`.
- The `notes` provide context, corrections, or clarifications related to the claim—often pointing out inaccuracies or mixing of facts (e.g., a person’s role changing over time, place names nested within larger administrative regions, or multiple entities sharing a profession).
- Your `query` should:
  - Target precise fact verification or disambiguation based on the contradictions or confirmations evident in the `notes`.
  - Focus on identifying temporal, positional, or relational details critical to verifying the claim (e.g., exact job titles at a given year, locations down to country/state/city level, professional roles shared by named individuals).
  - Use clear and specific phrasing, typically in the form of yes/no or direct factual questions (e.g., “Was X the Y at time Z?”, “Are A and B located in the same country?”, “Do C and D share profession E?”).
- Avoid overly broad or vague queries; instead, refine the question to pinpoint the fact that needs verification or clarification.
- Where the claim conflates multiple facts (e.g., combining a present role with a role held in the past), address that directly in the query.
- When multiple entities or components appear in the claim (e.g., three different people, or two places), the query can explicitly reference all relevant entities and their factual relationships.
- The query should be expressed in natural, fluent English suitable for directing a subsequent retrieval or verification step.

Example approach:
1. Identify the main factual assertion(s) in the claim.
2. Note any conflicting or clarifying points in the notes.
3. Frame a succinct question that tests the claim's key factual claim(s), especially where the note highlights ambiguity or correction.
4. Ensure the question leads to retrieving authoritative evidence to support or refute the claim.

Overall, your role is to bridge the claim and the evidence by formulating precise queries that target the critical fact(s) necessary for fact-checking.
