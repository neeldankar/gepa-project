Given inputs consisting of the fields:

- `claim` (a factual or composite factual statement potentially involving entities, dates, relationships, sponsors, durations, or memberships), and
- `notes` (a list of supporting or contradictory factual sentences that provide evidential context about entities, their attributes, relationships, dates, and sponsorships relevant to the claim),

your task is to generate the field:

- `query` — a set of clear, concise, and focused factual verification questions derived directly from the claim and guided by the provided notes.

Instructions and detailed considerations:

1. **Objective:** The `query` should be designed to elicit precise answers that would verify the truthfulness or accuracy of the claim by checking key factual components separately or in small, verifiable units.

2. **Domain-specific factual elements often encountered:**
   - Verification of time durations or exact years related to collaborations or affiliations (e.g., "worked for exactly three years").
   - Proper identification of entities (e.g., musicians, bands, football clubs, institutions) with attention to possible name confusions or distinctions (e.g., "Ford Motors F.C." vs. "Vauxhall Motors F.C.").
   - Correct geographical or national origins of entities such as record labels or bands (e.g., American vs. British).
   - Historical establishment dates of institutions compared to one another.
   - Identification of league memberships and sponsorship relationships in the context of football clubs and leagues.
   - Confirming sponsorship affiliations and correcting any claim inaccuracies regarding sponsors.
   - Highlighting when multiple entities are conflated or misrepresented in the claim.

3. **Methodology:**
   - Carefully analyze the `claim` and parse it into its component factual assertions.
   - Use the `notes` to identify relevant key facts, contradictions, or missing evidence that pertain to these assertions.
   - For each assertion, formulate specific verification questions (queries) that are:
     - Factually focused (e.g., Did X occur? Is Y true? Was Z established before A?)
     - Disambiguating (e.g., Are entities A and B correctly identified and distinguished?)
     - Checking relationships or properties (e.g., Is league L sponsored by sponsor S? Is club C part of league L?)
   - When claims include multiple assertions, separate them into distinct queries for clarity.
   - Ensure the query covers points on which the claim is ambiguous or contested according to the notes.
   - Do not include reasoning or conclusion in the `query` — only the questions intended for factual verification.

4. **Formatting:**
   - The `query` should be written in clear, natural language, typically as one or more interrogative sentences.
   - Multiple questions can be combined or separated by conjunctions or clearly punctuated.

5. **Additional tips:**
   - Avoid assuming knowledge not present in the notes.
   - If the claim incorrectly conflates or confuses entities, reflect that in the query to clarify (e.g., "Is X actually Y?")
   - Incorporate all key disputable points that arise from the claim and notes for comprehensive verification.

By following this detailed approach, the `query` will serve as an effective tool to verify the claim's accuracy based on evidence from the `notes`.
