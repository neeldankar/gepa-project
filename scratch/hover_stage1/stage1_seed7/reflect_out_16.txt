Task Title:
Formulating precise natural language queries to fact-check claims by leveraging contextual evidence.

Task Overview:
You will be provided with a factual claim and a set of notes containing relevant contextual information—these notes may confirm, refute, or clarify specific aspects of the claim. Your goal is to produce one or more concise, focused natural language question(s) (the “query”) that directly target the core factual components or relationships within the claim. These queries will be used to retrieve authoritative documents or evidence to verify or refute the claim.

Detailed Task Description:
- The claim may include multiple entities (persons, bands, authors, songs, etc.), roles or titles, dates, locations, or layered assertions. Your query should break down and disentangle these components, especially when the notes indicate potential conflations, corrections, or ambiguities.
- The provided notes often point out specific nuances about:
  - Temporal aspects (e.g., a person’s role at a specific time, when a song reached a chart position).
  - Relational details (e.g., who first recorded a song, whether two people share a profession, collaborations).
  - Geographical hierarchies or clarifications (e.g., location of a band relative to city/country/state).
  - Identity clarifications (e.g., distinguishing between individuals with similar names, or bands with similar names).
  - Missing information or lack of evidence, highlighting unverified parts of the claim.
- Your query should:
  - Explicitly address any ambiguity or mixing of facts evident in the claim or notes.
  - Be phrased as precise, targeted natural language questions that address the key verifiable facts.
  - Follow clear formats such as:
    - “Was X the Y in year Z?”
    - “Did A collaborate with B in context C?”
    - “Are X and Y located in the same country?”
    - “Which songs by artist A reached #1 on chart B?”
    - “Was the first recording of song C by a country music singer?”
    - “Is there evidence linking person A to entity B?”
  - Avoid vague, broad, or multi-part questions that cannot lead to retrieval of focused evidence.
  - Disentangle claims that bundle multiple timeframes or roles, e.g., a present role mixed with a past title.
  - Reference all relevant entities when multiple are involved, to reflect their factual relationships or contrasts.
- When notes indicate information is missing or unverified, the query should explicitly seek clarifying or confirming evidence to address that gap.
- Where the claim is supported for one part but unverified or contradicted for another, the query should address both parts distinctly.

General Approach/Strategy:
1. Extract the main factual assertions in the claim.
2. Identify any conflicting, missing, or clarifying information in the notes.
3. Formulate concise and exact natural language questions focused precisely on verifying or clarifying these key points.
4. Use domain-specific knowledge to frame correct questions (e.g., understanding chart rankings, recording artist roles, geographical hierarchies).
5. Anticipate that the query will be fed into a retrieval or verification pipeline, so precision and clarity are critical.
6. Keep queries natural and fluent, suitable for human interpretation and automated evidence retrieval.

Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Music context: Know about chart rankings (e.g., Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks), first recording artist vs. performers, genre affiliations, collaborations.
- Biography context: Differentiate between persons with similar names; verify roles, hobbies, dates; clarify missing evidence.
- Location context: Understand nested administrative divisions (city/county/state/country).
- Artistic collaborations: Confirm whether a person was involved in bands or projects, and their genre classifications.
- When multiple entities or facts appear, each related query component should be explicit and separable to target the critical fact-checking needs.

Outcome:
Your generated query(ies) will guide data retrieval to confirm or refute the claim by precisely illuminating which factual components need investigation. The queries must be natural language questions phrased clearly for direct factual verification.
