Task Description:

You are given a claim (a statement that may be true or false) and a set of notes containing relevant factual information extracted from various documents. Your goal is to produce a precise, focused search query (or set of queries) that would enable retrieval of documents or evidence to verify the given claim. 

Input Format:
- claim: A single textual claim that references entities, events, or relationships.
- notes: A list of textual facts and observations related to the claim, often including information about named entities, connections between subjects, and the presence or absence of evidence supporting the claim.

Output:
- query: A concise, targeted query that aims to find the necessary information to verify or refute the claim. The query should mention key entities, relationships, or attributes relevant to the claim’s verification.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Guidance:
1. The claim may reference specific people, bands, films, awards, or other entities—pay close attention to the named entities and their roles (e.g., "Nivek Ogre" as a band member involved in a side project, "Frank Sinatra" as an award-winning singer).
2. Notes often explicitly mention entities involved, whether a certain connection or fact is present or absent, sometimes clarifying ambiguous aliases or clarifying title references (e.g., "German Frank Sinatra" maps to Frank Sinatra himself).
3. The claim may involve comparisons (e.g., number of awards) or equivalence between entities or require verification of multiple assertions within a claim (e.g., actor starring in a specific movie and portraying a role in another film).
4. Your query should target these key points:
   - Identification of entities mentioned in the claim that need confirmation (e.g., "co-director of Winning America").
   - Verification of specific attributes or facts tied to these entities (e.g., awards won, participation in a project, occupation).
   - Relationships between these entities and attributes/events (e.g., whether the same actor played Marilyn Monroe and starred in a thriller).
5. When the notes clarify a claim includes ambiguous or unclear entities, disambiguate or restate the most probable interpretation within your query (e.g., treat “German Frank Sinatra” as “Frank Sinatra”).
6. The queries should be directly answerable by factual documents and designed to retrieve documents referenced as "gold documents" potentially containing the evidence.
7. If the notes indicate absence of certain information, phrase queries to request confirmation or denial, reflecting the uncertainty.
8. Keep queries clear and focused, covering all necessary aspects of the claim in a logically structured manner, potentially using multiple sub-questions if the claim involves multiple linked facts.
9. Include in queries terms or entities that are relevant to the claim verification but not explicitly in the claim if the notes highlight them as critical (e.g., Rx (band) as a side project linking to the Skinny Puppy member).

Example Strategy:
- Break down complex claims into component facts.
- Identify entities and key relationships to verify.
- Use notes to disambiguate any ambiguous references.
- Formulate queries aiming to find facts on named entities, their roles, and their connections.
- Ask about specific attributes or facts critical for verifying the claim’s truth.

This approach enables systematic retrieval of factual evidence from external knowledge sources to verify or refute the claim efficiently.
