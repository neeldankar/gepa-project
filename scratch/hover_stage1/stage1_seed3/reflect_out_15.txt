You will be given three input fields: 
- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion that needs to be evaluated or elaborated upon.  
- `notes`: A list of sentences or short paragraphs containing evidence, background facts, or related information relevant to the claim. These notes often contain duplicate or overlapping facts, variations on the same information, and mention multiple relevant entities or data points.  
- `context`: A collection of detailed documents, encyclopedia-style entries, or extended factual descriptions that provide authoritative background information about entities or topics referenced in the claim and notes. These documents often include names, dates, definitions, historical context, classifications, and distinctions between similar or related entities.

Your task is to produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`:  
   - Distill and consolidate the essential, non-redundant factual information from the `notes` that directly support, refute, or clarify the claim.  
   - Remove duplicates and extraneous information while preserving necessary nuanced facts.  
   - When relevant, incorporate clarifications or disambiguations drawn from the `context` to distinguish entities with similar names or characteristics.  
   - The new notes should present a concise, logically coherent representation of evidence aligned with the claim’s truthfulness or falsehood.  
   - The notes should emphasize correct attributions (e.g., composer names, recognized dog breeds, or actor roles) and clarify any common confusions seen between similar items (e.g., mistaken identities, overlapping roles).

2. `titles`:  
   - Identify and list key entity titles or document names from the `context` that are most relevant and foundational for validating the claim.  
   - The titles should correspond to authoritative or primary sources that substantiate or clarify the claim and the notes.  
   - Include titles of people, works (e.g., musical compositions, films), breeds, or other concepts critical to the factual understanding of the claim.  
   - Prioritize titles that cover key differentiations or controversy points raised by the claim (e.g., correctly identifying the breed of a dog, the known roles of an actor, or attributions of musical compositions).

Additional domain-specific considerations and strategies to keep in mind for this task:  

- In cases involving dog breeds or animal classification, recognize distinctions among closely named breeds, crossbreeds, landrace breeds, and breed recognition status by authoritative kennel clubs. Clarify the breed’s regional origin and cultural significance if mentioned.  
- For claims about actors, verify that the roles and associations across different productions (movies, TV shows) align, especially when the claim relates to an actor being known for multiple works. Address common confusions between actors with overlapping but non-identical credits.  
- When dealing with classical music compositions and composers, ensure the right attribution, nationality, gender, catalog numbers, and temporality of the work are confirmed. Disambiguate composers with similar or related works, noting birthplaces and historical contexts.  
- Employ logical reasoning to compare the claim’s assertion against the evidence in notes and context, identifying contradictions or confirmations.  
- Deliver concise reasoning in the `new_notes` by summarizing key facts without narrative opinion but highlighting the fact-checking logic implicitly.  
- The `titles` should serve as direct pointers to definitive resources used for the evaluation, facilitating retrieval of further authoritative information if necessary.

Overall, your goal is to synthesize and clarify factual evidence relevant to the claim using the provided notes and context, producing a precise and informative set of notes and an accurate list of document/entity titles that form the foundation of the claim’s verification.
