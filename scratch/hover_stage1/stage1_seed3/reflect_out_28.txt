Task Description:  
You will be provided with three distinct inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your objective is to analyze these inputs to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. This task is focused on verifying or evaluating the truthfulness, validity, or accuracy of the given claim using the provided evidence.  

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A statement that asserts facts or relationships involving one or more entities, dates, events, or attributes. It may contain domain-specific information related to fields such as music, sports, literature, history, or television.  
- `notes`: A list of textual statements or snippets extracted from various sources relevant to the claim; these may be partially redundant, repetitive, overlapping, contradictory, or tangentially related.  
- `context`: A list of background documents, descriptions, or comprehensive text segments containing detailed, authoritative information on entities, events, awards, organizations, or concepts directly or indirectly related to the claim. These serve as supporting evidence to verify parts of the claim.  

Your Task:  
1. Generate `new_notes` — a refined, concise, and factually relevant synthesis filtered from the `notes` and `context` that directly address the claim’s truthfulness. These should:  
   - Remove duplications, contradictions, and minor or indirect mentions present in the original `notes`.  
   - Emphasize key factual content that supports or refutes the claim with evidence drawn from both `notes` and `context`.  
   - Present clear, standalone factual statements that can be understood without needing the original inputs.  
   - Include domain-specific factual details that relate to the claim’s subject, such as:  
     * In sports: specifics about seasons, venues, player or team affiliations, and chronology of events (e.g., which stadium hosted a final, which team plays at which venue, and event dates).  
     * In music: confirmation of songwriter identities, band nationalities, song release details, and artist affiliations.  
     * In literature and awards: identifying laureates, their nationalities, prize details, and their professional roles or categorizations (e.g., poet-diplomat, novelist).  
     * In television and media: series premiere years, actor participation in specific roles, or show distinctions when multiple similar series or characters might cause confusion.  
   - Reconcile any conflicting data points by logically reasoning the most accurate or supported facts.  

2. Generate `titles` — a short list of document or entity titles from the `context` that most effectively represent the critical background and facts used to verify the claim. These should:  
   - Align closely with the entities, events, or subjects mentioned in the `claim` and corroborated in the `new_notes`.  
   - Enhance traceability and understanding by highlighting authoritative or primary sources.  
   - Avoid including unrelated, vague, or peripheral documents.  
   - Strive for a balance between comprehensive coverage of essential aspects and conciseness (often 2-4 titles).  

Domain-Specific Reasoning Notes:  
- Establish clear chronological or categorical relationships when claims depend on timelines (e.g., event dates, prize years, or participation seasons).  
- Differentiate entities that might be confused due to similarities in names, functions, or categories (e.g., two different TV series with overlapping actor appearances, or multiple football stadiums in different countries).  
- Use domain knowledge to correctly interpret terminologies (e.g., knowing that a poet-diplomat is inherently a writer, or distinguishing between rally and circuit racing series).  
- Verify geographic and organizational associations accurately (e.g., correct country affiliations of clubs, artists, or venues).  
- Detect whether the claim conflates related but distinct facts and clarify the true relationships.  

Generalizable Strategy for Task Completion:  
- First, thoroughly read and comprehend the claim to identify the core fact(s) being asserted.  
- Review `notes` to extract relevant evidence but recognize and remove redundancies or repeated information.  
- Use `context` materials to cross-verify, deepen understanding, and resolve contradictions or gaps in the `notes`.  
- Synthesize a logically consistent, concise set of statements (`new_notes`) that clearly support or refute the claim, including all critical factual details needed for evaluation.  
- Select the most pertinent `titles` from the `context` that correspond directly to the verified facts to aid traceability.  

Output Format:  
- `new_notes`: A list of succinct, standalone factual statements addressing the claim.  
- `titles`: A list of 2–4 relevant titles representing authoritative context documents or entities.  

This approach ensures a focused, evidence-based verification process that integrates complex domain-specific knowledge and clarifies ambiguous or conflicting input information.
