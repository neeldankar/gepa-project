Task Description:
You will be provided with three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to critically analyze these inputs and produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A factual or semi-factual statement that needs to be assessed.
- `notes`: A list of extracted key points from relevant documents or knowledge sources related to the claim. These may contain affirmations, contradictions, or clarifications regarding elements of the claim.
- `context`: A list of source document snippets or summaries providing background and detailed information pertinent to the claim. These documents cover niche, domain-specific, and factual knowledge ranging from biographical details, organizational headquarters, product flavors, burial locations, to historical background.

Outputs:
- `new_notes`: A distilled, concise, and factually accurate summary derived from the `notes` and `context` that directly supports or refutes the `claim`. These notes should be carefully curated to emphasize key verified facts and eliminate redundancy, contradictions, or irrelevant details. The `new_notes` should represent a coherent summary of the relevant factual information that clarifies the truthfulness or accuracy of the claim.
  
- `titles`: A list of document or entity titles from the `context` that were most relevant and critical for forming the `new_notes`. These should correspond to the key sources that jointly enable verification or negation of the claim.

Detailed Requirements and Best Practices:
1. **Claim Analysis:** Examine the provided `claim` to identify all factual components that can be verified, refuted, or remain unsupported.

2. **Cross-Referencing:** Utilize `notes` and `context` in tandem to:
   - Confirm or dispute statements in the claim.
   - Identify errors, misconceptions, or inaccuracies presented in the claim.
   - Identify missing or correcting information that clarifies the claim.

3. **Domain-Specific Knowledge:** Be aware that claims and notes might involve:
   - Specific knowledge about people (e.g., their professions, burial places, biographical facts).
   - Organizations (e.g., airline headquarters, company origins, product lines).
   - Product attributes (e.g., flavors of soft drinks, advertising campaigns).
   - Geographic and historical domains (e.g., cemetery locations, park boundaries, historical facts about companies or events).
   
   Many claims may involve niche details, such as the headquarters location of airlines, distinctions between similarly named individuals, or specific flavors of branded products.

4. **Fact Consistency:**  
   - Identify and resolve contradictions within the `notes` and between the claim and notes/context.
   - Clearly indicate when a claim is unsupported vs. contradicted by evidence.
   - Avoid assumptions not supported by any note or context data.

5. **Summary Generation (`new_notes`):**  
   - Provide a non-redundant, accurate summary that addresses and clarifies the claim.
   - Incorporate only those facts essential for evaluating the claim.
   - Ensure the summary is easy to understand, factually precise, and domain-informed.

6. **Source Attribution (`titles`):**  
   - List the names or titles of the key documents, articles, or entities from the `context` that were essential in verifying the claim.
   - These should be the most pertinent and authoritative documents corroborating or negating the claim.

7. **Reasoning (implicit in outputs):**  
   If useful, internally reason about the claim's validity and the relationships between notes and context, but do not include explicit reasoning text in the final output fields.

Summary:
Your task is to validate or invalidate the provided claim by synthesizing factual evidence from both extracted notes and broader contextual documents, producing a focused factual summary `new_notes` and referencing the most relevant source `titles`. This requires careful attention to niche domain factual information, resolving ambiguities, and clarifying complex or nuanced claims about persons, organizations, products, or locations.
