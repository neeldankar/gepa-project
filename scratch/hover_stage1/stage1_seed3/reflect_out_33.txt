You are given three input fields:

- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion.
- `notes`: a list of sentences, extracted from various sources, that may contain evidence, partial evidence, or contradictory information related to the claim.
- `context`: a list of documents or document summaries, each describing entities (people, places, films, etc.) or related topics relevant to the claim.

Your task is to process these inputs and produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: a concise, focused subset of the given `notes` that directly supports, partially supports, or provides essential context for evaluating the claim. Filter out redundant or irrelevant notes. Summarize and paraphrase if needed to maintain clarity and relevance. When contradictory information exists, include notes that clarify uncertainties or indicate lack of verification.

2. `titles`: a list of document or entity titles from the `context` that are most essential to understanding or verifying the claim. These titles typically correspond to key entities (persons, films, places, concepts) mentioned in the `claim` and supported or explained by the `notes` and `context`. Include all core documents that are central to confirming or disproving the claim.

Guidelines and domain-specific details for effective performance:

- Claims often involve relationships between persons, films, places, or other entities, such as birthplaces, directorship credits, and geographical or biographical attributes.
- Pay careful attention to roles and titles in the context, such as who directed which film (e.g., co-directors vs directors), birthplace details (city, state, country), film release dates, and filmography finalities.
- Be aware of geographical relationships (e.g., comparing countries by size) and population rankings of cities within states or countries.
- For claims comparing entities (e.g., country sizes, city population ranks), verify the hierarchy with explicit statements from notes or context.
- Carefully identify all key entities referenced directly or indirectly in the claim. The titles output should comprehensively cover these entities to ensure full coverage of claim verification.
- When the claim involves ambiguous or unsupported information (e.g., birthplace of a person is unknown), reflect this uncertainty in `new_notes` and do not fabricate supporting information.
- Avoid including tangential or unrelated documents in `titles`; prioritize completeness and relevance.
- When multiple valid notes convey similar information, prefer succinct, clear, and corroborated versions.
- The reasoning to select notes and titles should be traceable: notes should reflect the claim's key assertions, and titles should enable direct retrieval of verifying documents.

This approach supports downstream fact verification or claim validation by isolating critical evidence and pertinent document references.
