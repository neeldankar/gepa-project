Task Description:  
You will be given two inputs:  
- `claim`: a factual or comparative statement about entities primarily from domains such as films, television, music bands, literature, or sports. These claims often involve classifications (e.g., genres), relationships (e.g., character or actor roles), temporal orderings (e.g., release or premiere dates), or quantitative attributes (e.g., number of group members).  
- `notes`: supporting factual sentences extracted from relevant knowledge sources that provide partial evidence, clarifications, or contradictions about the claim.

Your output must be:  
- `query`: a concise, precise, and focused factual question or set of closely related questions. The query should explicitly target the missing, unclear, or confirmatory facts necessary to verify or refute the claim based on the information given.

Detailed Guidance and Domain Nuances:  

1. **Core Verification Focus:**  
   Your query should explicitly seek:  
   - Exact or nuanced classifications or categories (e.g., specific genre labels for movies or bands).  
   - Precise membership counts or comparisons between groups or team rosters.  
   - Temporal sequencing of events, especially regarding release dates, premieres, or activity periods (e.g., the order of movie releases or broadcast dates for TV seasons).  
   - Disambiguation of entities with similar or ambiguous names (e.g., distinguishing between multiple versions of a movie, similarly named bands, or similarly titled works).  
   - Confirmation of individual roles or associations (e.g., which actor played which character in which version of a film).

2. **Analyze the Claim with Domain Context:**  
   - Movie genres may overlap but often emphasize certain elements (family, comedy, adventure, action); specify which genre classification must be affirmed.  
   - Voice actor roles are tightly coupled with specific film versions and release years; link roles explicitly.  
   - Band or group comparisons require counting members or verifying genre labels carefully to confirm exclusivity or overlap.  
   - Temporal claims hinge on exact release or premiere dates; ensure your query clearly identifies which entities' timelines you want compared.

3. **Leverage Provided Notes:**  
   Use notes to detect which aspects are already resolved, conflicted, or absent. Your query should aim only to fill missing gaps or clarify doubts identified. Avoid asking about information already explicit or confirmed in the notes.

4. **Formulating the Query:**  
   - Keep queries as narrowly focused as possible while covering all unresolved claim points.  
   - When multiple facts need verification, bundle closely related questions to avoid unnecessary breadth.  
   - Include relevant contextual identifiers, such as years (e.g., film release year), full names, or specific event titles, to prevent ambiguity.  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions; prioritize those that yield decisive data to confirm or refute the claim.

5. **Generalizable Strategy:**  
   - Break down compound claims into distinct factual components that must be checked.  
   - For each component, identify whether the notes provide confirming info, contradictions, or no info.  
   - Frame your query exclusively to resolve unresolved components with specificity and clarity.  
   - Think in terms of "What minimal, precise factual question(s) will determine the truth of the claim?"

6. **Domain-Specific Examples:**  
   - When verifying genres, do not just ask "What is the movie genre?" but "Is [movie title] classified as [specific genre or genres]?"  
   - To verify temporal order: "Was [Movie A] released before or after [Movie B]?" or "What are the release dates of [Entity 1] and [Entity 2]?"  
   - For actor roles: "Did [Actor Name] perform in [Film/Season/Role]?" or "Which character did [Actor Name] portray in [Specific Film Version]?"  
   - For membership counts: "How many members did [Band A] have during [specific year]?" or "Does [Band A] have more members than [Band B]?"  

Your objective is to produce a **minimal but sufficient set of factual questions** that, when answered with relevant documents or data, supports a definitive verification or refutation of the claim.
