- `claim`: a statement asserting specific facts or relationships about named entities, such as movie genres, actor nationalities and roles, band member counts, temporal orderings (e.g., release dates or event sequences), character involvement, or location details including administrative boundaries. The notes may also contain background details to help disambiguate or clarify entities.

Your task is to generate a single output:

- `query`: a concise, focused, and targeted question (or minimal set of closely related questions) formulated to retrieve or confirm the precise missing or uncertain factual information necessary to verify or refute the claim.

  
Key aspects and domain-specific considerations for generating the `query`:

1. **Focus on Specific Verification Needs:**  
   Identify the exact key factual elements or relationships in the claim that require confirmation. This includes:  
   - Exact genre or category classifications (e.g., movie genres such as comedy, family, adventure, action, or band musical genres).  
   - Precise membership counts or comparisons of group sizes.  
   - Nationality or identity of actors or individuals, especially when overlapping names or ambiguous claims are involved.  
   - Temporal relationships such as debut dates, release years, or event sequences.  
   - The accurate involvement of characters or actors in specific works.  
   - Geographic or administrative distinctions relevant to location claims (e.g., differences between counties, cities, and boroughs).

2. **Use Notes to Pinpoint Missing or Contradictory Information:**  
   Scrutinize the provided notes to determine which parts of the claim are fully covered, contradictory, or unsupported. The `query` should seek specifically those missing or unclear facts necessary for full adjudication, without redundantly querying well-established points.  

3. **Targeted, Minimalist, and Context-Rich Queries:**  
   Frame your question(s) precisely to avoid broad or vague requests. Include relevant context (e.g., full movie titles with years, full entity names, geographic or organizational details) so that retrieved documents focus sharply on the fact-checking need. This will also aid retrieval of the exact authoritative sources (e.g., confirming a lead actor's nationality tied to a specific film, or whether a particular railway terminus is in a named county).

4. **Handle Complex or Multi-Faceted Claims Carefully:**  
   If the claim integrates multiple distinct facts (e.g., a person’s nationality, film debut, and co-starring in a specific movie), include all necessary verification points in the query, but keep the question set minimal and grouped logically to maintain clarity and focus.

5. **Domain-Specific Background Knowledge to Keep in Mind:**

   - Actor roles often require linking to specific films and release years to avoid confusion with similarly named projects or roles.  
   - Band member numbers and genre classifications can be critical for identifying differences in group comparisons.  
   - Temporal relationships depend on accurate dates of release, debut, or official occurrence. Query these dates explicitly when relevant.  
   - Geographic and jurisdictional boundaries (e.g., cities vs counties) are crucial to verify location claims accurately.  
   - Disambiguate similarly named entities by including contextual information like dates, location, or alternate identifiers.

6. **Overall Strategy to Adopt:**  
   - First, parse the claim to extract key fact-check elements.  
   - Next, contrast these with the notes to understand which are supported and which require additional confirmation.  
   - Finally, craft a concise but comprehensive query that efficiently requests all information needed for verification, without redundancy or ambiguity.

  
By following these guidelines, your output `query` will uniquely and precisely direct fact-checking resources or retrieval systems to supply the minimal sufficient information that confirms or refutes the claim in question.
