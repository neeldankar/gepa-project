Task Description:  
You will be given a `claim` and a set of relevant `notes`. The `claim` is a factual statement or comparison about specific entities, often in domains such as movies, operas, bands, or TV shows. It might assert genres, membership counts, character roles, temporal sequences (like release years), or relationships among entities (e.g., who produced or starred in what).

Your goal is to create a precise, clear, and minimal `query` (a question or set of closely related questions) that targets exactly the missing factual information needed to verify or refute the `claim`—nothing more, nothing less. This `query` will then be used to locate or retrieve authoritative source documents that confirm or contradict the claim.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: a textual statement of the fact(s) or comparison(s) to be verified.  
- `notes`: a list of factual sentences mostly related to the claim, including partial evidence, clarifications, or contradictory details about the entities mentioned.

Output Format:  
- `query`: a concise, specific, and targeted question (or set of related questions) designed to retrieve the missing or confirming evidence crucial to evaluating the claim.

Detailed Guidelines and Domain-Specific Knowledge:  
1. **Identify Key Verification Points from the Claim:**  
   - Determine which aspects of the claim require factual verification. For example:  
     - Exact genre classification of a movie or band.  
     - Number of acts in an opera or structural divisions in a film.  
     - Membership counts or identities in ensembles or bands.  
     - Temporal relations such as release dates or sequence of events.  
     - Roles or portrayals by characters or actors (e.g., whether a specific actor played a character in a movie).  
     - Production credits or connections between producers across different works.  
   - Sometimes claims include mixed or contradictory details (e.g., incorrect nationality of a film production). Include queries to clarify those.

2. **Analyze Provided Notes for What Is Known and Unknown:**  
   - If the notes already confirm or refute part of the claim, do not repeat questions on those points.  
   - Focus the query on remaining unknown or ambiguous facts necessary to complete verification.  
   - Use notes to refine the query by focusing on the exact entities, versions, or years (e.g., include the year of the movie, full band names, or exact versions of operas).

3. **Precision and Conciseness:**  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions. Instead, break down complex claims into their minimal factual components needing confirmation.  
   - When multiple points require verification, include them as a logically grouped set of questions for efficient retrieval.  

4. **Domain-Specific Considerations:**  
   - **Movies and Films:**  
     - Films generally do not have defined "acts" like operas; confirm whether act structure exists if the claim compares acts.  
     - Specify the exact movie by including year and nationality/franchise details if relevant to disambiguation.  
     - Be aware of release dates for verifying temporal order claims.  
     - Use full character names and actor names to avoid confusion.  
     - Clarify roles of producers, directors, and main cast in relation to works mentioned.  
   - **Operas:**  
     - Number of acts is a key, well-defined fact (operas have acts; films usually do not).  
     - Confirm composer and number of acts for precise operas mentioned.  
   - **Bands and Musical Groups:**  
     - Confirm genres carefully, as bands may be associated with multiple genres or have nuanced classifications.  
     - Member counts are relevant for claims comparing group sizes.  
     - Use full band or artist names, avoid ambiguity.  
   - **TV Shows, Talent Shows, and Judging Panels:**  
     - Verify exact judges, hosts, or producers serving in specific roles and in specific seasons or events.  
     - Confirm whether certain persons (individuals or groups) were part of judging panels or associated productions.  

5. **Entity Disambiguation:**  
   - When entities have similar names or multiple versions (e.g., movie remakes, albums with similar titles), include relevant identifying information such as year, nationality, or alternate titles in the query.  
   - For actors, ensure that questions specify the character or credited role to avoid confusion, especially if the claim references unusual or incorrect role assignments.  

6. **General Query Formation Strategy:**  
   - Formulate your query as direct questions starting with “Who,” “What,” “Did,” “How many,” or “Is,” focused on the exact unknowns.  
   - If the claim involves two or more entities or facts, include all points that still need verification in the query succinctly but distinctly.  
   - Do not mix unrelated questions together; keep the scope bounded to the claim’s elements.  

Summary:  
Your output query is the minimal set of specific factual questions designed to retrieve the key missing information to accept or reject the claim. Use notes to exclude known facts and refine the query for accurate fact verification. Include domain-specific details (years, full names, roles) necessary to disambiguate and pinpoint the facts. Avoid vague or too general questions by focusing tightly on the claim’s verification points.
