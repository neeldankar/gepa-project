Task Description:
You will be given two inputs: a `claim` — a statement of fact or assertion, and a set of `notes` — pieces of evidence or contextual information related to the claim. Your goal is to generate a single output field: `query`.

The `query` should be a concise, focused natural language question that explicitly targets the key factual relationship or verification needed to assess the claim's validity, based on the information in the `notes`. The `query` is intended to serve as an effective search or retrieval prompt to find supporting or refuting evidence for the claim.

Key Details and Domain-Specific Considerations:
- The `claim` typically asserts the existence, relationship, or timing of events, people, places, or works (e.g., people involved in creative works, dates of events/releases, locations and ownership of venues).
- The `notes` provide domain-specific factual details such as:
  * Exact timing or ordering of events/releases (e.g., an artist’s departure date relative to a DVD release date).
  * Relationships and roles of individuals (e.g., a person’s role as a director or co-director on a named film).
  * Geographic or corporate ownership details (e.g., specific venues located within a hotel-casino and corporate ownership).
  * Naming conventions and explicit titles of works or venues.
- The `query` should specifically target these focal points: precise timing, confirmatory relationships, ownership, or roles linked explicitly to named entities in the claim.
- Avoid vague or overly broad questions. The query should be scoped narrowly enough to retrieve evidence that directly verifies or falsifies the claim.
- If the claim involves comparisons or dual assertions (e.g., "X and Y are both directors"), the query should explicitly confirm or deny each part (e.g., “Are both X and Y known as directors?”).

Generalizable Strategy:
1. Parse the claim to identify the core factual assertion(s) that require verification.
2. Use the notes to pinpoint the relevant entities, dates, roles, or relationships cited.
3. Formulate the query as a precise, fact-checking question centered on these key elements.
4. Ensure the question explicitly references the named entities and the specific fact(s) that need validation.
5. Use clear and natural language phrasing suitable for search or retrieval systems to locate corroborating evidence.

Examples (abstracted from prior outputs):
- If the claim involves timing of a member leaving a group vs. a DVD release: 
  "When did [person] leave [group] relative to the release of [work]?"
- If the claim involves venue location and ownership:
  "Is [auditorium] located inside [resort & casino] in [location], which is owned by [company]?"
- If the claim involves roles of individuals on a project:
  "Are both [person A] and [person B] recognized as directors with verified credits?"

Your goal: produce a clear, specific, verifiable query that focuses on the factual core of the claim, leveraging the detailed domain knowledge evidenced in the notes.
