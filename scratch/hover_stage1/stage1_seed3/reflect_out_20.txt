You will be given two fields as input: `claim` and `notes`.

- The `claim` is a factual statement (or set of statements) that needs to be verified or refuted.
- The `notes` field is a list of sentences or facts relevant to evaluating the claim.

Your task is to produce a single output field called `query` that represents a clear, focused, and specific information need derived from the `claim` and supported by the essential factual details in `notes`. This query will be used to retrieve documents that help verify or refute the claim, so it should be formulated as a precise and answerable question.

Detailed guidance:

1. **Understanding the Claim:**  
   - Parse the claim carefully, noting entities (people, places, products, dates, concepts), relationships, and any comparisons or conjunctions within it.  
   - Pay particular attention to complex claims involving multiple entities, attributes (like birthdates or nationalities), and relationships (such as "is younger than", "are from different countries", or "appeared in X and Y").

2. **Leveraging the Notes:**  
   - The `notes` provide evidence related to the claim, often containing key factual information such as dates of birth, origins of products or entities, roles played by actors, or associations between entities.  
   - Identify factual details that are directly relevant to verifying the claim. For example, birth years, nationality origins, roles portrayed, product provenance, or other specific attributes mentioned in the claim.  
   - The notes may contain multiple slightly different facts or clarifications; use these to enrich and specify your query.

3. **Formulating the Query:**  
   - The query should be a natural language question that clearly and unambiguously asks for the key factual information necessary to verify the claim.  
   - Include in the query all critical entities and attributes needed to disambiguate the query, such as full names, specific dates, roles, or relationships involved.  
   - If the claim is a comparative or multi-part claim, the query should reflect the structure, asking for the relevant relationships (e.g., comparing ages, confirming mutual exclusivity, verifying joint presence).  
   - Avoid overly broad or generic queries; specificity will lead to better retrieval of relevant documents.  
   - Prefer "Are X and Y ... ?" style or "Who is ... and what is ... ?" style queries that map directly to key facts or conditions in the claim.

4. **Generalizable Strategy Observed:**  
   - Analyze the claim’s entities and predicates.  
   - Map complex claims into simpler, direct questions that encapsulate the claim’s factual verification needs.  
   - Use the notes to ensure the query captures the essential details indicated by the evidence, especially to capture multiple entities, dates, and relations.  
   - If the claim involves verifying the existence or non-existence of a fact or a particular configuration (e.g., existence of an actor with specific traits), phrase the query accordingly.

Examples from prior assistant responses:  
- For a claim about different country origins of two computers, the query asked explicitly if the two computers originate from different countries and named those countries.  
- For a claim about an actor’s birth year, ethnicity, and roles in TV and commercials, the query explicitly asks if any actor fits all those conditions together.  
- For a claim comparing two musicians’ ages, the query asks for identification of the relevant persons and their birthdates to enable age comparison.

---

**In summary:** Given a claim and notes, generate a concise, precise factual query that directly corresponds to the verification task implied by the claim, using the details found in the notes to inform the formulation and scope of the query.
