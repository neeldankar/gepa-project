You are given three inputs:
- `claim`: a sentence or set of sentences asserting a fact or relationship.
- `notes`: a list of textual evidence snippets, each containing factual statements related to the claim.
- `context`: a list of document abstracts or encyclopedia-style entries relevant to the claim, each with a title and descriptive content.

Your task is to analyze all three inputs and produce two outputs:
1. `new_notes`: A concise, clear set of synthesized evidence statements distilled from the provided `notes` and `context`. These should:
   - Summarize the key factual points that directly support or refute the claim.
   - Eliminate redundant, irrelevant, or speculative content found in the original `notes`.
   - Clearly indicate if evidence is absent or inconclusive with regards to parts of the claim.
   - Reflect any nuanced conclusions about the claim’s accuracy based on the evidence.

2. `titles`: A list of document titles from `context` that are most relevant for verifying the claim. These should include:
   - Documents explicitly cited in `new_notes`.
   - All entities or concepts that are central to confirming or denying the claim.
   - Titles should exactly match those in the provided `context`.

Additionally, provide a brief `reasoning` explanation (for internal use, not output) demonstrating how you synthesized the evidence and arrived at your assessment of the claim’s veracity, showing how you linked the claim, notes, and context.

Important domain-specific considerations:
- The `context` documents often include biographical, bibliographic, filmographic, or definitional information about persons, works, professions, or concepts.
- The `notes` can be statements referring to dates, professions, publications, roles in films, or definitions.
- Pay close attention to temporal sequences (e.g., release dates of films), professional affiliations, publication types (e.g., distinguishing magazines, periodicals, organizations), and named entities.
- When claims involve comparisons (e.g., "both are periodicals" or "share a profession"), ensure evidence supports or contradicts these precisely.
- If data is missing or the claim cannot be fully verified, clearly note that in `new_notes`.

Generalizable strategy:
- Cross-reference the `claim` with the detailed factual statements in `notes` and the authoritative background from `context`.
- Identify which facts confirm or deny aspects of the claim; remove contradictory or unsourced assertions.
- Select document titles that correspond exactly to the chosen synthesized facts.
- Keep the final outputs concise, focused, and faithful to evidence, ensuring clarity for downstream adjudication.

Format your output exactly as:
```
### new_notes
[ list of concise, evidence-grounded statements ]

### titles
[ list of relevant document titles ]
```

Do not include the reasoning or any internal commentary in your final output.
