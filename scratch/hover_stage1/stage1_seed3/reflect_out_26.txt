Task Description:  
You are given two input fields:  
- `claim`: a factual statement or comparison that asserts specific information about entities such as movies, bands, people, or publications. This may involve genres, character roles, membership counts, timeline or birthdate comparisons, published frequency, or other fact-based relationships.  
- `notes`: a set of sentences or extracted fact fragments relevant to the claim. These often provide partial, conflicting, or limited information about the entities mentioned in the claim, including birth dates, release years, roles, genre classifications, membership details, or well-known facts such as Hall of Fame induction years.

Your goal is to generate a clear, concise `query` designed to retrieve the exact piece(s) of missing or confirming factual information necessary to verify or refute the claim. The `query` should function as a targeted question or set of closely related questions that will guide a retrieval system to find specific facts absent or ambiguous in the `notes`.

Domain-Specific Knowledge and Considerations:  
- Movie genre classifications can be nuanced and multidimensional (e.g., family, comedy, action, adventure), so queries verifying genre claims should explicitly ask for exact genre classifications or subgenres for specific movie titles, including release years when appropriate.  
- Character or actor roles should be linked precisely to the specific movie version or release date to avoid ambiguity (e.g., voice actors tied to a particular animated movie iteration).  
- Band-related claims often involve verifying member counts, roles (e.g., lead guitarist), or genre classifications which can be fluid (e.g., rock, pop, punk). When comparing birth dates of band members or relevant individuals, include full names and birth years for clarity.  
- Temporal relationships often concern event sequences such as birth dates, release years of movies, or award induction dates. These should be precisely tied to relevant entities and checked for accuracy.  
- Disambiguation of entities with similar names requires including identifying context, such as full names, titles, years, or specific roles to ensure the query targets the correct entity.

Generalizable Strategy for Query Formulation:  
1. Identify each factual point or comparison in the claim that requires verification (e.g., verifying that one movie is categorized under a certain genre, that one person was born before another, or that a band has more members than another).  
2. Cross-check the `notes` for supporting or contradicting information and detect gaps — what relevant fact(s) are missing or ambiguous?  
3. Craft the query to explicitly ask for that missing or uncertain fact, focusing tightly on the claim’s key verification points. Avoid overly broad or vague questions.  
4. If multiple factual points need verification, combine them into a logically grouped, yet concise set of queries that can be answered together.  
5. Include contextual details — such as full names, years, release titles, or dates — to precisely specify the subject of the query and avoid confusion with similarly named entities.

Examples of query focus areas include but are not limited to:  
- What are the exact genre classifications for [movie, including year]?  
- Who is the lead guitarist for [band], and what is their birth year?  
- How many members did [band] have at a certain time?  
- Was [person] inducted into [specific Hall of Fame] in [specified year or era]?  
- Was [character/actor] involved in [movie/version]?  
- What are the birth months or years of [list of people]?  
- Which publication has a higher publishing frequency than [other publication]?  
- In what order were [events, births, or releases] ordered chronologically?

By adhering to this approach, your generated `query` will pinpoint the minimal factual information needed to validate or refute the claim effectively.
