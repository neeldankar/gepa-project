Your task is to generate a precise, focused verification `query` for a given `claim` and associated `notes` designed to guide fact-checking or detailed information retrieval. The inputs are:

- `claim`: A statement containing factual assertions involving people, events, works (films, books, music), dates, relationships, roles, or sequences.
- `notes`: A list of factual sentences providing context, partial evidence, entity attributes, or clarifications relevant to the claim. These may include direct facts, corrections of misinformation, ambiguous or conflicting information, and multiple references to the same entity or event.

Your output is a succinct yet comprehensive `query` formed as a set of connected, targeted questions that will enable verification of the entire claim, including all critical aspects and nuances.

Key points and domain-specific considerations:

1. **Claim Components to Identify:**  
   Clearly break down the claim to identify key factual elements (persons, dates, works, relationships, roles, events) that require confirmation.

2. **Address Partial, Conflicting, or Ambiguous Information:**  
   Use the notes to spot where information is incomplete, ambiguous, or contradictory (e.g., variant spellings or names, conflicting nationalities, uncertain dates, unclear relationships). Your query must explicitly seek resolution of these ambiguities, including explicit confirmation or refutation.

3. **Entity Disambiguation:**  
   When multiple plausible entities or name variants exist, include questions clarifying identity by full name, alternative names, or specific attributes to avoid confusion.

4. **Temporal and Relational Verifications:**  
   For claims involving chronological sequences (birthdates, release dates, event timing) or relationships between people or works (such as who did what, sequels, predecessor-successor roles), queries must confirm these temporal or relational links precisely.

5. **Coverage of All Critical Claim Assertions:**  
   Include verification points separately when attributes or actions relate to different entities (e.g., confirming the professional status or starting age of each person involved individually).

6. **Avoiding Redundancy while Maintaining Completeness:**  
   The query should be as concise as possible without omitting any crucial info needed to fully verify the claim.

7. **Formulation Style:**  
   Frame queries as direct, clear questions explicitly targeting unresolved facts, e.g., "Did X happen?", "Who was Y?", "Is Z considered...?", "When did A occur?", "Is person B the same as person C?".

8. **Ultimate Goal:**  
   Enable retrieval of all necessary evidence and authoritative documents to fully verify or refute the claim, especially addressing any lingering information gaps or contradictions the notes reveal.

Summary of effective approach:

- Analyze the claim to extract factual points and entities.  
- Cross-reference the notes for facts, corrections, conflicts, or omissions.  
- Identify ambiguities or mismatches requiring explicit queries (e.g., overlapping names, disputed dates).  
- Formulate a connected question set that verifies identities, roles, dates, relationships, contradicting points, and missing facts.  
- Ensure coverage is complete but phrased succinctly for efficient retrieval.

This methodology supports precise, domain-specific fact-checking across history, sports, film/music, biographies, and complex entity relationships.
