Task Description:
You are given three inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`. This is a fact verification and synthesis task focused on verifying or evaluating the truthfulness or accuracy of the `claim` by refining and integrating information from the `notes` and `context`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A statement or assertion that involves one or more entities, events, or facts. This claim may include complex or domain-specific information such as the number of species in a biological genus, the timeline and roles of film or television creators, or historical dates and biographical details.
- `notes`: A list of statements, pieces of evidence, or factoids related to the claim. These notes can be redundant, contradictory, or only indirectly related. They may mix information from different domains such as film, botany, historical figures, or TV shows.
- `context`: A list of detailed documents, biographies, descriptions, or authoritative excerpts that provide comprehensive background or factual information pertinent to the claim. These often include domain-specific data such as details on plant species, film release years, actor involvement, or institutional history.

Outputs:
1. `new_notes`: Provide a concise, coherent, and non-redundant synthesis of the relevant information from `notes` and `context`. These notes should:
   - Address directly the truthfulness, validity, or accuracy of the claim.
   - Remove repetitive, contradictory, or extraneous details.
   - Focus on key factual information that either supports or refutes the claim.
   - Be presented as clear, standalone sentences that provide evidence or critical facts.
   - Reflect domain-specific knowledge pertinent to the claim topic. Examples include:
        * For film/TV claims: clarify actor roles, debut films, director nationalities, film release years, or multi-series involvement.
        * For botanical claims: specify taxonomy, number of species, endemic regions, and common names with linguistic references.
        * For historical or biographical claims: reconcile dates of birth and death, timelines of events, or affiliations.
   - When contradictions or conflicting data appear, reconcile them logically and explicitly.

2. `titles`: Select a short list of the most relevant document or entity titles from the `context` that best capture the main subjects or entities involved in verifying the claim. Titles should:
   - Include key persons, organizations, works, taxa, or topics directly referenced in the claim or relevant facts.
   - Provide clear traceability to the verified evidence.
   - Avoid including unrelated or only tangentially related documents.
   - Maintain a balance between comprehensive coverage and focus on the claim's core context.

Reasoning and Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Carefully establish timelines by resolving any inconsistencies in dates or overlapping lifespans, particularly when claims involve co-existence or sequential events.
- Disambiguate entities with similar or overlapping contexts, such as differentiating between actors appearing in distinct but related TV series or film franchises.
- Leverage domain knowledge to detect possible conflations or misconceptions in the claim (e.g., misunderstanding which film an actress debuted in, or which genus has more species).
- For botanical claims, verify taxonomy and species counts precisely, considering endemic areas and authoritative classifications.
- For entertainment industry claims, verify debut roles, director nationalities, and detailed cast lists.
- Use logic to reconcile sometimes contradictory notes or gaps in evidence, explicitly stating when the claim is unsupported or confirmed.
- Provide a clear, reasoned explanation to support whether the claim is true, false, or unverified based on the evidence.

Output Format:
- `new_notes`: A list of concise, factual statements synthesizing the core evidence relevant to the claim.
- `titles`: A list of concise document or entity titles from the context that most directly relate to the claim verification.

Goal:
Your response should enable a user to verify and evaluate the claim's accuracy efficiently by presenting distilled, critical evidence and elucidating the core contextual references while eliminating noise, redundancy, and ambiguity.
