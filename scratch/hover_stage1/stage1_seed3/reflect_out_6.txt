Given an input containing two fields, `claim` and `notes`, your task is to generate a precise, comprehensive, and focused `query` designed to guide the retrieval of all remaining essential factual evidence needed to fully verify the truthfulness or specifics of the `claim`.

### Input Description:
- `claim`: A factual assertion or statement involving people, places, works (such as operas, songs, films), events, or attribute comparisons that requires verification.
- `notes`: A collection of factual context, references, clarifications, and partial evidence related to the claim, including correct or incorrect details, ambiguities, conflicting information, and key entity attributes.

### Your Objective:
Use careful reasoning to analyze the `claim` against the information present in the `notes`. Your reasoning should identify unresolved or contradictory facts, ambiguous names or identities, unclear roles or relationships, and missing key data necessary to confirm or refute the claim.

Based on this analysis, formulate a multi-part `query` consisting of clear, targeted verification questions that:

1. **Isolate Key Entities and Facts**  
   Identify and explicitly name the primary entities, works, events, or attributes in the claim that need disambiguation or confirmation.  
   Examples: persons by full name (including variants), specific works by title and date, exact geographic or institutional names.

2. **Address Ambiguities or Potential Errors**  
   If the notes reveal possible misattributions (e.g., incorrect nationality, confused individuals with similar names, uncertain authorship), explicitly query for clarification of these factual points.

3. **Target Critical Missing Information**  
   If the notes lack direct evidence for parts of the claim (e.g., specific roles, dates, authorship credits, relationships), include questions that seek these gaps to complete the fact-checking.

4. **Verify Temporal or Sequential Details When Relevant**  
   For claims involving chronology (birthdates, event dates, revivals), include questions about exact or relative timing to verify temporal consistency.

5. **Separate Verification for Each Ambiguous Entity or Role**  
   When a claim compares persons or works (e.g., who wrote what, who performed where), include separate questions for each entity or work to avoid conflating answers.

6. **Be Succinct but Comprehensive**  
   The query should be as concise as possible while covering all outstanding verification points so that retrieving documents using these questions will fully address the claim.

### Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Names often have variants or alternate spellings; always specify full names or distinguishing attributes to disambiguate identities.
- Nationalities, professions, or roles may be misattributed; explicitly ask about these attributes if notes show inconsistencies.
- For creative works (operas, songs, films), verify authorship/composition, performer/participant roles, dates of creation or performance, and relationships among involved individuals.
- When multiple institutions or geographic locations are mentioned (e.g., universities within a county), clarify precise relationships and locations.
- If a claim involves an individual’s activity (e.g., playing baseball at a college), confirm both the individual’s association and the entity’s existence/location.
- When no direct evidence appears in notes, queries must specifically seek out missing or absent information.

### Reasoning Expectations:
- Break down the claim into component factual elements.
- Compare these elements against notes to identify contradictions, uncertainties, or gaps.
- Highlight ambiguous names, misattributions, or overlapping entities.
- Determine which precise facts remain to be settled by information retrieval.
- Use this logical analysis as the foundation for each question in your query.

### Output Format:
- Provide your reasoning explaining the key fact-checking elements and gaps.
- Provide a clear, structured query (set of verification questions) that targets the unresolved information points to fully verify the claim.

---

Following these instructions will enable comprehensive and efficient retrieval of factual documents needed to conclusively confirm or refute claims, addressing any partial, conflicting, or missing evidence in the supporting notes.
