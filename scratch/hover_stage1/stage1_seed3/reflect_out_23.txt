Task Description:
You will be provided with three inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to analyze and synthesize these inputs to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A declarative statement that asserts a fact or relationship involving entities, events, or data points.
- `notes`: A list of statements or evidence snippets that are loosely or directly related to the claim. These may be repetitive, redundant, or sometimes contain conflicting or tangential information.
- `context`: A list of documents or descriptions providing detailed background information, factual data, or authoritative knowledge about the entities, events, or concepts involved in the claim.

Outputs:
1. `new_notes`: A concise, refined set of factually relevant statements that:
   - Directly address the truthfulness, validity, or accuracy of the claim.
   - Integrate and reconcile information from both `notes` and `context`.
   - Remove redundancies, contradictions, and peripheral information.
   - Emphasize key factual points establishing support or refutation of the claim.
   - Stand alone clearly without requiring the original notes or context to understand.
   - Include domain-specific factual information relevant to the claim’s topic (e.g., correct dates of establishment for institutions, detailed season participation for sports or racing events, official actor roles in TV series, exact chart positions for songs, botanical endemicity and locations, or any relevant specialized domain knowledge).

2. `titles`: A curated list of the most relevant document or entity titles from the provided `context` that:
   - Represent key entities, organizations, or subjects explicitly mentioned or strongly implied by the claim and evidence.
   - Offer users clear traceability to critical background sources needed to understand or verify the claim.
   - Balance coverage and focus, omitting unrelated or indirectly connected documents.
   - Typically include primary subjects like named people, places, events, entities, or works (e.g., TV series names, plant species names, national park titles, discography or album names).

Approach and Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Carefully reconcile contradictory or conflicting dates, facts, or roles mentioned across notes and context to form an accurate timeline or fact chain.
- Detect and differentiate between closely related but distinct entities, such as separate TV series an actor participated in, or subspecies within botanical taxa.
- Understand domain-specific terminology and contextual cues:
  - In entertainment: Recognize which series or films an actor portrayed certain roles in, when they received awards or nominations, and roles' historical or thematic relevance (e.g., a drug lord nicknamed "The King of Cocaine").
  - In botany: Know plant subspecies distributions and their endemic locations relative to geographic areas like national parks or islands.
  - In music charts: Identify which songs reached #1, whether they were original or covers, and the original genres of first recording artists.
  - In institutional history: Verify accurate establishment years and correct geographic placements.
  - In sports/racing: Distinguish between different series seasons, driver participation, and event types.
- Integrate general reasoning to identify claims that conflate related but different facts (e.g., mixing actors’ roles in different series or incorrectly assigning award nominations).
- Judge claims against the strongest factual evidence, providing summary notes that confirm or refute with clarity.
- Generate `new_notes` that function like a distilled fact sheet relevant to the claim, avoiding ambiguity or unnecessary detail.
- Select `titles` that act as direct pointers for users seeking authoritative, detailed verification.

Formatting:
- `new_notes` should be presented as a list of succinct, factually precise statements.
- `titles` should be a list of succinct document or entity names relevant to the claim's core factual verification.
- Your reasoning, if required, should be logically consistent and clearly explain why the claim is supported, refuted, or unconfirmed based on evidence.

Goal:
Enable thorough verification and evaluation of the claim through integrated, domain-aware factual synthesis, removing noise and ambiguity while maintaining traceability to key context sources.
