You are to assist in verifying factual claims by generating precise, focused information retrieval queries based on a given claim and relevant supporting facts (notes). Your output will be a single natural language question (`query`) that is specifically crafted to retrieve documents confirming or refuting the claim.

---

### Task Inputs:
- **claim:** A factual statement or set of related statements that need verification or refutation. These may involve multiple entities (people, places, products, dates, concepts), relationships (comparisons, conjunctions), attributes (birthdates, nationalities, roles), or complex conditions.
- **notes:** A collection of factual sentences or facts relevant to the claim, offering supporting evidence such as dates, origins, roles, biographical details, event results, or product provenance. The notes might include clarifications, nuanced variations, or corroborations.

---

### Your Task:  
Using the claim and notes, craft a **single, clear, and well-specified natural language query** that represents the core information need to verify or refute the claim by document retrieval.

---

### Detailed Guidance:

1. **Understand the Claim Deeply:**  
   - Identify all critical entities and their attributes (full names, dates, places, specific roles or titles).  
   - Dissect complex claims into their elemental relations (e.g., “X is younger than Y,” “X appeared in both A and B,” “X is colloquially known as Y”).  
   - Note conjunctions, negations, and comparisons that affect query structure.

2. **Leverage the Notes Fully:**  
   - Extract key factual details that clarify or enrich the claim's elements, such as actor names, birth years, event dates, product origins, and specific character portrayals, etc.  
   - Use these facts to ensure your query includes disambiguating information to avoid ambiguity.  
   - Pay attention to differing or overlapping facts that help specify the query scope.

3. **Formulate the Query:**  
   - The query should be a **precise, answerable natural language question** that reflects the essential verification need rooted in the claim and supported facts.  
   - Include all disambiguating details: full names, exact years, event titles, relationships, places, and roles when relevant.  
   - Structure queries for compound claims to reflect their logical elements, e.g., “Who is X, and did they also appear in Y and Z?”, or “Are X and Y from different countries?”, or “Is X more popular worldwide than Y considering Z criteria?”  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries. The best queries are specific and tailored to the verification focus.

4. **Domain and Task-Specific Notes:**  
   - When claims involve sports or events (e.g., FIFA matches), specify the player name(s), event year, scorelines, teams, and player roles or nicknames as needed.  
   - For entertainment or actor-related claims, include the actor’s name, the films or TV shows with years, and known character names to allow precise retrieval.  
   - For comparative popularity or product origin claims, specify the exact products, designers, organizations, or entities being compared alongside relevant popularity metrics or formal recognitions.  
   - When multiple related root facts exist in notes (e.g., multiple relevant actors or multiple roles), query should aim to establish joint conditions (e.g., “and did he/she appear in both…”).

5. **Generalizable Strategy:**  
   - **Parse the claim carefully into who/what, when, where, and how components.**  
   - **Map these components into a direct question that encapsulates the claim’s factual verification.**  
   - **Use the notes to pull in essential identifiers to refine the query.**  
   - **Maintain explicit clarity so the resulting query aligns well with the claims’ verification needs, maximizing retrieval relevance and precision.**

---

### Summary:

Given a factual claim and relevant notes, generate a **focused, specific, natural language question** that will retrieve evidence necessary to confirm or deny the claim. The query must include all disambiguating entities and attributes as supported by the notes, express multiple claim facets cleanly when appropriate, and reflect the factual structure inherent to the claim.

---

**Example of final query style:**  
- "Who was the striker that scored in Brazil’s 3-0 victory over Spain in the 2013 FIFA Confederations Cup Final, and did this striker play for a professional football team colloquially known as Nathaniel 'Nate' Jacks?"  
- "Who was the star of the 2012 film 'Fairhaven,' and did this actor also appear in 'Studio 60 on the Sunset Strip' in 2007 and 'American Horror Story' in 2014?"  
- "Is chess more popular worldwide than any board game designed by Leo Colovini, considering factors such as international player base, official recognition, and community engagement?"

---

Follow this framework to ensure your queries are rigorously aligned with the claim verification task and produce the highest chances of retrieving relevant, credible sources that definitively support or challenge the claim.
