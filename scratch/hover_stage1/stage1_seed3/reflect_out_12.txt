Task Description:
You are given two fields as inputs: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a single output field called `query`.

- The `claim` field contains a statement or assertion, sometimes partial or ambiguous, that requires verification or fact-checking.
- The `notes` field is a list of snippets or extracted information relevant to the claim. These notes may contain partial information, repeated facts, or related entities but often lack complete evidence to definitively verify the claim. The notes may also include some documents or data mentions that are related to the claim but missing from the current context.

Your goal is to generate a focused, clear, and comprehensive `query` (in the form of one or more questions) intended to retrieve the missing or confirming evidence necessary to verify or refute the claim in full. This query should be constructed based on a careful reasoning process that:

1. Identifies key entities, events, or attributes mentioned in the claim.
2. Checks the coverage of these in the provided notes, noting gaps or ambiguities.
3. Determines what specific information is missing or uncertain that is needed to fully evaluate the claim.
4. Integrates domain-specific acronyms, alternative names, relevant background (e.g., film festival editions, band memberships, historical events), and relationships between entities mentioned in the claim and notes to ensure precision.
5. Forms the query to explicitly request the missing facts or clarifications needed to judge the truthfulness of the claim, potentially splitting compound claims into multiple sub-questions if necessary.

Additional domain- and task-specific details:
- Claims often involve proper nouns such as specific species, films, band members, awards, or historical persons/events.
- Notes may provide habitat info for taxa, awards for individuals, film festival awards and dates, musician side projects, or military service connected to actors.
- Extracting the correct documents or entities related to the claim is essential (e.g., if claim concerns "Alopecurus creticus," queries must explicitly focus on that species if the note does not clarify its habitat).
- Queries must be both general enough to retrieve precise verification evidence but targeted enough to avoid retrieving irrelevant information.
- The reasoning preceding query formulation should involve identifying missing key data to be searched (e.g., awards won by specific persons that are not present in the notes).

Generalizable Strategy:
- Parse the claim to understand its components and relationships.
- Analyze the supplied notes to evaluate coverage against the claim.
- Identify what critical fact(s) or linking data is absent or incomplete.
- Compose a query that explicitly asks for those missing factoids or clarifications.
- If the claim references multiple entities or subclaims, combine relevant questions into one coherent query.
- Use domain-specific terminology and context cues to produce precise, unambiguous queries.

Example:
- If claim: "Echeveria is native to arid or semi-desert areas, while Alopecurus creticus is."
- Notes mention habitat info only for Echeveria and general alopecurus species, lacking info for Alopecurus creticus.
- Query should ask: "What are the native habitats of Echeveria and Alopecurus creticus? Is Echeveria native to arid or semi-desert areas, and what type of environment does Alopecurus creticus inhabit?"

Your output is only the `query` representing the best information need to verify the claim based on the analysis.
