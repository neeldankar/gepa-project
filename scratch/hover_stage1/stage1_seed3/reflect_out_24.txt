Task Description:  
You are given two input fields:  
- `claim`: a statement that asserts factual information or relationships involving entities such as genres of movies or music groups, roles or identities of characters or people, temporal sequences (e.g., order of release), or quantitative facts about membership numbers in groups or bands. Claims may include comparisons or conjunctions of multiple facts that need verification.  
- `notes`: a set of factual sentences or pieces of extracted information relevant to the claim. These often provide partial evidence, clarifications on attributes, relationships between entities, or identity disambiguations.  
Your task is to generate a focused, concise `query` designed to retrieve the *missing* or *confirming* factual information necessary to verify or refute the claim.

Key Objectives for the `query`:  
- Confirm exact classifications or categories, e.g., movie genres (family, comedy, adventure, action, etc.), musical genres of bands, or professional roles of individuals.  
- Clarify quantitative facts, such as the membership count of bands, or numerical comparisons of group sizes.  
- Establish temporal relations or sequences, e.g., release dates of movies or order of events.  
- Disambiguate entities with similar or overlapping names, including specific versions or years of movies or works.  
- Verify specific roles or presence of characters/individuals in specific works, confirming identities and attributions precisely.

Domain-specific knowledge and considerations:  
- Movie genres often overlap or have nuanced classifications; the query should seek explicit genre confirmations rather than guesses.  
- Voice actor roles require exact linking of actor, character, and specific movie release/version.  
- Bands may cross genres or have variable member counts over time; queries should ask for precise counts or classifications at relevant times.  
- Temporal claims require cross-checking exact release dates or event dates.  
- Entities with similar names (e.g., multiple movies titled the same but from different years) demand clarifying descriptors in queries.  
- When characters or actors are involved, queries should ask about the presence or role in the exact named work/version.

Recommended strategy when generating the `query`:  
1. Carefully analyze the claim to identify the core facts or relationships needing verification (e.g., is the claim about genre, actor presence, membership count, temporal order, or affiliation?).  
2. Review the notes to find which elements are already supported, contradicted, or missing.  
3. Formulate the smallest sufficient set of explicit questions in the `query` to retrieve exactly the missing confirmations or refutations needed to decide the truth of the claim.  
4. Include relevant context (years, full names, versions) to disambiguate entities explicitly.  
5. Avoid overly broad or vague queries; focus on targeted, minimal questioning to efficiently pinpoint needed facts.  
6. If the claim involves multiple related facts or entities, include all verification points in the query to enable a comprehensive fact check.

Example style:  
- If verifying band membership sizes:  
  "How many members did Band A have at the time of Album X's release? How many members did Band B have at that time?"  
- If verifying a claim about actor roles and productions:  
  "Which actors starred in [Production Name] at [Location/Year]? Among them, were there any English actors? Did the same English actor also star in [Other Production]?"  
- If verifying territorial jurisdiction and geographic relations:  
  "Which city has jurisdiction over [County Name]? Is that city located in [Province Name], [Country]? Where is [Other City] located?"  
- If verifying genre classifications and temporal ordering of movies:  
  "What are the official genres of [Movie Name] (Year)? What is the release date of [Movie A] and [Movie B]?"

The final output, `query`, should be one or more tightly focused questions directly targeting the essential missing factual information to determine the claim’s veracity.

- Always incorporate domain-specific disambiguations (e.g., include years for movies, exact names for people/bands) when multiple entities could be confused.
