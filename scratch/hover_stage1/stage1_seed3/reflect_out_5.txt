You will be given three inputs:

1. `claim`: A statement that makes one or more factual assertions about people, places, events, or entities.
2. `notes`: A list of extracted information snippets and facts related to the claim, some supporting and some contradicting the claim.
3. `context`: A list of longer informational passages or documents providing background, biographical details, geographic data, or organizational information relevant to the claim.

Your task is to carefully analyze the `claim` in light of both the `notes` and `context`. Using this evidence, you must:

- Produce a concise and accurate `new_notes` list of factual statements that directly support, refute, or clarify the claim. Your `new_notes` should summarize verified information, highlight contradictions or lack of evidence, and make clear distinctions when certain aspects of the claim remain unsubstantiated.
- Extract or produce a corresponding set of `titles` representing the key topics, entities, or documents that form the basis for your reasoning and the content in `new_notes`.

### Important details and domain-specific considerations:
- Claims often involve proper nouns: personal names (e.g., Ariel Ramírez, King ov Hell), geographic locations (e.g., Bergen, Hordaland, Eurobodalla Shire), organizations or bands, and specialized terminology (e.g., "science teacher," "black metal band," "shire council").
- Verify specific relationships or affiliations mentioned in the claim (e.g., whether a teacher taught both persons, whether a band is located in a particular county).
- Use the `notes` for discrete factual confirmations or rebuttals, but corroborate and contextualize them within the broader, detailed `context` passages.
- When evidence contradicts the claim or is insufficient to confirm it fully, clearly note this in `new_notes`.
- When geographical, administrative, or organizational details are involved, verify connections (e.g., "Bergen is in Hordaland county," or "the town is under the Eurobodalla Shire council") using contextual information rather than assumptions.
- The goal is to produce a factually accurate, nuanced summary of what is supported or not supported in the claim based on all available data, rather than simply asserting truth or falsehood.

### Approach:
- Carefully cross-reference each factual component of the claim against `notes` and `context`.
- Summarize confirmed facts and unresolved points for `new_notes`.
- Identify and output the relevant main topics or documents as `titles`, reflecting the primary entities or concepts underpinning your assessment.
- Ensure clarity and precision in your output to aid verification, downstream use, or further research.

This task requires a careful synthesis of multiple information sources to evaluate complex factual claims, often involving domain-specific knowledge such as music educators, Norwegian geography, or Australian local government administration.
