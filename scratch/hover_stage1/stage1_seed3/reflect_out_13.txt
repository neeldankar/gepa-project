You will be given the following inputs:

- `claim`: a factual statement or assertion that often includes multiple parts or entities.
- `notes`: a list of concise factual statements extracted from various sources relevant to the claim.
- `context`: a list of longer text passages or document summaries that provide background information and evidence pertaining to entities mentioned in the claim and notes.

Your task is to analyze the claim, notes, and context to:

1. Assess the factual accuracy of the claim by verifying each part against the evidence in the notes and context.
2. Synthesize a brief, clear summary that:
   - Confirms which parts of the claim are supported or refuted by evidence.
   - Clarifies any inaccuracies or misconceptions present in the claim.
   - Uses only information present in the notes and context, avoiding unsupported assumptions.
3. Produce two outputs:
   - `new_notes`: a concise, curated subset of notes that accurately reflect the verified information supporting or correcting the claim. Avoid redundant or duplicate notes and focus on the most relevant facts needed to evaluate the claim.
   - `titles`: a list of relevant document or entity titles that were used or should be retrieved for a full evidence base about the claim. These should correspond to key people, places, works, or organizations central to understanding or verifying the claim.

Additional guidance and domain-specific details to keep in mind:

- Claims often reference specific persons (e.g., authors, actors), creative works (e.g., films, bands, albums), locations (e.g., cathedrals, cities), organizations (e.g., record labels), or collaborations. Your verification must consider detailed relationships such as who wrote or starred in a work, locations of buildings, or nationalities and collaborations of bands and labels.
- Pay special attention to temporal details, such as premiere dates, duration of collaborations, or timeframes referenced in the claim, comparing these to dates or time periods extracted from the notes/context.
- Identify and clarify any factual errors related to:
  - Names (e.g., full names or name variants).
  - Dates (incorrect premiere, release, or time periods).
  - Nationalities or geographic locations.
  - Roles (e.g., whether someone wrote or directed a work).
  - Collaborations or associations (e.g., which bands worked with which labels or other bands).
- When summarizing, explicitly state which parts of the claim are true, which are false or inconsistent, and what accurate facts replace any inaccuracies.
- Construct `titles` as concise, commonly used entity or work names as evidenced in the context data (e.g., 'Disturbia (film)', 'Christopher B. Landon', 'Sleeper (band)', 'Guildford Cathedral'). These titles should aid in retrieval or referencing key evidence sources.

Generalizable strategy:

- Break down the claim into parts or sub-claims.
- Match each part to corresponding evidence in notes/context.
- Note contradictions, clarifications, or additional context that impact the claim’s truth.
- Select only the most relevant notes that directly affect the claim’s validity for `new_notes`.
- Identify the key subjects, works, or places involved and output their canonical titles for `titles`.
- Avoid redundancy in the outputs.
- Keep the reasoning implicit in generating new_notes; the outputs should be succinct and focused on critical evidence.

This approach ensures accurate fact verification, evidence curation, and identification of authoritative sources to support or refute complex domain-specific claims.
