Task Description: 

You will be provided with three fields: 
- `claim`: A statement potentially involving persons, works (films, TV shows, music albums, songs), events, or other cultural/media references.
- `notes`: A list of factual, detailed assertions extracted from relevant documents that address various parts of the claim, often correcting misconceptions, clarifying associations, or confirming facts.
- `context`: Background information comprising summaries or excerpts from relevant knowledge sources (e.g., Wikipedia-like entries) about entities involved in the claim (such as directors, films, TV shows, albums, episodes, songs, or programming blocks).

Your task is to produce the following two fields:
- `new_notes`: A concise, clear, and factual summary synthesizing the key relevant details from the `notes` and `context`. These should directly clarify or refute the claim, explicitly addressing any errors or misunderstandings present in the claim. The points should systematically separate confirmed facts from debunked or unsupported assertions. Avoid redundancy or overly verbose detail. Include all pertinent entities and relationships necessary to understand the claim’s validity.
- `titles`: A list of the most relevant document titles or topics (derived from or corresponding to those referenced in the `context` or `notes`) that best represent the key knowledge sources underpinning the `new_notes`. These should be specific to the entities, works, or factual points discussed to facilitate precise fact-checking or retrieval.

Key Domain-Specific Guidance:
- Claims often involve complex media relationships, such as who directed, wrote, or scored particular films, TV shows, or albums; relationships between works (e.g., songs featured in specific episodes of shows); or associations between people and productions.
- Explicitly disambiguate potentially conflated or incorrectly linked individuals or works (e.g., distinguishing between similarly named films or identifying that a director of one project is not connected to another).
- Note that some claims may wrongly attribute creative roles (e.g., direction, writing) or incorrectly connect persons from one media work to another unrelated one. Your summaries must correct these points.
- Titles in `titles` should correspond to real, verifiable entities or works mentioned in the claim, notes, or context, such as:
   - Names of directors, producers, or writers (e.g., "Ettore Scola", "Jonathan Lynn", "Ruggero Deodato", "Alejandro Springall").
   - Names of specific films, TV series, episodes, albums, or songs (e.g., "Che strano chiamarsi Federico", "Yes Minister", "Cannibal Holocaust", "La La Land (Wax Fang album)", "American Dad! - Lost In Space (Season 8, Episode 151)").
   - Programming blocks or broadcasting information (e.g., "KidsClick programming block").
- When titles involve episodes, be as specific as possible (including season and episode number) to reduce ambiguity.
- Extract and clarify temporal, geographic, or role-based facts as they relate to the claim (e.g., director nationality, broadcast days, album release dates).

Generalizable Strategy:
- Decompose the claim into discrete factual components.
- Verify each component independently using the notes and context.
- Integrate confirmed facts and contradictions into a reasoned summary (the `new_notes`) that is factual, clear, and unambiguous.
- Select relevant titles to cover all major entities and key factual points involved.
- Strive for hierarchical clarity: general claim validity → component-level fact confirmation/refutation → naming of key sources.

Formatting Requirements:
- `new_notes` should be a list of clear, stand-alone factual statements.
- `titles` should be a list of succinct document or topic titles corresponding to the entities/entities in new_notes.
