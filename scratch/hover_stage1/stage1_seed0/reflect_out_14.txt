Task Description:
You will be provided with three fields as input: 
- `claim`: a factual statement or set of statements that may contain accurate, partially accurate, or inaccurate information.
- `notes`: a list of textual evidence items or extracted facts relevant to the claim, often containing overlapping or contradictory information.
- `context`: a list of background documents or knowledge snippets that relate to entities, events, or subjects mentioned in the claim and notes. These documents provide authoritative or encyclopedic data to verify or refute parts of the claim.

Your goal is to process these inputs thoroughly to produce two output fields:
- `new_notes`: a distilled and corrected summary of the notes that accurately reflects verified information relevant to the claim. This should:
  - Correct or clarify factual inaccuracies in the original notes.
  - Retain key evidence that supports or refutes parts of the claim.
  - Incorporate or reconcile information from the context to resolve contradictions or add missing important details.
  - Remove misleading, unfounded, or irrelevant details found in the notes or claim.
  - Summarize efficiently with clarity and precision, focusing on factual correctness.

- `titles`: a list of knowledge resource titles (e.g., Wikipedia article titles or document names) that best correspond to the authoritative sources referenced or needed to support the corrected notes and the verification of the claim. This includes:
  - Titles of entities (people, films, TV shows, campaigns) central to the claim.
  - Titles of events, episodes, or specific works referenced.
  - Titles that fill factual gaps or correct misunderstandings.
  - Aim to include all documents necessary to provide a comprehensive and factually accurate verification trail for the claim and notes.

Generalizable Approach:
1. Carefully parse the `claim` to identify the main factual assertions, including named entities, dates, roles, and relationships.
2. Analyze the `notes` to identify which pieces support, contradict, or add context to the claim.
3. Cross-reference all information with the `context` documents to confirm or refute factual aspects, paying special attention to:
   - Exact dates (e.g., birthdates, premiere dates).
   - Named entities and their roles/attributes (e.g., actors, character roles, professions).
   - Relationships between entities (e.g., who starred in which film, who wrote a script).
   - Nationalities, professions, or other biographical details relevant to correctness.
4. Correct any factual errors detected in the notes or claim, explicitly noting these corrections in `new_notes`.
5. Collect the set of all document titles from the context that are authoritative sources for any part of the corrected notes or claim, ensuring coverage of all main entities and facts.
6. Summarize the corrected information clearly and concisely in the `new_notes` with the goal of creating a factually accurate, reliable, and informative summary that would clarify the claim's validity.

Domain- and Task-Specific Details:
- Pay close attention to specific claims about media productions including exact premiere or release years, cast and crew details, and plot context.
- Consider distinctions between similarly named entities (e.g., individuals with similar names, different works sharing names).
- Clarify and correct date-related assertions, especially those that are frequently miscited (e.g., premiere years, birth months).
- Address nationality or ethnic background claims, especially if contradictory or unsupported.
- When noting roles played by actors, state character names and productions clearly.
- Identify and include all relevant authoritative documents, including those about persons, films, TV episodes, campaigns, or other named things cited.
- Exclude irrelevant or anecdotal information not substantiated by the context or notes relevant to the claim.
- When flaws or conflations appear in the claim, clearly expose and correct these in the new notes.
- Titles must represent key knowledge resources essential to verifying and justifying the edits.

The purpose is to produce a factually validated, concise, and evidence-supported summary of the claim and relevant notes, along with a set of knowledge source titles that underpin this validation.
