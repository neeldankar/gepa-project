You are tasked with generating a single, concise, and precise natural language query that fact-checks or verifies a given claim using provided notes. The claim and notes belong to diverse domains such as sports, literature, film, botany, corporate identity, and board games, among others. Your query should be designed to efficiently retrieve authoritative information confirming or disputing every key factual element of the claim.

Task Inputs:  
- `claim`: A single declarative statement potentially containing multiple factual assertions and implied relationships.  
- `notes`: A collection of extracted facts, context, background data, and domain-specific details relevant to verifying the claim.

Your Output:  
- A single, focused verification query that incorporates all critical claim components and their relationships. This query should strategically include relevant domain-specific entities, proper names, titles, dates, roles, and relationships to enable precise retrieval.

Detailed Task Instructions:

1. **Analyze the claim thoroughly:**  
   - Identify **all** key factual assertions that require verification.  
   - Detect implied connections or temporal context linking multiple components.  
   - Recognize the domain (e.g., literary awards, film credits, botanical taxonomy, sports teams) to apply domain knowledge.

2. **Leverage the notes effectively:**  
   - Extract relevant data such as names (full names preferred), titles (e.g., film titles, game names), roles (e.g., composer, head coach), affiliations (e.g., leagues, countries, awards), counts (species, team seasons), sponsorships, dates/years, and any other domain-specific facts.  
   - Use these details to enrich the query for precision.

3. **Formulating the query:**
   - Conceptualize the query as a **verification question** seeking authoritative confirmation or denial (e.g., "Did X do Y in Z?", "Is A the B of C?", "Is X the principal sponsor of Y?").  
   - Integrate multiple linked assertions into a single logical question to avoid fragmented queries.  
   - Mention full domain-relevant entities to reduce ambiguity (e.g., use "G. V. Prakash Kumar" instead of "the composer," or "Leo Colovini's German-style board games such as Cartagena" instead of generic terms).  
   - Include temporal references if present (e.g., "currently," "in 2013").  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions. The query must be precise and targeted to the claim’s facts.

4. **Domain-specific guidance:**  
   Examples of details to incorporate based on domain:  
   - **Literature:** Full names of individuals (e.g., Alan Sillitoe, Elfriede Jelinek), award names (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), nationality, roles (writer, diplomat, poet), and specific years or editions.  
   - **Film and music credits:** Film titles and years (e.g., "Veyyil (2006)"), composer names, roles (e.g., director, co-director, composer), and actor names tied to the film.  
   - **Botany:** Genus names, count of species, vernacular names, taxonomic ranks/status.  
   - **Sports:** Team names, leagues/conferences, roles (head coach, coordinator), seasons or years.  
   - **Corporate:** Company name, nationality, headquarters location, operational footprint.  
   - **Games:** Designer names, game titles, genres, and scope of popularity.

5. **General strategy:**  
   - Begin by identifying the most crucial facts or entities in the claim.  
   - Use the notes to find authoritative, detailed, and precise names and descriptors.  
   - Combine these insights into a succinct yet comprehensive verification question that, if answered accurately, confirms or refutes the entire claim.  
   - Ensure the query is self-contained and unambiguous to facilitate direct use in authoritative source retrieval or verification.

Outcome:  
A single, well-structured, domain-aware verification query that addresses all factual components and connections in the claim, informed and enriched by the notes provided.
