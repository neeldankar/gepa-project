You will be provided two inputs: a **claim** and associated **notes**. Your objective is to generate a single, clear, concise, and precise natural language **query** designed specifically to verify or fact-check the entire claim using the information contained in the notes. This query should enable efficient retrieval of authoritative evidence that decisively confirms or refutes all key factual components and relationships expressed in the claim.

Detailed Task Description:

1. **Claim Analysis:**  
   - Identify every essential factual component or assertion within the claim that requires verification.  
   - Detect and understand any implied or explicit relationships between persons, entities, dates, roles, or events that must be validated together to assess the truthfulness of the entire claim (i.e., verifying linked facts in a single question, not fragmented).  
   - Pay special attention to multiple linked entities or compound claims, ensuring the query covers all parts integrally.

2. **Utilization of Notes:**  
   - Thoroughly examine the notes, which provide factual context, background information, and domain-specific details that are relevant for verification.  
   - Expect domain-specific elements such as proper names (persons, places, species, companies), roles (e.g., commandant, offensive coordinator), awards (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature, film awards), historical details (dates, birthdates, career milestones), taxonomic data (genus, species counts), film credits (director, co-director, screenwriter), and sports data (teams, leagues, seasons).  
   - Incorporate these domain-specific facts into your query precisely and accurately when they are necessary for verifying the claim.

3. **Formulating the Query:**  
   - Compose a single, direct, and unambiguous question targeting **all** critical factual elements and their relationships within the claim.  
   - Use full names, titles, roles, dates, and other identifiers as needed to differentiate entities clearly and avoid ambiguity.  
   - When the claim contains multiple related assertions, logically combine them into one comprehensive verification question rather than multiple independent queries.  
   - Avoid vague or open-ended phrasing; the question should be constructed to yield definitive validation or refutation when answered by authoritative sources.  
   - Incorporate temporal information when relevant (e.g., “in 1982,” “during World War II,” “currently”).

4. **Domain-Specific Guidance and Examples:**  
   - **Historical and Biographical Claims:** Use full names (e.g., Rudolf Höss, Gabe Saporta), specify roles or relations (e.g., Auschwitz commandant, former bassist and lead vocalist), and include dates or periods when applicable.  
   - **Sports-Related Claims:** Include full player names, event names, match details, team names, league or season context, and roles (e.g., head coach in 2013 Big Ten Conference).  
   - **Film and Literary Claims:** Use precise movie or book titles, director or author full names, award names (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), and roles such as co-director or screenwriter.  
   - **Botanical and Biological Claims:** Mention genus or species names, counts, common names, and taxonomic status where relevant.  
   - **Corporate/Geographic Claims:** Use exact company names, nationality, headquarters city, and operational information.

5. **General Strategy:**  
   - Begin by identifying all verifiable factual elements from the claim.  
   - Cross-reference these elements with the notes for any relevant data, paying attention to potential gaps or confirmation.  
   - Integrate all essential verification points into a single comprehensive question.  
   - Phrase your query clearly to avoid multiple interpretations or the need for multiple follow-ups.  
   - Always prioritize clarity, completeness, and domain-specific precision.

6. **Objective:**  
   - The resulting query should be a precise tool enabling retrieval or validation of all aspects of the claim in one authoritative search or fact-check, thereby minimizing the need for multiple queries and improving accuracy of verification.

Example of query construction strategy:  
- If a claim states, “Person A defeated Person B in Event X in Year Y, and Person C began competing at age Z,” formulate a single query like:  
  “Did Person A defeat Person B in Event X in Year Y, and did Person C begin competing at age Z?”  
- If a claim includes relational or genealogical data, e.g., “The grandfather of Person A was the longest-serving in Role B and conducted negotiations with Entity C,” your query should explicitly ask:  
  “Is the grandfather of Person A the longest-serving individual in Role B, and did he conduct negotiations with Entity C?”

By following these detailed instructions, you will consistently create focused, efficient queries that successfully verify complex claims using comprehensive evidence from the notes.
