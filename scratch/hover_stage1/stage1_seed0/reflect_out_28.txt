- `claim`: A factual statement or set of statements that may be true, false, or partially true.  
- `notes`: A list of extracted facts, sentences, or information snippets that pertain to the `claim` and provide supporting or contradictory evidence. These may contain some repetition or overlapping information.  
- `context`: A list of encyclopedia-style or detailed descriptive paragraphs or entries that provide background, definitions, or detailed explanations about entities, works, or subjects connected to the `claim`.

Your task is to produce two outputs:  
1. `new_notes`: A distilled, concise, and factual subset of the `notes` that directly address and relate to the `claim`, removing redundancy, irrelevant or contradictory information, while preserving the core supporting or refuting evidence. These should be accurate, focused, and reflect a clear, evidence-based summary relating to the claim.  
2. `titles`: A list of document or article titles referencing the main entities or topics that underpin the claim and your `new_notes`. These should represent the key authoritative sources or concepts relevant to validating or invalidating the claim.

Detailed guidance and domain-specific considerations:

- The `claim` often involves multiple components or sub-claims, typically about persons, works (e.g., musical compositions, films, albums), or entities, and their relationships (authorship, directorship, number of works, nationality, etc.). Address each part of the claim explicitly in your reasoning and decisions about what notes to select.  
- The `notes` may include duplications or near-duplicates and sometimes conflicting statements. Your role is to synthesize and filter them into a coherent, non-redundant set that best supports or contradicts the claim based on the combined evidence from both `notes` and `context`.  
- The `context` provides additional authoritative information, background, or disambiguation about the entities or works mentioned in the claim and notes, often in the form of encyclopedia entries or synopsis paragraphs. This is crucial for verifying facts such as nationalities of persons, dates of composition or release, relationships among people and works, band membership, number of albums, and so forth.  
- Typical domains include music (composers, albums, bands), film and television (directors, films, TV series, co-writers), and related biographical or discographic data. Expect to encounter detailed cataloging data (e.g., opus number, catalogue number, premiere dates), co-creators, variations of works, and possible confusion of entities with similar or overlapping names.  
- Verify carefully the factual correctness of each piece of information relative to the claim; clarify any ambiguity when possible with information from the context. When data is insufficient to confirm or refute part of the claim, explicitly note this uncertainty in your new notes.  
- The `titles` output should cover all main entities and works directly related to the claim and your final notes. These titles typically correspond to the exact document or article names that provide primary evidence or background, such as biographies of persons, specific albums or compositions, films or TV series, or notable concepts referenced.  

Generalizable strategy to follow:  
- Parse and understand the claim fully, breaking down complex or compound assertions.  
- Analyze the notes for relevance to each component of the claim, identifying supporting, neutral, or contradictory information. Remove duplicates.  
- Leverage the context to verify dates, relationships, and factual details that may not be explicit or clear in the notes.  
- Compose a summarized set of `new_notes` accurately reflecting only the pertinent facts relevant to the claim validation.  
- Select `titles` that correspond to all significant named entities and works involved, ensuring coverage of the claim’s scope.  
- If components of the claim cannot be verified or have contradictory evidence, state the uncertainty or correct the misunderstanding clearly and comprehensively in your notes and reasoning.  

This task requires both fact verification and summarization to produce accurate, clear, and concise evidence and source indicator outputs reflecting the intricate relationships among creators, artworks, and their attributes.
