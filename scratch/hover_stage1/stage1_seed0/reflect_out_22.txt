Task Description: 

You will be given three input fields: 
- `claim` (a statement or assertion containing one or more factual claims), 
- `notes` (a list of sentences or short paragraphs that contain relevant extracted facts, evidence, or observations related to the claim), 
- `context` (a list of document snippets or summaries relevant to the claim, usually containing background information and source material). 

Your goal is to produce two output fields: 
1. `new_notes`: a concise list of the most relevant and factual notes that directly support or refute the factual claims made in the `claim`. Select or synthesize key insights from the inputs that clarify whether the claim is supported, contradicted, or unverifiable given the data. Avoid redundancy, and prioritize clarity and factual precision.

2. `titles`: a list of document titles or named entities relevant to the claim and the reasoning. These titles should represent the main sources or key subjects that correspond to the claim and the supporting/refuting evidence in `new_notes`. Include all pertinent entities even if the note does not explicitly mention them but they are necessary for understanding or verifying the claim.

Detailed Guidance: 
- Carefully analyze the factual claim(s) presented. Claims may relate to biographical facts, geographic or institutional associations, relationships between entities, origin or national identity of products or people, among other facts.
- Evaluate the `notes` for relevant information that affirms, denies, or fails to support the claim. Select the most informative, directly relevant sentences from `notes` to form `new_notes`. If multiple notes repeat similar information, synthesize or select the clearest and most precise ones.
- Examine the contextual `context` snippets to understand domain-specific facts or background that may clarify ambiguous points, verify factual accuracy, or suggest missing relevant entities for `titles`. Context often contains meta-information about organizations, individuals, locations, or historical data.
- In `titles`, include all key entities, concepts, or documents that are prime sources or holders of relevant knowledge supporting your notes and reasoning. This may include individuals (people named in the claim or context), places (specific parks, cemeteries, institutions), products (computers, games), or organizations. Include names even if the exact link in the notes is indirect but implied or essential.
- If facts in the claim relate to complex or niche domain knowledge (e.g., specific historical figures, national parks' boundaries, software developed for particular hardware), incorporate this domain-specific information in your reasoning and outputs, as it frequently determines claim veracity.
- Your reasoning (not required in output, but integral to selection) should clarify any assumptions, highlight contradictions or missing information, and indicate when claims cannot be verified.
- The format for outputs will be:

  ```
  new_notes
  [list of concise, relevant note sentences]

  titles
  [list of key document or entity titles relevant to the claim and notes]
  ```

Generalizable Strategy: 
- Identify and prioritize notes that directly address the claim components.
- Cross-reference the claim facts with context to detect missing but critical entities or connections.
- Assemble `titles` that fully cover the claim’s factual scope along with relevant domain specifics mentioned in the context.
- When claim aspects cannot be confirmed or contradicted, explicitly acknowledge the absence of sufficient evidence in `new_notes`.
- Use domain knowledge from context snippets to provide nuanced and precise answers relating to niche subjects (e.g., musical teachers, national park boundaries, computer hardware national origin).

This approach ensures your outputs are factually accurate, domain-aware, and comprehensive concerning the claim’s verification.
