Task Description:  
You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:  
- `claim`: A statement or assertion involving one or more entities, facts, or relationships that needs verification or contextualization. Claims often include references to people, places, events, works (e.g., films, songs), or other domain-specific entities.  
- `notes`: A list of extracted or derived sentences related to the claim and entities mentioned within it, possibly from prior retrievals or reasoning steps. These notes vary in specificity and reliability, and some might contain partial or contradictory information.  
- `context`: A list of factual passages, encyclopedia-like summaries, or descriptions that provide detailed background knowledge on entities mentioned in the claim and notes. These passages may contain historical, biographical, geographical, or cultural information relevant to understanding or verifying the claim.

Outputs:  
- `new_notes`: A concise and relevant subset of notes, accurately reflecting verified or strongly supported facts from the `notes` and the `context` in relation to the `claim`. These should clarify, confirm, or correct aspects of the claim with explicit references to entities, places, dates, or relationships where applicable. Overlap and redundancies should be minimized; select the strongest, factually consistent notes that best support or refute the claim.  
- `titles`: A list of document or entity titles referring to key articles, entities, or nodes in the knowledge base that are essential for understanding or verifying the claim. These should correspond to the most relevant entries found in the `context` and `notes`. Titles typically represent people, places, works (films, songs), or thematic subjects that are central to the claim.

Detailed Instructions & Domain-Specific Guidance:  
1. Understand the claim carefully, identifying all key entities (people, places, organizations, films, songs, products, events, etc.) and their asserted relationships.  
2. Use the `notes` as a preliminary set of statements touching upon these entities or relevant facts, but verify their content against the `context`.  
3. The `context` offers authoritative or encyclopedic background information; use it to confirm the accuracy and completeness of facts in the claim and notes.  
4. When generating `new_notes`, include:  
   - Verified birthplaces, dates, and biographical details for persons.  
   - Accurate titles, roles (e.g., actor, director), and participation in films or other works.  
   - Geographical descriptions, rankings (e.g., largest city, population rank), and political subdivisions.  
   - Product attributes such as flavors, origins, or marketing connections when relevant.  
   - Corrections or clarifications where claims or notes are inaccurate or incomplete.  
5. Avoid including general, unrelated, or unsupported information in `new_notes`. Focus on facts that directly verify or clarify the claim.  
6. For `titles`, capture all entities and topics crucial to the claim’s verification, prioritizing those explicitly mentioned in both claim and context.  
7. If certain relevant entities referenced in the claim or notes are missing completely in the context, note this gap in `new_notes` and include plausible titles based on the claim’s terms that would be expected to provide relevant information if available.  
8. Maintain clarity, factual correctness, and relevance. Responses should be concise without losing necessary detail.  
9. Ensure consistency between `new_notes` and `titles` — the notes should reflect facts drawn from or supported by the documents or entities named in the titles.

Generalizable Strategy:  
- Break down the claim into component factual assertions.  
- Cross-check the assertions with notes and validate with authoritative context passages.  
- Select or correct notes to produce a distilled, precise summary of the claim’s factual status.  
- Identify and extract the core entities or documents related to these assertions to form the titles list.

This procedure enables effective verification, summarization, and explanation of complex claims leveraging multiple knowledge sources in a coherent and concise manner.
