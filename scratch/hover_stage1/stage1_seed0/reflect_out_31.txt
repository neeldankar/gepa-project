You are tasked with generating a single, concise, and precise natural language query designed to verify or fact-check a given claim using the associated notes. The query must enable efficient retrieval of authoritative evidence that confirms or refutes all key factual components of the claim together.

Task Inputs:
- `claim`: A statement containing one or more factual assertions that need verification.
- `notes`: A collection of relevant factual information, background data, domain terms, names, dates, locations, affiliations, and indirect evidence related to the claim's subject matter.

Detailed Task Instructions:

1. **Identify Key Factual Elements in the Claim:**
   - Extract every critical factual detail or assertion (e.g., names, roles, places, relationships, numbers, titles).
   - Detect implied or explicit relationships among claim elements that must be verified simultaneously to confirm the claim’s truth (e.g., person X’s role in event Y, the location of company Z during a specified time period).

2. **Leverage Notes to Inform Query Formulation:**
   - Carefully review provided notes for all relevant domain-specific information and context.
   - Identify any proper names (full names of individuals, organizations, places), titles (awards, job roles), temporal details (years, seasons), and domain-specific terminology.
   - Pay attention to clarifying or disambiguating information present in the notes (e.g., differentiating multiple entities with similar names, location specifics).

3. **Domain-Specific Entities and Examples to Consider:**
   - **Literary domain:** Full person names (e.g., Alan Sillitoe, Elfriede Jelinek), awards (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), nationalities, professions (poet, diplomat, writer).
   - **Film and music:** Film titles (e.g., Finding Dory, A Bug's Life), directors/co-directors, screenwriters, song titles, band affiliations, co-writers.
   - **Botany and taxonomy:** Genus and species names, species count, vernacular names in multiple languages, taxonomic or classification status.
   - **Corporate and geographic data:** Company names, nationality, headquarters locations, operational or historical details.
   - **Sports and teams:** Names of coaches with specific roles (e.g., head coach, offensive coordinator), team names, relevant seasons, leagues, or conferences.

4. **Formulate a Single, Clear, and Specific Verification Query:**
   - Integrate all key factual elements and their relationships into one direct question.
   - Use precise domain-specific terms and full names to avoid ambiguity.
   - Explicitly specify temporal or contextual details when relevant (e.g., “in the 2013 Big Ten Conference season”).
   - Avoid vague or open-ended wording. The query must directly target verification of all parts of the claim simultaneously.
   - If the claim includes multiple related assertions, combine them logically into one cohesive question instead of multiple fragmented queries.

5. **General Strategy for Query Construction:**
   - Frame the query as a verification question such as “Is/Was X the Y in context Z?”, “Did person A write film B and co-write song C?”, or “Are both building A and building B located in country C?”
   - Reference all necessary domain-specific entities to ensure the query leads to unequivocal evidence retrieval.
   - Maintain clarity and conciseness while covering all factual components at once for efficient fact-checking.

6. **Examples of Effective Queries:**
   - “Are Guildford Cathedral, for which Dennis Huntley provided sculptures, and St Paul's Cathedral in London both located in England?”
   - “Is the town administered by the Eurobodalla Shire council and located near Higgin's Creek as well as Deua National Park?”
   - “Is Matt Thiessen the Relient K vocalist who co-wrote the song ‘Rebuild’ with Jon Foreman of Switchfoot, and do both Matt Thiessen and George Thorogood share the profession of musician?”

By following these instructions, you will create targeted, domain-aware verification queries that ensure precise and efficient fact-checking of complex claims using the given notes.
