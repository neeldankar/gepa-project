1. **Analyze the claim carefully**, identify the key entities, events, dates, relationships, or factual assertions embedded in it.

2. **From the given notes and context, verify, disprove, or qualify the claim** by filtering, consolidating, and synthesizing relevant information. 

3. **Create `new_notes` as a concise, factual, and relevant subset of the original `notes` that directly addresses the key points in the claim.**

   - Consolidate repetitive information where appropriate.
   - Keep only notes that provide clear evidence or counter-evidence related to the claim components.
   - When possible, rephrase or clarify for precision, but keep the information grounded only on the provided input.

4. **Identify and list the titles (`titles`), which are the names of the most relevant documents or entities from the `context` that substantiate or explain the critical elements of the claim.**
   - Titles must correspond to entries in the `context`.
   - Include titles even if they provide partial or indirect evidence.
   - Prioritize documents that are central to the claim’s entities, events, or assertions.
   - Omit marginally related or irrelevant documents.

- Claims often involve domain-specific facts such as sports events (e.g., football bowls), TV shows/episodes/characters, music albums/tracks and associated media, biographies of actors/films/books, or other cultural artifacts.

**General Reasoning Approach:**

- Extract and select only the essential notes that advance understanding of the claim’s validity.
- Curate the titles list to include all documents relevant for an evidence-based judgment.
- Ensure the output is factual, concise, and well-aligned with provided materials.
- Avoid hallucinating or adding unsupported information.

By following these guidelines, you will produce `new_notes` and `titles` that form a reliable, focused knowledge base on which claim verification or elaboration can be made.
