You are given three inputs: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim` is a factual statement or assertion that requires verification, clarification, or nuanced evaluation.
- `notes` is a list of textual evidences, observations, or previously extracted facts relevant to the claim and the context.
- `context` is a list of background documents, excerpts, or detailed factual information from which relevant evidence can be sourced.

Your task is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

### Task Details and Goals

1. **new_notes**:  
   - Carefully analyze the `claim` to identify all distinct factual components, including specific entities (people, organizations, works), relationships, temporal details, or negations that need verification.  
   - Cross-reference `notes` with the provided `context` to confirm accuracy and consistency, prioritize facts directly addressing claim elements.  
   - Select and consolidate a concise subset of relevant, accurate, and non-contradictory facts from `notes` that either support, refute, or clarify ambiguities in the claim.  
   - Minimize redundancy by synthesizing repeated or closely related notes.  
   - Exclude notes that are irrelevant, speculative, contradictory, or fail to substantially clarify or evaluate the claim.  
   - When claims involve negations or refutations (e.g., denying a classification), highlight this clearly in your selection.  
   - Ensure that all factual information is traceable to and consistent with the context documents.

2. **titles**:  
   - Identify and list the titles, names, or key entities of primary documents or entities from the `context` most pertinent to evaluating or verifying the claim and the `new_notes`.  
   - These should be sources that provide the essential evidence substantiating the `new_notes`.  
   - Include all main relevant sources that are critical to understanding or validating the claim, but avoid marginal or unrelated titles.  
   - Cover different facets of the claim if it involves multiple entities or dimensions, e.g., people, organizations, publications, or events.

### Additional Domain-Specific Guidance

- Claims often concern detailed, domain-specific facts such as award recipients, dates and authorship of works, affiliations, official titles, or classification of entities (e.g., music genre, institution type).  
- Pay close attention to hierarchical or affiliative relationships—for example, distinguish between federal and provincial political parties or parent and side projects in bands.  
- Disambiguate similarly named entities by verifying context.  
- Assess temporal claims carefully—confirm dates and publication years explicitly if available or note when such data is absent.  
- When claims compare multiple entities (awards, periodicals, institutions), ensure you consider all relevant facts to assess if the comparison is supported or contradicted.  
- If some facts are not supported by the context or notes, explicitly note such information is missing or unconfirmed.

### Reasoning and Methodology to Apply

- Carefully parse the `claim` into its factual components—entities, events, attributes, relationships.  
- Systematically cross-reference each component with notes, then verify those notes against the full context to confirm support or contradiction.  
- Discard unsupported or irrelevant notes early to maximize conciseness and factual focus.  
- Synthesize overlapping or repetitive notes into a coherent, distilled set of `new_notes`.  
- Identify the key, authoritative context documents or excerpts that underpin the chosen `new_notes` to generate the `titles`.  
- Ensure that your final outputs (notes and titles) provide a clear, traceable, and well-supported basis for evaluating the claim.

By following this approach, your outputs will enable a precise, concise, and factual evaluation of complex claims grounded on verified evidence and well-chosen authoritative sources.
