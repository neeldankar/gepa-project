You will be provided with two inputs: a `claim` (a statement containing one or multiple factual assertions) and a set of associated `notes` (text snippets containing relevant domain-specific information, context, or indirect evidence). Your task is to analyze these inputs and generate a single, concise, precise natural language `query` that is intentionally designed to verify or fact-check the entire claim using authoritative sources, ideally through efficient retrieval of relevant evidence.

---

### Purpose of the Task

Your query should explicitly target all key factual components within the claim, including entities, relationships, temporal details, and domain-specific attributes, so that answering it with the information from the notes—or in authoritative external sources—can conclusively confirm or refute the claim.

---

### Detailed Instructions

1. **Identify Key Factual Elements in the Claim**  
   - Break down the claim into all its distinct factual assertions that require verification.  
   - Recognize implied or explicit relationships connecting these elements (e.g., one person performing an action, roles linked to dates, or multiple entities involved together).  
   - Include temporal information (e.g., years, decades, event sequences) where relevant.

2. **Leverage the Notes Thoroughly**  
   - Extract domain-specific facts, names, titles, dates, organizations, taxonomic ranks, awards, or any specialized terminology that relate to the claim’s components.  
   - Use these domain-specific details to disambiguate or specify query entities (e.g., full personal names, official titles, award names, seasons, and roles).  
   - Reflect any repeated or overlapping information in the notes that can help ensure completeness.

3. **Consider Common Niche Domains and Entities**  
   Your queries must account for factual verification in a variety of specialized domains, including but not limited to:  
   - **Historical and Political Figures:** Full names, roles, dates of office, claims about events or rivalries (e.g., Cuban presidents, journalists, revolutionary movements).  
   - **Sports:** Team names, nicknames, player roles (goalkeeper, coach), seasons (with years), leagues or conferences involved, and leader roles in strike forces or play units.  
   - **Literature and Awards:** Full names of authors, award titles (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), their professions (poet, diplomat, writer), nationalities, and award years.  
   - **Film and Media:** Film titles, director and co-directors, screenwriter relationships, release years.  
   - **Botany and Taxonomy:** Genus/species names, numbers of species, common and local names (vernacular), and taxonomic classification status.  
   - **Corporate Entities:** Company names, country nationality, headquarters location, and operational linkages.  
   - **Academic and Scientific Training:** Persons’ affiliations (universities, mentors), temporal establishment of institutions.

4. **Formulate the Query**  
   - Integrate all essential fact components and the relationships between them in a single, direct, and clear question.  
   - Use explicit phrasing that makes it clear which entities and relationships are under scrutiny (e.g., “Did [Person A], who [role or action], also [fact about Person or entity] during [time period]?”).  
   - Avoid vague, overly broad, or open-ended questions; the question should be specific enough for a precise yes/no or confirm/refute answer.  
   - Where multiple related facts must be verified, combine them logically into one composite question rather than splitting into separate queries.  
   - Include temporal references where relevant (e.g., “in 1940,” “during the 1928–29 season”).

5. **General Strategy and Best Practices**  
   - Start by clarifying who, what, when, and where with full names, official titles, and dates as applicable.  
   - When verifying claims with multiple interlinked parts, the query must explicitly establish those connections (e.g., "the person who claimed X, disproved by Y, and held position Z in year W").  
   - Use domain-specific terminology exactly as given or as is standard in authoritative sources to ensure precise retrieval.  
   - Ensure the query references all entities and assertions central to the claim to achieve coverage and avoid partially verified answers.

---

### Outcome

The final `query` must serve as a focused fact-verification question that queries all main factual elements and their relationships from the claim in one statement, effectively enabling retrieval or validation from the given notes or other authoritative databases.

---

### Example Summary

- Claim involving political claimants and presidents → Query specifying the names of people, contested claims, their roles, and timelines.  
- Claim about a sports team nickname and player history → Query specifying team nickname and verifying a particular player’s link to the team and role during a specific season.  
- Claim about a person’s postdoctoral training at a university established before another → Query confirming the person’s training institution and relative founding dates of the entities involved.

Remember, this combination of factual identification, domain-specific terminology usage, and precise connective phrasing in a single question is key to generating an optimal query that efficiently validates or refutes complex claims.
