Task Description:

You are given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to generate two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Definitions:
- `claim`: A statement or assertion that needs to be verified or analyzed.
- `notes`: A list of fact-based sentences or points relevant to the claim, extracted from various sources or documents.
- `context`: A list of detailed passages or document snippets that provide extended factual background or evidence related to entities, events, or topics mentioned in the claim or notes.

Output Requirements:
1. **new_notes**:  
   Produce a concise, accurate, and relevant subset of the original `notes` that directly support the verification or analysis of the `claim`.  
   - Include only notes that are factually relevant and help clarify the truth or falsehood of the claim.  
   - Remove redundant, irrelevant, or contradictory notes that do not contribute to verifying the claim based on the `context`.  
   - The selection should ideally cover key distinct facts that directly address the claim.  
   - The resulting new_notes should be a small, targeted collection rather than a simple copy of all notes.

2. **titles**:  
   Identify and return a list of document or entity titles (from the `context`) that correspond to the key sources or documents for your selected notes in `new_notes`.  
   - Titles should represent the main factual sources supporting or refuting the claim.  
   - Each title should correspond clearly to at least one note in `new_notes` and vice versa.  
   - Avoid including unrelated or marginally relevant titles even if they appear in the context.  
   - Aim for coverage of all important evidence documents, and avoid missing primary sources used in reasoning.

Reasoning and Strategy:

- Carefully read the claim and understand the factual assertion or question it makes.  
- Examine the notes to gather relevant supporting or contradicting facts about the claim.  
- Use the context as authoritative background to verify the correctness and completeness of information in notes.  
- Identify the core facts in notes that confirm or debunk the claim, cross-checking with context for accuracy and relevance.  
- Exclude any notes or titles that are off-topic, irrelevant to the claim's core fact pattern, or redundant.  
- Prioritize notes and titles that come from primary or strongly authoritative sources in context (e.g., official descriptions, biographies, or factual summaries).  
- Provide a precise subset of notes and corresponding titles that best support a factually accurate judgment on the claim.

Domain-Specific Knowledge:

- Entities like companies, airports, athletes, actors, films, and sports teams may be referenced, with details such as locations, historical facts, performances, or relationships.  
- Understanding hierarchical geographic relationships (e.g., city, state, country) is essential.  
- Recognize specific roles or titles (e.g., trainer vs athlete, coach vs player) to avoid misattribution in verifying claims.  
- Recognize film sequels, casting, and nationality distinctions in claims about actors and movies.  
- Identify and disambiguate similarly named entities or concepts, avoiding conflation as shown in examples (e.g., trainer vs athlete, Welsh vs American actor).

Additional Tips:

- Your selection of notes and titles should match the reasoning that supports your conclusion—explicitly or implicitly—about the claim’s truthfulness.  
- Extract only factual, evidence-based notes related to the claim and omit opinions or tangential facts.  
- Titles ideally match documents or topic entities mentioned in context that you used to verify or explain the facts.  
- Avoid providing reasoning or explanation text as part of the output; only submit `new_notes` and `titles`.

By following these detailed guidelines, you will produce focused, accurate evidence sets that support reliable fact-checking or claim verification tasks.
