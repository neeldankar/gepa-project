You will be provided with two inputs: a `claim` (a factual statement potentially containing multiple related assertions) and associated `notes` (relevant textual information containing factual data, context, and domain-specific details). Your task is to generate a single, concise, and precise natural language `query` that can be used to verify or fact-check the entire claim by retrieving authoritative evidence. This query should be designed for maximum retrieval efficiency and should explicitly target all critical factual components within the claim, reflecting their relationships, key entities, and temporal context as provided or implied.

---

### Task Objectives and Requirements:

1. **Analyze the claim comprehensively:**  
   - Identify *all* factual assertions or components that must be verified to confirm the claim’s overall accuracy.  
   - Detect implied relationships or dependencies between multiple components that should be verified in conjunction (e.g., person-role-in-event, entity-location-time, species-subspecies-habitat, film-director-year).  
   - Note any temporal qualifiers (dates, periods) or geographical details relevant to pinpoint verification scope.

2. **Use the notes effectively as background and supporting context:**  
   - Extract domain-specific facts—such as full names of individuals, roles, awards, species data, locations, organizational details—from the notes.  
   - Pay attention to distinctions within domain concepts (e.g., difference between species and subspecies, distinctions among locations within a region, exact titles, or official designations).  
   - Be mindful of clarifying or resolving contradictory or overlapping information in notes, especially to identify key factual conflicts in the claim.

3. **Compose a single, integrated, targeted verification query:**  
   - The query must be a clear, closed-form question designed to confirm or refute *all* key factual elements and their correct relationships in one retrieval step.  
   - Where the claim contains multiple linked facts (e.g., director and film, species and habitat, person and role in a time frame, band membership and location), incorporate them logically and cohesively into a single question.  
   - Use full proper names, official titles, taxonomic nomenclature, complete geographic designations, and temporal qualifiers to remove ambiguity and ensure precise disambiguation.  
   - Avoid vague, broad, or compound questions that cannot yield a definitive yes/no or factual answer relevant to the claim’s verification.  
   - Reflect any relevant timeline information explicitly (e.g., “in 2006,” “currently,” “in 2013 season”).

4. **Domain-specific entities and factual elements to incorporate as needed:**  
   - **Individuals and roles:** full names, nationalities, roles/positions (e.g., head coach, offensive coordinator, diplomat, poet, director) with associated temporal context.  
   - **Awards and recognitions:** full award names (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature), awardees, their relevant fields or roles.  
   - **Film and media:** film titles with release years, directors and co-directors, screenwriters, production credits.  
   - **Botanical and zoological:** genus and species names, subspecies distinctions, counts of species, endemic regions, and habitat details.  
   - **Geopolitical and geographic details:** city, county/state/province, country names, and hierarchical geographic relations (e.g., Bergen is in Hordaland county, Norway).  
   - **Corporate identities:** company names, nationality, headquarters cities.  
   - **Sports teams and league info:** team names, coaches’ roles, seasons, conferences/leagues.

5. **General strategy for constructing the query:**  
   - Formulate verification questions like:  
     - “Was [Person Full Name] the [Role] of [Team/Organization] in [Year/Season]?”  
     - “Is [Plant/Animal Taxon] endemic to [Location] or found elsewhere?”  
     - “Did [Director Full Name] direct [Film Title] released in [Year], and did [Co-Director] also co-direct this film?”  
     - “Is [Company Name] headquartered in [City, Country] and operating in [industry]?”  
   - Ensure the query logically links all relevant claim components (entities and relationships) into a *single* question, avoiding fragmented multiple queries.

6. **Avoid common pitfalls:**  
   - Don’t neglect verifying temporal and spatial relationships explicitly when relevant to resolve claim accuracy.  
   - Avoid partial queries that only test some aspects of the claim or omit key linked elements.  
   - Prevent ambiguity by always using precise, domain-specific identifiers from the notes.

---

### Output format:

- Deliver the query as a single, clear question sentence that explicitly references all necessary entities and relationships from the claim and notes.

---

By following these detailed instructions, you will create a concise and precise fact-checking query that fully captures the scope of the claim’s factual content and enables efficient retrieval of authoritative verification evidence.
