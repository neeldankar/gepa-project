Task Description:  
You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your goal is to analyze and synthesize these inputs to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.  

Inputs:  
- `claim`: A statement or assertion potentially involving factual information about people, films, music, events, or other entities.  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets extracted from various sources that relate to the claim, often containing facts, dates, relationships, and details pertinent to verifying or explaining the claim. These notes may corroborate, contradict, or provide relevant contextual background.  
- `context`: A list of longer passages or encyclopedic entries providing detailed information about entities or topics referenced in the claim or notes. These passages typically contain factual data that can support or refute the claim, and often include biographical details, release dates, production companies, roles, significant events, or other relevant domain-specific content.  

Outputs:  
- `new_notes`: A concise, coherent, and focused subset or synthesis of the most relevant factually accurate pieces of information from the `notes` and `context` that directly pertain to evaluating the claim. These should summarize or clarify the key details needed to understand or judge the validity of the claim. Avoid redundant information and remove extraneous or speculative content.  
- `titles`: A list of the most relevant document or entity titles from the `context` (or corresponding references) that best support the synthesis and explanation in `new_notes` and relate substantially to the claim. These titles often correspond to key Wikipedia-style documentation entries or named entities pivotal to the claim's verification.  

Approach and Strategy:  
1. Carefully parse the `claim` to identify all entities, temporal references, and relationships it asserts. Note any persons, films, bands, products, or events involved and the specific factual assertions (e.g., birth dates, casting, production companies, release dates).  
2. Review the `notes` as preliminary evidence gleaned from various sources. Identify any direct confirmations, contradictions, or uncertainty expressed about the claim’s components.  
3. Deeply analyze the `context` documents. These tend to be encyclopedic entries with detailed factual descriptions that can fill in gaps or resolve ambiguities in the `notes`. Look for:  
   - Explicit birth dates, biographical details, or membership roles to disambiguate similarly named entities (e.g., differentiate people with similar names).  
   - Precise film titles, production companies, release dates, and cast listings to evaluate claims about movies or shows.  
   - Clear musical attributions, band memberships, songwriting credits, and tour details to assess music-related claims.  
4. Cross-reference all three inputs to determine:  
   - Which facts from the notes and context directly validate or invalidate the claim?  
   - Whether the claim’s elements align with authoritative information from the context.  
   - The minimal essential evidence needed to explain the truthfulness or falsity of the claim.  
5. Compose `new_notes` that summarize this essential information clearly and factually without extraneous speculation or overlong details. Focus on direct evidence rather than ambiguous or unsupported statements.  
6. Select `titles` that correspond exactly to the most relevant, fact-rich context documents or entities underpinning the claim, ideally those which include names, titles, or topics explicitly mentioned in the claim and notes.  

Domain-specific considerations and common pitfalls to avoid:  
- Names that are very similar (e.g., Darren Hayes versus Darren Middleton) require exact birth dates or career roles to distinguish reliably.  
- Song or album titles may be ambiguous without context; confirm connections clearly before assuming.  
- Film casting claims should be corroborated strictly by cast lists or production notes; beware of omitting or wrongly including actors.  
- Production companies and release years are often crucial to verifying film claims; include those when relevant.  
- Avoid mixing up unrelated works or artists, especially when multiple instances involve same or similar names/facts.  
- When a claim includes temporal relations (e.g., one person born before another, or one movie release predating another), carefully identify and confirm dates from the context.  
- If a claim references obscure or unclear items (e.g., a song title not clearly linked in context), state that uncertainty in `new_notes` or note lack of evidence instead of assuming unknown connections.  

In summary, your task is to produce a factual, succinct explanation and relevant title pointers that enable understanding and verification of the claim, based on carefully integrated information from the given notes and context.
