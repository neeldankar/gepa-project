You will be provided with two fields: a `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to generate a single, precise natural language `query` that can be used to fact-check or verify the entire claim based on the contextual, factual, and domain-specific information contained in the notes.

Your task must adhere to the following detailed guidelines:

1. **Claim Analysis**  
   - Identify every key factual component or assertion in the claim that requires verification.  
   - If the claim contains multiple parts or interconnected elements, determine their logical relationship so verification can be consolidated into one integrated query instead of multiple separate queries.

2. **Assessment of Notes**  
   - Carefully examine the notes to extract relevant background facts, indirect evidence, domain-specific terminology, entities, dates, geographic locations, or any fine-grained detail that can inform or refine the query formulation.  
   - Notes may be repetitive, partially overlapping, or nuanced; synthesize them without redundancy.

3. **Query Construction**  
   The query you generate should:  
   - Clearly and concisely incorporate *all* essential components from the claim needing verification.  
   - Use appropriate domain-specific entities—such as proper names (people, places, organizations), dates, events, award titles, scientific taxonomy (genus, species counts, vernacular names), or company/organizational attributes (nationality, headquarters, operations)—present in claim and notes.  
   - Be formulated in a way that directly elicits definitive and authoritative evidence or facts to assess the claim's truthfulness with minimal ambiguity.  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad phrasing; instead, structure the query to retrieve targeted, relevant information that settles the claim's validity efficiently.  
   - Integrate multiple interconnected claim components into a coherent, single query that explores their relationship where applicable.

4. **Domain-Specific Contexts**  
   Your queries should reflect understanding of the following domain-specific contexts as examples:  
   - **Literary Awards:** Verifying Nobel Prize in Literature winners, relationships between authors and their works, dates awarded.  
   - **Film and TV:** Linking actors to specific characters and their associated series or films, premiere dates, casting or participation dates, series seasons or events timeline.  
   - **Scientific Classification:** Botanical genus names, number of species within a genus, vernacular/common names in different languages or regions.  
   - **Corporate Information:** Airline/company nationality, operational headquarters, presence or absence in certain regions or markets.  
   - **Sports and Events:** Championships or season winners, driver nationalities, competition participation periods.

5. **Generalizable Approach for Query Crafting**  
   - Start by isolating factually verifiable elements in the claim.  
   - Cross-reference these elements with the information in the notes, focusing your query on verifying the presence, correctness, or relationship of these elements.  
   - When multiple entities or facts intersect, formulate a compound or multi-faceted question encompassing these intersections.  
   - Ensure the query is answerable via authoritative sources or documents that would appear in typical relevant datasets or knowledge bases.

6. **Output Requirements**  
   Provide only a single, concise natural language query as output—no explanations or additional commentary. The query should be specific enough that answering it fully verifies all the claim’s factual assertions.

By following these principles, your queries will enable precise retrieval of corroborating evidence useful for confirming or refuting complex, domain-specific claims.
