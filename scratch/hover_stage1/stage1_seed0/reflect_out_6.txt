You will be given two fields as input: a `claim` and associated `notes`. Your task is to generate a single, concise, precise natural language `query` that is intended to verify or fact-check the `claim` based on information given in the `notes`. The final query should be designed for efficient retrieval of authoritative evidence that confirms or refutes all key factual elements presented in the claim.

Detailed task instructions:

1. **Analyze the claim:**  
   - Identify all key factual components or assertions that need verification.  
   - Recognize any implied relationships between entities or multiple claim components that must be verified together to confirm the claim’s overall truthfulness.

2. **Utilize the notes:**  
   - Review the notes carefully as they provide relevant factual data, context, and background information.  
   - Notes may include domain-specific facts, indirect evidence, overlapping or repetitive information, dates, names, titles, awards, species counts, corporate details, etc.  
   - Incorporate any domain-specific terminology (e.g., proper names of individuals, awards, companies, taxa) relevant to the claim verification.

3. **Formulate the query:**  
   - Compose a single, clear, direct question that targets all essential factual elements and their connections (when multiple parts exist).  
   - The query should explicitly reference relevant domain-specific entities (e.g., a person’s full name and role, award titles, film titles and director names, species genus/species count, airline nationality and base).  
   - Where the claim contains multiple related parts, integrate them logically into one query to avoid fragmented checks needing multiple queries.  
   - Avoid vagueness or open-ended questions; the query should lead to precise validation or refutation.

4. **Examples of domain-specific entities and components to consider (to be used as needed based on claim content):**  
   - **Literary awards and persons:** full names of individuals (e.g., Alan Sillitoe, Miguel Ángel Asturias, Elfriede Jelinek), Nobel Prize in Literature, their nationality, and specific roles (poet, diplomat, writer).  
   - **Film and screenwriting credits:** film titles (Finding Dory, A Bug's Life), directs/co-directors, authorship relations.  
   - **Botanical classification:** genus names, number of species, vernacular names in various languages, and taxonomic status.  
   - **Corporate identity and location:** company names, nationality, headquarters city, operational details.  
   - **Sports teams and leadership:** individual roles (head coach, offensive coordinator), team names, relevant league or conference (e.g., Big Ten Conference 2013 season).  

5. **General strategy:**  
   - The query should be framed as a verification question (e.g., “Was X the Y in [context or time period]?” or “Did A direct B and also co-direct C?”).  
   - Combine multiple linked facts into one integrated query for efficiency and clarity.  
   - Reflect any temporal information if relevant (e.g., “in 2013”, “currently”).  
   - Where entities might have similar names or multiple aspects, use full names and specific details to avoid confusion.  

The objective is to produce a targeted query that, when answered using the notes or queried in authoritative sources, decisively clarifies the accuracy of the claim based on its main factual components and their relationships.
