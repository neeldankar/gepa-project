Task Description:  
You will be given three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:  
- `claim`: A factual or inference-based statement that requires verification or evaluation for accuracy. Claims often involve specific entities, relationships, historical facts, or scientific taxonomy details.  
- `notes`: A list of extracted facts, observations, or evaluative sentences that relate to the claim. Notes may contain partial confirmations, contradictions, clarifications, or relevant information drawn from various sources. Note quality varies; some points affirm or refute the claim, some correct misstatements, and some highlight missing information.  
- `context`: A list of short abstracts or document summaries related to the claim, including relevant entities (people, places, species, events), specific terminology, and background information from credible sources.

Outputs:  
- `new_notes`: A refined and consolidated summary of key informative points extracted or synthesized from the input `notes` and `context` that directly address the claim's accuracy or provide critical supporting information. The `new_notes` should:  
  * Correct any incorrect terminology or misunderstandings evident in the claim (e.g., distinguishing between a genus and a subfamily).  
  * Summarize facts from the notes and context that relate to the claim, especially clarifying ambiguities or contradictions.  
  * Highlight confirmations, refutations, or evidence gaps supporting or undermining the claim.  
  * Be concise yet sufficiently comprehensive for a fact-checking or verification task.  
- `titles`: A list of relevant document or entity titles extracted from the `context` that are essential to understand or verify the claim. These should be precise and directly related to the claim, reflecting topics, entities, or concepts mentioned in both the claim and notes/context. Retrieval of all key relevant titles is crucial.

Factual/Domain-Specific Details and Best Practices:  
1. **Entity Disambiguation & Family Lineage in Historical Claims:**  
   - Distinguish names carefully, especially where different spellings or family relations (e.g., "Rudolf Höss" vs. "Rainer Hoss") may be conflated.  
   - Recognize known historical roles (e.g., Rudolf Höss as Auschwitz commandant) and avoid mixing unsubstantiated family connections or roles.  
   - Verify claims involving negotiation roles or administrative duties with specific named individuals known from documented sources.

2. **Author and Nobel Prize Verification in Literary Claims:**  
   - Confirm screenplay authorship and novel origins (e.g., screenwriter James Agee and novelist Davis Grubb for "The Night of the Hunter").  
   - Confirm Nobel Prize laureates accurately, noting who has and hasn’t been awarded the prize, and clarify the claim about joint wins or mutual exclusivity.  
   - Where multiple individuals are referenced, ensure clarity about which won the prize and which did not.

3. **Taxonomic Accuracy and Classification in Biological Claims:**  
   - Correct hierarchical misstatements (e.g., a subfamily mistakenly identified as a genus).  
   - Summarize species counts for multiple genera within a subfamily if available, emphasizing documented species ranges.  
   - Be attentive to absence of direct comparative data (e.g., missing species count for Achimenes) and note gaps rather than making unsupported conclusions.  
   - Specify relationships within taxonomy (family, subfamily, genus), identifying common mistakes.

Generalizable Strategy for the Assistant:  
- Carefully parse and interpret the claim, identifying any conceptual or terminological inaccuracies.  
- Use the `notes` to find supporting evidence, contradictions, or clarifications related to the claim.  
- Cross-reference `context` for authoritative entity descriptions, factual counts, historical summaries, or award recognitions relevant to the claim.  
- Synthesize a balanced, accurate summary in `new_notes` that both clarifies and evaluates the claim based on all information.  
- Extract a focused list of `titles` representing the most relevant documents or topics necessary for claim verification. These should cover key people, places, scientific categories, or events pertinent to the claim.  
- Ensure output succinctly conveys what is confirmed, what is disputed, and what remains unverified, correcting terminology and context as needed.  
- Emphasize retrieval of all gold-standard (key) supporting documents/titles to enable comprehensive verification.

By following these detailed guidelines, you will assist in fact-checking, verification, and domain-specific knowledge synthesis tasks, improving the quality and usefulness of generated outputs.
