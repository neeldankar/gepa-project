- `claim`: A statement or assertion that may be true or false.  
- `notes`: A list of factual statements, observations, or findings related to the claim, drawn from various documents and sources. These notes may include confirming or contradicting information, nuances, or context relevant to assessing the claim.  
- `context`: A list of document summaries or excerpts providing background information and factual data relevant to the entities, events, or concepts referenced in the claim and notes.  

Your task is to produce two output fields:  
- `new_notes`: A concise and focused synthesis of the relevant facts from the `notes` and `context` that directly support, refute, or clarify the claim. These should distill the information into a clear set of points that accurately summarize the evidence relating to the claim, removing redundancies and irrelevant details.  
- `titles`: A list of document or topic titles corresponding to key sources or entities strongly relevant to validating or understanding the claim, drawn from the `context`. These often represent proper nouns (e.g., names of people, films, games, operas) or major concepts essential to the claim’s fact-checking.  

Instructions and guidelines:  
1. **Understand the claim carefully** – identify what is being asserted, including any comparisons, qualifications, or specific factual claims (e.g., production details, popularity comparisons, number of acts in an opera).  
2. **Use the notes and context** as evidence to assess the claim’s validity. The notes often contain synthesized facts, but can be repetitive or incomplete; context provides authoritative or detailed background references.  
3. **Reason explicitly** about the relationship between claim, notes, and context, including correcting errors or clarifying misunderstandings when detected (e.g., correcting film nationalities, unpacking which actors starred together, noting differences in forms like films vs operas).  
4. **Summarize key evidence in new_notes** succinctly and clearly. Include only those points that directly support or refute the claim or clarify relevant factual aspects. Avoid speculation or unsupported assertions.  
5. **Select titles** that represent the most important sources or topics needed for a reader or retriever system to understand or verify the claim. Titles should be precise (e.g., including years for films if applicable) and reflect entities central to the claim’s verification.  
6. **Domain-specific nuances:**  
   - Recognize that popularity claims may need comparisons of global vs niche popularity, including reference to variants (e.g., chess and its variants vs designer’s games).  
   - For film-related claims, distinguish between actors’ co-starring roles, production companies, release years, nationalities, genres, and director credits.  
   - For operatic vs film-based claims, clarify structural differences—e.g., operas have acts, but films have scenes or sequences, and do not follow the same act structure.  
   - Identify and correct potential discrepancies (e.g., nationalities, production companies).  
7. **Generalizable problem-solving approach:**  
   - Extract core factual assertions from notes and context.  
   - Reconcile any conflicts or nuances.  
   - Organize findings into a logically coherent summary (reasoning).  
   - Provide clear and unambiguous new_notes and titles that reflect the most relevant factual data supporting the final assessment.  

By following these detailed instructions, you will facilitate accurate evaluation and retrieval guidance for fact-checking, knowledge verification, or information synthesis tasks involving complex, multi-source input data.
