Task Description:

You will be given a factual `claim` that may encompass multiple interrelated facts involving named entities such as people, places, organizations, species, events, or products. Alongside the claim, you will receive a set of `notes` containing partial background, contextual details, or observations highlighting missing, ambiguous, or conflicting information regarding components within the claim. Your goal is to generate a precise set of search `queries` to verify or fact-check all aspects of the claim by retrieving relevant authoritative documents (e.g., Wikipedia articles).

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A compound statement containing multiple factual assertions, often including specific named entities or complex relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of observations summarizing current knowledge about the claim’s components, including contradictions, knowledge gaps, ambiguities, or missing links that affect verification.

Output:  
- `query`: One or more clear, focused, and well-structured factual questions or search prompts designed to retrieve documents that directly address every key point of the claim. The queries should be suitable for document retrieval and fact verification.

Key Guidelines and Domain-Specific Heuristics:

1. **Entity Disambiguation:**  
   Always identify and disambiguate entities by including relevant descriptors (e.g., geographic qualifiers, domain-specific attributes, or alternate names) to uniquely distinguish them. For instance, specify "Solo (Norwegian soft drink)" rather than just “Solo,” or "Echium wildpretii subsp. trichosiphon" instead of merely the species name.

2. **Decompose Complex Claims into Components:**  
   Break down multipart claims into logically connected verification questions, each targeting a single fact or relationship. This ensures comprehensive evidence retrieval covering all facets of the claim.

3. **Focus on Missing, Ambiguous, or Conflicting Details:**  
   Use the notes to identify gaps or contradictions, and generate queries specifically designed to resolve these (e.g., geographic overlap of entities, precise timeline alignments, or cross-relationships between named entities).

4. **Ensure Full Coverage of the Claim:**  
   Confirm that the generated queries collectively address every factual claim component to avoid partial verification.

5. **Precise and Specific Language:**  
   Frame queries as direct, factual questions or entity-relationship questions to maximize retrieval relevance. Avoid vague or overly broad queries.

6. **Incorporate Domain Context:**  
   When domain knowledge is pertinent (such as historical timelines, biological taxonomy, or geographic subdivisions), integrate this context in queries to target correct and relevant documents.

Generalizable Strategy:  
- Carefully identify all named entities and relationships in the claim.  
- Cross-check with notes to identify knowledge gaps or conflicts.  
- For each fact or connection, generate a precise question or search phrase aimed at retrieving confirming documents.  
- Organize queries to cover the entire claim systematically, addressing each fact separately but logically.  
- Use domain-specific qualifiers and terminology to avoid ambiguity and enhance query precision.

Examples:  
- For a claim about a film actor’s roles and directorial credits:  
   - "Who starred alongside [Actor Name] in [Film Title]?"  
   - "Did [Actor Name] appear in [Second Film Title]?"  
   - "Who directed [Second Film Title]?"  

- For a burial place and geographic containment claim:  
   - "Where is [Person Name] buried?"  
   - "Is [Cemetery Name] located within [Park or Recreation Area Name]?"  

By following these instructions, the queries you generate will be optimized for document retrieval systems to find relevant facts necessary for thorough and complete verification of the entire claim, including nuanced domain-specific knowledge and resolving conflicting or missing information.
