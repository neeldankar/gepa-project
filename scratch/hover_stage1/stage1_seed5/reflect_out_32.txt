Task Description:
You are given three types of inputs to evaluate the factual accuracy of a specific claim:
- `claim`: A statement making a factual or historical assertion involving multiple entities, dates, events, or relationships, frequently related to niche domains such as sports clubs, historical figures, film and TV personalities, or political events.
- `notes`: A list of factual observations or extracted details derived from provided context documents. These include supporting or contradictory information relevant to the claim.
- `context`: A collection of background excerpts, documents, or entity descriptions containing detailed evidence. This includes biographical summaries, organizational affiliations, timelines, media credits, event records, or sponsorship details that directly or indirectly bear on the claim.

Your goal is to produce two outputs:
1. `new_notes`: A distilled, concise, and precise list of key factual points directly supporting or contradicting the claim. These must be synthesized from the original `notes` and thoroughly cross-checked and corroborated by the `context`. Avoid repetition and prune less relevant or redundant observations. When contradictions arise, explicitly clarify which parts of the claim are unsupported or disproven.
2. `titles`: A list of the most relevant document or entity titles from the provided `context` that substantiate or elucidate the claim and its fact-checking. This includes key people, organizations, events, sports clubs, stadiums, film or TV productions, or relevant concepts referenced in the claim or notes.

Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Claims often involve verifying entity relationships, affiliations, specific roles (e.g., voice acting roles in films), participation in group bands or organizations, locations and timelines of sports events or venues, or media production credits.
- Context may include specialized information such as:
  * Names and roles of actors or voice actors linked to films and animated features.
  * Details about sports clubs, including home stadiums, locations, founding dates, notable titles, and other club-specific historical data.
  * Information on music releases (albums, EPs) including associated artists, their nationalities, and group membership.
  * Historical or event-specific details such as dates and venues of finals, premieres, or releases.
- All dates and location details must be carefully correlated to validate timelines or venue claims.
- Clarify distinctions when multiple similarly named entities or events might cause confusion (e.g., different artists with similar EP titles or multiple "Winnie the Pooh" films).

Generalizable Strategy:
- Methodically cross-reference each factoid in `notes` with the relevant information found in `context` to confirm, refute, or qualify the claim.
- Extract a minimal yet comprehensive set of statements that directly address key claim components — especially entity relationships, timelines, roles, and locations.
- Where context contradicts or does not support claim elements, explicitly note the unsupported or disproven aspects.
- Select the central documents or entity titles from the context that most authoritatively undergird the claim verification, avoiding tangential material.
- Maintain clarity, precision, and factual accuracy in both outputs to facilitate straightforward fact-checking or summarization.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A string containing the factual assertion.
- `notes`: A list of strings, each representing a factual observation or attempted validation derived from the context.
- `context`: A list of textual document snippets, each describing entities, events, or concepts related to the claim.

Output Format:
- `new_notes`: A list of concise, distinct, factually supported or contradicting statements derived by synthesizing `notes` and verifying with `context`.
- `titles`: A list of strings representing key documents or entities from `context` that are critical for confirming or understanding the claim.

Ensure:
- All new notes and titles must be grounded in and explicitly supported by the provided context.
- Avoid unsupported speculation or inclusion of facts not backed by context.
- Focus on the nexus of evidence relevant to evaluating the claim.
