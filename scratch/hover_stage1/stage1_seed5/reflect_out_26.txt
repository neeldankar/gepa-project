Task Description:

You will be provided with a factual `claim` that may include multiple interconnected facts or named entities (e.g., people, films, places, products, organizations, historical events, or domain-specific terms). Alongside the claim, you will receive a set of `notes` containing partial, known, or ambiguous background information about the claim and its elements.

Your goal is to generate one or more precise and clearly formulated `queries` to verify or fact-check every factual component of the claim. These queries will be used to search for authoritative evidence (such as Wikipedia or other trusted factual sources) to confirm or refute each element of the claim.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Details:

1. Entity Disambiguation:
   - Explicitly identify and disambiguate *all* named entities in the claim.
   - Use relevant contextual descriptors for each entity to avoid confusion. For example:
     - For films: include the release year or director (e.g., “midnight Madness (1980 film)”, “Return to Oz (1985 film)”)
     - For individuals: specify profession, nationality, or other relevant attributes (e.g., “Jay Park, South Korean singer”)
     - For companies: specify geographic base or industry (e.g., “AutoZone, Memphis-based company”)
     - For events or units: specify time frame or nature (e.g., “2016 Liberty Bowl (January)”, “2015 Kansas State Wildcats football team”)
   - This ensures queries retrieve targeted, relevant documents avoiding ambiguity.

2. Decompose Complex Claims into Discrete Queries:
   - Break down complex claims that contain multiple assertions or facts into multiple, focused queries.
   - Each query should verify exactly one factual element (e.g., film release year, membership status, sponsorship details).
   - Compose queries so that collectively they cover every component of the claim.
   
3. Focus Queries on Missing, Ambiguous, or Contradictory Information in the Notes:
   - Analyze the notes to detect confirmed information, gaps, ambiguities, or contradictions related to the claim.
   - Formulate queries to directly address these unknowns or conflicting points.
   - For example, clarify uncertain release years, define whether an entity is a group or individual, or distinguish between similarly named products or events.
   
4. Ensure Comprehensive Query Coverage:
   - Queries must verify every factual assertion embedded in the claim.
   - Avoid partial coverage that leaves any fact unchecked.
   
5. Precise and Clear Query Formulation:
   - Use explicit, well-structured factual questions or entity-relationship prompts suitable for document retrieval.
   - Avoid vague, overly broad, compound, or open-ended questions.
   - Formulate queries in natural language or search-prompt style that clearly specifies what fact is being checked.
   
6. Incorporate Relevant Domain Context:
   - When applicable, embed domain knowledge or contextual details to tailor queries for maximum precision.
   - Examples:
     - For film-related claims, specify film name and release year or director.
     - For publications, specify title, author, and whether the work is a book, article, or periodical.
     - For geography, specify if a cemetery is located within a park or city.
     - For sports or history claims, specify team seasons, event dates, or sponsorship relation.
   
Generalizable Strategy:

- Parse the claim to extract all named entities with contextual disambiguation.
- Identify each embedded factual assertion.
- Cross-reference with notes to find what is known, ambiguous, or contradictory.
- Decompose the claim into a set of focused queries, each verifying one factual element or gap.
- Use domain-specific knowledge to construct queries with rich descriptors.
- Ensure the final query set comprehensively covers and targets all claim components.
- Output the queries only, without additional explanations.

Output Format:

- Provide one or more lines beginning with `query:` each containing a single explicit factual verification query.
- No commentary or reasoning should be included in the output.
- Each query must be clear, precise, and fully disambiguated.

Example Patterns:

- Film claim queries:
  - “What year was [Film Name] ([Release Year]) released?”
  - “Who composed the musical score for [Film Name] ([Year])?”
  - “Is [Film Name] ([Year]) considered a sequel or part of [Other Film] ([Year])?”
  
- Publication claim queries:
  - “Is [Title] by [Author] a book or a periodical?”
  - “When was [Publication Name] first published?”
  
- Geography or burial claims:
  - “Where is [Person] buried?”
  - “Is [Cemetery] located within [Park or Geographic Area]?”
  
- Historical or sports claims:
  - “When was [Military Unit] formed?”
  - “Did [Sports Team] participate in [Specific Event] in [Year]?”
  - “Who sponsors the [Event Name] held in [City]?”

This detailed guidance ensures the system formulates targeted, well-scoped queries that together fully verify all factual parts of complex claims, resolving ambiguities or contradictions found in the notes, while leveraging relevant domain context.
