Task Description:
You will be provided with three inputs: 
1. `claim` — a statement asserting a factual or historical event involving entities such as people, organizations, events, or concepts, often with intricate relationships (e.g., affiliations, timelines, roles).
2. `notes` — a list of factual observations and supporting or contradictory details related to the claim, extracted or derived based on provided contextual knowledge.
3. `context` — background documents, excerpts, or biographical summaries that give detailed evidence relevant to the claim and its associated entities or events.

Your goal is to critically analyze these inputs to produce two outputs:
1. `new_notes` — a concise, distilled list of factually precise key points that directly support or contradict the claim. These should be derived from the original `notes` but strictly corroborated by evidence present in the `context`. Avoid repetition or less relevant details and focus solely on the crux facts that determine the validity of the claim.
2. `titles` — a list of the most relevant document or entity titles from the `context` that substantiate or illuminate the claim. These may include key people, organizations, events, concepts, or media productions explicitly referenced by the claim or notes.

Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Claims frequently pertain to nuanced, detailed facts about entities such as sports clubs, historical figures, film and TV personalities, political events, or musical groups.
- Claims often involve multiple, interconnected entities and require verifying relationships, affiliations, chronology, roles, or factual narratives (e.g., who collaborated with whom, actor roles, awards won, timelines of events).
- Context includes but is not limited to biographical profiles, historical timelines, organizational memberships, sponsorship affiliations, filmographies, album releases, media credits, festival records, and award listings.
- Evidence corroboration hinges on establishing direct support or contradiction of the claim’s assertions based on these detailed context sources.
- Contradictions or unsupported parts must be noted clearly by excluding them from `new_notes` or by clarifying their absence in context.
- Avoid introducing information not substantiated by the context provided.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Cross-check all elements of the claim against multiple relevant context documents to verify entity relationships, chronology, or roles.
- Synthesize the essential, directly relevant facts from the given notes that are confirmed by the context in a clear, concise manner.
- Include only non-repetitive, critical factual elements that influence the claim’s truth.
- Identify and list the most authoritative or substantial context documents (entity or event titles) related to the claim for added clarity and traceability.
- Clearly distinguish which parts of the claim are supported or unverified based on the available evidence.

Input and Output Format:
- Inputs:
  - `claim`: A textual factual assertion.
  - `notes`: A list of factual statements related to the claim.
  - `context`: A list of text snippets (documents or entity descriptions) providing evidence.
- Outputs:
  - `new_notes`: A concise list of key factual points directly supporting or refuting the claim, grounded in the context.
  - `titles`: A list of relevant document/entity titles from the context that substantiate the claim and its fact-checking.

Ensure your outputs strictly adhere to evidence found in the provided `context` and are informed by the `notes`. Prioritize clarity, factual precision, and relevance for efficient fact-checking or summarization.
