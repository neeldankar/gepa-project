You will be given the following inputs:  
- `claim` (a textual assertion involving entities, events, or relationships)  
- `notes` (a list of textual evidence snippets related to the claim)  
- `context` (a list of additional textual background materials or documents relevant to the claim)

Your task is to produce two outputs:  
1. `new_notes`: a concise, relevant subset or distilled version of the `notes` that directly support or refute the claim or are essential for verifying the claim’s factual content. Remove any duplicates or redundant information to streamline the evidence.  
2. `titles`: a list of titles (i.e., document or article titles) representing the key source documents you have drawn from or that would best support fact-checking or reasoning about the claim.

Detailed instructions and domain-specific facts:  
- The `claim` will often involve complex relationships such as jurisdictional or geographical hierarchies, timelines of performances or releases, filmography and actor/actress film credits, or director nationalities. You should carefully verify temporal claims (e.g., whether one event happened before or after another) and hierarchical or geopolitical relationships (e.g., whether a county is under jurisdiction of a specified city and whether those locations are in the same country).  
- The `notes` may contain partially overlapping or repeated information. Your `new_notes` should consolidate this to the most relevant, non-duplicated facts that directly bear on the claim’s validity.  
- Use the `context` to understand the background and definitions of entities involved (cities, actors, films, directors, etc.), timelines (release dates, premieres), and cross-links (e.g., actors’ roles in multiple films, who directed which film, nationality of directors).  
- When confirming or refuting a claim involving multiple entities, infer logical connections (e.g., if two cities are located in provinces in the same country, then those cities are in the same country).  
- For timeline-related claims (e.g., whether one actor performed in one film before or after another event), extract and compare dates carefully, using context or notes if needed.  
- Titles should be specific and relevant to the claim and your filtered notes, such as official film titles, actor or director biographies, geographic administrative division articles, or TV series and film pages that clarify timeline or casting.  
- Ensure the titles cover all critical entities or events needed to validate the claim.  
- The goal is to provide a minimal yet sufficient set of factually precise notes and a set of titles representing the best supporting sources that would enable a human or system to verify the claim directly.

Summary of generalizable approach:  
- Carefully analyze the claim to identify key entities and relations.  
- Filter and consolidate `notes` to evidence that directly addresses the claim’s components.  
- Reference background `context` to clarify or verify ambiguous information.  
- Extract clear, factual statements about jurisdiction, chronology, cast and crew involvement, and nationality as given or inferable from the texts.  
- Choose document titles that correspond precisely to the sources for those facts, targeting gold-standard or authoritative topics/entities related to the claim.

This approach ensures accuracy, relevancy, and completeness in fact-checking complex, entity-rich claims with multi-source evidence.
