Task Description:

You will be given a factual `claim` that may involve multiple interconnected facts or named entities (such as people, films, places, products, organizations, historical dates, or other domain-specific items). Together with the claim, you will receive a set of `notes` consisting of partial background information, known details, plus observations that highlight gaps, ambiguities, or contradictions around the claim’s elements.

Your goal is to generate a focused set of one or more `queries` designed to verify or fact-check each component of the claim. These queries will be used to retrieve relevant evidence or documents (e.g., from Wikipedia or other authoritative sources) to confirm or refute the claim.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Heuristics:

1. **Entity Disambiguation:**  
   - Explicitly identify all named entities mentioned in the claim.  
   - Always disambiguate entities carefully using relevant descriptors, such as: clarifying a film’s release year, a person’s profession or nationality, distinguishing similarly named entities (e.g., “Solo (Norwegian soft drink)” instead of just “Solo”).  
   - This avoids confusion and ensures queries retrieve targeted, relevant information.

2. **Decompose Complex Claims into Components:**  
   - If the claim includes multiple assertions (e.g., an actor acted in multiple movies, a director’s involvement, a formation date of a military unit), break it down into multiple specific queries, each verifying one discrete fact.  
   - Queries should be connected logically, covering the full scope of the claim without leaving holes.

3. **Target Missing, Ambiguous, or Contradictory Information from the Notes:**  
   - Use the notes to identify what is already known, what is uncertain or missing, and contradictions that must be resolved.  
   - Formulate queries that directly address these gaps or conflicting points to retrieve clarifying evidence.  
   - For example, if it’s unclear whether a publication is a book or a magazine article, or exact formation years of military units, or the nationality of a named individual, queries should reflect these specifics.

4. **Cover Every Fact Embedded in the Claim:**  
   - Ensure your queries collectively verify all factual statements contained in the claim.  
   - Avoid partial coverage that might retrieve documents relevant to only some parts of the claim.

5. **Precise and Clear Query Formulation:**  
   - Queries must be explicit, well-structured factual questions or entity-relationship prompts, suitable for document retrieval.  
   - Avoid vague, overly broad, or open-ended questions.

6. **Include Relevant Domain Context:**  
   - When necessary, incorporate domain knowledge or contextual clues into the queries.  
   - For example, when verifying a film claim, specify the film’s release year or director; for geographical entities, specify if a cemetery is within a park; for military units, specify their formation dates and relation to associated conflicts or films.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Carefully read and parse the claim to extract all named entities and discrete fact assertions.  
- Examine the provided notes thoroughly to uncover what details are confirmed, missing, ambiguous, or contradictory in relation to the claim.  
- For each factual element and especially for each gap or contradiction, generate a focused, specific question or search prompt to obtain clear supporting or refuting evidence.  
- Ensure queries are comprehensive and well-targeted to verify the full claim.  
- Format the final output strictly as one or more `query` lines, with no additional explanation or commentary.

Example Query Patterns:

- For a film claim:  
  - “Who starred in [Film Name] ([Year])?”  
  - “Who directed [Film Name] ([Year])?”  
  - “Is [Person’s Name] an actor in [Film Name] ([Year])?”  

- For a publication claim:  
  - “What is the publication format and date of [Title] by [Author]?”  
  - “Is [Publication Name] a periodical or a book publisher?”  

- For geography or burial claims:  
  - “Where is [Person] buried?”  
  - “Is [Cemetery Name] located within [Park or Geographic Area]?”  

- For military or historical claims:  
  - “When was [Military Unit] formed?”  
  - “What units are featured in [Film or Book Title]?”  

By following these instructions carefully, you will produce precise queries capable of guiding the retrieval of comprehensive, relevant documents to validate or refute complex factual claims.

Input Format:  
- `claim`: A factual statement with one or more components involving named entities or facts.  
- `notes`: A list of strings summarizing current knowledge, including unknowns or contradictions.

Output Format:  
- `query`: One or more explicit, decomposed, and disambiguated search queries targeting factual verifications for each part of the claim.

Remember:  
- Queries must be factual questions fit for document retrieval.  
- Always cover all aspects requiring verification.  
- Disambiguate entities using descriptive context.  
- Use notes to identify which facts require particular attention.  
- Structure queries to maximize retrieval precision and relevance.
