Task Description:

You will be given a `claim`—a factual statement that often includes multiple interconnected facts or entities—and a set of `notes` containing partial background, observations, or contextual details relevant to the claim. These notes may reveal gaps, ambiguities, contradictions, or missing information needed to fully verify the claim.

Your goal is to convert the claim and notes into one or more precise, well-structured `queries` to fact-check or verify each assertion in the claim. These queries will serve as search prompts to retrieve relevant documents or evidence from external sources like Wikipedia.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Guidelines:

1. **Careful Disambiguation of Named Entities:**  
   - Identify all named entities (people, films, TV shows, products, places, events) in the claim.  
   - Include identifying qualifiers or disambiguators when entities have ambiguous or common names (e.g., for films: year, genre, or country; for persons: nationality or profession).  
   - This prevents confusion and ensures correct retrieval.

2. **Decompose Complex or Multi-Part Claims:**  
   - Break down claims involving multiple interconnected facts into discrete queries, each targeting a specific sub-assertion or relationship.  
   - For example, if a claim involves an actor starring in multiple films or a director with multiple credits, formulate separate queries addressing each part to enable comprehensive verification.

3. **Utilize Notes to Identify Verification Gaps and Ambiguities:**  
   - Analyze the notes to find what information is missing, uncertain, or contradictory.  
   - Generate queries specifically targeting these gaps (e.g., questions about whether a person starred in a certain production in a particular year, the exact release date/premiere year, or geographic relationships between locations).

4. **Cover Every Factual Assertion in the Claim:**  
   - Compose a set of queries collectively addressing every factual point embedded in the claim to avoid partial fact-checking.  
   - This includes verifying named entities’ identities, roles, time periods, and relationships as needed.

5. **Use Precise, Specific, and Search-Optimized Language:**  
   - Frame queries as clear, direct factual questions or entity-relationship questions designed to retrieve relevant documents efficiently.  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries that may return irrelevant information.

6. **Incorporate Domain Context Where Relevant:**  
   - When domain knowledge informs interpretation (e.g., distinguishing among director vs. co-director roles, understanding a film’s release year vs. premiere year, the hierarchical relationships between geographic entities), incorporate this context in query formulation to guide targeted retrieval.

Generalizable Strategy to Follow:

- Read the claim carefully and identify all named entities, relationships, and assertions.  
- Cross-reference these with the notes to identify missing, ambiguous, or conflicting details.  
- For each fact or link requiring verification, produce one or more precise, well-scoped queries that directly target that fact or relationship.  
- Ensure the full set of queries covers all critical components of the claim.  
- Format the output strictly as queries without additional commentary or explanation.

Examples of Effective Query Construction:

- For verifying actors and their films or roles:  
  "Who starred in [Film Title] ([Year or distinguishing details])?"  
  "Did [Actor Name] appear in [TV Show Title] during [Year or range]?"  

- For verifying directors and their film credits:  
  "Who directed [Film Title] ([Year])?"  
  "Did [Director Name] co-direct [Film Title]?"  

- For geographic or biographical verification:  
  "Where is [Person Name] buried?"  
  "Is [Cemetery Name] located within [Park or Region Name]?"  

Ensure query phrasing is explicit enough to avoid ambiguity (e.g., include years, descriptors, full names) and aligned with how facts might appear in documents.

By following these detailed instructions, the queries you generate will comprehensively cover the claim’s facts, precisely target gaps identified in the notes, and maximize retrieval accuracy for external document-based verification.
