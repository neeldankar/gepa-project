You will be given three input fields:

- `claim`: A statement or assertion involving one or more entities, facts, or relationships that may be true or false.
- `notes`: A collection of extracted factoids, statements, or evidentiary snippets related to the claim. These often include specific factual information, clarifications, or contradictions relevant to parts of the claim.
- `context`: A set of reference documents or document abstracts relevant to the entities or topics mentioned in the claim, providing background information, definitions, or additional details.

Your task is to analyze the `claim` in light of the `notes` and the `context`, and produce two output fields:

1. `new_notes`: A concise, fully-synthesized, and factually accurate summary of the essential information from the notes and context that directly supports or refutes parts of the claim. This summary should combine overlapping points, eliminate unnecessary repetition, and clarify which parts of the claim are supported or not supported by the evidence given. Use clear, factual phrasing suitable for evidence presentation.

2. `titles`: A list of relevant document or topic titles derived from the context (or notes if applicable) that correspond to the sources or subjects where the key supporting or refuting evidence was obtained. This serves to identify the main evidentiary sources behind the reasoning.

Important details and domain-specific considerations:

- The claim may contain multiple clauses or comparative parts (e.g., about two species, two people, two films). Your reasoning and new_notes should explicitly address each clause or entity separately to clarify which parts are supported or not.

- The notes often include detailed scientific, historical, or domain-specific facts (e.g., botanical habitats, professional roles of named individuals, film production years and collaborations). Your role is to synthesize these into clear conclusions about the claim’s validity.

- Avoid redundancy in new_notes by consolidating repeated facts into single, clear statements.

- Extract and highlight negative evidence or absences of proof where relevant (e.g., “no evidence found that X is native to Y”, “no indication that person Z shares profession with person A”).

- Titles should reflect the main entities or documents discussed (e.g., species names, person names, film titles), and help the reader locate the provenance of the summary evidence.

General strategy to solve the task:

- Carefully parse the claim to identify discrete factual assertions.

- Cross-check all relevant notes and context passages for each assertion, extracting key supporting or contradicting facts.

- Organize evidence logically, separating support and contradiction, and note absence of evidence where this influences conclusions.

- Prepare new_notes as a succinct but comprehensive evidence summary for the claim.

- Identify and list titles of the main source documents from the context or notes that substantiate the summarized evidence.

This task requires careful fact-checking, synthesis of multiple evidence snippets, and precise mapping between claim components and supporting/refuting information, emphasizing clarity, factuality, and relevance in the outputs.
