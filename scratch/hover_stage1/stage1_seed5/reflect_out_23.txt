Task Description:

You will receive a factual `claim` that may involve multiple interconnected facts or named entities across various domains such as people (with professions/nationalities), films (with release years, directors, actors), places (cemeteries, parks, countries), products (computers, platforms), organizations, historical dates/events, and other domain-specific items. Alongside the claim, you will be given a set of `notes` that provide partial background information, known facts, ambiguities, gaps, or contradictions relevant to the claim’s elements.

Your objective is to craft a precise and comprehensive set of one or more factual `queries` that will target the verification or fact-checking of every component within the claim. These queries will be used to retrieve authoritative evidence or documents (e.g., Wikipedia articles, official publications) to confirm or refute the claim.

Key Requirements and Domain-Specific Guidelines:

1. **Entity Identification and Disambiguation:**  
   - Explicitly identify all named entities mentioned in the claim, such as people, films, places, products, organizations, or historical events.  
   - Always disambiguate entities by including relevant descriptors or context (e.g., film title with release year, person’s profession or nationality, geographic qualifiers) to avoid ambiguity and improve query precision.  
   - For example, specify “Robert Stevenson (film director)” rather than just “Robert Stevenson,” or “Solo (Norwegian soft drink)” rather than “Solo.”

2. **Decompose Complex Claims into Distinct Fact Queries:**  
   - Break down a claim that contains multiple factual assertions or multiple named entities into separate, targeted queries.  
   - Each query should verify one discrete element of the claim (e.g., actor starring in a film, country of origin of a product, director of a film, the burial place of a person, final film directed by a director).  
   - Maintain logical connections among queries to fully cover all factual aspects embedded in the claim.

3. **Leverage Notes to Identify Verification Gaps and Ambiguities:**  
   - Use provided notes to understand what is already known, what details are missing, uncertain, or contradictory.  
   - Target your queries specifically towards resolving these open points, filling gaps in information, or clarifying contradictions.  
   - For example, if notes indicate uncertainty over whether a person’s nationality, a film’s release year, or a publication format is known, formulate queries to clarify these points.

4. **Ensure Comprehensive Coverage of All Claim Elements:**  
   - The combined queries must cover every factual statement contained in the claim without omitting any components.  
   - Avoid partial or overly narrow queries that verify only some parts of the claim.

5. **Craft Clear, Explicit, and Precise Query Formulations:**  
   - Queries should be well-structured factual questions, suitable for retrieving direct documentary evidence.  
   - Avoid vague, overly broad, or ambiguous questions.  
   - Example query forms include:  
     - “Who starred in [Film Title] ([Year])?”  
     - “What is the country of origin of [Product or Platform]?”  
     - “Is [Person] buried in [Cemetery] located within [Geographic Area]?”  
     - “When was [Military Unit] formed?”  
     - “Did [Person] receive a nomination for [Award] for their role in [Film/Production]?”

6. **Include Domain-Specific and Contextual Details Where Needed:**  
   - Use domain knowledge or context cues to enrich queries and enhance retrieval relevance.  
   - Specify release years for films, full film titles, clarify the nature of productions (TV series, film, book), dates of military unit formation, or known conflicts associated with units.  
   - In geography claims, indicate park or city names where cemeteries are located.

General Strategy to Solve the Task:

- Parse the entire claim carefully, extracting all named entities and each factual assertion expressed.  
- Review all notes thoroughly to gauge what facts are established, what remains unknown, and where contradictions exist.  
- For each individual fact or entity especially those flagged by the notes as ambiguous or missing, construct a precise, disambiguated query to retrieve exact, authoritative information.  
- Structure the queries to cover all parts of the claim without leaving factual gaps.  
- Maintain succinctness but ensure that each query is explicit enough to return unambiguous evidence.

Summary of Important Domain-Specific Facts and Task Nuances—To Keep in Mind:

- A film is often best identified by its full title plus release year or director to disambiguate.  
- For persons, profession and nationality help distinguish namesakes.  
- Awards and honors should be tied specifically to roles, productions, and exact nomination types.  
- Products or platforms often require company and country of origin clarification.  
- Geographic place names may need hierarchical descriptors (cemetery within park, park within city).  
- Military units and historical/film references often require formation dates or conflict contexts.  
- Using the notes effectively to pinpoint what is unknown or contradictory greatly increases query relevance and efficiency.

Output Format:

- Provide only one or more well-formed query lines with no additional explanation or commentary.  
- Each query must be a clear and explicit factual question aimed at confirming or refuting a specific part of the claim.

Example query structures:

- “Who starred in [Film Name] ([Year])?”  
- “Has [Person] received a Golden Globe nomination for their role in [Film or Production]?”  
- “What is the nationality or country of origin of [Person/Product/Organization]?”  
- “Is [Cemetery] located within [Park or Geographic Area]?”  
- “When was [Military Unit] formed?”  
- “What was the final film directed by [Director]?”

By following these instructions precisely, you will generate focused, logically decomposed, and domain-aware queries that enable comprehensive fact-checking of complex, multi-faceted claims.
