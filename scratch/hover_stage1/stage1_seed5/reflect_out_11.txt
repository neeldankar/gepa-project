You will be given two fields as input:  
- `claim`: A factual statement that may combine multiple pieces of information or assertions about entities, events, or relationships.  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets containing background, contextual, or factual information related to entities or topics relevant to the claim. These notes may include direct facts, clarifications, or partial information.  

Your task is to generate a `query` — a precise, focused, and information-seeking question (or set of questions) that targets the specific key points in the claim needed to verify or falsify it, using evidence mentioned or implied in the `notes`.  

Additional guidance and domain-specific considerations:  
1. **Identify and isolate key claim components:** Break down the claim into its fundamental assertions and relationships. For example, if the claim compares two entities, you will need queries that retrieve facts about both entities individually and any comparative data.  
2. **Use contextual clues in notes to guide query focus:** The notes often contain specific entities, dates, locations, or attributes linked to the claim. Extract these and formulate queries that explicitly request confirmation or factual information about them.  
3. **Disambiguation is critical:** If multiple entities or terms could be confused (e.g., multiple bands, multiple colleges in the same county), the query should clarify these ambiguities by naming the exact entities or by specifying relevant qualifiers (e.g., location, genre, time period).  
4. **Coverage and completeness:** To verify complex claims, your query should request all critical pieces of information needed. For example, if a claim involves two bands’ formation years and album releases, your query should cover the formation year of both bands and the album release details.  
5. **Support retrieval of all required evidence documents:** The queries should be framed in a way that retrieving relevant evidence about all key entities or facts is encouraged, avoiding overly narrow or partial queries that miss critical parts of the claim.  
6. **Reflect logical reasoning in the query:** The query should mirror the reasoning needed to assess the claim by asking about relationships or comparisons explicitly if they are part of the claim being verified.  
7. **Format:** The output should contain:  
   - `reasoning`: A concise explanation of why the query is formulated as such — linking the claim components to the notes contextually.  
   - `query`: One or more direct questions that precisely target the validation of the claim components using information implied or explicitly referenced in the notes.  

By following these guidelines, your queries will effectively guide retrieval of all necessary evidence to verify complex factual claims involving multiple entities, attributes, and relationships.
