Given input fields `claim` (a factual statement or assertion) and `notes` (a list of supporting or refuting factual sentences or evidence snippets), produce an output field `query` which is a concise, focused information retrieval or verification query to check the veracity of the claim against the notes.

The task requires you to extract and synthesize the key factual elements and relationships present in both the `claim` and supporting `notes`. The `query` should explicitly mention all critical entities, attributes, dates, titles, relationships, and contextual elements that would allow a search system or knowledge base to retrieve all relevant supporting or contradicting evidence.

Key domain- and task-specific points to include and consider:

- Identify all named entities (people, works of art, bands, films, TV shows, albums, songs, etc.) and their relationships present in the claim and notes.
- Include relevant attributes such as birth dates, nationalities, professions, song or episode titles, show programming blocks, and episode numbers when they appear as evidence or are needed to validate the claim.
- If the claim involves multiple facts, decompose them into logically connected parts and include query components for each (e.g., authorship of a song, nationality comparisons, genres of films, actors’ birth months and professions, airing days and programming blocks of TV shows, correspondence between album/song and episode/show names).
- Ensure that the final `query` is precise and explicitly formulated as a set of fact-checking questions or information requests, often structured as multiple short questions connected for clarity.
- The `query` should be structured to help retrieval of all necessary documents that bear on the claim's validity (e.g., biographical data, titles, dates, episode information, programming schedules, and cross-referencing between media).
- Avoid vague or overly broad queries; be specific with the facts that must be verified or disproved.
- Reflect all key factual components from the `claim` and the evidence `notes` in the `query` so that a retrieval model can target missing or confirming documents.
- When evidence from notes refutes or contextualizes parts of the claim (e.g., genre misclassification, incorrect birth dates, unlinked persons or entities), incorporate that as a negative or verification query part.
- In summary, the generated `query` is a fact-check-oriented, multi-part retrieval prompt crafted from both the claim’s assertions and the detailed factual notes, designed to retrieve all and only relevant supporting or contradicting information to conclusively verify the claim.

Example structure for queries:
- "Did [Person A] write [Song/Book/Work] titled '[Title]'?"
- "Is [Person A] from [Country], and is [Person B] from another country?"
- "Which actors starred in [Film], what are their birthdates, and were any of them professors?"
- "Did [Album] by [Artist] include songs featured in [TV Show] episodes named '[Episode Title]'?"
- "Did [Show] air on [Programming Block] on [Day]?"

Use conjunctions ("and", "or") to combine multiple related fact-check questions logically.

This approach enables comprehensive retrieval and verification of the claim by covering all nuanced factual angles presented in both claim and notes.
