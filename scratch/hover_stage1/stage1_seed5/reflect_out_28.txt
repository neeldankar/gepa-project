Task Description:

You will be provided with a factual `claim` potentially composed of multiple interconnected assertions involving diverse named entities (such as people, films, organizations, places, works of art, historical dates, or domain-specific items) and a set of accompanying `notes`. The notes summarize existing knowledge, partial information, ambiguities, gaps, or contradictions related to the claim's components.

Your core objective is to generate a precise, well-structured set of one or more `queries` that comprehensively verify or fact-check every factual element of the claim. These queries are intended to retrieve targeted, authoritative evidence (such as Wikipedia articles or other dependable sources) that can confirm or refute each assertion within the claim.

Detailed Task Instructions and Domain-Specific Heuristics:

1. **Entity Extraction and Disambiguation:**
   - Identify all named entities explicitly mentioned in the claim.
   - Disambiguate each entity carefully by adding concise yet relevant descriptors to avoid confusion (e.g., "Bombay Talkie (1970 film by Merchant Ivory Productions)," "Frankfurt in Hesse, Germany," "Werner Gunter Jaffé Fellner, chemist," or "Manchester United Football Club").
   - This is crucial for the retrieval of accurate and relevant documents.

2. **Decompose Complex Claims Into Discrete Factual Components:**
   - Break down claims containing multiple assertions into multiple distinct queries. For example, if the claim refers to a film, its cast, director, and historical context, generate separate queries verifying each fact.
   - Ensure all components are covered exhaustively without omission.

3. **Use Notes to Target Missing, Uncertain, or Contradictory Elements:**
   - Analyze the notes to identify:
     - Established facts that may still warrant verification.
     - Missing or unknown information needing explicit querying.
     - Contradictory or ambiguous data that require clarification.
   - Formulate queries directed specifically at these points, e.g., verifying a person’s birthplace when unknown, a film’s exact release year, familial relationships between entities, or the roles of specific individuals in a historical sports team or film.

4. **Ensure Full Coverage of All Claim Elements:**
   - Confirm every factual statement embedded in the claim is addressed by a query.
   - Avoid partial or incomplete verification that might overlook key aspects.

5. **Construct Precise, Clear, and Fact-Based Queries Suitable for Retrieval:**
   - Queries should be explicitly factual and structured as clear questions or entity-relationship prompts.
   - Avoid vague, ambiguous, or overly broad queries.
   - Include relevant context for clarity (e.g., specify film release years, exact organizational titles, geographic or historical context).

6. **Incorporate Domain Knowledge in Query Formulation:**
   - When the claim involves domain-specific knowledge, incorporate that context in the query to enhance precision.
     - For films: mention director, release year, production company.
     - For people: mention profession, nationality, or generational qualifiers.
     - For geography or burial claims: specify exact locations or relationships (e.g., cemetery within a park).
     - For sports or historical claims: specify seasons, player positions, club nicknames, or known lineups.
   
7. **Examples of Effective Query Construction:**

   - Film Verification:
     - "Who starred in [Film Name] ([Year])?"
     - "Who directed [Film Name] ([Year])?"
     - "Did [Person] act in [Film Name] ([Year])?"
   - Biographical / Family Link Verification:
     - "What is the birthplace of [Person, with descriptor]?"
     - "Is [Person A] the father of [Person B]?"
     - "Did the father of [Person] act in [Film Name] ([Year])?"
   - Sports / Historical Verification:
     - "Who was the goalkeeper for [Football Club] in the [Year] season?"
     - "Did any Leicester City F.C. player from the 1928–29 season later play for Manchester United F.C.?"
     - "Is [Football Club] known as '[Nickname]'?"
   - Geographic / Burial Verification:
     - "Where is [Person] buried?"
     - "Is [Cemetery Name] located within [Park or Geographic Area]?"
   
Generalizable Workflow:

- Read and parse the claim carefully, extracting all named entities and factual assertions.
- Review the notes to identify confirmed information, gaps, ambiguities, or contradictory data.
- For each identified entity and factual component, generate a pointed, disambiguated retrieval query designed to acquire definitive verification from authoritative sources.
- Ensure query coverage spans all claim components, especially focusing on areas of uncertainty or contradiction identified in the notes.
- Keep queries succinct, explicit, and contextually informed to maximize retrieval relevance and precision.
- Do not provide any explanation or reasoning in the output—only output one or more `query` lines.

Output Format:

- Return one or more lines, each beginning with the keyword `query:`, followed by the concise factual question or entity-specific prompt.
- No additional commentary, narrative, or reasoning should be included.

By following these comprehensive instructions and heuristics, you will produce precise, targeted queries capable of retrieving authoritative evidence that fully verifies or refutes complex multi-fact claims involving intricate relationships among named entities.
