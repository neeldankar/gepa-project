Given input fields `claim` and `notes`, generate a concise and precise `query` that can be used to verify or fact-check the claim based on the provided notes. The goal is to formulate the query in a way that effectively targets key factual elements or entities from the claim that require evidence or disambiguation, while leveraging the information contained in the notes.

Detailed guidance for producing the `query`:

1. **Input Understanding**:
   - The `claim` is a factual statement (often composite or multi-part) that requires verification.
   - The `notes` field is a list of evidentiary sentences or facts extracted from various documents relevant to the claim. These notes provide partial or full evidence supporting, partially supporting, or refuting parts of the claim.

2. **Task Purpose**:
   - The output `query` should be a targeted, fact-checking question or set of questions designed to guide information retrieval or verification.
   - The query should be specific enough to cover the critical factual components of the claim that appear uncertain, contradicted, or unsupported in the notes.
   - If the claim has multiple factual assertions, the query should explicitly address each one in a clear and interrogative manner.
   
3. **Intended Use of the Query**:
   - The query is meant to help retrieve gold standard documents or evidence necessary to fully verify or refute the claim.
   - It should maximize coverage of essential keywords, entities, dates, or relations present in the claim and related notes.
   - Favor formulations that directly question the existence, timing, affiliation, or participation of named entities and events mentioned in the claim.

4. **Domain-Specific Details and Examples**:
   - When the claim mentions named individuals, events, dates, or titles (e.g., judges on a show, release dates of movies, political leadership roles), isolate these and phrase queries that seek direct confirmation or contradiction.
   - If notes mention conflicting or missing evidence for parts of the claim (e.g., a person not mentioned as a judge, absence of evidence about an event), include queries asking if such persons or events occurred or participated as stated.
   - When multiple factual components are present (e.g., “Did X hold position Y, and did Z also join?”), include all these components explicitly in the query.
   - Use natural language question forms (e.g., “Did [entity]…?”, “Was [event]…?”, “List the…”), ensuring clarity and completeness.
   - Avoid overly broad queries; focus on factual verification based on the claim and notes rather than open-ended exploratory questions.

5. **Reasoning Strategy (Implicit to Query Construction)**:
   - Identify discrepancies or lack of supporting evidence in the notes related to claim components.
   - Target these with direct questions to resolve ambiguity or confirm details.
   - Cover temporal and relational facts if the claim involves dates or party affiliations.
   - Include qualifiers such as "the same party affiliated with X" if stated in the claim.
   - When required, break down a complex claim into multiple subqueries that address each fact explicitly.

6. **Example Templates Derived from Provided Examples**:
   - “Did [Person A] serve as [Role/Title] on [Event/Show]?”
   - “Did the producer and singer of [Entity] join the judging panel of [Show]?”
   - “What is the release year of [Movie]?”
   - “List the films featuring [Actor] released before [Year].”
   - “Did [Person], born [Date], become leader of the [Party] on [Date], the same party affiliated with [Organization]?”

By following these detailed guidelines, the assistant will produce high-quality, domain-informed queries that effectively pinpoint the factual elements that require verification, improving retrieval of supporting documents and overall fact-checking accuracy.
