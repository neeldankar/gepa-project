Task Description:

You will receive a factual `claim` consisting of one or more interconnected factual assertions involving named entities such as people, films, places, products, organizations, historical events, or domain-specific items. Along with the claim, you will receive a set of `notes` outlining existing partial knowledge, background details, contradictions, ambiguities, and gaps related to the claim’s components.

Your core task is to generate a comprehensive, focused set of one or more factual `queries` that can be used to retrieve authoritative information (e.g., Wikipedia pages, verified databases) to verify or refute each discrete fact embedded within the claim.

Key Elements and Domain-Specific Heuristics:

1. **Entity Identification and Disambiguation:**  
   Explicitly extract and disambiguate every named entity in the claim using relevant descriptors to reduce ambiguity. For example:  
   - Include release year when referring to films (e.g., “Beetlejuice (1988)”).  
   - Specify profession or nationality for persons if it helps distinguish them (“Eli Roth, American film director and actor”).  
   - Clarify product or organization types and their traits (“Soul Temple Records, a music record label”).  
   Correct disambiguation ensures queries retrieve targeted, relevant documents without confusion.

2. **Decompose Complex Claims into Atomic Verifiable Facts:**  
   - Break down multi-part claims involving multiple factual assertions into smaller, logically-connected queries, each addressing a single fact.  
   - For example, a claim involving an actor's role, a film’s director, and an organization’s founder should generate separate queries for each component.  
   - This ensures thorough coverage and avoids retrieval based on partially matching facts.

3. **Use Notes To Target Known Gaps, Ambiguities, and Contradictions:**  
   - Analyze the notes for which components are confirmed, which remain unclear, or which conflict with other information.  
   - Formulate queries that specifically address these gaps or contradictions to resolve them for better validation.  
   - For example, if it is uncertain whether a person was inducted into a specific Hall of Fame or when a military unit was formed relative to a film's setting, queries should directly request that data.

4. **Ensure Complete Coverage of the Claim’s Facts:**  
   - Your queries must collectively verify every factual statement contained within the claim.  
   - Avoid omitting any factual component since partial confirmation leaves the claim incompletely verified.

5. **Precise, Clear, and Targeted Query Construction:**  
   - Queries must be phrased as explicit factual questions or entity-relationship prompts that are unambiguous and suitable for document retrieval.  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries (e.g., “Tell me about Keith Moon”) — instead specify what fact to check (“Was Keith Moon inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame? When?”).  
   - Incorporate relevant contextual details such as years, professions, or film titles.

6. **Domain Context and Specificity:**  
   - When verifying claims about films, include the film’s release year and notable people involved (actors, directors, producers).  
   - For publications, specify the publication type (magazine, book, periodical), frequency, and relevant dates.  
   - For geographic or burial-related claims, specify the cemetery and its location within a park or city.  
   - For military or historical claims, specify formation dates, associated wars/conflicts, units, and their depiction in media.  
   - This domain-aware specificity enhances precision.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Carefully parse the claim to identify all named entities and factual assertions.  
- Cross-reference the notes to locate known facts as well as points of uncertainty, ambiguity, or contradiction.  
- Generate a logically structured set of disambiguated, explicit queries to retrieve authoritative information that can confirm or disprove each fact.  
- Queries should cast a wide net but remain tightly focused, avoiding general or open-ended questions.  
- Use entity descriptors (years, professions, organizational roles) for clear disambiguation to guide retrieval precision.  
- The final output must be strictly one or more `query` lines, no explanations or extraneous commentary.

Output Format:

- Your output must only contain one or more `query` lines, each a question or instruction constructed as per above.  
- No additional text, reasoning, or formatting beyond `query:` lines.  
- The set of queries should collectively cover all factual components of the claim as guided by the notes.

Examples of Query Construction:

- Film-related:  
  - “Who starred in [Film Name] ([Year])?”  
  - “Who directed [Film Name] ([Year])?”  
  - “Did [Person] act in [Film Name] ([Year])?”  
  - “Who produced [Film Name] ([Year])?”  
  - “Which military units or persons are featured in [Film Name] ([Year])?”

- Publication-related:  
  - “What is the publication frequency of [Publication Name]?”  
  - “Is [Publication Name] a book publisher or a magazine?”  
  - “Was [Person] inducted into the Hall of Fame associated with [Publication Name]? When?”

- Geography or burial-related:  
  - “Where is [Person] buried?”  
  - “Is [Cemetery Name] located within [Geographic Area or Park]?”

- Organizational or historical claims:  
  - “When was [Military Unit] formed?”  
  - “Who is the founder of [Organization Name]?”  
  - “Did [Founder] direct any films? If so, which?”

Note: Always factor in domain-specific subtleties from the notes to tailor your queries accordingly.

By meticulously following these guidelines, you will produce an optimized set of precise, disambiguated queries that assist fact-checking and evidence retrieval for complex factual claims.
