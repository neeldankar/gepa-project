Task Overview:

You are given a factual claim involving one or more interconnected assertions about specific entities (such as people, films, compositions, places, or other domain-specific items) alongside a set of notes that summarize existing information relevant to the claim, highlighting confirmed facts, gaps, ambiguities, contradictions, or missing data points.

Your goal is to generate a set of concise, clear, and well-structured queries designed to retrieve authoritative, relevant documents or evidence from external sources (e.g., Wikipedia articles) to verify or fact-check every component of the provided claim.

Input Format:

- `claim`: A factual statement that may cover multiple related facts or relationships involving named entities (e.g., a composer and a composition, an actor and films, a burial place, or geographic entities).
- `notes`: A list of factual observations, contextual snippets, or partial information reflecting what is known, what is missing, ambiguous, or contradictory about the claim’s components.

Output Format:

- `query`: One or more precise, fact-targeting search questions or phrases that explicitly address each essential piece of information needed to verify the claim fully.

Detailed Instructions & Domain-Specific Heuristics:

1. **Entity Disambiguation:**
   - Explicitly name entities with sufficient descriptors to avoid confusion, using domain-specific qualifiers if necessary (e.g., "Johann Christoph Pepusch (German-born composer)" or "Solo (Norwegian soft drink)").
   - If multiple entities share similar names or titles, queries must clearly specify the correct one.

2. **Decompose Complex Claims:**
   - Break down multi-faceted claims into atomic verification points.
   - For each assertion or linked fact, generate a dedicated query targeting that particular fact or relationship.
     Example: For a claim about an actor starring in two films and directing another, generate separate queries for each starring role and direction credit.

3. **Identify Missing, Ambiguous, or Contradictory Elements:**
   - Use the notes to identify gaps in information, contradictions, or unclear points requiring confirmation.
   - Formulate queries specifically designed to resolve these ambiguities.
     For instance, if the burial place of a person is unclear or a cemetery’s association with a park is uncertain, ask directly for these details.

4. **Complete Coverage:**
   - Queries collectively must address every factual assertion embedded in the claim.
   - Avoid partial queries that leave any part of the claim unverified.

5. **Precise, Fact-Focused Query Language:**
   - Queries should be framed clearly and specifically to facilitate document retrieval suitable for fact-checking.
   - Use direct factual questions or entity-relationship questions rather than vague or broad prompts.
     For example:  
     - "Who composed Rhapsody, Op. 1?"  
     - "What is the nationality and gender of Béla Bartók?"  
     - "Is Johann Christoph Pepusch associated with Rhapsody, Op. 1?"  
     - "What is the cast of the film '400 Boys'?"  
     - "Has any actor from '400 Boys' portrayed Marilyn Monroe?"  
     - "Where is [Person Name] buried?"  
     - "Is [Cemetery Name] located within the [Park Name]?"

6. **Integrate Domain Contextual Knowledge When Needed:**
   - Include contextual cues or known domain relationships to focus queries and disambiguate information.
   - Example: When verifying a film claim involving a novel adaptation, query the film adaptation of that specific novel and cross-reference the cast or characters.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Carefully analyze the claim to identify all named entities and factual assertions.
- Cross-examine these against the notes to identify information completeness, contradictions, or ambiguities.
- For each fact or relationship:
  - Generate a precise, targeted query that can independently verify this piece.
- Ensure queries are distinct, cover every major claim element, and disambiguate entities explicitly.
- Construct queries in a form optimized for retrieving relevant verification documents.
- Present only the generated queries without additional commentary or explanation.

By following these detailed instructions and including all nuanced, domain-specific points and the generalizable verification strategy, the queries you generate will maximize coverage and retrieval accuracy for thorough fact-checking.
