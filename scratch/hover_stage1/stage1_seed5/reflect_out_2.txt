- `notes` is a list of extracted facts, statements, or observations derived from various sources that may support or relate to the claim.
- `context` is a list of background documents or passages providing detailed information about entities, events, works, people, or other domain-specific knowledge relevant to the claim and notes. These context documents often include biographical data, historical timelines, work descriptions, or industry-specific details.

Your task is to process these inputs and produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. **Reasoning and Evidence Selection**  
   - Carefully analyze the claim and determine what factual components or relationships it asserts.
   - Use the `notes` to identify supporting or relevant information that directly confirms, explains, or clarifies the claim.
   - Leverage the `context` to verify facts, resolve ambiguities (such as identity or chronology), and retrieve authoritative details about named entities (e.g., persons, films, operas, creators) or events referenced in the claim.
   - Pay special attention to domain-specific information such as:
     - Biographical data (dates of birth/death, professions, collaborations)
     - Creative works (films, operas, novels) and their creators, production details, historical context
     - Historical timelines, overlaps, or relationships (e.g., contemporaneity of persons, authorship attribution)
     - Disambiguation of works with similar titles or themes but by different authors/composers/time periods
   - Your reasoning should establish clear factual support or clarification for the claim, often by confirming identities, timelines, or authorship.

2. **Creation of `new_notes`**  
   - Synthesize concise and relevant supporting statements extracted or derived from the `notes` and verified/expanded using the `context`.
   - The `new_notes` should directly address the claim's core assertions and provide precise factual backing.
   - Avoid redundant or overly broad statements; focus on the specific elements that validate or elucidate the claim.
   - Where possible, consolidate multiple facts that together form a coherent argument supporting the claim.

3. **Selection of `titles`**  
   - Provide a list of the most relevant document or topic titles from the `context` that were critical for supporting or verifying the claim.
   - Titles should correspond to entities, works, or subjects central to understanding or validating the claim (e.g., key people involved, specific films, operas, or other creative works).
   - The selected titles effectively serve as pointers to authoritative knowledge sources that underpin the claim's validation.
   - Ensure all key referenced entities mentioned in the claim or important to your reasoning are covered by the titles, to maximize completeness.

4. **Generalizable Strategy**  
   - Identify key entities (persons, works, events) in the claim.
   - Match and cross-reference these with notes and context to establish identities and timelines.
   - Resolve ambiguities, homonyms, or similar titles by carefully examining dates, creators, or descriptions.
   - Use domain-specific knowledge such as the standard practice that operas or films are credited to known composers, directors, or screenwriters, and that contemporaneity is established by lifespan overlap.
   - Apply critical evaluation to consolidate fragmented notes into coherent, verifiable facts.

By following these guidelines, your outputs will accurately reflect the factual support for the claim, grounded in domain-specific research provided by the notes and context, while being precise, relevant, and thorough.
