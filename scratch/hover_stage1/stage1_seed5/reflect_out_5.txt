Task Description:

You will be provided with a `claim` (a factual statement that may involve multiple interconnected facts or entities) and a set of `notes` that include partial background information, relevant contextual details, or observations regarding the claim. Your goal is to generate a concise, clear, and focused `query` (or set of queries) that can be used to verify or fact-check the claim by retrieving relevant documents or evidence from external sources (e.g., Wikipedia articles).

Input Format:

- `claim`: A factual statement that may cover multiple aspects or assertions, often involving named entities such as people, films, places, products, or other specific domain-related items.
- `notes`: A list of context snippets or observations summarizing the currently known information, including what is missing, ambiguous, or contradictory about the claim’s components. These notes reflect gaps in the information or conflicting details discovered during initial investigation.

Output:

- `query`: One or more clear, well-structured questions or search prompts that directly target the critical pieces of factual information needed to verify each part of the claim. Each query should be specific enough to retrieve relevant documents corresponding to key entities, relationships, or facts mentioned in the claim.

Key details and domain-specific heuristics to include:

1. **Disambiguate Entities Carefully:** When the claim references specific named entities (persons, films, songs, products, places), the query should explicitly name these entities, including relevant descriptors or disambiguation to avoid confusion (e.g., “Solo (Norwegian soft drink)” rather than just “Solo”).

2. **Decompose Complex Claims:** If the claim involves multiple interrelated facts (e.g., an actor starring in two films and a director credit), break down the verification into stepwise, connected questions addressing each part individually, so that sources can be retrieved for all relevant components.

3. **Identify and Target Missing or Ambiguous Links:** Use the `notes` to spot what information is lacking or uncertain. Focus queries on resolving those uncertainties (e.g., if burial location or whether a cemetery is part of a park is unclear, query specifically for these facts).

4. **Cover All Verification Points:** Make sure queries collectively cover every factual assertion embedded in the claim to avoid partial retrieval of relevant resources.

5. **Use Precise and Specific Language:** Frame queries in a form suited for document retrieval, often as clear factual questions or entity-relationship questions. Avoid vague or overly broad queries.

6. **Include Domain Context When Needed:** When domain knowledge influences interpretation (e.g., a director’s known roles, or geographic relationships between cemeteries and parks), reference such context in formulating queries to guide retrieval towards the correct entities and relevant data.

Generalizable Strategy:

- Read the claim carefully, identify all named entities and relationships.
- Cross-reference those with the `notes` to find gaps or contradictions.
- For each gap or verification point, generate a direct factual question or phrase that would retrieve documents clarifying or confirming that point.
- Combine these into a coherent set of queries ensuring coverage of the entire claim.
- Format the output strictly as `query` without additional explanations.

Example guidance:

For a claim about a film actor and director, queries might include:  
- "Who starred alongside [Known Actor] in [Film Name]?"  
- "Did that actor appear in [Second Film Name]?"  
- "Who directed [Second Film Name]?"

For a claim about a burial place and its geographic association:  
- "Where is [Person Name] buried?"  
- "Is [Cemetery Name] located within the [Park or Recreation Area Name]?"

By following these detailed instructions, your generated queries will effectively target the key facts needed for thorough claim verification and ensure retrieval of all necessary supporting documents.
