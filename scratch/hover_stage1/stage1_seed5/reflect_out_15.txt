Task Description: 

You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim` is a statement or set of statements asserting specific factual information about people, events, or works, often involving associations between individuals and their professions, achievements, or relationships.
- `notes` is a list of sentence-level pieces of evidence extracted or derived from various sources relevant to the claim. These notes often include detailed facts clarifying or expanding on elements in the claim, such as biographical data, professional roles, tournament outcomes, publication information, or film credits.
- `context` is a list of document-level snippets or summaries from relevant Wikipedia or encyclopedia-style articles, containing background, detailed factual information, and corroborative data that can validate or invalidate the claim.

Your goal is to produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Definitions and guidelines:

1. **new_notes**: This should be a concise, focused subset of the `notes` input (or synthesized versions thereof), filtered and selected because they directly support the claim's key factual components. These notes must help explain, confirm, or clarify the claim and should exclude extraneous or contradictory information. Prioritize notes with factual, domain-specific details that relate to:
    - Names and identifications of individuals involved
    - Their professional roles or titles (e.g., tennis player, film director, author)
    - Specific accomplishments or relevant activities (e.g., defending a tennis title, co-writing a TV series)
    - Temporal and geographic details if directly relevant (e.g., years of participation, locations)
    
2. **titles**: This should be a list of document titles or proper subject headings from the `context` that are essential to the verification or understanding of the claim. These are often Wikipedia article titles or named entities corresponding to:
    - Persons mentioned (e.g., "Angelique Kerber", "Jonathan Lynn")
    - Works or events referenced (e.g., "2005 China Open – Men's Doubles", "How Strange to Be Named Federico")
    - Concepts or entities that clarify relationships mentioned in the claim (e.g., "Norman Lindsay", "The Hundred Secret Senses", "Yes Minister").
    
3. **Reasoning Approach** (implicit in the task):
   - Carefully correlate information in `notes` and `context` to assess the truthfulness or factual coherence of the claim.
   - Use domain-specific factual knowledge to understand contextual clues, such as:
      - Tennis tournaments structure: Defending champions refer to previous winners; doubles events distinguish men's and women's tournaments; players' nationalities may be relevant.
      - Film and TV production credits: Directors, writers, and co-writers may hold overlapping or distinct roles.
      - Authorship and interests: Recognize authors' works and any explicit or implicit interests in activities mentioned (e.g., boxing).
   - Identify whether the claim incorrectly conflates different persons or misattributes roles (as in example 3).
   - Exclude or flag information when it is unsupported by the provided context or notes (especially for negative claims).
   - Recognize when particular biographical facts (e.g., place of origin like "Texas native") are not supported by the evidence.
   
4. **Consistency and Redundancy**:
   - Avoid highly redundant notes; if multiple notes state the same fact, include the most concise or informative.
   - The titles should comprehensively cover the key subjects/events without extraneous unrelated titles.
   
5. **Domain-Specific Knowledge Embedded in the Task**:
   - Tennis: Defending champions in doubles tennis, tournament history, player nationalities, and tournament outcomes are critical.
   - Literature and authorship: Recognizing specific authorship (e.g., Amy Tan for The Hundred Secret Senses) and linking that to personal interests or biographical data.
   - Film and TV: Understanding roles such as director versus writer or co-writer, relationships between productions and their creators, and distinguishing factual credits from claims.
   
Summary of the output format:
- `new_notes`: List of carefully curated notes that support or illuminate the claim.
- `titles`: List of relevant document or article titles from the provided context, corresponding to key entities and works related to the claim.

---
Use this detailed procedure to filter, validate, and summarize the relevant information from `notes` and `context` to form the outputs `new_notes` and `titles` that best support or explain the claim for fact verification or claim understanding purposes.
