Task Description:
You will be given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce a single output field `query`.

- The `claim` is a complex factual statement or a set of related assertions that involve multiple entities, attributes, relationships, and timelines.
- The `notes` are contextually relevant fragments of factual information, background details, or evidence that support or refute parts of the claim.

Your core objective is to formulate a **precise, comprehensive, and executable information retrieval or database query (`query`)** that would, if run, return **all supporting documents or structured data necessary to verify every aspect of the claim**. This query should effectively capture all entities, relationships, attributes, and temporal conditions expressed or implied in the claim.

Detailed Instructions and Considerations:

1. **Identify Key Factual Components in the Claim:**  
   - Extract all key **entities**: people (with roles), organizations, teams, films, songs, breeds, events, places, dates, and titles.  
   - Determine all **relationships or assertions**: e.g., "Person A played tennis," "Person B was the defending champion," "Entity X is a recognized dog breed," "Band Y recorded album Z," "Person C was born before Person D," "Individual E starred with Individual F in Film G," or "Team H was founded in Year J."  
   - Note all **attributes or predicates** to verify: e.g., birthplace, profession, breed status, birth date, co-appearances, founding dates, event participation, timelines.

2. **Leverage Notes as Contextual Guidance:**  
   - Use notes to understand which entities and facts are central and which documents or data types likely exist to verify them.  
   - Notes may mention entities not explicitly named in the claim but which are necessary to include in the query (e.g., co-stars, defending champions, bands associated with songs).  
   - The notes may indicate multiple concepts around a single entity (e.g., confirming that "Angelique Kerber" is a tennis player and her nationality).

3. **Formulate a Natural Language Query That is Both Comprehensive and Specific:**  
   - Include requests for all key entities mentioned or implicated.  
   - Ask for verification of all roles, professions, chronological relationships, and event participations inferred from the claim and notes.  
   - If the claim involves comparisons or chronological ordering (e.g., one person born before another), query the birthdates or timeline data for both entities.  
   - For claims involving domain-specific structures (e.g., sports teams, filmographies, music credits, dog breeds), query detailed attributes such as rosters for specified years, cast lists and director credits, breed origin and classification, etc.  
   - Ensure the query explicitly requests all relevant documents or data points that would verify the **complete** claim, including all central components identified from claim plus any additional entities from notes.

4. **Generalizable Querying Strategy:**  
   - For co-participation or co-occurrence claims, query all related personal profiles, event participation, or work involvement to check overlaps.  
   - For claims about professions or roles, query explicit profession or role information for each individual.  
   - For claims about musical bands, query the band’s recording history, personnel (especially lead roles like lead guitarist), and associated recordings.  
   - For claims about competitions or sports, query event results, defending champions, player rosters, and founding or management dates.  
   - For claims about pedigree, classification, or identity status (e.g., dog breed vs individual dog), query breed registration, classification, and relationship between individual names and breed names.  
   - Prioritize an exhaustive retrieval of all gold-standard documents or data points referenced or implied in notes/feedback.

5. **Output Format and Style:**  
   - Output **only** the final `query` as natural language, structured as a single or multi-part well-formed request.  
   - Avoid including reasoning or explanation in the output, but the query must be detailed enough to ensure the retrieval covers every factual aspect in the claim and notes.  
   - Use explicit phrasing such as "Retrieve information on...", "Identify...", "Provide birth dates for...", "Confirm the profession of...", or "List all cast members and director of...".

Examples of Niche Domain Knowledge (from previous cases):  
- Distinction between an individual dog’s name and a dog breed.  
- Separation of men’s and women’s events in tennis competitions (e.g., Angelique Kerber cannot compete in men’s doubles).  
- Role-specific entities within bands, such as "lead guitarist," related to recordings or albums.  
- Temporal ordering involves retrieving birth dates to confirm who was born earlier.  
- The concept of defending champions implies querying prior winners of specific tournaments or championships.  
- Films have casts and directors that must be queried to verify co-appearances.  
- Sports teams and related persons require historical rosters, managerial records, and founding dates queries.  

The goal is a **single query capable of retrieving every piece of essential evidence** to support or refute the entire claim, constructed by expertly connecting claim components with relevant fact verification needs derived from notes.

Summary:
To produce the query:

- Fully parse the claim for all relevant entities, relations, attributes, and timelines.  
- Use notes as context to ensure no verifying entity or fact is missed.  
- Craft a detailed, natural language retrieval request covering all these components thoroughly.  
- Ensure the query explicitly requests all relevant information necessary to corroborate or invalidate each point in the claim comprehensively.

This ensures a complete, accurate, and verifiable approach to fact-checking complex claims involving multiple entities and relationships.
