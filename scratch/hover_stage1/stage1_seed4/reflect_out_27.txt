You will be given the following input fields:
- `claim`: a statement or assertion that makes factual or historical claims about people, events, creative works (such as operas, films, characters), or related topics.
- `notes`: a list of extracted factual statements or evidence snippets relevant to evaluating or clarifying the claim. These notes often consist of detailed information from multiple sources about people, works, dates, roles, relationships, venues, organizations, or historical facts.
- `context`: a list of background documents or excerpts providing additional detailed information about key entities (people, works, venues, teams, or events) mentioned in or related to the claim.

Your task is to produce:

1. `new_notes`: a concise, accurate, and directly relevant subset or summary of the `notes` and/or `context` that clarify or verify the claim. These new notes should focus sharply on the core factual points needed to:
   - confirm or refute the claim,
   - understand relationships or attributions,
   - highlight relevant dates, roles, or identities,
   - and distinguish closely related but different entities or events.

2. `titles`: a list of specific, informative document or topic titles that represent the key subjects or sources referenced in the `new_notes`. These titles should enable targeted further research and cover all critical components of the claim including people, groups, works, venues, events, or thematic areas cited in your output.

Important domain-specific and task-specific considerations to apply:

- Claims often involve verification of roles or attributions, such as which person voiced a character, composed a work, coached a team, or played a particular role. Pay close attention to correctly attributing these roles.
- Operatic works and artistic compositions may have different versions or composers. Distinguish clearly between similarly named works or persons, especially across time periods or cultural backgrounds.
- Sports-related claims may conflate athletes, coaches, or trainers, or confuse venues and tenant clubs. Carefully resolve such conflations by cross-referencing team affiliations, stadium locations, event hosts, and timeline facts.
- Voice acting and dubbing roles can involve multiple actors across languages or versions; attribute only those with explicit direct links to the claim.
- Nationality, ethnic identity, and historical timelines (e.g., formation dates of groups, release dates of albums or DVDs) often affect claim validity and should be verified precisely.
- Multiple similar or repeated notes may appear. Your `new_notes` should avoid redundancy while preserving key facts.
- The final output should enable judgment on the claim’s truthfulness or clarify the factual core to understand it fully.
- Titles should represent the key entities and themes covered, not just some aspects. For example, if a claim involves a musical album, artist, and related tour, then at least those distinct titles should be included.

Generalizable approach to the task:

- Identify and isolate the key entities and factual elements in the claim.
- Cross-reference notes and context to confirm or deny essential relationships, role attributions, dates, or venues.
- Extract and summarize the minimal but comprehensive evidence needed to understand the claim’s accuracy.
- Remove redundant, irrelevant, or overly broad information that does not clarify or verify the claim.
- Compile a list of precise document or topic titles representing all critical subjects referenced in your notes.
- The output must be both succinct and precise, aiding downstream users in quickly grasping the claim's factual grounding and enabling further research.

Output format must always include two fields: `new_notes` and `titles`. Ensure `new_notes` are factual, concise, relevant, and informative, and that `titles` cover all necessary key subjects for deeper lookup or verification.
