Task Description:  
You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your objective is to generate a single natural language output field `query` which is a precise, comprehensive, and executable information retrieval or database query. Running this query should retrieve all relevant evidence (documents, structured data, or records) needed to verify every factual aspect of the claim. The query must explicitly address all entities, relationships, attributes, and temporal or contextual conditions present or implied in the claim, as well as any additional relevant entities or concepts derived from the notes.

Key Guiding Principles:  

1. Parsing the Claim:  
- Identify all key factual components, including:  
  - Entities: people (with roles or professions), organizations, films, songs, albums, sports teams, breeds (e.g., dog breeds), events, places, dates, titles, and publications.  
  - Assertions/Relationships: e.g., a person’s profession or role, participation in events, co-appearances in films, birth order comparisons, founding dates of organizations, record credits in music, defending champion status in sports, adaptations of novels into films, and associations between individuals and works.  
  - Attributes or predicates to verify: e.g., birthplace, birth dates, professions, roles in works, co-stars, source material for adaptations, release dates, genre classifications, championship titles, magazine types (periodical or not), and award-winning status.

2. Leveraging Notes:  
- Use the notes to:  
  - Identify relevant supporting or refuting information linked to the claim’s components.  
  - Detect additional entities or supporting facts that must be included in the query to ensure exhaustive verification (e.g., co-stars, source novels, related organizations).  
  - Understand domain-specific nuances (e.g., the distinction between an individual dog’s name and a dog breed, or men’s vs women’s sports events, or what qualifies as a periodical).  
  - Recognize when multiple separate but related facts must be verified (e.g., a magazine’s periodical status and a different publication’s status).

3. Query Construction:  
- Generate a thorough natural language query that:  
  - Requests retrieval of all relevant entities, their attributes, and their relationships as per the claim and notes.  
  - Covers comprehensive details to verify each assertion, including, when applicable:  
    - Film details: full cast lists, director names, source materials (novels adapted), genre, and release dates.  
    - Person details: birth dates, professions, awards, participation in films/sports/events, and any linking roles (e.g., co-star in the same film).  
    - Musical entities: band members, specific roles (lead guitarist, etc.), album and song credits, recording timelines.  
    - Sports data: event participants, defending champions, founding dates, and timelines relative to events.  
    - Publishing context: magazine type, whether the publication is a periodical, publication schedule, and relation to other publications.  
    - Breed vs individual distinctions: breed registration and classification separate from individual animals’ names.  
  - Explicitly addresses temporal relationships (e.g., birth order, release order) by requesting dates and timeline data.  
  - Incorporates any contrasting or comparative claims, such as confirming that two entities are or are not both periodicals, or one event preceded another.  
  - Ensures no entity or relevant aspect mentioned or implied in the claim or notes is omitted.

4. Domain-Specific Knowledge to Apply:  
- Recognize that some domains require special attention:

  - Sports: separate events by gender, defending champion concepts, timelines of championships, and team founding dates.

  - Dog breed facts: distinction between breed names and individual dogs.  
  - Temporal ordering requires precise date retrieval.  
  - Award status must be verified explicitly when relevant.

5. Output Format:  
- Output only the fully formed natural language query (no reasoning or explanation).  
- The query should be sufficiently detailed and precise to enable retrieval of all verification materials necessary to confirm or refute every factual component of the claim in its entirety.  
- Use clear, explicit action phrases like:  
  - “Retrieve all cast members and director of [Film].”  
  - “Identify all films starring [Person] released before/after [Year].”  
  - “Confirm the profession and award status of [Person].”  
  - “Provide publication schedule and periodical classification of [Magazine].”  
  - “List the founding date and historical rosters of [Sports Team].”

- For claims involving multiple entities and their relationships, frame queries so each relationship can be verified independently and collectively.

By adhering to the above instructions, you will produce a single, detailed, fully inclusive query capable of retrieving every piece of requisite evidence to effectively fact-check complex multi-entity claims with nuanced domain-specific considerations.
