You will be provided with three types of input data:

- `claim`: A factual or historical statement asserting specific details about people, events, works (such as operas, films, characters), or related topics.
- `notes`: A collection of extracted factual statements or evidence snippets relevant to verifying, clarifying, or refuting the claim. These notes may include detailed information such as biographical data, roles, dates, or relationships and can sometimes contain overlapping or redundant facts from multiple sources.
- `context`: A set of background documents or excerpts providing comprehensive information on key entities mentioned in or related to the claim. This may include biographical information, descriptions of works, historical background, or clarification on roles and attributions.

Your task is twofold:

1. **Produce `new_notes`**: Generate a concise, accurate, and relevant subset of the `notes` and/or `context` that directly address and clarify the validity of the claim. Your summary should:
   - Highlight the most critical facts needed to understand whether the claim is true or false or grasp the core factual relationships.
   - Avoid redundant, irrelevant, or tangential information.
   - Emphasize domain-specific distinctions essential for evaluating the claim (e.g., roles versus attributions, composer periods, ethnic identities, sports roles, or voice acting distinctions).
   - Reflect nuanced understanding of potentially complex attributions, such as multiple voice actors in different languages or versions, distinctions between athletes, trainers, and coaches, or differentiations between works with similar names or different versions.

2. **Produce `titles`**: Provide a list of informative, specific document or topic titles that represent the key subjects or sources referenced in your `new_notes`. These titles should be:
   - Suitable as search topics or references for deeper investigation.
   - Comprehensive across all entities and thematic areas critical to the claim (people, works, roles, events, or thematic contexts).
   - Reflective of all essential components covered in the `new_notes`, not just a subset.

### Important domain-specific considerations:

- Many claims involve verifying the correct attribution of roles, voices, or achievements, especially when multiple individuals are involved with similar or overlapping associations.
- Be mindful of historical and cultural distinctions such as composer eras (e.g., Baroque vs. Romantic), nationality, or ethnic identity when relevant.
- Differentiate between related but distinct entities (e.g., distinct composers with similar names, separate versions of a work, or distinct sports positions).
- In voice acting and dubbing, focus only on those actors with documented, direct attributions in the particular language or version referenced by the claim.
- For operatic and musical works, it is critical to distinguish compositions accurately by composer and version.
- In sports claims, clearly separate the identities and achievements of athletes, coaches, and trainers, and cross-check team affiliations and timelines carefully.
- Claims frequently combine unrelated or conflated entities; detect and clarify such confusions.

### Generalizable strategy:

- Carefully parse the claim to identify key entities, roles, compositions, events, and relationships.
- Cross-reference names, dates, incidents, or titles from the claim with the provided notes and context to confirm or refute those specifics.
- When notes are numerous or repetitive, extract the minimal yet comprehensive set of facts sufficient to judge the claim.
- Omit extraneous or unsupported details that do not affect the truth value or key factual relationship under examination.
- Ensure your `titles` correspond precisely to the key sources underpinning the facts in `new_notes`, covering all critical aspects of the claim.
- Use domain knowledge implicitly to recognize important distinctions even if not explicitly stated in the input.

### Output format:

Your final output must include two fields:

- `new_notes`: An array of concise, accurate, and relevant factual points directly addressing the claim.
- `titles`: An array of document or topic titles representing the key subjects or sources used within `new_notes`.
