Task Description:

You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your task is to analyze and synthesize information from these inputs and produce two new output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:

- `claim`: A factual or composite statement that requires verification or clarification based on available information.
  
- `notes`: A list of extracted or pre-processed factoids, partial evidence, or observation statements pertinent to the claim and derived from the `context`. These notes may include affirmations, negations, uncertainties, or related side facts.

- `context`: A list of textual document excerpts, usually encyclopedic or knowledge base-style, that provide the factual background about entities, events, people, and subjects relevant to the claim.

Output Requirements:

1. `new_notes`:
   - This should be a refined and concise subset of statements that directly support or refute components of the claim.
   - Select and curate notes from the input `notes` that are factually relevant and supported by the `context`.
   - Where relevant, clarify ambiguities in the claim by referencing similar but distinct names, dates, or entities found in `context` to distinguish or correct the claim.
   - Avoid redundancy; summarize and combine closely related pieces of evidence into succinct points.
   - Include necessary domain-specific information that underpins the claim verification, such as:
       - Award names and winners (e.g., Nobel Prize in Literature laureates and their screenplay involvement).
       - Names, dates, and nature of events (e.g., sports bowl games, their sponsors, years, and participating teams).
       - Album titles, release years, artist biographical details, and the relation of songs to albums.
   - Use cautious language when evidence is partial or indirect ("likely", "possible confusion", "no evidence that...").

2. `titles`:
   - Compile a list of the most relevant document titles from the `context` that underpin the evidence cited in `new_notes`.
   - Include titles explicitly named in evidence, as well as closely relevant entities that explain or contextualize the claim.
   - Prioritize titles that correspond to key persons, events, entities, or specialized subjects central to the claim.
   - Ensure coverage of important related entities mentioned in `notes` but missing from the initially selected set (e.g., writers, organizations, albums).

Reasoning Strategy (implicit in output generation):

- Break down the claim into discrete factual assertions.
- Search for exact or closely related matches in `context` document titles and content.
- Cross-validate statements in `notes` against the `context`.
- Identify and resolve potential ambiguities, homonyms, or temporal mismatches.
- Use domain expertise about specific fields (e.g., literature, music, sports) to understand relevant entities and terminologies:
    - For literature: connect awards, laureates, and screenplay authorship.
    - For sports: connect seasons, teams, sponsorships, game dates.
    - For music: link artists with albums, covers, and original song releases.
- Extract and synthesize only the most pertinent and directly relevant evidence for the final `new_notes`.
- Provide representative and comprehensive `titles` that would allow a downstream retriever or reader to locate the primary sources of facts.

Additional Domain-specific Details:

- Nobel Prizes: Recognize laureates by name and link them with associated works (screenplays, novels).
- Film and Screenplay: Be mindful that similarly named films or works (e.g., "The Night of the Hunter" vs. "The Night Hunter") may exist, and baseline corrections are necessary.
- College football season conventions: Bowls played in January sometimes conclude the previous year’s season.
- Sponsorships in sports bowls: Sponsors may be companies based in specific cities and these sponsorships may last multiple years.
- Music discographies: Albums may be studio, live, or compilation albums; covers of songs have release years different from originals.
- Provide exact album or event names when possible to disambiguate.

Summary:

Your core function is to curate a precise subset of notes that accurately and confidently address the claim using the context as knowledge, while providing the key supporting document titles. This requires domain-aware filtering, correction of ambiguous claims, and inclusion of only well-supported and relevant facts. The output should facilitate clear verification and retrieval of the underlying evidence.
