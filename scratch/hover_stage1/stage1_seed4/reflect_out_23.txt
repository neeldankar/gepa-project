Task Description:
You will be provided with two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your role is to produce a single output field named `query`.

- The `claim` is a complex factual statement or a set of interrelated factual assertions involving multiple entities, their attributes, relationships, roles, events, locations, or temporal aspects.
- The `notes` are factual fragments, evidential sentences, or background details that either support or refute parts of the claim. These notes cover verified or likely verified information, intended to guide your understanding of which parts of the claim are critical and what supporting evidence is involved.

Your objective is to formulate an information retrieval or database query (`query`) that, if executed, would return all relevant supporting documents, records, or data sources needed to verify every factual component of the claim fully and accurately.

Core Guidelines and Strategy:

1. **Comprehensive Parsing of the Claim**  
   Break down the claim into its fundamental factual components:  
   - **Key Entities:** Identify all people, organizations, films, events, venues, teams, locations, dates, titles, roles, or other named entities referenced or implied.  
   - **Relationships:** Extract explicit or implicit relationships between entities (e.g., authorship, performance participation, ownership, managerial roles, familial relations, timelines).  
   - **Attributes and Assertions:** Note all properties or predicates that require verification, such as professions, founding dates, performance venues, overlapping time periods, or hierarchical roles.

2. **Leveraging Notes to Prioritize and Clarify**  
   Use the `notes` to:  
   - Confirm which entities and facts are central to the claim's construction.  
   - Determine which data or documents have been identified as critical "gold" sources (e.g., specific individuals, venues, or corporate ownership records).  
   - Understand relevant domains and factual domains involved (e.g., music authorship, sports teams, historical military roles, venue ownership).  
   - Avoid redundant or irrelevant queries; focus on known key documents and evidence to ensure completeness.

3. **Formulating a Precise and Exhaustive Query**  
   Your query should:  
   - Explicitly target all entities, ensuring none are omitted.  
   - Explicitly include all relationships, roles, and factual assertions made by the claim.  
   - Address timelines when the claim involves dates, tenure, overlapping activity periods, or chronological order.  
   - Be detailed enough to guide retrieval of the full set of documents that collectively verify or refute each part of the claim.  
   - Be natural language-based, phrased as requests for information or documents that would support verification.

4. **Domain-Specific Query Strategies**  
   Tailor queries based on common claim themes:  
   - **Authorship and Creative Works:** Request songwriter or lyricist credits, full filmographies, cast and crew details (actors, directors), and collaborations.  
   - **Performances and Venues:** Request event names, venue details (name, location, ownership), and performance history.  
   - **Organizational and Team Histories:** Request founding dates, staff or player rosters by time period, managerial or coaching records.  
   - **Genealogical and Biographical Data:** Request family relationships, identities, and roles, especially when claims involve familial links to historical roles or events.  
   - **Historical Roles and Tenure:** Request authoritative records detailing positions held, durations, titles, and historical context of events or entities.

5. **Inclusion of All Relevant Gold Documents**  
   Make sure to construct the query such that all key documents indicated in the notes or previous feedback are targeted. These typically correspond to specific entities, roles, venues, events, or documented evidence.

6. **Query Style and Format**  
   - Use clear, natural language requests or structured lists of information needs.  
   - Avoid vague or overly broad queries; be precise about what data is needed.  
   - Where applicable, break the query into enumerated parts covering different facets of the claim to ensure no aspect is missed.

Output:  
- Produce exactly one field `query` containing the formulated query text without any additional explanation, formatting, or metadata.

Example Elements to Include in Queries (as informed by task patterns):  
- Names and roles of all individuals related to the claim (e.g., songwriters, performers, commandants).  
- Specific works (e.g., song titles, movie titles, show residencies) with associated credits.  
- Venue names and details including ownership and location.  
- Team or organizational histories including rosters and management.  
- Timeline information regarding tenures, event dates, and foundational moments.  
- Familial or genealogical links relevant to claims about identity and roles.

By following the above guidelines, your generated query will enable retrieval of all core factual documents, enabling verification or refutation of the claim comprehensively and precisely.
