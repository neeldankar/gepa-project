You will be given the following input fields:

- `claim`: A statement or assertion making factual or historical claims about specific people, events, works (such as operas, films, characters), or related topics.
- `notes`: A list of extracted factual statements or evidence snippets relevant to verifying or clarifying the claim. These notes often contain detailed, sometimes repetitive information drawn from various sources about persons, artistic or political works, dates, roles, relationships, or habitats.
- `context`: A collection of documents or excerpts providing background information on key entities mentioned in or related to the claim. This includes biographical information, work descriptions, historical facts, clarifications about entities, their roles, or thematic details.

Your task is to:

1. Analyze the key components and entities named in the claim carefully.
2. Use the `notes` and `context` to identify, confirm, or deny the factual connections or assertions in the claim.
3. Summarize a concise, accurate, and relevant subset of the `notes` and/or `context` into `new_notes` that directly address the validity of the claim or clarify the core factual relationships.
   - These notes must highlight the most important evidence necessary to understand whether the claim is true or false or to understand the key factual relationships.
   - Remove irrelevant, redundant, or partially relevant information to keep the summary precise and focused.
4. Produce `titles`: a list of informative and specific document or topic titles representing the key subjects or sources referenced in your `new_notes`.
   - Each title should correspond to significant people, works, teams, or thematic aspects critical to the claim verification.
   - Titles should cover all essential entities or thematic contexts (e.g., "Michael York," "New Democratic Party (Canada)," "Alopecurus creticus species overview," "Dorothy Chandler Pavilion").
   - Including all important aspects ensures comprehensive coverage for deeper research or verification.

Important domain-specific and factual considerations:

- Claims frequently involve verification of roles and attributions—verifying who voiced a character, who composed a work, who coached a team, or who led a party.
- Historical distinctions may be important, such as differentiating composers by era (e.g. Baroque vs Romantic), identifying nationality or ethnicity, and understanding timelines of events.
- Distinguish clearly between individuals with similar or overlapping contributions (e.g., athlete versus coach versus trainer).
- Voice acting and dubbing credits can span multiple actors across languages and versions—only trust direct attributions.
- Works of art or opera can have multiple composers or versions; clarify these details accurately.
- Political claims may require matching birthdates, leadership dates, and affiliations within party structures.
- Botanical or ecological claims may require linking species to their native habitat and climate zones carefully.
- Identify and resolve conflation of multiple individuals or entities in claims (e.g., mixing English and American actors, or separate political figures).
- Use repeated facts in notes to reinforce key points but do not duplicate them unnecessarily in your `new_notes`.

Generalizable strategy for solving the task:

- Parse the claim to identify its core factual assertions and involved entities.
- Cross-reference these assertions with detailed `notes` and supplementary `context` to establish facts.
- Select and condense only the relevant evidence that directly impacts the truth of the claim.
- Avoid irrelevant or tangential information that doesn't clarify or verify the claim.
- Assemble an informative set of document or topic `titles` covering all critical elements surfaced in your evidence.
- Present your findings clearly and concisely, making it straightforward to understand the claim's accuracy or the core factual relationships involved.

Output format requirement:

- Your output must contain two fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.
- `new_notes` must be a concise, accurate, and relevant subset or summary of notes and context.
- `titles` must be a list of informative and specific document or topic titles that cover all essential entities and thematic contexts referenced in `new_notes`.

This approach ensures high-quality, verifiable, and domain-sensitive evaluation of complex claims involving distinct people, works, roles, or contextual relationships.
