You will be given two fields as input: `claim` (a factual statement or hypothesis involving named entities, events, dates, and relationships) and `notes` (a list of relevant background facts, context, or partial evidence related to the claim). Your task is to produce a SPARQL query or a precise, structured query that can be executed on a knowledge graph or linked data source to verify the claim, extract the necessary information, or retrieve relevant structured data.

Key points and domain-specific details to consider when producing the query:

1. **Entities and Their Properties**: Identify all named entities in the claim (people, institutions, films, awards, events, dates, etc.). Queries should retrieve relevant properties such as dates of establishment, release/premiere dates, actors and their roles, institutional affiliations, award recipients, military service records, and other factual attributes depending on context.

2. **Temporal Comparisons**: Many claims involve temporal relationships (e.g., whether one event occurred before another, or one institution was established before another). The query should retrieve dates tied to entities/events for temporal ordering or comparison.

3. **Multi-step Logical Structure**: Claims often involve multi-part conditions (e.g., an actor appearing in multiple films, awards won by specific films, military status of actors). Your query should reflect this complexity, linking multiple entities through their relationships (e.g., connecting actors to films, films to awards, actors to military service).

4. **Domain-specific Knowledge**:
   - **Film domain**: Use properties such as `dbo:starring`, `dbo:releaseDate`, `dbo:award`, and links between actors and films.

   - **Historical Institutions**: Use properties such as `dbo:established` to retrieve founding years.
   - **Military Service**: Identify military service attributes of persons where applicable.
   
5. **Query Completeness**: Include retrieval of all entities and facts necessary to verify the claim fully, even if the claim mentions only some elements explicitly. For example, when verifying a postdoctoral institution's founding date, also retrieve the other institution’s founding date for comparison.

6. **Disambiguation and Specificity**: Use exact entity URIs or labels augmented with disambiguating information (e.g., "Wonder Woman (2017 film)" rather than a generic name). Limit results with appropriate constraints `LIMIT 1` or as relevant.

7. **Use of Reasoning in Query Construction**: Before generating the query, mentally break down the claim into information units and relationships. Construct queries that reflect these relationships to retrieve supporting data that will enable claim verification.

- Analyze the claim to extract entities, dates, roles, and relationships.
- Leverage the notes to understand relevant contextual facts and establish which properties or entities to target.
- Generate a formal query retrieving all data points that can confirm or refute the claim, focusing on temporal relationships, roles in films, award recipients, and factual backgrounds such as military service or institution founding dates.
- Ensure queries retrieve all necessary linked entities and properties to enable comprehensive fact-checking of the claim.
