1. `claim`: A statement or assertion that makes factual or historical claims about specific people, events, works (such as operas, films, characters, musical compositions), or related topics. Claims often involve verifying details like authorship, roles, nationalities, timeframes, or attributions.

2. `notes`: A list of extracted factual statements or evidence snippets that may support, clarify, or refute the claim. These often include detailed, sometimes repeated facts from diverse sources, covering persons, works, dates, roles, relationships, or career timelines relevant to the claim.

3. `context`: A list of documents or excerpts providing background information about the key entities involved in the claim — this includes biographical data, descriptions of works and compositions, historical records, and clarifications that help determine the claim’s validity.

Your task is to produce:

- `new_notes`: A concise, accurate, and relevant summary or subset of the input notes and/or context that directly address, clarify, and verify the core factual points related to the claim’s truthfulness or correctness. This summary should:

  * Highlight the most critical factual points needed to assess the claim.

  * Distill the evidence to avoid redundancy and irrelevant information.

  * Include clarifications about potentially confusing or conflated entities (for example, different composers with similar names, multiple voice actors in various languages/versions, or distinctions between roles such as athlete vs. coach).

  * Identify and emphasize key historical distinctions (e.g., composer periods such as Baroque vs. Romantic, nationality or ethnic identity, timelines of activity).

  * Reflect domain-specific knowledge, such as:

    - For musical compositions: distinguishing between composers with similar titles or opus numbers; verifying attributions by catalog numbers or composition dates.

    - For voice acting and dubbing: acknowledging multiple actors might exist but focusing only on well-documented direct attributions per version/language.

    - For sports and racing claims: verifying driver participation, team roles, series names, and event years to ensure accuracy about who competed or won.

- `titles`: A list of informative, specific document or topic titles that correspond to and represent the key subjects or sources used or referenced in the new_notes. These should:

  * Cover all essential people, works, roles, teams, or thematic areas involved in examining the claim.

  * Be suitable as authoritative or focused reference topics for further lookup.

  * Include relevant domain-specific topics (e.g., a composer's catalog, detailed team or series histories, biographical articles, or work-specific pages).

Detailed domain-specific considerations:

- Claims often center on verifying proper attributions of works, roles, or achievements. Distinguish carefully between multiple individuals with similar names or titles.

- Be aware of potential conflations such as mixing achievements of different people or confusing roles (e.g., composer vs. arranger, player vs. coach).

- Voice acting is complex with multiple versions and languages; rely on documented direct attributions for the exact version referenced.

- Compositions may have multiple opus numbers, versions, or arrangements; catalog numbers, dates, and stylistic periods are critical for verification.

- Sport/racing claims require checking specific season years, event names, driver roles, and verify whether a claimed competitor actually participated or won.

- Nationality, ethnicity, and timeline details are essential to confirm identity and historical accuracy.

Generalizable approach:

- Carefully dissect the claim’s key components and entities.

- Cross-reference notes and context to identify precise factual connections.

- Extract and condense only those facts directly impacting the validity or clarification of the claim.

- Avoid including unrelated, redundant, or overly general information.

- Provide titles that comprehensively cover every major component involved in the claim for thorough follow-up.

Your output format must include both `new_notes` and `titles`. Ensure the `new_notes` are tightly linked to the claim’s verification and the `titles` cover all critical entities and themes.

This approach maximizes accuracy in verifying nuanced, domain-specific factual claims about persons, works, events, and related attributions.
