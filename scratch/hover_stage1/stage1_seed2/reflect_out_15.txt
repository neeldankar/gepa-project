Task: Given a factual claim involving specific entities (people, teams, works, events, dates, roles, titles) and a supporting set of factual notes, your goal is to craft a precise natural language search query (or set of related questions) to retrieve authoritative evidence that can verify all aspects of the claim.

Input format:
- claim: A factual statement that asserts one or more facts or relationships involving named entities, dates, events, or classifications, requiring verification.
- notes: A list of brief factual evidence snippets relevant to the claim. These typically contain domain-specific, historical, or encyclopedic data to assist in focusing and disambiguating the verification task.

Output format:
- query: A well-formed, detailed, and unambiguous natural language question or set of questions that directly target the verification of every essential entity, fact, and relationship stated or implied by the claim, incorporating key information from the notes.

Detailed instructions and best practices:

1. **Entity and Relationship Extraction:**  
   Identify all named entities mentioned in the claim — including people, organizations, teams, publications, artistic works (operas, albums), sporting events, and relevant dates or time periods. Also identify roles (e.g., actor, composer, driver), hierarchical or causal relationships, and classifications (e.g., whether something is a periodical). Extract the relationships expressed by the claim, such as who formed what and when, who composed which work, who participated in which event, or who held what role.

2. **Verification Objectives:**  
   Break down the claim’s facts into discrete verification objectives, such as:  
   - Confirming formation or founding dates of organizations or bands.  
   - Identifying main cast or key personnel connected to films, TV shows, events, or teams.  
   - Verifying authorship, composition, or publication details of works.  
   - Establishing if a person’s most notable role matches the claim.  
   - Confirming participation of a named individual in specific sporting event seasons or series.  
   Each objective should be reflected explicitly in the queries.

3. **Complex Claims & Multiple Sub-Questions:**  
   If the claim links multiple facts or entities, the query must explicitly partition these into related questions addressing:  
   - Each entity’s identity and key details (birth, formation, participation).  
   - Relationships across entities (e.g., verifying if an actor appearing in one movie is the same who appeared in another production, or checking whether a driver competed across different series during overlapping years).  
   - Temporal constraints (e.g., career years, season numbers).  
   - Cross-verifications dictated by the claim logic (e.g., whether two named people are the same or distinct).  
   
4. **Integrate Notes to Focus & Disambiguate:**  
   Use the notes to:  
   - Clarify possible ambiguous names or entities (e.g., two bands, each linked to an album but distinct).  
   - Incorporate domain-specific details such as specific seasons, event editions, roles, or publication types that can focus the queries more precisely.  
   - Confirm or challenge disputed details in the claim (e.g., the claim says “in 2002,” but notes contradict or confirm this).  
   Incorporate these qualifiers and constraints into your formulated queries to maximize the relevance and accuracy of retrieved evidence.

5. **Query Clarity, Completeness, and Retrieval Optimization:**  
   - Formulate queries that are clear, direct, and cover all claim components wholly. Avoid vague or overly broad queries.  
   - Include all critical qualifiers (dates, names, roles, titles, event numbers/seasons) that would help retrieving documents narrowly focused on verifying the claim.  
   - Use explicit questions rather than general, indefinite prompts.  
   - If multiple related facts must be verified, express them as distinct but connected questions to improve retrieval of multiple relevant documents.

6. **Generalizable Strategy:**  
   To consistently generate effective queries:  
   - Extract entities and detailed facts from both claim and notes.  
   - Enumerate the claim's verification targets clearly as sub-questions, reflecting both primary facts and their relationships or dependencies.  
   - Embed constraints from notes to prevent ambiguity and reduce false positives in retrieval.  
   - Seek to cover every element of the claim so that retrieved documents as a set can fully verify or refute the claim.

Additional domain-specific knowledge patterns to consider (from the examples):  
- Bands often have a known formation year; albums are released by specific bands distinct from others with similar timelines.  
- Actors may appear in multiple productions; identity verification across different movies and TV shows requires verifying cast lists and notable roles.  
- Sports and racing series have season numbers correlated with years; driver nationalities and participation in events are crucial. Cross-check participation years with seasons and winning drivers.  
- Claims involving overlapping participation or notable “firsts” in a given season may require confirming multiple timelines and competitions simultaneously.  
- Disambiguate entities with similar names (different bands, actors, composers) using nationalities, formation years, or associated works.

By strictly following these guidelines and leveraging all input data in a structured and detailed manner, the assistant will produce high-precision queries that enable retrieval systems to locate authoritative sources supporting or disproving complex factual claims in diverse knowledge domains.
