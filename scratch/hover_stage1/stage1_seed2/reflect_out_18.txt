Task Description:

You will be given two input fields:

- claim: A statement or assertion containing one or more factual claims. These claims will typically involve people, works (films, TV shows, albums), events (film festivals, awards), chronological sequences, geographic or demographic comparisons, and relationships or attributions between entities. The claim may have multiple related factual components, some involving temporal, biographical, geographic, or comparative details.

- notes: A set of fact-based sentences extracted from diverse knowledge sources pertinent to the claim. These notes may partially overlap, have nuanced or conflicting data about dates, roles, awards, geographic facts, or entity associations. The notes do not necessarily confirm the claim outright but provide background relevant to verifying the claim.

Your Core Task:

Formulate a precise, unambiguous verification query (or a logically connected set of queries) that directly addresses all critical factual components of the claim. This query should be constructed so that answering it with relevant factual evidence would confirm or refute the claim in full.

Detailed Domain-Specific Considerations & Guidelines:

1. **Identify and Parse Key Factual Components in the Claim**  
   - Claims often involve:  
     - Attribution of works (e.g., who wrote or directed a film, and whether it was a final work for a director).  
     - Chronological ordering (e.g., whether a film premiered before or after another event/work or performance).  
     - Biographical or role-based confirmation (e.g., was person X holding position Y at time Z or did they serve in a particular role).  
     - Awards and honors attribution (e.g., Did someone win a Nobel Prize? Did a film win a particular festival award?).  
     - Geographic or demographic comparisons (e.g., country A is smaller than country B, related to persons from those countries).  
   - Break down multi-part claims into discrete fact-checkable units.

2. **Incorporate Entities and Relationships Precisely**  
   - Explicitly name persons, titles (films, shows, albums), events (film festivals, awards ceremonies), time frames/years, and geographic entities mentioned in the claim.
   - Include multiple relevant entities connected via relationships or sequence (e.g., film A, directed by person X, winning award Y in year Z).

3. **Temporal and Comparative Context is Crucial**  
   - When the claim involves timing (e.g., “after,” “before,” or during a particular year or event), the query must incorporate these temporal qualifiers.  
   - For comparisons (e.g., geographic size, award eligibility), make the query explicitly comparative: Is A larger than B? Did A occur before B?  

4. **Leverage the 'Notes' to Pinpoint What Must be Verified**  
   - Use notes to check for potential confusions (e.g., similar titles with slight name variations—"The Night Hunter" vs. "The Night of the Hunter").  
   - Identify whether key entities have associated attributes or conflicts in the notes (e.g., Nelly Sachs did win the Nobel Prize, but associated writers did not).  
   - Confirm that queries cover nuances where claims hinge on partial truths or a negation (e.g., claims that “both X and Y have not won a prize” require querying prize status of both).  
   - Include known domain-specific facts stated or implied in notes, such as festival years, award recipients, or artist biographies.

5. **Formulating the Query**  
   - Phrase queries as clear, direct questions targeting each key factual element or their logical conjunction.  
   - If multiple facts in the claim are connected, the query can be composed of multiple related questions, clearly and logically linked.  
   - Avoid vague or general phrasing; your query must be sufficiently specific to enable retrieval of precise confirming or refuting evidence.  
   - Include exact titles, dates, names, roles, and other identifying details as they appear in the claim or notes.

6. **Example Strategies for Query Construction:**  
   - Parse claim into distinct fact assertions.  
   - Cross-reference notes for clarifications or conflicts.  
   - Formulate queries that ask:  
     - “Did film X’s screenplay base on a novel written by Y?”  
     - “Did both person A and person B win the Nobel Prize in Literature?”  
     - “Did musical film Z win the Golden Bear at festival W in year Y?”  
     - “Was actor X a US Navy combat veteran in World War II and star in the musical film Z?”  
     - “Did TV show A air on KidsClick on Sunday?” and “Did an episode titled A of American Dad feature a song from album B?”

7. **Goal**  
   - The query(s) should explicitly cover all components of the claim, enabling factual verification for confirmation or refutation.  
   - Queries should optimize precision and recall for search/retrieval systems that will use these questions to locate relevant evidence.

Summary:

Given a claim and a set of notes with factual information, your task is to craft precise verification question(s) that target all relevant factual elements (entities, attributions, dates, roles, geographic/demographic comparisons) required to confirm or deny the claim. Your queries should reflect temporal orderings, comparative facts, and nuanced relations, informed by the notes, and avoid ambiguity or overly broad questions.
