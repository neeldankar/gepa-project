Task Overview:  
You are given two inputs:  
- A **claim**, which is a factual statement containing one or more entities (people, places, works, events, dates, titles, roles, etc.) and relationships that require verification or evidence support.  
- A set of **notes**, which are short factual content snippets relevant to the claim, including domain-specific, historical, or encyclopedic details that may help verify or refute the claim.

Your goal is to produce a precise and comprehensive natural language **query** (or set of related queries) that can be used to retrieve authoritative documents or evidence to verify the entire claim.

---

Key Instruction Details & Best Practices:  

1. **Entity and Relationship Extraction:**  
   - Identify all named entities in the claim such as people, organizations, geographic locations, dates, events, artistic works (operas, songs, publications), roles (manager, composer), titles, and their interrelationships.  
   - Pay attention to compound claims where multiple entities and relationships interact or are compared.

2. **Verification Focus:**  
   The query should explicitly target the exact facts or connections needing verification, for example:  
   - Dates of establishment/founding or classification of entities (e.g., “when was X founded?”).  
   - Identifying roles or titles held by individuals over specified times.  
   - Confirming authorship, composition, or first recording of works.  
   - Determining if entities with similar attributes or names are the same or different.  
   - Cross-asserting claims that link two or more entities (e.g., did person A compose the same work as person B?).

3. **Use Notes to Disambiguate and Focus Query:**  
   - Incorporate relevant details from the notes to clarify or add precision (e.g., specific dates, geographic locations, exact chart names, known related entities).  
   - Use domain-specific terminologies, such as “Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks chart” or “Caldera de Taburiente National Park” rather than vague terms.  
   - Include contextual information from notes that may reveal contradictions, clarifications, or multiple entities with close relationships.

4. **Handling Complex and Compound Claims:**  
   - Break down complex claims into multiple sub-questions reflecting each fact or entity relationship.  
   - Ensure all parts of the claim are represented, especially when different parts involve different entities, dates, or locations.  
   - Include cross-comparison queries needed to verify overlap or distinction (e.g., “Is composer A the same as composer B?”).

5. **Query Clarity and Completeness:**  
   - Queries must be unambiguous, clear, and detailed enough to retrieve specific, relevant, and authoritative supporting documents.  
   - Avoid vague or general queries that could lead to irrelevant results.  
   - Where appropriate, use multiple related questions rather than one overly broad question.

6. **Examples of Query Construction Strategies:**  
   - Start by verifying core statements: names, dates, locations, roles.  
   - For entities with potential ambiguity, explicitly query for identification or distinguishing features.  
   - When claims involve sequences or priority (e.g., first recording, initial author), explicitly ask for this information.  
   - When claims involve relationships between two or more entities, include questions to verify each entity’s attributes and then their relational connection.

---

**Generalizable Approach to Formulating Queries:**  
1. Extract entities and their attributes/roles from the claim.  
2. Map each fact or linkage from the claim into a natural language question aiming to retrieve verifiable data.  
3. Consult notes to add specificity (dates, locations, official titles) to queries to enhance focus.  
4. Structure queries logically—single facts get single focused questions, complex claims split into multiple precise questions.  
5. Use domain-specific terms and exact names/phrases as found in the notes and claim for maximal retrieval precision.

---

**Summary**  
Produce clear, precise, detailed, and logically structured natural language queries that cover all elements of the claim by leveraging entities, dates, roles, and relationships contained in the claim and refined by the detailed factual hints provided in the notes. The queries must be designed to retrieve authoritative and relevant documents that can confirm or refute the entire claim in all its complexity.
