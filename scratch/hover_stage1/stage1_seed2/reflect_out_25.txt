You are given three inputs:

- `claim`: a factual or semi-factual statement or assertion involving domain-specific entities such as persons (e.g., artists, scientists, athletes), events (e.g., sporting finals, historical occurrences), objects (e.g., songs, software, computers), roles (e.g., TV judges, authors), dates, places, or relationships (e.g., birthplace, origin of a platform).

- `notes`: a list of sentences or short passages collected from various sources, which contain factual information related in some way to the claim’s entities and elements. The notes may include direct statements or overlapping and partially redundant evidence. Some notes may also contain contradictions or irrelevant details.

- `context`: one or more longer, more comprehensive background documents or article extracts that provide fuller factual descriptions about the domains, entities, events, or other key elements mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to verify and clarify the claim by analyzing the provided notes and context, then produce two outputs:

1. **new_notes:**  
   - Create a concise, focused list of factual notes directly relevant to the claim and its components.  
   - These must include clear, explicit evidence that supports, refutes, or clarifies the specific factual elements of the claim (for example: confirming named entities, roles, dates, event results, places, or attributes).  
   - Avoid including irrelevant, unsupported, contradictory (without basis), speculative, or redundant information.  
   - When multiple notes convey the same fact, merge or summarize them succinctly to improve clarity and conciseness.  
   - Explicitly highlight when key claim components lack supporting evidence in the provided materials.  
   - Differentiate between explicit confirmation (e.g., “Person X is credited as the lyricist of Song Y”) and absence of evidence (e.g., “No evidence that Person X had a role in this event”).  
   - Notes should be factual, precise, and domain-specific rather than generic.

2. **titles:**  
   - Provide a list of concise, descriptive document or article titles representing the primary information sources underpinning the curated new_notes.  
   - These titles function as metadata references to the most relevant documents or sources needed for verifying the claim with minimal additional research.  
   - Each title should be specific and clearly indicate which entity, event, or topic it covers (e.g., a biography of a person, a description of a game or song, or a document about a company or city).  
   - Include all key documents that correspond to the main factual elements verified or discussed in the new_notes.

---

**Domain-specific considerations and best practices:**

- Claims often revolve around domain-specific entities and details: persons (including exact full names and roles), events (dates, results, players), artistic or technological products (songs, platforms, software versions), places (birthplaces, origins), or relationships/associations (e.g., who authored a song, where a person was born, or company national origins).

- Notes and context can contain overlapping or repeated information and sometimes contradictory claims; your task includes synthesizing and consolidating these into coherent, non-redundant points.

- Carefully distinguish between persons/entities with similar names by relying on roles, dates, or other identifiers.

- Explicitly confirm or deny roles or participation rather than speculating.

- Verify numeric or superlative claims (e.g., “fifth-largest city”) by checking if the provided information supports it or if there is no evidence.

- When the claim references relationships (e.g., “born in the birthplace of X”), validate both sides of the relationship, ensuring the link is factual.

- For geographical claims, ensure the claim’s place is correctly situated within the stated administrative or political boundaries.

- Use evidence from notes and/or the context interchangeably, synthesizing across them for clarity and completeness.

- Avoid generic knowledge or assumptions not supported by the provided notes/context.

---

**Generalizable strategy for verification:**

- Identify the core factual elements/claims (entities, relationships, dates, places).

- Extract from notes/context the explicit factual statements about those elements.

- Consolidate overlapping facts and discard irrelevant or unsupported notes.

- Create clear factual confirmations or point out absence of confirming evidence.

- Output a clean, focused summary (new_notes) directly addressing these facts.

- List corresponding document titles that best summarize the origin or coverage of these facts.

---

By following these instructions, you effectively fact-check the claim using the supplied evidence, present distilled and clear factual findings, and provide meaningful metadata for verification and retrieval.
