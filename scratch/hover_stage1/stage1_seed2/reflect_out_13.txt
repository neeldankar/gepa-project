Given the input fields:

- `claim`: a factual statement involving entities, dates, events, or named items that require verification or information support.
- `notes`: a list of short factual evidence snippets relevant to the claim, containing domain-specific, historical, or encyclopedic information that may support or refute the claim.

Produce the output field:

- `query`: a concise and precise natural language search query or set of related questions designed to retrieve supporting documents or evidence to verify the claim. This query should capture all essential elements and relationships mentioned in the claim, broken down if needed into sub-questions, to maximize effective retrieval of relevant information.

Task details and best practices:

1. **Entity identification:** Identify named entities, dates, events, titles, and their relationships in the claim. Examples of entities include named people, teams, operas, publications, dates, roles (e.g., manager, composer), and titles of works.

2. **Verification objectives:** The query must target the facts or relationships to verify or find evidence for, such as:
   - Confirming existence and founding dates of organizations (e.g., sports teams).
   - Identifying people connected to events or roles (e.g., players, managers).
   - Clarifying authorship or composition of works (e.g., operas, books).
   - Establishing classifications of items (e.g., whether a magazine is a periodical).
   - Cross-referencing overlapping facts or claims (e.g., whether the same person held two roles).

3. **Complex claims:** For compound claims linking multiple facts or entities, the query should reflect the complexity by explicitly including sub-questions that consider:
   - Multiple entities’ identities and relationships.
   - Time frames specified.
   - Cross-comparisons (e.g., are composers the same or different?).

4. **Use claim and notes collectively:** Use the domain knowledge embedded in the `notes` to disambiguate or focus the query by emphasizing key details or constraints (e.g., dates, specific seasons, role titles, publication type) that are potential retrieval targets.

5. **Query clarity and completeness:** Formulate the query to be direct, unambiguous, and sufficiently detailed to locate authoritative documents or data that provide clear answers. Avoid vagueness or omission of critical conditions.

6. **Generalizable approach:** 
   - Extract entities and their relationships.
   - Translate claim’s logic into question(s) that can be answered by factual sources.
   - Incorporate qualifiers (dates, roles, composition, publication types) from notes.
   - Cover all elements of the claim to verify the entirety of the statement.

By following these instructions, the assistant will craft focused queries that improve retrieval accuracy of supporting evidence for verifying complex factual claims across diverse domains such as sports history, musicology, publishing, and more.
