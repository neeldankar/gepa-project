Task Description:

You are given a claim and a set of notes (contextual information relevant to the claim). Your task is to produce a well-formulated search query (or a set of queries) that, when issued to a knowledge source or database, can retrieve authoritative documents or evidence that verify or refute the claim.

Input Format:

- claim: A textual statement that requires verification or fact-checking.
- notes: A list of textual notes or snippets extracted from various sources that may contain partial or conflicting information about the claim.

Output Requirements:

- Generate one or more natural language search queries aimed at efficiently retrieving documents or data that will allow a fact-checker or automated system to confirm or deny the claim.
- Queries should be precise but comprehensive enough to cover all key elements of the claim.
- If the claim has multiple parts (e.g., compound facts), the query should address all parts explicitly or generate multiple queries, clearly framed.
- The query should directly reference key entities, dates, events, or relationships mentioned in the claim.
- Queries should be focused on retrieving authoritative and relevant sources, including data about people (e.g., actors, musicians), works (e.g., films, albums, songs), dates (e.g., death dates), or relationships (e.g., teachers of specific individuals).

Generalizable Strategy:

- Analyze the claim for named entities, events, dates, and relationships.
- Compare the claim against the notes to identify which elements are already supported, contradicted, or missing.
- Identify what information is crucial to verify the claim fully.
- Formulate queries that explicitly target this information, including all relevant qualifiers (e.g., location, nationality, work titles, dates).
- When the claim involves overlap or intersection conditions (e.g., an actor who starred in two works), query should seek to identify such intersections or memberships.
- When contextual or ambiguous information arises (e.g., conflicting death dates, or unknown teacher connections), queries should be split or combined to cover all parts of the claim for thorough verification.

Domain-Specific Considerations:

- Persons with multiple roles (e.g., an actor’s nationality, roles in specific films).
- Titles of artistic works (films, albums, songs) and their metadata (release date, performers).
- Relationship and biographical information (e.g., teachers of individuals, dates of death).
- Contextual location and event details (venue names, performance places).
- The queries should be formulated in a way that they can be used in knowledge bases or search engines to retrieve fact-based documents or data tables relevant to verifying the claim.

Example:

For a claim involving an English actor who starred in two productions, a good query would specify the actor’s nationality, the exact works, and the venue to retrieve precise cast lists confirming or denying the claim.

Summary:

Produce detailed, specific, and factual natural language queries derived from the claim and notes that enable thorough fact verification by retrieving relevant authoritative documents, covering all claim components and resolving ambiguities or multi-part claims by crafting queries accordingly.
