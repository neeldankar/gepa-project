- `claim`: a natural language statement about factual information involving people, films, dates, roles, or relationships.
- `notes`: a list of text snippets containing supporting or contradicting information relevant to the claim. These notes may include extracted facts, summaries, or partial pieces of evidence.

1. `new_notes`: a concise, non-redundant collection of key evidence snippets selected or synthesized from the original `notes` that directly support or refute parts of the claim. The `new_notes` should cover all distinct factual components of the claim, such as names, dates, roles, and relationships, and should summarize the gist of the relevant evidence without unnecessary repetition. If multiple synonymous or similar notes appear, combine or select the most informative ones to avoid redundancy.

2. `titles`: a list of titles or labels identifying the most relevant documents, works, or entities drawn from the `context` that provide essential background or authoritative evidence for the claim. These should be entity or document titles (e.g., film titles with years, names of key persons) that correspond to the main facts in the claim and clarify relationships, timings, or roles.

Additional guidelines and domain-specific considerations:

- Claims often involve relationships between people (e.g., familial relations, roles like director or actor), film titles with release years, voice actors in animated movies, film production details, and timelines.

- Verify claims by cross-referencing the notes and context to confirm:
  * Correct identity and roles of persons involved in films (e.g., who starred or directed a film).
  * Accurate chronological order of film releases.
  * Familial relationships when mentioned (e.g., father-son actor pairs).
  * Whether specific voice actors performed in given film versions and release years.
  * Distinctions between similarly named films, studios, or production companies.
  * Geographic or nationality information when relevant (e.g., confirming nationality of directors).
  
- Be alert for cases where the claim involves negation or corrections (e.g., no Albanian director was involved), and ensure `new_notes` reflect authoritative negation or contradiction accordingly.

- Avoid including irrelevant or redundant information present in the notes; focus on evidence that directly impacts the truthfulness or the clarification of the claim components.

- The `titles` selection should focus on documents or entities referenced directly in the claim and critical to substantiating or refuting it; prioritizing consensus and canonical sources.

- When a claim includes multiple entities or films, ensure all are represented in both the `new_notes` and `titles` as required to illustrate their relationship or the contrast being evaluated.

- The reasoning process involves verifying factual claims by triangulating details from multiple notes and contextual documents to form a concise, evidence-based summary and referencing the core canonical documents/entities most central to the claim.

Remember, your outputs (`new_notes`, `titles`) are intended as distilled, clear, and comprehensive factual support or refutation, summarizing and selecting evidence from potentially noisy or repetitive source notes and focused documents.
