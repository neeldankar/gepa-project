Given input fields:

- `claim`: A statement or assertion that needs to be verified, involving factual information about people, events, works (e.g., films), timelines, geographic or biographical data, or relationships between entities.
- `notes`: A list of sentences extracted from relevant documents or knowledge sources that contain factual information related to the claim. These notes may contain partial, overlapping, or nuanced facts, such as dates, roles, geographic sizes, timelines, or biographical details.

Your task is to generate the output field:

- `query`: A precise, well-formed, factual verification question (or set of related questions) that, if answered, would confirm or refute the claim. The query should be formulated to directly address the key factual components of the claim, making explicit any temporal, geographic, or biographical details involved.

Detailed instructions and domain-specific considerations:

1. Understand that the claim may involve multiple related facts, such as:
   - Confirmation of a person's role or position during a specific time period or event (e.g., coaching a team in a given season).
   - Chronological ordering of events or performances (e.g., whether one film premiered before or after another work involving the same actor).
   - Geographic or demographic comparisons (e.g., whether one country is geographically smaller than another, related to persons from each country).
   - Attribution of works (e.g., identifying the director of a particular film and confirming it as their final work).
   
2. The `notes` provide evidence snippets that may:
   - Include partial or conflicting information regarding dates, roles, or associations.
   - Contain biographical or geographic metadata relevant to the claim.
   - Sometimes refer to external, well-known facts beyond the supplied notes (e.g., a director's known final film).
   
3. Use the `notes` to identify the key elements required to verify the claim:
   - Entities involved (people, works, teams).
   - Their relationships and timelines.
   - Geographic or demographic comparisons.
   
4. Formulate your `query` to:
   - Combine all essential factual components into a single question or a logically connected set of questions.
   - Explicitly reference the entities, events, or facts from the claim.
   - Phrase the question in a way that directly targets validation or refutation of the claim.
   - Incorporate temporal relations when relevant (e.g., "Did actor X perform in film A after the premiere of film B?").
   - Incorporate comparative facts when claiming ordering or size differences (e.g., "Is country A geographically smaller than country B?").
   - Avoid vague or overly general questions; be specific to the key claim points.
   
5. The overall goal is to produce a clean, unambiguous query that can guide retrieval or verification systems in finding evidence either confirming or contradicting the claim, ensuring all critical aspects from the claim are covered.

Example strategy (generalizable across claims):

- Parse the claim into distinct factual assertions or conditions.
- Cross-reference these assertions with the provided `notes` to identify which facts need verification.
- Construct one or more precise, fact-focusing query questions that map to these assertions.
- Ensure the queries logically fit the reasoning needed to prove or disprove the claim.

This approach helps maximize precision in search/retrieval while capturing the full complexity of the claim being evaluated.
