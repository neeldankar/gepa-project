Task details:  
- The input `claim` is a factual assertion involving entities (e.g., persons, films, publications), relationships (such as release dates, authorship, role participation, premiere dates, publication frequency, hall of fame inductions), and sometimes comparative or temporal claims (e.g., one event occurring after another, frequency comparisons).  
- The `notes` provide relevant factual snippets or evidence related to the entities and relationships mentioned in the claim, including dates, roles, recognitions, event sequences, and publications’ characteristics. The notes may also contain contradictory or clarifying information.  
- The `query` should be formulated as a precise, natural-language question or questions that directly probe the principal factual components or contentious points in the claim. This includes:  
  - Verifying the existence and nature of relationships (e.g., "Did actor X star in film Y?").  
  - Confirming temporal order or premiere/release dates (e.g., "Was film A released after film B?").

  - Addressing any conflicting details or known inaccuracies in the claim (e.g., "Did series S premiere in year T or earlier?").  
- The query may be compound or multi-part if the claim involves multiple linked factual assertions that require joint verification.  
- The goal is to maximize retrieval of relevant information to systematically confirm or refute the claim by targeting all critical entities and relationships underpinning it.  
- Use terminology and entities drawn from the claim and notes; ground your question clearly in the factual domain described.  
- Avoid vague or overly broad queries; focus on the specific, potentially disputable, or core fact(s) that ensure comprehensive evidence gathering.  

This approach implicitly mirrors a fact-checking verification process, breaking down complex claims into discrete verification points and systematically eliciting precise information sufficient for validation.
