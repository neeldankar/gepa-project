You will be given three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A factual statement or assertion whose accuracy or validity needs to be assessed or elaborated upon.
- `notes`: A list of extracted relevant information, facts, or observations derived from various sources related to the claim; these often include corrections to misconceptions, clarifications of taxonomy or terminology, species or entity counts, ownership or location details, or identifying key individuals or events relevant to the claim.
- `context`: A list of short encyclopedic or informational passages (e.g., from Wikipedia entries) that provide background information or factual data about entities mentioned in the claim or notes.

Your task is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

### Detailed Task Description

1. **Reasoning Logic (Implicit Step):**  
   - Analyze and cross-check the claim against the provided `notes` and `context`.  
   - Clarify scientific, geographic, historical, or domain-specific details mentioned in the claim. For example, recognizing that a botanical term might be a subfamily rather than a genus, or recognizing ownership relationships of venues in a corporate structure, or correcting biographical errors about an athlete's origin or participation record.  
   - Identify and correct any inaccuracies or errors in the claim based on the combined evidence from notes and context.  
   - Highlight relevant taxonomical, botanical, or domain-specific distinctions (e.g., difference between genus and subfamily, or specifics about species count).  
   - Resolve ambiguous references or identify key players, events, or entities if relevant to the claim's validation.

2. **Producing `new_notes`:**  
   - Create a concise and coherent summary of the factual information derived from the `notes` and verified or clarified by the `context`.  
   - Strip out redundancies and contradictory statements present in the original `notes`, focusing on verified and clarified facts.  
   - Clearly point out common misconceptions or errors found in the claim (e.g., "the claim mistakenly calls X a genus, but it is actually a subfamily").  
   - Include key numeric or categorical data from context such as species counts, event dates, ownership details, or player participation as applicable.  
   - Organize facts that best support or refute the claim, ensuring they are factual, unambiguous, and domain-correct.

3. **Producing `titles`:**  
   - Extract and list the relevant document or article titles (from the broader corpus or knowledge base) that contributed significant factual information to verify or clarify the claim.  
   - These titles should correspond to core entities, events, or concepts—e.g., genus names, venue names, sports events, players, or other domain-specific subjects.  
   - Aim for comprehensive coverage of key relevant articles but avoid extraneous or unrelated titles.  
   - The set of titles typically includes those that provide definitions, ownership information, species counts, biographies, or tournament details as needed.

### Domain-Specific Notes  
- Botanical taxonomy requires careful attention: many terms like "Caesalpinioideae" may refer to subfamilies rather than genera. As such, phrase corrections accordingly. Include species counts per genus or group where relevant.  
- Ownership/venue claims require verifying location, ownership structure (e.g., casinos/hotels owned by a specific corporation), and venue naming, especially for residency shows or events tied to locations.  
- Sports-related claims need to clarify player origins, participation in specific tournaments or events, defending champions, and relevant biographical data to confirm or refute claims. Know that players may have similar surnames but differ in sport or nationality.

### Generalizable Strategy
- Systematically compare claim assertions to the evidence in notes and context.  
- Identify and correct factual inaccuracies or misconceptions within the claim.  
- Summarize verified, relevant facts in `new_notes` for clarity and precision.  
- Identify and list the supporting documents or entities used as evidence in `titles`.  
- When combining biological, geographic, biographical, and event information, ensure domain-appropriate terminology and data fidelity.

By following the above methodology and domain considerations, your output will provide precise factual summaries and relevant document references that reflect the evidence backing or refuting the input claim.
