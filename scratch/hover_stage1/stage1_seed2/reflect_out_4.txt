Task Overview:
You are given three fields as input: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`. Your objective is to analyze these inputs and produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Inputs:
- `claim`: A factual or comparative statement that may include references to people, films, bands, operas, acts, collaborations, production companies, or other domain-specific entities.
- `notes`: A list of supporting or contradictory statements regarding the claim. These notes often contain nuanced information, partial confirmations, or refutations extracted from various sources.
- `context`: A list of related document summaries or descriptions that provide background knowledge and additional facts.

Outputs:
- `new_notes`: A filtered and concise list of factually accurate, claim-relevant notes derived from the input `notes` and reinforced by `context`. These should:
  - Clarify or correct elements of the claim.
  - Highlight affirmations or contradictions with specific factual detail.
  - Avoid redundancy by consolidating repeated information or overly similar notes.
  - Incorporate relevant domain-specific information (e.g., the nature of film production companies, opera structure, band genres, collaboration types).
- `titles`: A list of document or topic titles from `context` that are most relevant and authoritative for substantiating or refuting the claim. Include key sources that underpin the selected `new_notes`.

Key domain considerations and reasoning strategies to embed in your approach:

1. **Entity Disambiguation and Correctness**  
   - Distinguish between similarly named entities (e.g., bands vs. directors, films with similar titles, operas versus films inspired by them).  
   - Verify the accuracy of actors’ or musicians’ affiliations with films or bands, including co-stars and production companies.

2. **Fact Checking and Contradiction Resolution**  
   - Evaluate contradictory claims by cross-referencing notes and context to identify the most credible sources.  
   - Clarify ambiguous or partially incorrect claims by highlighting precise facts (e.g., exact cast members, production companies, years, genres, and roles).  
   - Address differences in formats (e.g., operas with acts vs. films that lack formal acts) and genre classifications (rock band vs. other genres).

3. **Contextual Linkage**  
   - Connect claims to broader or related contexts (e.g., filmographies of actors, production company portfolios, band genre categorizations, the typical structure of operas and films).  
   - Incorporate domain-specific knowledge such as typical film production practices, naming conventions, and artistic genre definitions.

4. **Conciseness and Clarity**  
   - Summarize complex or redundant information succinctly in `new_notes`.  
   - Present `new_notes` as factual and neutral statements avoiding speculation where evidence is absent.  
   - The `titles` should offer clear pointers to authoritative or relevant documents that support `new_notes`.

5. **Handling Partial Information and Uncertainty**  
   - When evidence for a claim element is lacking or contradictory, state this clearly in `new_notes`.  
   - Avoid asserting unverified facts; instead highlight absence or conflicting evidence.

6. **Synthesis Strategy**  
   - Treat the task as a detailed fact validation and synthesis problem: use `notes` as initial evidence and `context` to confirm, complement, or refute these notes.  
   - Prioritize detailed, specific evidence directly related to the claim’s main entities and assertions.  
   - Maintain awareness of domain-specific conventions and typical characteristics (e.g., an opera typically has acts, a film usually does not; band genre classification nuances).

Summary Instruction:
Given a `claim` that makes factual statements or comparisons about cultural artifacts (films, music bands, operas), their participants, or production details, review all `notes` and `context` to produce a refined set of truthful, claim-relevant notes (`new_notes`) and a curated list of corresponding authoritative titles (`titles`). In doing so, carefully validate each claim component against facts in both notes and context, resolve contradictions, incorporate domain-specific knowledge, and provide clear, concise, and supported outputs. The goal is to enhance reliability and traceability of factual verification relevant to the claim.
