Task Description:

You will be given three inputs: `claim` (a factual statement or assertion), `notes` (a list of supporting or related sentences extracted from various documents or contexts), and `context` (a list of detailed document excerpts relevant to the claim). Your task is to analyze these inputs and produce two outputs: `new_notes` and `titles`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. Understand the Claim:
   - Carefully read the `claim` to identify what factual information needs to be verified, explained, or inferred.
   - The claim may involve comparing dates, locations, identities, sponsorships, or other factual relationships.

2. Analyze the Notes:
   - The `notes` are snippets or sentences derived from documents relevant to the claim.
   - Identify the facts from `notes` that directly support, contradict, or provide essential background for evaluating the claim.
   - Remove duplications and focus on key evidential sentences that contribute clearly to the claim’s validation or explanation.

3. Use the Context:
   - The `context` consists of longer excerpts from documents related to the entity/entities mentioned in the claim.
   - Use it to:
     - Verify factual details mentioned in notes or the claim,
     - Identify critical missing information not present or unclear in the notes,
     - Extract authoritative facts (such as dates, locations, persons, organizations) needed to substantiate or refute the claim.
   - Cross-check and validate dates, names, relationships, geographic and organizational information via the context.

4. Construct `new_notes`:
   - Compose a concise list of sentences/facts that best summarize authoritative evidence responding to the claim.
   - Include relevant, factual, and verified statements from the original notes and supported by the context.
   - Avoid redundancy and remove irrelevant or speculative notes.
   - When some required information does not exist in the context or notes (e.g., identity or date missing), clearly state that the information cannot be found.

5. Construct `titles`:
   - Provide a list of document titles or authoritative sources that are most relevant and informative for the claim.
   - Titles should be drawn from the context or notes, prioritizing direct and related documents that best support or explain the claim.
   - Include titles that help corroborate critical facts, even if indirectly relevant to the claim.

6. Reasoning and Verification:
   - Your output should reflect a coherent reasoning chain that explains how the evidence supports or refutes the claim.
   - Identify key entities, dates, locations, or events, linking them clearly.
   - When no evidence decisively supports the claim, clearly indicate this by stating the absence of necessary information.

7. Domain-Specific Considerations:
   - Some claims relate to factual details about:
     - Birth years and identities of musicians or band members,
     - Sponsorships and names of sports events related to specific seasons or years,
     - Geographic locations of companies, airports, or facilities within countries,
     - Relationships between multiple individuals or groups,
     - Historical or production details of albums or songs.
   - Maintain awareness of domain terminology (e.g., album titles, company headquarters, sports seasons) and temporal references (e.g., event dates corresponding to sports seasons).

8. Quality and Completeness:
   - Provide a well-organized, factual, and complete summary in `new_notes`.
   - Ensure `titles` are exhaustive and relevant but avoid overly broad or unrelated documents.
   - Aim to retrieve and highlight all gold or key documents that substantiate the claim.

Summary:

Your primary goal is to synthesize from the given `claim`, `notes`, and `context` a precise set of verified, relevant facts (`new_notes`) and a list of authoritative document titles (`titles`) that best ground or invalidate the claim. Your output should be succinct, factual, and based explicitly on the provided inputs without introducing external knowledge beyond what is in the provided context or notes.
