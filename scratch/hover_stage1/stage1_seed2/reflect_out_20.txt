You are given three inputs: 
- `claim` (a factual or semi-factual statement or assertion),
- `notes` (a list of sentences or short passages derived from various sources that are relevant to the claim),
- `context` (one or more longer background documents or article extracts related to the domain(s) and entities mentioned in the claim).

Your task is to analyze the claim in light of the given notes and context and produce two outputs:

1. **new_notes**: a concise, curated list of factually relevant notes extracted or synthesized from the given `notes` and `context` that directly support, refute, or clarify the claim. These new notes should focus on key factual evidence pertinent to verifying the claim’s components. Exclude irrelevant, contradictory without basis, redundant or speculative notes. Highlight the presence or absence of evidence for important claim details (e.g., named entities, roles, dates, events). Summarize or merge overlapping notes where appropriate to improve clarity, and explicitly note when there is no supporting evidence available for a claim element.

2. **titles**: a list of concise document or article titles that best represent the relevant topics or sources underpinning the notes in `new_notes`. These titles serve as metadata to guide retrieval or reference to the most pertinent informational sources relating to the claim and its key factual elements.

Guidelines and domain-specific considerations:

- Claims often involve domain-specific entities like persons (e.g., film directors, football players), events (e.g., FIFA Confederations Cup Final 2013), roles (e.g., judges on a television show), or relationships (e.g., a nickname relating to a team).
- Notes and context may contain nuanced information such as:
  - Confirmation or denial of a person’s role or participation in an event.
  - Disambiguation of similar names or entities.
  - Specific details about events (dates, results, participants).
  - The presence or absence of explicit evidence supporting claim elements.
- When relevant, differentiate between:
  - Explicit confirmation (e.g., “Todd Field directed Little Children and works in media”).
  - Lack of confirmation or contradiction (e.g., “No evidence Junaid Khan was judge on Music Icons”).
- Emphasize the importance of retrieving or producing notes and titles pertaining to the primary entities or factual elements mentioned in the claim (e.g., individual names, event names, team names, film titles) to maximize downstream retrieval or evaluation relevance.
- Your reasoning should be reflected in the choice of notes and titles, ensuring they provide clear, factual support or refutation.
- Avoid including generic or peripheral information unrelated to the direct verification of the claim.
- When multiple notes convey the same fact, consolidate them into a clear, succinct statement.
- Titles should be specific enough to identify meaningful documents or sources that would enable someone else to verify the claim with minimal additional search.

In summary, your role is to fact-check or verify the claim using the evidence given in notes and context, then output a distilled and focused set of key factual notes and descriptive titles of relevant source documents, balancing precision, clarity, and informativeness while acknowledging supported and unsupported parts of the claim.
