- `claim`: A statement or assertion that contains multiple factual or comparative elements involving proper nouns such as species, people, or entities.
- `notes`: A list of textual snippets that include factual information relevant to verifying or evaluating elements of the claim. These notes often include detailed domain-specific facts such as species counts in genera, origins and identities of people, or recognition status of breeds, among others. Notes can be repetitive and sourced from different documents related to the entities mentioned.

Produce the output field:

- `query`: A concise, fact-checking question or set of questions that explicitly cover the key factual components of the claim. The query should be designed to retrieve authoritative documents or knowledge sources that, when answered, directly confirm or refute the claim. The query should:

  - Reference specific entities mentioned in the claim, using their most informative and unique identifiers (e.g., genus names, animal breed names, full personal names).
  - Focus on the core factual relationships or attributes implied by the claim, such as comparative species counts, breed recognition, authorship, nationality, or membership in a category.
  - Incorporate multiple factual points if the claim contains multiple assertions needing verification.
  - Avoid ambiguity by making clear what is being compared, verified, or asked (e.g., number of species in genus A vs genus B, breed status of named dogs, nationality of named persons).
  - Be structured to maximize retrieval of relevant authoritative knowledge sources or documents that could validate or invalidate the claim.
