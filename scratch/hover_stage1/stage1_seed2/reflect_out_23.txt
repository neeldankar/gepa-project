Task Description:

You will be given two fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce the field `query`. The goal of the `query` is to encapsulate, clarify, and explicitly formulate the key verification questions required to fact-check the claim using the evidence contained in the `notes`.

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Input Understanding**:
   - `claim`: A textual statement that makes one or more factual assertions, often about people, works (films, TV shows, characters), roles, dates, or relationships.
   - `notes`: A list of sentences extracted from documents that serve as potential evidence related to the claim. These sentences may support, refute, or be neutral towards the claim. The notes often contain relevant domain-specific information, such as:
       - Actor names linked to characters or works.
       - Premiere or release dates of TV shows or films.
       - Character real names or aliases.
       - Relationships or roles between people in creative works.
       - Presence or absence of evidence regarding certain claims.

2. **Output Requirement - `query`**:
   - The `query` should be formulated as a clear, focused, and direct question (or series of related questions) that explicitly asks for the specific factual points or verification steps needed to confirm or refute the claim.
   - The query should break down complex claims into components if multiple factual elements need verification.
   - The query should reference the entities, roles, titles, or dates explicitly mentioned or implied in the claim.
   - The query should reflect any ambiguity or contradictions in the claim that appear in the notes.
   
3. **Reasoning Prior to Query Formulation**:
   - Although you do not need to output a reasoning section in final results, internally consider:
       - Which facts in the claim can be verified directly by the notes?
       - Are there contradictions or missing evidence in the notes relating to the claim?
       - What would be the minimal set of factual questions to clarify the truth of the claim?
   - The reasoning from examples involves verifying the claim's components against factual statements about actors, characters, premiere dates, and other detailed metadata.
   
4. **Domain-Specific Knowledge & Context**:
   - Actors and characters: Often focus on verifying whether a named actor played a specific character in a named production.
   - Dates: Premiere or release dates of films or episodes are frequently claimed and disputed.
   - Role definitions: Positions such as “director,” “co-director,” or “star” are often subjects of claims.
   - Relationships between multiple productions: e.g., distinguishing between different TV series an actor appeared in.
   - Real names vs. stage names or character names: Some claims involve verifying if a character's real name corresponds to a known person or alias.
   
5. **Query Style**:
   - Use natural language, formal and explicit.
   - Frame queries to capture multiple interrelated facts when the claim contains them.
   - The query should be answerable by consulting documents related to the entities present.
   - Avoid vague or overly broad questions; be specific and tightly focused on the verification needs.
   
Example pattern based on provided inputs:

If the claim states:
- “X actor played character Y in production Z, premiered on date D”  
Then a suitable query might be:  
“Did actor X play character Y in production Z? Also, did production Z premiere on date D, or on a different date?”

If the claim states:
- “Person A was the co-director of film B, and is known as a director along with person C.”  
Then a suitable query might be:  
“Who was the co-director of film B? Is this person recognized as a director? Is person C known as a director?”

Summary:  
Formulate queries that explicitly articulate the factual verifications needed to confirm or refute a claim, incorporating all its key components, appropriately disambiguating complex claims, and leveraging domain-specific information related to actors, characters, productions, dates, and professional roles.
