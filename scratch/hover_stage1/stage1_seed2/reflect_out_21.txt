You are tasked with generating precise, comprehensive search queries from factual claims and related evidence snippets to verify or refute the claims using external authoritative sources.

### Inputs
- **claim**: A factual statement linking entities (such as people, organizations, dates, places, artistic works, events) that requires verification or further information.
- **notes**: A list of short, domain-specific factual evidence snippets relevant to the claim. These snippets contain encyclopedic, historical, or domain knowledge that helps clarify or contextualize the claim.

### Output
- **query**: A concise, unambiguous, and detailed natural language search query (or set of related queries/questions). This query is designed to retrieve authoritative documents or evidentiary support that fully verifies or refutes all assertions embedded in the claim.

---

### Detailed Task Description and Best Practices

1. **Entity Extraction and Relationship Identification:**
   - Identify all named entities in the claim: people (including birthdates), organizations, roles, teams, works (operas, books, songs), dates, locations, events.
   - Detect relationships between these entities as expressed in the claim (e.g., person X led organization Y on date Z; band A recorded song B; person A buried in cemetery C located inside park D).

2. **Verification Dimensions:**
   - Map each fact or relationship in the claim to questions that would verify existence, authorship, role tenure, affiliation, composition, location, or classification.
   - Examples of verification types:
     - Confirming a leader’s identity, birthdate, succession date, and party affiliation.
     - Determining which band recorded a particular song and comparing members counts.
     - Establishing burial locations and their geographic or administrative relationships.
     - Clarifying the nature or affiliation of organizations or sub-organizations.
     - Confirming composition authorship or cross-checking if entities are the same or different.

3. **Use Notes to Disambiguate, Add Constraints, and Focus Queries:**
   - Incorporate critical qualifiers from the notes such as exact dates, roles, organizational hierarchies, genre classifications, time frames, common confusions or differentiations.
   - Use the notes to recognize potential conflations or ambiguities in the claim and explicitly address these in the queries (e.g., the note points to confusion about death place locations, or band genres).

4. **Handle Complex and Compound Claims:**
   - If the claim includes multiple facts or multiple entities and relationships, break down the query into relevant sub-questions.
   - Ensure queries cover:
     - Each entity’s identity and distinguishing features (e.g., birthdate, profession).
     - Relationships between entities (e.g., affiliation, succession, recording credits).
     - Temporal constraints (e.g., specific dates or periods).
     - Cross-references to confirm or reject overlaps (same/different people or entities).

5. **Query Clarity and Completeness:**
   - The query should avoid vagueness or omission of key details from both claim and notes.
   - Use precise language that directs retrieval to specific facts or data points.
   - The query should be sufficiently detailed to locate authoritative sources for all elements in the claim, including dates, names, roles, affiliations, and locations.

6. **Generalizable Approach to Formulating Queries:**
   - Parse claim → identify entities and their relations.
   - Analyze notes → identify focus points, constraints, or contradictions.
   - Translate claim logic into specific, answerable natural language questions.
   - Combine related queries logically to cover the entire claim comprehensively.
   - When multiple entities or facts are presented, formulate queries as sets of sub-questions to cover each verification objective.

---

### Examples of Application (based on past tasks)

- For a political leadership claim with birthdates and affiliations:
  - Query who leads the named party, confirming birthdate and leadership succession date.
  - Verify the party’s youth wings affiliations to link the leader to subordinate organizations.

- For band membership and recording claims:
  - Query which band recorded a named song and the band’s membership size.
  - Query membership size of the other band claimed, including genre to check potential mislabeling.
  - Compare memberships explicitly.

- For burial and location claims:
  - Query identity and profession of the named person.
  - Query burial locations associated with that person.
  - Query definitions and boundaries of named geographic or administrative areas (e.g., cemeteries, park boundaries).
  - Query cross-relations between burial sites and geographic areas, clarifying if the burial location is inside the named area.

---

This instruction encapsulates both the logical approach and the domain-specific nuances critical for constructing effective search queries aimed to verify complex factual claims across domains such as political history, musicology, geography, and more.
