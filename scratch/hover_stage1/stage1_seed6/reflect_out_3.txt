Task Description:  

You will be provided with three input fields:  
- **claim**: a factual statement or hypothesis that needs to be verified or evaluated.  
- **notes**: a list of extracted factual observations, findings, or evidence pieces related to the claim and context. These notes often summarize or highlight key relevant facts that help assess the claim’s accuracy.  
- **context**: a list of textual excerpts or document summaries which include detailed background information, relevant entities, events, and facts that can be used to verify or refute the claim.

Your task is to analyze the claim, notes, and context to produce two output fields:  
- **new_notes**: a refined subset of the input notes that directly supports or refutes the claim, emphasizing critical factual details uncovered from the context and notes. These should be concise, accurate, and focused on the central evidence relevant to the claim verification.  
- **titles**: a list of descriptive document or entity titles, derived from the context, that best capture the key sources or topics underpinning the claim’s verification or falsification. These can be the names of persons, events, works, or distinct documents that were instrumental in your reasoning.

Instructions and Guidelines:  

1. **Focus on Claim Verification:** Use both the notes and context to determine whether the claim is supported, refuted, or unverifiable. Extract and highlight facts that pertain to the claim’s key components such as dates, people, rankings, nominations, or chart positions.  

2. **Domain-Specific Knowledge:**  
   - When dealing with music-related claims: Understand chart ranking terminology (e.g., Billboard Hot Country Singles & Tracks chart positions), discographies, songwriters, and what it means for a song to be first recorded by another artist, particularly within the country music genre.  
   - For film and TV-related claims: Recognize the importance of lead roles, awards (especially Golden Globe nominations), and the association between actors and their roles in given productions or series.  
   - For sports or rally championship claims: Interpret season numbering, driver nationalities, ranking positions, continuous participation across years, and event-specific victories.  
   
3. **Reasoned Synthesis:** Provide a concise reasoning behind your selection of notes and titles, implicitly or explicitly demonstrating how the evidence relates to the claim. Even though the reasoning text itself is not requested as output, it should guide the selection and ensure factual and logical consistency.  

4. **Note Selection:** From the large input notes list, discard redundancies and tangential information. Select only the notes that are critical in verifying the claim’s truthfulness, such as confirmed rankings, documented award nominations, key event summaries, or explicit counter-evidence. Avoid speculative or unsupported statements.  

5. **Title Selection:** Identify and output document or entity titles that best represent the main information sources. Titles should be precise (e.g., “2000 World Rally Championship season overview” rather than “World Rally Championships”), clearly connected to the claim, and informative about the domain aspect in question (e.g., a specific actor’s biography, a music single’s chart performance, or a rally season summary).  

6. **Use Consistent Terminology:** When referencing entities in titles or notes, prefer the official or commonly recognized names as appearing in the context field (e.g., full names of individuals, official song titles, or event names).  

7. **Handling Missing or Unverified Claims:** In cases where the claim cannot be verified due to lack of evidence or contradictory information, clearly reflect this by selecting notes emphasizing missing data or negative evidence, and choose titles that represent the most relevant known sources on the subject.

By following these instructions, your outputs will aid in accurately verifying complex, domain-specific factual claims, facilitating evidence-based fact-checking aligned with authoritative sourced documents.
