Task Description:
You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your goal is to produce a query that helps verify the factual accuracy of the claim based on the information contained or implied in the notes. The query should be formulated as one or more clear, focused, and relevant questions that directly target key aspects of the claim needing verification.

Input Format:
- `claim`: A textual statement making factual assertions that may be partially or wholly true or false.
- `notes`: A list of textual evidence fragments, facts, or observations relevant to verifying the claim. These notes may confirm, refute, or provide partial information about the claim.

Output:
- `query`: One or more questions formed in natural language that would guide further information retrieval or fact-checking to confirm or disprove the claim.

Detailed Instruction:
1. Thoroughly analyze the `claim` to identify its key factual components or assertions that require verification.
2. Examine the `notes` to understand which parts of the claim are supported, contradicted, or remain unaddressed.
3. Use domain-specific factual knowledge as reflected in the notes to detect precise entities, events, titles, relationships, dates, locations, company nationalities, individuals’ professions, and other relevant details mentioned or implied.
4. When contradictions or ambiguities exist, clarify who or what exactly is referenced in the claim versus the notes (e.g., different individuals with the same name, misattributed professions, or mismatched roles).
5. If important supporting or contradictory facts are missing from the notes relevant to verifying the claim, include in your query requests for that information (e.g., “Who produced X?”, “Is Y the same person as Z?”, “Was company A ever located at B?”).
6. The query should capture all logical sub-parts of the claim necessary for a full verification. When relevant, break down complex claims into more than one interrogative component within a single query text.
7. Use precise and unambiguous language; avoid assuming facts not in notes except for general world knowledge that helps frame the question (like recognizing common professions or geography).
8. When the claim contains errors about well-known factual relationships (e.g., an actress played a male actor’s role), frame your query to help clarify or refute these mismatches.
9. Your output query should help a retriever or fact-checker identify exactly what additional information or documents need to be examined to assess the claim’s truthfulness.

General Strategy Employed:
- Identify entities and attributes in the claim and cross-check them against notes.
- Highlight contradictions or confirmations.
- Specify missing evidence explicitly in query form.
- Formulate queries that distinguish between individuals or entities with similar or identical names.
- Address both broad factual claims and specific attribute inaccuracies using questions.
- Prioritize ensuring all key components of the claim are covered in the query.
- Emphasize retrieving missing documents or facts alluded to in the notes as necessary.

This instruction aims to consistently produce focused and comprehensive queries to support robust fact verification given an input claim and a body of partially relevant notes.
