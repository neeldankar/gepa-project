Task Description:

You will be given three input fields: 
- `claim` (a statement or assertion about entities, events, or relationships),
- `notes` (a list of factual statements or evidences related to the claim, often with some verification or refutation information),
- `context` (a list of longer text excerpts, typically encyclopedia-style entries or authoritative descriptions related to the claim and notes).

Your task is to process these inputs and produce two output fields:
- `new_notes`: a concise and focused subset of the `notes` that directly support critical aspects about the validity or invalidity of the claim, reflecting accurate, relevant, and verifiable information gleaned from both the `notes` and the `context`.
- `titles`: a list of document or entity titles selected from the `context` that are most relevant as authoritative sources or key references supporting the reasoning used to evaluate the claim.

Detailed Instructions and Domain-Specific Guidance:

1. Understand the claim carefully, identifying the key entities, relationships, and assertions made. Claims often involve specific proper nouns (e.g., names of people, events, media titles, products, organizations, dates).

2. Cross-examine the `notes` and `context` thoroughly:
   - The `notes` often contain direct starts of evidence, partial truth, or denial/refutation statements. They may be repetitive or overlapping, so select those that are concise, non-redundant, and most evidentially pertinent.
   - The `context` entries are encyclopedia-style or detailed descriptions of entities mentioned in the claim and notes. Use them to verify truthfulness and resolve ambiguities (e.g., confirming whether a TV show or album with a certain name exists, or which country developed a specific computer).

3. Special attention should be given to:
   - Matching names and titles exactly, especially when multiple similarly named entities exist (e.g., "La La Land" as an album vs. a TV show).
   - Correctly associating production or origin countries for products/platforms (e.g., Prológica CP-400 from Brazil vs. Commodore 64 from US).
   - Verifying individuals’ professional domains and roles (e.g., differentiating between an American football player Nate Jacks versus soccer players).
   - Clarifying ambiguity where claims conflate unrelated entities or events.

4. Reasoning approach:
   - Identify contradictions, confirmations, or lack of evidence concerning the claim in the notes and context.
   - Distill the evidence to confirm or refute the claim.
   - Organize key factual notes that clearly demonstrate the support or refutation.
   - Select from context the document titles most relevant and authoritative for understanding or verifying the claim’s central aspects, prioritizing directly relevant entities or works.

5. Output construction:
   - `new_notes`: Provide a concise list of factual, evidence-based notes that best summarize and support your conclusion about the claim’s validity, avoiding redundancy and overly broad statements.
   - `titles`: Provide a list of relevant Wikipedia-style article titles or entity names (as found in `context`) that are central references for this fact-checking task.

6. The goal is precise, informative, and focused output that enables clear understanding and verification of the claim’s truthfulness.


Summary:

Given `claim`, `notes`, and `context`, your task is to fact-check the claim by extracting the most pertinent facts (`new_notes`) and identifying the most relevant sources (`titles`), using domain-specific knowledge about entities such as media titles, sports players, video games, computing hardware, and organizational origins. Your response should reflect precise cross-validation of claim details and consistent, well-reasoned extraction of key factual points and authoritative document titles.
