Task Description:
You are given two fields: `claim` and `notes`. The `claim` is a statement or a set of statements containing multiple factual assertions about entities, places, people, or events. The `notes` field consists of a list of factual supporting or relevant context sentences that may or may not fully confirm the claim.

Your goal is to produce a single coherent `query` field designed to retrieve the key supporting documents or evidence from a knowledge base that will collectively verify the entire claim. The `query` should be formulated as a clear, concise, and targeted question (or questions) that, if answered by retrieved documents, would fully confirm or refute all parts of the claim.

Detailed Instructions:

1. **Understand the claim completely:**  
   - Identify each distinct factual assertion embedded within the claim. Claims often combine multiple facts that need separate verification.  
   - Look for entities (people, places, organizations), their attributes, relationships, actions, or qualifications.

2. **Analyze the notes carefully:**  
   - Extract named entities, facts, and relationships that appear repeatedly or provide overlap regarding the claim’s components.  
   - Identify key pieces of information that support or dispute parts of the claim. Note gaps or missing evidence for parts of the claim.

3. **Formulate the query strategically to cover all claim aspects:**  
   - The query must encompass all individual factual pieces from the claim to ensure comprehensive evidence retrieval.  
   - Use specific named entities, precise relationships, and attributes mentioned in the claim and corroborated by the notes.  
   - Include variants or alternate names/abbreviations found in the notes (e.g., "University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV)").   
   - Frame the query as a direct question or a combination of questions that a knowledge retrieval system can use to find exact supporting documents or facts.  
   - If the claim includes multiple entities or conditions, include all in the query to avoid partial retrieval. For example, ask about both institutions and persons, or locations and administrative authorities together.

4. **Domain-specific considerations observed from examples:**  
   - When claims mention locations and institutions, the query should connect institutions to locations explicitly (e.g., "Is [College] located in [County]?").  
   - When verifying if persons performed an action at multiple institutions, queries should ask about all institutions together ("Did [Person] play baseball at [College1] and [College2]?").  
   - When claims mention categories or accolades (e.g., poet-diplomats who won the Nobel Prize), queries should explicitly request identification or confirmation of these categories and persons.  
   - Queries should explicitly address ambiguities detected in the notes (e.g., if proximity to a place is unconfirmed, phrase the query to check that proximity).

5. **Goal for retrieval performance:**  
   - The ultimate aim is to retrieve *all* gold standard documents (key supporting pieces) that fully verify the claim.  
   - Therefore, the query must be comprehensive and precise enough to avoid retrieving only partial evidence.

6. **Query clarity and completeness:**  
   - Avoid vague or broad queries that could retrieve unrelated information.  
   - Avoid overly narrow queries that capture only some parts of the claim.  
   - Use conjunctions and clear wording to include all relevant entities and conditions within the query.

In summary, your task is to convert a fact-based claim and its supporting notes into a precise, comprehensive, and fact-anchored query that will enable full retrieval of evidence necessary to verify the claim. This involves careful entity recognition, relationship mapping, and inclusion of all claim aspects in the query formulation.
