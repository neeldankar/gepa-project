You are given two input fields: `claim` and `notes`. Your task is to produce an output field: `query`.

**Task Objective:**  
Formulate one or more precise, focused natural language queries that, if answered, would allow verification or refutation of the factual claim made in the `claim` field using the information hinted at or referenced in the `notes`.

**Input Format:**  
- `claim`: A single factual statement or claim requiring verification. It may contain multiple factual components or implicit assumptions.  
- `notes`: A list of text snippets or observations derived from a corpus or documents relevant to the claim, including facts, contradictions, or information gaps related to the claim. These notes may mention entities, dates, works (films, books, awards), people, places, and their attributes (e.g., publication frequency, nationality, filmography, geographic size, etc.). The notes also often point out missing evidence or uncertainty related to parts of the claim.

**Output Requirements:**  
- Produce a clear, concise `query` field consisting of one or more coherent questions that, when answered, would collect the critical pieces of factual evidence needed to assess the truthfulness of the claim.  
- The queries should be specific and targeted to verify key components of the claim by referencing the relevant entities or relationships identified in the `claim` and `notes`.  
- Formulate queries to explicitly capture the key aspects that link the claim and notes, such as:  
  - identities or roles of persons (e.g., who starred in a film or portrayed a character)  
  - connections between entities (e.g., whether a person is associated with a particular publication, award, or work)  
  - temporal or geographic facts (e.g., dates, geographic sizes, publication frequencies)  
  - attributes or comparisons (e.g., is one publication more frequent than another)  

**Domain-Specific Considerations:**  
- Entities may include but are not limited to: publications, actors, films, directors, musical artists, awards/hall of fames, novels, geographic entities, and their attributes (publication frequency, national origin, role portrayal, final works).  
- Familiarity with the structure of citation, such as Hall of Fame inductions, filmographies, adaptations, and geographic size comparisons, will help in targeting queries.  
- When multiple pieces or entities appear in the claim, queries should be multi-part to cover each fact or to confirm relationships between these entities.  
- If notes indicate missing or contradictory information, questions should aim to elicit information that resolves these gaps.  
- Avoid vague or overly broad queries; focus on extracting the minimal but sufficient factual information needed from the notes or external knowledge to test the claim’s validity.

**Reasoning/Strategy (implicit from examples):**  
- Analyze the claim to identify its factual components and implied relations.  
- Review notes to recognize which entities and facts are partially confirmed, missing, or contradictory.  
- Construct queries asking about these entities, their relationships, and relevant attributes that would prove or disprove the claim.  
- Use "Did X do Y?" or "Is A more than B?" type formulations aligned with the claim’s assertions.  
- Ensure the query references entities mentioned both in the claim and in the notes to increase precision.  

This structured approach will facilitate validation by retrieving or confirming key documents and facts, ultimately enabling a robust assessment of the claim.
