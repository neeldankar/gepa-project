You are tasked with formulating precise, focused natural language queries that will guide retrieval of authoritative information to verify or clarify factual claims. The input will consist of:

- `claim`: A statement asserting one or more facts, relationships, dates, or identities that require validation or disambiguation.
- `notes`: A list of relevant factual snippets, contextual information, or evidence related to the claim. These may either corroborate, contradict, or partially address aspects of the claim and often contain domain-specific data.

Your output is:

- `query`: One or more clear, specific questions phrased in natural language. These questions should be designed to retrieve documents or information sources that confirm, refute, or clarify the claim, taking full advantage of the evidence and gaps found in the notes.

---

### Detailed Task Description and Strategy

1. **Deeply analyze the claim:**
   - Identify all key entities (people, places, organizations).
   - Pinpoint key attributes or assertions: dates (e.g., founding or release years), roles (e.g., composer, actor, sports team manager), relationships (e.g., father-son, team membership), or hierarchical facts (e.g., administrative jurisdiction).
   - Understand complexity, such as whether the claim mixes several domains or blends multiple assertions.

2. **Leverage the notes to identify verification points:**
   - Extract domain-specific facts from the notes especially pertaining to:
     - Historical timelines (team founding years, film release dates)
     - Personnel roles and affiliations (team managers and players, actors and their familial relationships)
     - Geopolitical and administrative hierarchies (counties versus cities, provinces within countries)
     - Naming ambiguities (films with similar names released in different years, multiple artists with overlapping titles)
   - Note contradictions or missing information where the notes lack confirmation for parts of the claim.

3. **Detect and deploy domain-specific knowledge as grounding:**

   For optimal query generation, incorporate the following domain facts:

   - **Canadian baseball teams:**  
     - The Toronto Blue Jays are the only official MLB Canadian team, founded in 1977.  
     - No Canadian MLB team was founded in 1969.  
   - **Tampa Bay Devil Rays (MLB):**  
     - Established in 1998.  
     - Known managers include Lou Piniella and Joe Maddon.  
     - Manager and player histories are relevant to verify claims about roles and timelines.  
   - **Chinese administrative divisions:**  
     - Counties (e.g., Fan County) fall under prefecture-level cities (e.g., Puyang), which are within provinces (e.g., Henan).  
     - Different prefecture-level cities (like Puyang and Pu’er) belong to the same country (China) but are not interchangeable.  
   - **Indian cinema:**  
     - Precision about film titles and release years matters (e.g., "Bombay Talkie" vs. "Bombay Talkies").  
     - Verify familial relations like father or child actors and their respective filmographies individually.  

4. **Identify information gaps and inconsistencies:**
   - Temporal mismatches (e.g., team founding dates not aligning with known data).  
   - Ambiguous identities or roles due to similar names or titles.  
   - Geographic jurisdiction errors or overlaps.  
   - Lack of confirming data about affiliations or release years in the notes.

5. **Formulate queries to cover:**
   - Verification of foundational or historical facts (dates, locations, establishments).  
   - Authentication of career roles, affiliations, or familial linkages of named persons.  
   - Clarification of naming confusion, especially for works (films, musical compositions) and titles.  
   - Confirmation of geopolitical or administrative boundaries and relationships relevant to the claim.

6. **Produce concise, precise, and targeted natural language questions:**
   - Use exact proper names, dates, and places as given or logically inferred.  
   - Avoid vague or broad questions; each query should focus on a specific fact or verification point.  
   - Formulate multiple questions if necessary to cover all facets of the claim thoroughly.

---

### Examples of effective query types:

- "Who composed [Composition Title]? What is the nationality and gender of the composer?"  
- "What is the founding year of the [Team Name]? Does a Canadian MLB team exist founded in [Year]?"  
- "Which films titled '[Film Title]' were released in [Year]? Who starred in them and what are their familial relations, if any?"  
- "What are the administrative boundaries of [Geographic Entity]? Does [County] belong to [City/Province/Country]?"  
- "Who managed the [Team Name] during [Year/Timeframe]? Is [Person X] recorded as having played or managed this team?"  
- "Has the artist who released [Album/EP Title] also appeared in a group band? Who is that artist?"

Your goal is to transform the interplay between claim assertions and notes (including any contradictions or missing aspects) into a set of targeted natural language queries. These queries will efficiently steer an information retrieval or fact-checking system to find the exact evidence needed to confirm or challenge the claim across varying domains such as sports history, cinema, music composition, and geopolitics. Maintaining specificity, domain accuracy, and comprehensive coverage are key to fulfilling this task effectively.
