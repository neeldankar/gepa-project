Task Description:

You will be given three inputs: `claim` (a statement containing one or more factual assertions), `notes` (a list of evidentiary points or retrieved sentences related to the claim), and `context` (a list of document abstracts or summaries related to relevant entities or concepts referenced in the claim).

Your goal is to produce two outputs:
1. `new_notes`: a concise, synthesized subset of notes that directly address the factual accuracy or inaccuracy of the claim. These new notes should:
   - Explicitly confirm, refute, or clarify each discrete assertion within the claim based on the evidence from both `notes` and `context`.
   - Correct any misunderstandings or inaccuracies in the claim (e.g., wrong genres, professions, relationships).
   - Provide clear, factually supported statements that can independently justify the final judgment on the claim.
   - Avoid redundancy and aggregate relevant evidence succinctly.

2. `titles`: a list of key Wikipedia-style document titles or named entities central to verifying the claim. These titles should:
   - Correspond to the primary entities or concepts relevant to the claim’s truthfulness.
   - Include all entities clearly discussed or referenced in `new_notes`.
   - Maintain correct and disambiguated document naming as found or implied by the `context` (e.g., including disambiguation in parentheses, like "Make Them Gold (Chvrches song)").

Detailed Guidance:

- The `claim` may contain multiple factual assertions (e.g., biographical dates, professions, relationships, group memberships, or comparative statements).
- Use the `notes` as initial evidence to identify contradictions or confirmatory facts.
- Use the `context` as authoritative background information, including biographies, discographies, filmographies, or definitions, to verify or disprove assertions or to clarify ambiguous terms.
- Identify and explicitly address each component or claim clause separately if needed, noting which parts are correct and which are incorrect.
- Where the claim involves relationships (e.g., teacher-student, membership of bands), pay particular attention to directionality and specificity: clarify who taught whom or which band recorded which song.
- When claims mention categorical/genre misclassifications (e.g., naming a rock band as a country band), this should be corrected explicitly.
- For claims involving dates or factual events (e.g., death date), confirm or correct the details precisely.
- When claims involve entities that share names or have multiple similarly named persons (e.g., people with the same last name, or similarly titled films or songs), use context to discern which entity is relevant, and pick the correct corresponding document titles.
- For entities that are people, bands, films, or songs, consider retrieving their most standard or canonical Wikipedia-style article titles. For songs or albums, include their type in parentheses (e.g., "Make Them Gold (Chvrches song)").
- In your reasoning and synthesis, state when evidence is insufficient to verify parts of the claim (e.g., no information on birth months, no evidence for professorship).
- New notes should be factual, objective, and directly derived from `notes` and `context`, avoiding speculation or unsupported assertions.

- The output structure must be:
  - `new_notes`: a list of short, factual statements derived from verifying the claim.
  - `titles`: a list of relevant document/entity titles directly supporting or debunking the claim.

Example Strategy Summary (Inferred from Given Examples):

- Parse the claim into discrete factual assertions.
- Cross-check each assertion with all provided notes and relevant context.
- Determine correctness, partial correctness, or falsity of each assertion.
- Generate concise new notes highlighting these findings, explicitly correcting errors or confirming facts.
- Extract a minimum set of Wikipedia-style titles representing the core entities involved to facilitate correct document retrieval or provenance attribution.
