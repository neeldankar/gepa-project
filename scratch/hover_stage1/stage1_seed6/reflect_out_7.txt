Given three inputs: 
- `claim` (a statement that may be true or false about some entities, events, or facts), 
- `notes` (a list of extracted evidence snippets related to the claim), and 
- `context` (a broader set of documents or passages providing background or detailed information relevant to the claim),

your task is to produce two outputs: 
1. `new_notes`: a concise, non-redundant summary of key factually relevant evidence drawn primarily from the provided notes and context that supports or refutes the claim. Ensure that `new_notes` contain factual information directly pertinent to verifying the claim, removing duplicated points and emphasizing clarity and specificity. Paraphrasing is allowed if it increases clarity or accuracy.

2. `titles`: a list of the most relevant document titles or named entities referenced from the context (and possibly reflected in notes) that directly pertain to the claim and evidence you produce in `new_notes`. These should correspond to authoritative or primary sources of information that substantiate your factual summary.

Detailed guidance and domain-specific considerations to follow:

- Claims often assert factual relationships, such as existence or identity of entities (e.g., dog breeds), biographical roles (e.g., film directors, co-writers), historical dates (e.g., formation years of military units), or involvement in particular productions or events. Carefully verify the claim against multiple evidence snippets.

- The `notes` may contain repeated information, partial confirmations, or nuanced data. Consolidate overlapping information about the same fact to avoid redundancy in `new_notes`. 

- The `context` provides more authoritative and detailed descriptions, often including descriptions of individuals, entities, or historical background. Use this information to validate or refine points found in `notes`.

- When multiple entities or documents are relevant to the claim, ensure that the `new_notes` cover all necessary facts to explain the truthfulness or falsity of the claim.

- Document titles in `titles` should be the primary subjects whose information was used to verify the claim. Include exact main titles of articles, films, persons, organizations, or events as given in `context`.

- If the claim involves negations or comparative assertions (e.g., two dogs are not both breeds), ensure your evidence clearly addresses the relation and correctness of this assertion.

- When claim components involve multiple parts (e.g., a claim about a director and a co-writer), address each part distinctly and clearly show where the claim is accurate or inaccurate based on evidence.

- Historical and domain-specific facts (e.g., dates of military unit activation, breed origin and recognition, film production details, personnel involvement) need explicit and precise support.

- Assume that all entities, events, and references mentioned in the inputs are from formal knowledge bases or encyclopedic sources; maintain formal tone and exactness.

- Maintain a structured reasoning approach in your generation:
   * Start by interpreting the claim.
   * Evaluate the evidence in `notes`.
   * Cross-reference with the `context`.
   * Determine the accuracy of the claim.
   * Summarize the verifiable facts in `new_notes`.
   * Identify the key source documents or entities for `titles`.

- Avoid introducing information beyond what is present in `notes` and `context`. If information is contradictory, represent the most reliable consensus based on the context input.

- The final output should clearly support or refute the claim implicitly by the chosen `new_notes`, providing a factual basis that a reader can use to assess the claim's validity, and list source titles for traceability.
