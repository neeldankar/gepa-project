Task Description:
You will be given two textual fields as input:
1. `claim`: A factual or comparative statement involving one or more entities, events, or topics.
2. `notes`: A bulleted or enumerated list of gathered contextual information or evidence related to the claim, often including relevant facts, corrections, or clarifications to assist in verifying or evaluating the claim's components.

Your goal is to produce the field:
- `query`: A precise, focused set of one or more natural language questions that can be used to fact-check or verify critical components of the claim by retrieving additional specific, authoritative information.

Key Details and Strategy for Formulating Queries:
- The `claim` typically involves multiple implicit sub-claims or assertions, such as:
   - Identity or relationship between people, works, or events (e.g., whether a screenplay writer and an actor match, or if a director corresponds to a specific film).
   - Factual attributes (e.g., whether a screenplay was adapted from a novel, or if an individual has won a specific award).
   - Comparative popularity or influence between two entities.
- The `notes` provide partial, often nuanced factual context, including:
   - Confirmations or contradictions about entities named in the claim.
   - Potential ambiguities or common confusions (such as similar or misspelled titles, or incorrect role associations).
   - Additional details about award winnings, career roles, dates, or relationships between entities.
- Use the combined `claim` and `notes` to decompose the claim into distinct, verifiable questions that cover:
   - Any ambiguities about identity or naming (e.g., distinguishing between similar names or titles).
   - Fact-checking each key assertion in the claim, especially when the notes indicate uncertainty or correction.
   - Verifying relevant credentials or attributes (e.g., awards won, roles played, release years).
- The queries you produce should be:
   - Precise and unambiguous, targeting specific facts needed to confirm or refute the claim.
   - Comprehensive enough to address all components of the claim that require verification.
   - Structured as clear, factual questions (e.g., "Who wrote the screenplay for X?", "Did Y win a Nobel Prize in Literature?", "How does the popularity of game A compare to game B worldwide?").
- Avoid overly broad or ambiguous questions; focus queries on gaps or contradictions indicated in the notes.
- When the claim seems to confuse or conflate entities (e.g., misspelled titles or misattributed roles), include queries that seek to clarify the correct entity identification before verifying other details.
- If the claim involves a comparison (e.g., popularity or recognition), include comparative queries that specify the entities and the kind of comparison (popularity, global reach, critical acclaim).
- Repeat or confirm well-established facts only if necessary to address possible gaps revealed by the notes.

Example Strategy Summary:
1. Identify individual assertions or components within the claim.
2. Cross-check these against the notes to find which parts are uncertain, contradicted, or unconfirmed.
3. Generate focused queries for each uncertain or critical aspect.
4. Phrase the queries in natural, clear, and specific language that maps directly to the fact(s) you want to check.
5. When appropriate, include multi-part queries to cover complex claims involving several facts or relationships.

End Goal:
To enable a fact-checking retrieval or verification system to efficiently gather the relevant authoritative documents or data needed to confirm or refute the claim with high precision and clear targeted questions.
