Task Description:
Given an input with two fields: `claim` and `notes`, your goal is to produce a `query` that will guide further fact-checking or evidence retrieval to verify the claim. The `claim` is a statement that potentially involves multiple factual assertions or entities, often crossing several domains (e.g., sports, film, beverages). The `notes` field contains relevant contextual information, partial evidence, or contradictions relating to the claim. This information may clarify, dispute, or fail to confirm aspects of the claim.

Your task is to:
1. Carefully analyze the `claim` to identify all distinct factual assertions contained within it.
2. Analyze the `notes` to understand which elements of the claim are confirmed, contradicted, unclear, or unsupported.
3. Identify key entities (people, events, products, cultural references) and relationships among them that need disambiguation or fact-checking.
4. Construct a comprehensive, concise, and focused multi-part `query` that will retrieve or verify the factual evidence necessary to confirm or refute each core component of the claim. The query should:
   - Explicitly ask about all uncertain or contested facts found in the claim and notes.
   - Address potential ambiguities in names, titles, or attributes (e.g., nationality, debut dates, flavor profiles, event champions).
   - Include relevant domain-specific concepts and terms to maximize accurate document retrieval (e.g., tournament names, film titles, product brands, associated individuals).
   - Disambiguate similarly named entities and correct potential misreferences in the claim as exposed by the notes.
5. Ensure queries are structured to elicit direct answers or definitive information about the main factual points, avoiding vague or overly broad questions.

Domain-Specific Considerations:
- Distinguish between people’s identities, professions, nationalities, and affiliations (e.g., tennis players and their origins).
- Check filmographies thoroughly, focusing on debut roles, co-star appearances, nationality, and possible confusion in film titles.
- Verify product attributes such as flavors or advertising campaigns, especially when multiple related names or cultural references appear.
- Pay attention to subtleties like event subcategories (men’s doubles vs singles), potential confusion between similar names (e.g., "Krupa (song)"), and the relationship between the named entities and relevant attributes.
- Recognize and accommodate contradictions or gaps in notes that either confirm, refute, or leave unverified key parts of the claim.

Generalizable Strategy:
- Break down the claim into atomic factual units.
- Cross-reference the notes to identify the factual status or missing information about each unit.
- Formulate clear, targeted retrieval questions addressing each unresolved or contradicted fact.
- Cover missing links exposed by the notes, ensuring no critical fact related to verifying the claim is left unqueried.
- Avoid assuming external knowledge not present in notes; focus queries on uncovering or confirming information that bridges gaps found.

Output Format:
Produce only the `query` field as a set of natural language questions or a single multi-question query that covers all relevant factual gaps for verification, based on the analysis above.
