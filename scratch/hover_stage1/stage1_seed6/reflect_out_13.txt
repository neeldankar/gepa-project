Task Description:

You will be provided with three input fields: `claim`, `notes`, and `context`.

- `claim`: A factual or historical statement that needs to be evaluated or explained.
- `notes`: A list of statements or data points that relate to the claim, often summarizing or repeating information from the context.
- `context`: A list of longer text excerpts, often encyclopedia-style or biographical entries, providing detailed factual information on persons, institutions, events, or concepts mentioned in the claim.

Your goal is to produce two output fields: `new_notes` and `titles`.

1. **Reasoning Approach** (internal, used to generate outputs):

   - Carefully analyze the claim and identify what factual components or assertions it contains.
   - Use the `context` to verify or refute parts of the claim, confirming relevant dates, associations, roles, or biographical details.
   - Compare and analyze the `notes` in the light of the `context` and the claim, to select the most accurate, relevant, and non-redundant points.
   - Correct inaccuracies or explicitly note when information is missing or unsupported for a part of the claim.
   - Identify key factual elements, entities, and topics necessary to understand or verify the claim.

2. **New Notes (`new_notes`)**:

   - Provide a concise, accurate, and factually correct subset or summary derived from the input `notes` and validated by the `context`.
   - Include clarifications or corrections (e.g., remove clearly false statements or outdated assertions).
   - Emphasize key factual points that support or refute the claim.
   - Avoid redundant or contradictory information.
   - When information is unavailable or unconfirmed in the context, state this clearly.

3. **Titles (`titles`)**:

   - Extract and list the main entities, concepts, or document titles that are essential to understanding, verifying, or contextualizing the claim.
   - These often include names of people, institutions, historical events, or specialized terms appearing in the claim or relevant to the reasoning.
   - Aim to retrieve as many relevant titles as possible (at least those necessary to validate the claim) but avoid irrelevant or tangential titles.
   - Titles resemble document or article headings directly relevant to the claim and context.

4. **Domain-specific considerations:**

   - The task often involves precise historical, biographical, or institutional facts such as founding dates of universities, postdoctoral training institutions, commandants of historical camps, authorship of literary works, and professional/academic affiliations.
   - Pay close attention to temporal relationships (e.g., founded before or after certain institutions or events).
   - Recognize that some claims mix entities with similar or related names (e.g., “Rainer Hoss” vs. “Rudolf Höss”); careful disambiguation is needed.
   - Understanding academic career stages like postdoctoral training, command structures in historical contexts, or literary author identities may be required.

5. **Generalizable strategy:**

   - Start by decomposing the claim into atomic factual statements.
   - Cross-reference each statement with the `context` to confirm accuracy or falsehood.
   - Use `notes` as a supporting pool but refine based on `context` truthfulness.
   - Summarize and synthesize the validated information into clear and accurate `new_notes`.
   - Select `titles` that reflect the key entities or documents most directly related to the claim and its verification.
   - Explicitly state if the claim is unsupported or false based on the given information.

By following these instructions, your responses will be well-structured, factually accurate, and provide clear evidence-based summaries and relevant document references for each claim.
